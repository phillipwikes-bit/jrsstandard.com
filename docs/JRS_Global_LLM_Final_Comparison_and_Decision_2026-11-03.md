# Global LLM Ecosystem, Final Comparative Assessment and JRS Decision Record

**Research cutoff:** 3 November 2026  
**Disposition:** DOCUMENTARY COMPARISON COMPLETE. JRS-SPECIFIC EMPIRICAL VALIDATION NOT CONDUCTED.  
**Audience:** JRS research, method governance and potential privately authorized experimental work.  
**Supersedes for interpretation:** The provisional conclusions and single-value price illustrations of the earlier Global LLM Ecosystem Benchmark. The original benchmark and source verification register are retained, not silently rewritten.  
**No release authorization:** No model API was invoked, no live case data was processed, no expenditure was authorized, no production website or PDF changed, and the closed/local-only Review Engine remains closed.

## Executive decision

The defensible conclusion is not that one frontier model is "best." Independent general-purpose intelligence scores, current provider prices and advertised context windows are **insufficient to establish the reliability of a retained-record review workflow**. For JRS, the strongest experimental direction is a **source-first, read-only, human-confirmed assistance process** that requires complete evidence coverage, exact edition and passage identity, explicit uncertainty and a retained reviewer disposition. Autonomous record grading, finalization, legal inference and case submission are outside the current mandate.

**Five decisions supported by the research:**

1. Keep the existing public JRS method and free resources human-applied. Do not make an AI engine central to the public offering or confuse model capability with validation of the Standard.
2. Use an approved, versioned **source manifest plus deterministic document and chronology checks** before introducing generative assistance. Test completeness rather than only relevance.
3. For a *future, separately authorized synthetic-data comparison*, use representative providers from three classes, low-cost extraction, balanced reasoning, high-effort reference, plus at least one released-weight alternative; do not preselect a winning model.
4. Reject any architecture that allows a model or retrieved document to approve records, execute unapproved tools, release information between cases, or alter published JRS documents.
5. Judge cost per *accepted, independently verified review observation*, including missed evidence, corrections, latency and risk, not cost per token alone.

These decisions concern research direction, not verified technical implementation.

## A. Strict comparison basis

There are five distinct objects of comparison:

| Object | What published sources can establish | What they do **not** establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A specific model at a specified effort and endpoint | Composite benchmark performance, nominal context, API or weight availability | Accuracy on JRS conditions, source completeness, legal correctness |
| The hosted inference service | Published tariff, API features, regional/contract controls | Real JRS p95 latency, reproducibility, privacy conformance |
| An open-weight checkpoint | Stated license, downloadable artifacts, hardware-related specifications | Affordability of self-hosting, safe multi-tenancy, support SLA |
| An orchestration/retrieval framework | Features and published control APIs | That an end-to-end application actually enforces security or retrieves all evidence |
| A product interaction interface | Documented source linking and review features | Measured reviewer comprehension, accessibility acceptance or actual adoption |

**Evidence grades in this report are descriptive only:** I = independent third-party model benchmark; V = primary vendor documentation; R = peer-reviewed or published research; P = proposed JRS design; NT = not tested on JRS. An I or V label does not upgrade a P into a validated capability.

### Source reconciliation that changes the matrix

**1. Google pricing is tier-dependent, not contradictory in the canonical pricing table.** The current canonical page identifies Gemini 4 Argon **Standard** at $2 input/$10 output per million through 30 November 2026, becoming $4/$20 on 1 December. It identifies **Batch and Flex** separately at $1/$5, becoming $2/$10. An earlier short search excerpt appeared to attach the $5 output figure to Standard; direct inspection of the canonical, tier-labeled source resolved that ambiguity. At the illustrative workload, Standard is **$60** now and **$120** after the published change; Batch/Flex is **$30** now and **$60** afterward. Batch/Flex availability, latency and work constraints must be evaluated separately from Standard. Source: https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing

**2. Mistral Large 4 has materially different displayed price presentations.** Some official published pages show approximately $0.68/$2.09 (sale), $0.70/$2.10 (launch or another current view), or $1.36/$4.18 (full-price display). Resulting illustrative costs are respectively **$17.78, $18.20 and $35.56**. These are snapshots/tiers, not proof of conflicting billing for the same account and date. The actual account/endpoint invoice rate must be checked before making a purchasing recommendation. Sources: https://docs.mistral.ai/models/mistral-large-4-0 and https://huggingface.co/blog/mistral-large4

**3. Price is not cost per successful review.** Hidden reasoning tokens, context surcharges, cache writes, search tools, failed requests, source-verification retries, infrastructure and reviewer time are excluded. A real cost-quality frontier cannot be computed without matched JRS case outcomes and a common charging model.

**4. Open-weight is not equivalent to open-source services or free deployment.** DeepSeek's specific released model card states MIT for its checkpoint; Mistral's ecosystem license includes model-as-a-service limitations. GLM, other models and hosting services have their own agreements. Nor does a nominal million-token context imply high effective recall across every source in that window.

**5. Model index rankings are effort-sensitive and time-bound.** The 27 October v5 launch had Opus max at 58 ahead of Astra at 57. The later limited Carbon high model scored 59. The two observations are chronologically consistent; a restricted-access model does not become a general deployment option through a leaderboard result.

## C. Architecture and platform layer comparison

| Operating vector | Documented candidate approach | Strength | Risks/limitations | JRS choice for a private experiment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow orchestration | OpenAI Agents SDK, LangGraph, Semantic Kernel | Tool calls, handoffs, tracing or durable review interrupts | Unequal guardrail scopes, replay/side effects, complexity | **Prefer a simple explicit state machine first**. Adopt a framework only where its checkpoint/approval feature is needed. |
| Retrieval | Deterministic source lookup, semantic top-k, hybrid, LlamaIndex citation facilities | Exact identity plus conceptual search and citation chunks | Top-k retrieves some rather than all required evidence, document metadata loss | Hybrid **candidate**, benchmark against full authorized context and exact search, with all-required-source gate. |
| Inference serving | Hosted APIs; vLLM or SGLang for approved released weights | Managed convenience or infrastructure control; prefix caching/batching | Hardware/TCO, data residency, shared-cache risks, logging and operational support | Start with separately authorized synthetic hosted evaluations; consider private serving only after security, license and TCO review. |
| Safety and alignment | Provider safeguards, structured output, access filters, independent validators | Can constrain format and surface ambiguity | Prompt injection and policy noncompliance remain; formatted output may be wrong | Do not trust model self-assessment. Enforce source IDs, permissions and valid spans outside the model. |
| Audit and replay | Immutable manifests, model snapshot, prompt hash, reviewer disposition | Reproducibility across model generations | Sensitive data exposure in traces and state history | Log minimal approved metadata and actual human corrections, never raw case data by default. |
| Runtime economics | Router, small-model extraction, selective escalation | May reduce costs or improve responsiveness | Routing error may omit evidence; multiple calls increase latency/cost | **Do not implement until an ablation proves value** and includes fallback/abstention. |

### Source-completeness finding

Two published October 2026 EMNLP studies offer the most directly relevant architectural caution:

- [The Bottleneck Is Retrieval, Not Reading](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.2222/) found that chunk-based similarity search can retrieve *a* relevant source without retrieving **all** passages needed for multi-fact answers. Splitting text can also remove the document context needed to resolve identity and attribution.
- [Diagnosing the Fact-Grounding Gap](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.834/) found that fact extraction can fail **after** the required passage is retrieved; those extraction failures are not fixed by a retrieval-only intervention.

**JRS experimental implication:** Measure (a) required-source retrieval, (b) fact extraction from the available source, (c) unsupported synthesis, (d) contradiction preservation, (e) chronology, and (f) reviewer disposition as **separate failure modes**. An apparently fluent answer with citations can still be incomplete. Neither study establishes a numerical JRS improvement from any proposed architecture.

## D. Safety controls and 2026 reference edition

Use the [OWASP LLM Top 10 for **2026**](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-genai-llm-top-10-2026/), not the superseded 2025 ranking, as the current application-security reference, alongside the [OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/) and the voluntary [NIST Generative AI Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence). Risk rankings and individual mitigations must be cited to their actual editions.

| Threat | Nondelegable gate | Verification required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Embedded hostile instructions in witness documents | Source files treated as data, no document-to-tool authority | Synthetic prompt-injection tests across all input routes |
| Unsupported assertion or fabricated source | Exact page/line and edition verification; explicit unknown/abstain output | Human-adjudicated claim/source precision |
| Wrong record, wrong case or wrong version | Permission filtering prior to retrieval and before each tool call; version lock | Cross-case and wrong-edition isolation tests |
| Leaking private content through tracing | Explicitly disable or minimize sensitive fields; govern retention/export | Review logs for forbidden source content |
| Agent performs unapproved writes | Read-only tools by default and separate server-side authorization | Denial tests for write, email, publication and private-source export |
| Cost exhaustion and failure truncation | Time/token/retry budgets and fail-closed incomplete states | Stress tests and incomplete-response inventory |
| Overstated JRS assurance | No automated acceptance, legal conclusion or production certificate | Signed human disposition and release controls |

**SDK caveat:** The OpenAI Agents SDK tracing documentation states sensitive inputs/outputs may be captured by default. Its setting trace_include_sensitive_data defaults to true; disable or restrict it and assess other logging systems. Source: https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/tracing/

**Recommended JRS interaction, not an implemented product:** Show a proposed documentation observation alongside the exact source title, edition, passage, relevant contradictions and missing evidence. Make the next action one of **confirm, correct, insufficient source, escalate**. Record who accepted the observation and when. The system must not report a legally defensible decision, certify compliance or silently finalize a permanent record. Keyboard, screen-reader, zoom/reflow and privacy acceptance remain independent prerequisites.

## F. Decision matrix and research shortlist

The following is a prioritization of **experimental roles**, not model quality rankings for JRS.

| Experimental role | Candidate(s) | Reason to include | Stopping condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Human baseline | Published JRS references with qualified reviewers | Establish current reviewer reliability and correction time | Invalid gold labels, insufficient agreement or unclear JRS outcome definition |
| Deterministic baseline | Edition/source/date/span check without LLM | Test how much benefit basic control logic supplies | Incorrect source identity or unapproved access |
| Low-cost extraction | GPT-6 Luna or GPT-6.1 Terra; optional DeepSeek hosted comparator | Test structured source identification without analytic authority | Unsupported extracted dates or sources |
| Balanced multi-vendor reasoning | GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Sonnet 5.5, Gemini 4 Argon Standard | Comparable public hosted options with independent-vendor diversity; similar nominal cost for Sol, Sonnet, Argon | Excess unsupported conclusions, source omissions, unapproved data handling |
| High-effort reference | Claude Opus 5.5 and/or GPT-6 Astra | Bound difficult-case performance and correction costs | Budget/run-cap exceeded; no incremental evidence value |
| Released-weight feasibility | DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017, GLM-5.3, or Mistral Large 4 | Test controllable deployment and different license/hardware constraints | License, infrastructure, isolation, accuracy or TCO not approved |
| Restricted/pre-release watch | Gemini 4 Carbon, unreleased Kimi checkpoint | May inform later research when generally available | **Excluded from present purchasable/self-hostable test set** |
| Autonomous multi-agent finalizer | None | Adds unvalidated authority and attack surface | **No-go under current JRS scope** |

There is **no empirically justified single model winner**. Any future model selection should be evaluated on identical, authorized cases using:
- Required-source completeness, exact source citation and edition correctness;
- Gap recall and false gaps across all five JRS conditions;
- Unsupported conclusions, chronology errors, contradiction retention and proper abstention;
- Reviewer correction time and independent acceptance;
- End-to-end p50/p95, actual tokens/tools, operational cost and security/permission incidents.

A model that is cheap but frequently produces ungrounded documentation findings does not have a favorable real-world cost profile.

## G. Reconciliation of the two proposed study sizes

Existing research documents contain **two alternative unapproved designs**, not an executed 200-plus-case program:

| Option | Structure | Strength | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 120 cases across six broad groups, with a 20-case preliminary pilot described in the older proposal | Larger nominal sample per group | Mixes edition/prompt-injection considerations into wider strata; test split needs clarification |
| B | 20 separate calibration cases plus 80 held-out cases in eight groups, 10 held-out per group | Explicit separation of editions, AI-polished and injected cases; cleaner source and failure taxonomy | Thin per-stratum estimation; not statistically powered by assertion |

**Design recommendation:** Select Option B as the initial *planning basis* for annotation feasibility, because it creates clearer coverage of publication-identity and adversarial source failures. The initial 20 are calibration cases and the 80 are an **additional** held-out set, not the same 20 counted twice. Before authorizing any model run, perform an independent sample-size/power analysis using plausible baseline gap rates, correlation across cases/reviewers and the minimal meaningful difference. Expand or revise the proposed number accordingly. Do not claim that either candidate has already been approved, assembled or run.

- End-to-end token counts, costs and p95 latency under JRS workloads;
- Tenant isolation, data retention compliance, penetration testing or contract approval;
- Accessibility and human-in-the-loop task effectiveness of an actual JRS AI interface;
- Outcome improvement in real organizational records.
