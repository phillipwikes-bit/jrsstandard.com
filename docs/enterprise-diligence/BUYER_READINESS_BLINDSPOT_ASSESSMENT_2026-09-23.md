# Buyer Readiness Checklist: Blindspot Assessment

**Date:** 2026-09-23 · **Classification:** M Transferability / Acquisition Readiness, with
G Commercial and A Strategic components · **Authority:** analysis only. No decision made.

> **This assesses what the checklist does not cover.** It does not restate what it covers
> well, which is most things. The checklist is unusually disciplined: it separates a plan
> from a result, refuses to let a completed document count as a completed test, preserves
> negative evidence, and states in Section 8 that the Engine is unvalidated. Those are the
> right instincts and nothing below disputes them.

---

## 1. Current situation and objective

**FACT.** The checklist contains approximately 100 items across eleven sections, with 45
marked P0. Every rights item, RT-01 through RT-14, closes on an action by counsel.

**FACT, from `research/IP_SALE_TRACKER.md`.** Live organizations 0. Sessions 0. Revenue $0.
The recorded licensing probability is 15 to 25 percent.

**FACT, from `docs/enterprise-diligence/HUMAN_DECISIONS_REQUIRED.md`.** D-6, submit the
evidence package to IP counsel, is open and has been since the register was built.

**INFERENCE.** The checklist is a diligence response prepared before a diligence request
exists. That is not wrong, and §XLI of the governing prompt endorses progressive data-room
readiness. It does change which blindspots matter.

## 2. The blindspots

### B1. No counterparty, and no gate that asks whether to proceed

**FACT.** CM-01 asks the owner to name a candidate user, technical evaluator, economic buyer
and decision owner. No such party is recorded anywhere in the estate.

**The structural issue.** Section 7's gate table runs from honest introductory discussion to
definitive agreement. **Every gate leads toward a transaction.** There is no gate whose
outcome is *continue building the asset and do not transact now*, although §II of the
governing prompt states plainly that commercialization is optional and §XLII requires every
infrastructure decision to answer what concrete trigger requires it now.

**A 100-item buyer readiness programme is itself infrastructure.** The checklist does not
apply the trigger test to its own existence.

**Assess:** what concrete trigger justifies executing the checklist now rather than holding
it as a template against a first genuine inquiry.

### B2. Key-person dependency is absent, and it is the largest transferable-value risk

**FACT.** The word *founder* appears nine times in the checklist. Every occurrence is a
transaction term: founder assistance, founder hours, founder interpretation. **Key-person,
bus factor and succession appear zero times.**

**FACT.** `.jrs/registries/DEPENDENCY_REGISTER.json` records a key-person section with
**12 MATERIAL** functions and a **bus factor of 1 on two human committer identities**.

**FACT.** §XL of the governing prompt names key-person dependency as *a material structural
risk* and sets reducing it as a transferability requirement.

**The gap.** The checklist treats the founder dependency as something to cap in a contract
(TX-04, CM-08) rather than something to reduce in the asset. **Capping founder hours does not
make the asset understandable without the founder. It just limits how long the buyer can ask.**

**Assess:** which of the 12 MATERIAL functions can be documented to the point where a
competent outsider performs them, and which genuinely cannot. That work raises asset value
whether or not a sale occurs, which is the test §II sets.

### B3. Unbounded counsel dependency with no cost gate

**FACT.** Fourteen rights items close on counsel returning, determining, stating, scheduling
or compiling something. Most are P0.

**FACT.** The checklist contains no budget, no cost estimate and no timeline. The word
*budget* appears zero times, *timeline* zero times.

**The gap.** The entire rights workstream is blocked on one unpurchased input, and the
document does not ask what it costs or whether the spend is justified before a counterparty
exists. **For a solo operator at $0 revenue, that is the decision, and it is not on the page.**

**Assess:** the narrowest counsel scope that unblocks the most items, and its price, before
commissioning the full RT-01 to RT-14 programme.

### B4. What is actually being sold, given that the standard is public

**FACT.** §XIV of the governing prompt sets the rule: publicize the standard, protect the
implementation. RT-11 and DR-02 handle public access as a rights question.

**The gap.** **No item asks what a buyer gets that the public does not.** If the Standard, the
DRR definition, the five conditions, the field guides, the training and the research are all
public and free, the purchasable asset is the implementation, the data, the marks and the
provenance record. That is a defensible bundle, but the checklist never states it as the
proposition.

**This is the first question a real buyer asks**, and the checklist has no answer prepared.

**Assess:** write the one-paragraph answer to *what do I get that I cannot already download*,
and test whether the 38-item schedule actually supports it.

### B5. The 38-item schedule is frozen before the deal type is chosen

**FACT.** BR-01 asks the owner to select the offer type. BR-02 freezes 38 inventory IDs.

**FACT.** §XXX of the governing prompt states the bundle *must be determined from the actual
rights architecture and transaction requirements*.

**The gap.** BR-02 fixes the bundle before BR-01's answer is known and before any buyer has
stated what they want. An evaluation licence, an exclusive licence and an outright
divestiture require materially different schedules.

**Assess:** whether freezing the inventory IDs for traceability, which is sound, has been
confused with freezing the offer contents, which is premature.

### B6. No expiry, and most completed items decay

**FACT.** EV-16 defines validation expiry. **Nothing defines checklist expiry.** *Abandon*
appears zero times.

**The gap.** BR-04 pins a release snapshot. AP-01 verifies a deployed contract. AP-09 verifies
deletion behaviour. RS-03 records a journal decision. **Every one of those is a fact with a
half-life.** If the checklist is executed over twelve months, the early items are stale before
the late ones are done, and a buyer inspecting in month twelve cannot tell which.

**Assess:** an as-of date and a re-verification trigger per item, not per document.

### B7. The disclosure schedule omits three of four journal declines

**FACT.** CM-09 requires a disclosure schedule of unresolved title, contributor and publisher
permissions, Engine error, unvalidated state, Manifest live gap and provider uncertainties.
RS-03 covers the AI and Ethics decision of 22 September.

**FACT.** The checklist contains **zero** mentions of the Journal of Civic Information, Records
Management Journal or the Journal of Contemporary Archival Studies.

**FACT, from the estate.** The public-records paper was declined by JOCI on 2026-09-01 on fit,
in 2h31m with no reviewer assigned, and by Records Management Journal on grounds that are
**not recorded anywhere**. JCAS declined at presubmission on 2026-09-23.

**The gap.** A buyer's diligence will find these. Disclosed, they are unremarkable: fit
rejections carry no information about quality. Discovered, they look concealed.

**Assess:** add the publication history to CM-09, and close the RMJ grounds gap, which is the
only one of the four whose reason the estate cannot state.

### B8. No competitive or substitutability assessment

**FACT.** *Competitor* appears zero times. TX-05 covers buyer build-versus-buy, but only after
a specific buyer exists and only using measures from that buyer's own test.

**The gap.** If a buyer can rebuild the implementation in a quarter, or an incumbent already
ships an equivalent control, the rights work and the validation programme do not change the
answer. **That assessment costs days and could reprioritise everything below it.**

**Assess:** what exists in adjacent markets, and what the honest replacement cost of the
implementation is.

### B9. Tax consequences are a decision input, not a diligence item

**FACT.** *Tax* appears four times, always as something for professional review inside a
transaction (CM-03, CM-06, TX-07).

**FACT.** §XXXVI of the governing prompt requires factual recording first and tax
characterisation second, and treats structure as trigger-driven.

**The gap.** For an individual selling personally developed IP, the tax treatment can
materially change whether a given structure is worth doing. **That belongs before deal design,
not inside it.** No item asks the question early enough to influence B5.

**Assess:** a bounded question to a tax professional about the shape of the likely outcomes,
before the deal type is selected.

### B10. Seller capacity is the binding constraint and is unmodelled

**OBSERVATION.** One person. Approximately 100 items. Forty-five at P0. Most requiring a third
party: counsel, independent adjudicators, an independent operator for AP-06 and EV-15,
qualified reviewers for EV-05, a buyer for DE-05 and EV-14.

**The gap.** The checklist is correct about what a complete diligence position requires. It is
silent on whether that position is reachable by the available resources, and therefore on
which subset to actually attempt.

**Assess:** the smallest subset that would let an honest introductory conversation happen,
which is the first gate in Section 7 and the only one currently reachable.

### B11. No item preserves the option of not selling

**FACT.** §II: *Commercialization is optional. The existence of commercial potential does not
create an obligation to commercialize immediately.*

**The gap.** Nothing in the checklist records what is gained by holding the asset, what
optionality a sale extinguishes, or what the reversion position is if a transaction is
declined. Section 7's *If not met* column always narrows the claim or defers the item. **It
never says stop.**

**Assess:** state the hold case explicitly, so that declining a transaction is a recorded
decision rather than a failure to complete a checklist.

### B12. Independence of the validation is asserted but unsourced

**FACT.** EV-05 requires qualified reviewers labelling without seeing Engine predictions.
EV-15 requires an independent operator to rerun without founder interpretation. AP-06 requires
a buyer operator to build and deploy. TE-02 requires independent adjudication.

**The gap.** **The checklist never asks where these people come from, what they cost, or
whether any are identified.** The estate records one independent second reader across the
entire research programme. Independence is the load-bearing element of Section 8, and its
supply is unaddressed.

**Assess:** name the pool, or narrow the validation claim to what can actually be
independently checked.

## 3. What the checklist gets right, stated so the corrections are not misread

- It separates a plan from a result, and says so: *adding a validation plan does not validate
  the Engine*.
- It refuses to let a completed document count as a completed test.
- Section 8 states the Engine is unvalidated for buyer-facing performance claims, and keeps
  E-037 (human detection panel) and E-038 (separate reliability sample, criterion not met)
  distinct from any Engine result.
- TE-06, WC-04 and EV-10 preserve a known adverse output rather than removing it.
- It instructs that grades do not advance when a box is ticked.
- RT-02 preserves the bounded zero-assignment finding as historically accurate even if new
  evidence appears, which is the correct handling under §XIII.

**None of the blindspots above is a defect in what the checklist asserts. Each is something
outside its frame.**

---

## 4. Evidence gap and smallest meaningful next action

**What would change the analysis:** a single genuine inbound inquiry. It would supply the
counterparty B1 lacks, fix the offer type B5 needs, scope the counsel work B3 cannot bound,
and answer B4 by revealing what that buyer actually wants.

**Smallest meaningful next action, one item:** write the B4 paragraph. *What does a buyer get
that is not already public?* It costs an hour, requires no third party, no counsel and no
spend, and it is upstream of B5, B8 and the entire Section 6 offer description. **If that
paragraph cannot be written convincingly, the rest of the checklist is premature and that is
itself the finding.**
