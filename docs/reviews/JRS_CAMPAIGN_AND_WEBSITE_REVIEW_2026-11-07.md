# JRS campaign and website review

**Prepared:** November 7, 2026  
**Scope:** Campaign precedents, the proposed AI apex-predator metaphor, and the public JRS website. “DRUG” is interpreted as DRR, Decision Reconstruction Risk. This interpretation remains an assumption. Campaign descriptions below derive from campaign-owner materials or identified archives. Recommendations are editorial judgments, not measured JRS campaign results.

## Assessment

The closest visual precedent is WWF's shark campaign. The stronger structural precedents for JRS are the Guardian's Points of View and Transport for London's Awareness Test. They give the audience an initial interpretation and then make that interpretation inadequate. For JRS, the resulting uncertainty should concern what the retained evidence establishes about a decision.

The shark is a usable visual metaphor. Describing DRR as “the AI apex predator” does not establish its prevalence, severity relative to other AI risks, or dependence on AI. DRR can arise in human-authored records. AI-assisted drafting can make an incomplete evidentiary foundation less apparent when the prose appears complete. The campaign should demonstrate that specific problem.

The central creative requirement is a change in the reader's understanding. The viewer should first be able to explain a recommendation and then discover that the final approval has not documented its controlling rationale. That distinction must be encountered in the record before it is explained by the campaign.

## Comparable campaigns

| Campaign | Documented creative mechanism | Application to JRS | Limitation of the comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Guardian, Points of View** | An apparent assault becomes a rescue when the frame widens. | Let the audience adopt a plausible account, then examine additional sources that test it. | JRS may end with a reason that cannot be established. Do not manufacture a final revelation of the true reason. |
| **Transport for London, Awareness Test / Moonwalking Bear** | The audience's assigned task directs attention; replay exposes something missed. TfL's qualitative research distinguishes experiencing the phenomenon from merely illustrating it. | Give the viewer a real reconstruction task. Return to the same approval after source examination. | TfL also recorded weak connections between the memorable trick and cycling for some respondents. A memorable shark does not establish understanding of DRR. |
| **WWF / DDB Turkey, 2010 print campaign** | A perceived threat is paired with its absence, changing which condition should cause concern. | Use the shark to attract attention, then make the documentary condition the unresolved concern. | The environmental argument does not establish any JRS or AI-risk claim. Borrow the reversal, not the artwork, wording or ecological analogy. |
| **Sandy Hook Promise, Evan** | A foreground relationship draws attention; subsequent reflection and replay examine missed warning signs. | Return to information already present in the file, allowing viewers to reconsider their first account. | Borrow the attention structure only. The subjects and consequences are not equivalent. Documentation gaps do not diagnose an individual's intent. |
| **Dove, The Code / Keep Beauty Real** | A specific AI-related concern is tied to a defined brand commitment about the use of AI in images of women. | State the precise documentation problem and the specific review action JRS supports. | Dove's pledge concerns its own imagery. It is not evidence about JRS effectiveness or AI-generated documentation generally. |
| **Partnership for Drug-Free Kids, Fried Egg 2016** | A familiar object metaphor is revisited through questions and supporting resources. The organization's report describes the move from awareness to direct help. | Pair a memorable visual with a practical next action: examine a fictional file, then use the manual review questions. | Recognizable symbolism is not proof of accurate comprehension or behavior change. No claim about the campaign's health effects is made here. |

These comparisons assess creative mechanisms. Awards, recognition and reported reach have not been treated as evidence that a JRS adaptation will work.

## Recommended campaign structure

1. **Encounter the completed file.** Show a recommendation, final approval and plausible retained-file index. Identify the material as fictional without pre-announcing the defect.
2. **Form an initial account.** Give the viewer time to follow the recommendation and its stated source basis.
3. **Examine the sources.** Show what supports the investigator's recommendation and a separate issue that also went to the final decision-maker.
4. **Return to the unchanged approval.** Preserve the same decision text. Let the viewer attempt to connect the available reasons to the organization's approval.
5. **Pause before interpretation.** Ask the neutral question only after the source examination: “What question does this record leave you with?”
6. **Explain the limit after the viewer's attempt.** The recommendation has a documented rationale. The final approval does not identify which rationale controlled. Other authorized evidence might resolve the issue, but the campaign does not invent it.
7. **Offer one practical next action.** Link to the relevant manual JRS review aid. Keep the viewer in the role of reviewer.

For the affected-person hiring version, retain the closing question: **“Why wasn't I hired?”** The preceding source examination must make that question arise from the documented nonselection and an unresolved controlling rationale.

## Treatment of the shark

Retain the recognizable shark and the requested paper-biting action as an expressive device. Keep the documentary puzzle visible and readable before the bite. If the shark destroys the only supporting record before viewers examine it, the scene supplies a different explanation: the evidence is unavailable because it was eaten. That weakens the intended reconstruction problem.

A suitable revision would have the shark consume a duplicate final notice marked “Not a good fit” after the viewer has examined the intact source packet. The returned approval and the evidentiary limitation should remain available. The bite cannot substitute for the distinction between a recommendation and final approval.

Motion should preserve document readability. Words should move with their paper surface and water displacement. Source identifiers, dates, quotations and the final approval must remain stable enough to compare. Reading time should be established through phone viewing, not assumed from animation smoothness.

**Proposed supporting line:** “The decision survives. Can its reasoning be reconstructed?”

This is proposed copy, not a claim of demonstrated field prevalence or effectiveness. The institutional website should continue to identify JRS as a human-applied documentation-review instrument in operational validation.

## Website revisions prepared

The professional campaign now presents a fictional termination file before its conceptual explanation. The retained sources support the investigator's safety-briefing rationale and place a separate credential issue before the board. The same board approval is displayed again. A neutral reflection prompt precedes a separately opened interpretation.

The public-interest campaign uses a fictional hiring packet. Its recommendation cites evening unavailability, while the retained candidate response records evening availability. The final notice does not establish whether that recommendation or another rationale controlled. Its interpretation retains “Why wasn't I hired?”

Both exercises use native disclosures and require no answer submission, scoring, timed reveal or new reveal telemetry. Existing optional support forms remain separate. The homepage links directly to the professional reconstruction. Three campaign routes and seven topic-reference routes now use the existing public reference stylesheet. The email drafting tool's sending behavior has not changed.

The current public page inventories and tests have also been aligned with retired API routes and conceptual Manifest guidance. No removed Engine, schema download or record-processing service has been restored. Existing research findings, editions, private owner surfaces, production configuration and PDF files remain unchanged.

## Acceptance and limits

The intended professional response should independently distinguish the investigator's recommendation from the organization's approval. Accept paraphrases that clearly identify that distinction. A generic request for “more information” does not establish that the viewer has made it.

For a future audience test, stop before the interpretation and before a leading closing question. Use only: **“What question does this record leave you with?”** Preserve the viewer's response before any follow-up. Record spontaneous identification separately from prompted recognition. A convenience sample can guide revision but does not establish a population success rate.

**Audience comprehension: NOT VALIDATED.** No unfamiliar professional viewer responses were collected in this work. No A+ production or comprehension grade is assigned. The current rendered film was not inspected or revised in this task, and no claim is made that its motion, audio or iPhone playback has been corrected.

The associated verification record identifies source, browser and publication boundaries. Publication of the prepared site revisions requires owner authorization under the repository's current handoff. No production deployment has occurred.
