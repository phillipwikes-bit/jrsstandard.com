# JRS Header Positioning: Competitive Narrative Review and Release Rationale

**Review date:** 3 November 2026  
**Scope:** Public messaging of ten direct or adjacent software vendors; JRS's institutional header, homepage and practitioner pathways.  
**Assessment status:** Qualitative editorial comparison. It is **not** a conversion experiment, traffic audit, customer perception study, SEO test, independent accessibility test or evidence that JRS outperforms another provider.

## Decision

**Selected header line: “Check the record. No new software required.”**

Use it as a *short point-of-difference in the open desktop header space*, immediately beside the JRS wordmark and before existing edition/page metadata. Keep the original homepage question **“Can the record explain the decision after its author is gone?”** as the principal headline. Keep the following full statement **verbatim and prominently placed** in the homepage, resource and organizational-information routes:

> Organizations do not need to purchase or replace a software platform to begin examining whether their decision records preserve sufficient evidence, chronology and reasoning for later independent review.

Do **not** replace the Standard's governing review maxim (“If it does not stand on its own, do not finalize it”), the five-condition method, published-edition distinctions or candid research limitations with sales copy. Decision Spellchecker remains a distinct prospective organizational product without a sales path on this site.

### What “disruption” means here

A practitioner can use the **public free method** to begin examining decision records without a procurement process. That is a differentiated *entry point*. JRS must not imply it supplies, replaces or surpasses case intake, document storage, SSO, audit logs, automated monitoring, integrated workpapers, SLAs or the specialized operational features of a commercial system. JRS also cannot claim superior detection rates until a controlled same-task trial establishes that outcome.

## Qualitative header options: editorial grades, not measured results

The rubric is **25 points each** for immediate clarity, competitive distinction, fidelity to JRS's actual scope, and fit within the institutional header (total 100).

| Candidate | Clarity | Distinction | Fidelity | Header fit | Overall |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| “If it does not stand on its own, do not finalize it.” | 24 | 18 | 25 | 22 | **89** |
| “You do not need to purchase or replace software to begin examining your AI-related records.” | 22 | 23 | 17 | 21 | **83** |
| **“Check the record. No new software required.”** | **25** | **24** | **24** | **22** | **95** |

**Why the selected line:** It gives the professional a concrete first action, rejects the false premise that a new platform is a prerequisite, includes records drafted without AI, and fits the available desktop header space. Its score is a structured editorial judgment and does not predict traffic, conversions, training completions or market disruption.

The original maxim remains valuable in method and training contexts where a practitioner is examining a proposed finalization. It does not convey the public resource's cost/access proposition as quickly as the challenger line.

## Implemented website scope

The short motto is a single static, semantic span next to the existing wordmark on the homepage, Resources, Investigator Guides, Standard methodology, Research, About, and Codebook headers. It does not add a navigation item, registration field, tracking event or commercial destination.

The institutional shared CSS makes it legible at full desktop widths, uses the otherwise empty center header space and preserves color contrast. At widths below 1,020 px it is hidden to avoid displacing navigation and the main action; the full public-interest proposition and DRR explanation remain available in page content. Native text is retained, with forced-colors handling.

The previous homepage question, free seven-point check action, 16-resource directory, six training modules, investigator guide/PDF edition labels, explicit research limitations, non-scoring boundary and protected owner endpoints remain as before. No public Decision Spellchecker sales route is created.

## Verification and release boundary

Require source-level checks that:
1. The original homepage H1 and precise long-form promise survive;
2. Each edited institutional header includes the short motto once, next to the JRS wordmark;
3. No accidental new sales or external case submission is created;
4. The free record check remains the initial primary action;
5. Public resources and edition identities remain unchanged;
6. The mobile compact navigation, keyboard focus and existing static/dynamic axe checks pass;
7. The final commit receives an authenticated exact-head hosted visual preview and independent accessibility acceptance.

**A-grade messaging readiness is a provisional editorial assessment, not an A-grade website certification.** The protected Vercel preview, independently tested assistive-technology acceptance and accessible PDF reissues remain separate release blockers. Do not merge or deploy PR #106 merely because the copy scores well.

## Evidence boundary for future growth claims

The claim “exponentially increase demand” is a desired outcome, not a measurable conclusion from copy review. Use approved, non-sensitive analytics for page-to-tool selection, guide download, training entry and resource return visits; do not transmit search terms, records or personal case details. Compare a predeclared baseline window with a post-release window and disclose traffic sources, denominators and uncertainty. Practitioner feedback or verified field outcomes may later strengthen the narrative, but must never be invented.
