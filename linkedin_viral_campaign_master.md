## FORMAT 2: "Documentation vs. Reality" Diagnostic Audit

**Asset:** Text-only high-dwell post. No image required.

**Body:**

Policy says no employee record is written with a public AI tool. Reality says the termination memo was drafted in a chat window at 4:45 on a Friday.

Both things are true at once, and the gap between them is where the evidence disappears.

Here is the audit, one row at a time.

**Intended Rule**
Official records are drafted in approved systems, tied to contemporaneous evidence, with a documented human review step.

**Operational Reality**
The manager pastes rough notes into a general AI tool, asks it to "make this defensible," then asks the same tool to supply the supporting facts. The output is fluent and internally consistent.

**The Hidden Risk**
There is no metadata showing which tool was used, what was entered, or when. The narrative and its justification came from the same source, so the file confirms itself and anchors to nothing. In discovery, ESI requests now reach the drafting layer directly: prompt logs, draft versions, tool-usage records. The absence of that trail is read as the absence of deliberation.

**The Structural Patch**
Every material claim traces to non-AI evidence that existed at the time. The tool and the human author are both identified. The review step is dated and attributed. Unapproved external tools are off-limits for official records.

The rule was never the problem. The distance between the rule and the keyboard was.

## FORMAT 4: "Historical Precedent / Metadata Void" Case Study

**Asset:** Long-form narrative text post. Optional single archival-style image.

**Body:**

In 1969, roughly 600 million people watched the first moonwalk. The tapes that carried the sharpest version of that footage were later erased and recorded over.

NASA did not lose the mission. It lost the highest-fidelity record of the mission. The data that would let anyone reconstruct the original signal in full is simply gone, written over because tape was expensive and no one flagged the record as the asset.

Now hold that next to the Incan quipu: knotted cords that encoded census counts, taxes, and accounts across an empire. The cords survive in museums. The key to reading most of them does not. The record persists while the ability to reconstruct its meaning has been lost for centuries.

Two civilizations, five hundred years apart, same failure mode. The artifact outlived the method for reconstructing it.

**The modern enterprise version is quieter and faster.**

An AI tool drafts a decision record. It reads as finished. But the prompts, the draft history, the reviewer identity, and the link back to source evidence are never preserved. The file survives. The ability to reconstruct why the decision was made does not. That is Decision Reconstruction Risk, and it now accrues at the speed of a text box, one record at a time.

You do not notice it until someone asks the file to explain itself: a regulator under the GDPR's accountability principle, an auditor under the EU AI Act's logging expectations, opposing counsel in discovery. By then the tape is already recorded over.

**The 3-step fix:**
1. Treat the record as the asset. Preserve the drafting layer: prompts, versions, tool used, reviewer, date.
2. Anchor every material claim to evidence that existed at the time, not to AI-generated narrative.
3. Run a reconstruction test before finalization. If a stranger cannot rebuild the reasoning from the file alone, it is not done.

The drafting tool changed. The way records fail did not.

## FORMAT 5: "Allegory of Operational Failure" (Cinematic Hook)

**Asset:** Narrative text post. Optional dark cinematic still.

**Body:**

There is a scene in Margin Call where a junior analyst finishes the model, sees the firm is holding something that could sink it, and the room goes quiet. Then a senior partner says the line that matters: "Explain to me as you would to a child."

Nobody in that building could reconstruct, on demand, how the number was reached. The output was correct. The reasoning was locked inside a process no one could walk backward through under pressure.

**That is the structural blind spot.**

Every organization now has models, and increasingly AI tools, producing outputs that land in permanent records: a termination rationale, a benefits denial, a risk rating, an investigation summary. The output is fluent. The moment someone senior says "walk me through how we got here," the reasoning is either reconstructable from the record or it is gone.

**The real-world compliance translation:**
A decision that cannot be reconstructed from its own record is not a decision you can defend. It is a position you are hoping no one tests. In a hearing, an audit, or a discovery request, "the system produced it" is not an answer. The record either carries the reasoning or it does not.

**Diagnostic questions for your own files:**
- If the author left tomorrow, could a stranger rebuild the reasoning from the record alone?
- Can every characterization be traced to evidence that existed at the time?
- If opposing counsel pulled the drafting history, would it show deliberation or a fluent narrative added after the fact?

The analyst in the film could explain his model. Most AI-assisted records cannot explain themselves. That is the gap worth closing before someone asks you to explain it as you would to a child.

## FORMAT 6: "Silo Translation" Bridge (Cross-Functional)

**Asset:** High-share reference text post. Optional 3-column image.

**Body:**

The fastest way to kill an AI governance initiative is to describe it correctly to the wrong audience.

Same risk, three languages. Here is the bridge.

**What Tech says:**
"The model output lacks provenance metadata and the generation isn't reproducible from logged inputs."

**What Executives hear:**
"Something technical is fine, probably." (Nothing happens.)

**What to say instead:**
"If we get sued or audited, we cannot show why this decision was made. The record does not defend itself."

Now it moves.

Run the same translation across the table:

**Legal** cares about: discoverability, post hoc rationalization, evidentiary sufficiency, records-of-processing obligations.
**Engineering** cares about: logging, version history, input traceability, reproducible output.
**Executive leadership** cares about: defensibility, regulatory exposure, and whether a bad record becomes a headline.

They are describing the same failure. A record that cannot be reconstructed is a legal risk, an engineering gap, and a board-level exposure at the same time.

**Translation rule:**
Stop selling the mechanism. Name the consequence in the listener's currency. Engineers hear "traceability." Executives hear "defensible or not." Lawyers hear "reconstructable under scrutiny." One risk, three sentences, and everyone finally agrees it is urgent.

## FORMAT 7: "Pre-Flight / Audit Trail" Pre-Checklist

**Asset:** High-save operational checklist. Text post or 1:1 card.

**Body:**

Before any AI-assisted record goes final, run five boxes. Each one is pass or fail. There is no partial credit, because a hearing does not give partial credit.

🔲 **Author + tool identified**
Pass: the human author and the specific AI tool used in drafting are both named.
Fail: the file is silent on how it was produced.

🔲 **Source materials preserved**
Pass: the underlying notes, logs, and communications still exist and are linked.
Fail: the finished text is all that remains.

🔲 **Claims tied to non-AI evidence**
Pass: every material assertion traces to evidence that existed at the time.
Fail: the narrative and its supporting facts came from the same generation.

🔲 **Human review step documented**
Pass: reviewer identity, date, and substantive changes are recorded.
Fail: "reviewed" with no name, no date, no diff.

🔲 **Reconstruction test passed**
Pass: a reviewer who never met the parties can rebuild the reasoning from the record alone.
Fail: the record needs the author standing next to it to make sense.

Boundary condition: if any box fails, the record is not finished. It is a draft carrying Decision Reconstruction Risk into every proceeding that later relies on it.

Save this. Run it before the file locks, not after the subpoena.

## FORMAT 8: "Red Flag vs. Green Flag" Signal Sheet

**Asset:** Binary signal comparison. 1:1 or 4:5 card, two columns.

**Body:**

Five signals that tell you, in ten seconds, whether an AI-assisted record will hold up or fall apart.

🚩 **Red Flag:** The record asserts a conclusion with no identifiable source.
🟢 **Green Flag:** Every characterization points to a dated artifact.

🚩 **Red Flag:** Subjective descriptors like "culture fit" or "struggles with change" recur across files.
🟢 **Green Flag:** Claims are specific, factual, and individualized to the record.

🚩 **Red Flag:** The same tool wrote both the narrative and its supporting facts.
🟢 **Green Flag:** The narrative and the evidence come from independent, contemporaneous sources.

🚩 **Red Flag:** No drafting history, no reviewer name, no date.
🟢 **Green Flag:** Tool, author, reviewer, and review date are all on the record.

🚩 **Red Flag:** The file only makes sense if the author explains it.
🟢 **Green Flag:** A stranger can reconstruct the reasoning from the record alone.

Count your red flags on the last consequential record you approved. That number is your exposure.
