The Right to Know Why Initiative

People deserve to know why. People affected by consequential decisions have an interest in understanding the reasons recorded for those decisions.

Public-interest principle, not a new legal entitlement. This initiative does not create a legal right, establish a universal disclosure requirement or authorize access to confidential records. Applicable obligations depend on governing law and context.

A fictional hiring decision

Read the retained packet as someone who was not present. Form a provisional explanation before comparing the source documents.

All identifiers, requirements and events are invented. No answers are collected or scored. The exercise does not establish a right to confidential selection records.

H-118 · Completed selection packet

Applicant not selected

Interviewer's recommendation, June 4. “The role requires evening availability. The candidate cannot work evenings. Recommend nonselection.”

Final notice, June 7.

“Not selected. Not the right fit.”

Retained-file index: role criteria; candidate's availability response; interview recommendation; final notice.

1. Examine the retained sources

Role criteria, May 20

“Evening coverage required twice each week.”

Candidate's availability response, June 2

“Available for evening shifts on Tuesdays, Wednesdays and Thursdays.”

Return to the final notice alongside the interviewer's recommendation.

2. Return to the recorded decision
“Not selected. Not the right fit.”

End of retained selection packet.

What question does this record leave you with?

Pause and form your question before opening the interpretation.

3. Compare your interpretation

The interview recommendation offers an apparent explanation. The retained availability response does not support its statement that the candidate cannot work evenings. The final notice does not identify whether that recommendation or another reason controlled the nonselection.

Why wasn't I hired? The outcome is documented. Its controlling rationale cannot be established from this packet. Other authorized records might resolve the question, but none should be invented to complete the account.

A missing or inconsistent explanation does not establish discrimination, unfairness or an unlawful decision. JRS does not supply missing evidence or determine the merits of the selection.

The question from the affected-person perspective

Can a person affected by a consequential decision understand the reasons recorded for it? A file may preserve an outcome while leaving its supporting information, chronology or reasoning unclear.

The decision may remain on file after the information needed to explain it becomes harder to find. Firsthand knowledge, contemporaneous notes and the sequence of events may not remain equally accessible over time. That is a reason to examine what the retained record actually supports, not a claim that a missing explanation proves wrongdoing or creates a right to confidential information.

The Justification Review Standard (JRS) examines this documentation problem as Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR). Missing documentary support does not, by itself, prove that the decision was unlawful or incorrect.

What to look for

Identifiable reasons

Can the reader distinguish the stated basis from undocumented assumptions or unsupported characterizations?

Reviewable chronology

Can a later independent reviewer follow the relevant sequence and examine the evidence without relying on the original author's memory?

The person affected by the decision

A person told they were not selected may reasonably want to understand which job-related requirements were considered and how the recorded decision was reached. This constructed example does not create a new disclosure right.

Different minds. Fewer blind spots. Stronger evidence.

Different experiences, professions and cultures can help people ask questions that a first reader missed. Constructive disagreement is useful when it identifies something the record has not yet explained.

“What are we not seeing?” is a question for independent examination. Diversity of perspective does not create missing evidence or prove that JRS improves outcomes. The record still needs identifiable support.
