Public education initiative

The Right to Know Why

A free educational initiative explaining what a record should preserve so someone without firsthand knowledge can understand the reasons given for a decision.

Explore free resourcesRead the standard

Why this matters

People may receive a decision letter or summary that states an outcome but not the facts, documents, chronology or reasoning behind it. A clear record makes later review more informed. It does not guarantee that the decision was correct or that all information may lawfully be disclosed.

Important limit: The initiative does not assert that every person has a universal legal right to an explanation. Applicable rights and disclosure obligations depend on the jurisdiction and decision context. Nothing here is legal advice.

Four questions worth asking

  1. What decision was made and by whom?
  2. Which records or observations support the stated basis?
  3. When did the relevant events and the decision occur?
  4. Which reasoning steps or unresolved facts are necessary to understand the outcome?

These questions can help someone inspect a record or seek an explanation through appropriate channels. They do not compel disclosure or resolve a legal dispute.
