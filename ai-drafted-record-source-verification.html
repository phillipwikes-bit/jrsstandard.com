AI-assisted record review

How to Verify Sources in an AI-Drafted Decision Record

Fluent AI-generated language can make a record appear complete without showing where its claims came from. Source verification requires examining the underlying record, not just approving the draft's wording.

A source-verification procedure

  1. Mark the conclusions, dates, quotations, amounts, attributed statements and rule references in the draft.
  2. Locate each underlying source in the approved record system. Check the exact document, version and relevant passage.
  3. Distinguish a true source identifier from a model-generated or incomplete citation. A credible-looking citation is not proof the document exists.
  4. Compare the strength of the draft's conclusion with what the source establishes. Look for generalizations, unexplained adjectives and omitted qualifying facts.
  5. Check that the decision-maker's actual reasoning is recorded rather than reconstructed after the fact by the model.
  6. Preserve the verification result and human review or escalation decision under the organization's records procedure.

Fictional example

AI draft: “The request was approved in accordance with all applicable security requirements.”

Evidence check: Locate the request, approving authority, applicable requirement version, approval time and access end date. A policy name without proof that it was applied does not establish compliance.

Disposition: Retain only verified assertions, or hold the record for review if support is missing.

Questions the final reviewer should be able to answer

  • Which source supports this sentence?
  • What does the source actually say?
  • Could someone who was not involved find and examine it?
  • Are gaps disclosed rather than silently filled by AI?
