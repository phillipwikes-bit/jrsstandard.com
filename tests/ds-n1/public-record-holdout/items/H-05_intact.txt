During the relevant time, Complainant worked as a Vocational Rehabilitation Specialist at the Agency’s Behavioral Service Line, Gulf Coast Health Care facility in Biloxi, Mississippi. Compensated Work Therapy Program Manager (Manager) was her first-level supervisor. Report of Investigation (ROI) at 85, 119. 1. On October 15, 2019, Manager, issued Complainant a sick leave certification letter; 2. On November 13, 2019, Manager issued Complainant a Letter of Admonishment; and 3. On November 20, 2019, Manager rated Complainant lower than deserved on her annual performance evaluation. At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant timely requested a hearing. The AJ assigned to the case determined sua sponte that the complaint did not warrant a hearing and over Complainant's objections, issued a decision without a hearing on March 4, 2022. The AJ found that the Agency provided legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions for each claim. In claim 1, the AJ noted that Manager issued Complainant a sick leave certification at the recommendation of Labor and Employee Relations. Complainant used 107 hours of sick leave between April 15 and October 10, 2019. ROI at 126. The sick leave had two concerning patterns. When Manager assigned Complainant a patient, Complainant would enter sick leave into the leave system, send Manager an email, and then go home. The second pattern was one in which Complainant would take sick leave on days immediately surrounding days when she was scheduled to be off work. ROI at 127. As to claim 2, Manager issued Complainant a proposed admonishment on October 22, 2019, for conduct and behavior issues. ROI at 30. There were six specifications enumerated on the proposed admonishment. Two of these specifications involved telling Manager not to come to scheduled meetings and four were concerning Complainant’s unwillingness to respond to Manager’s instructions. Id. After the proper procedure was followed, Manager upheld the admonishment on November 13, 2019. ROI at 276. Regarding claim 3, Manager asserted that he took the concerns outlined in the proposed admonishment into account with Complainant’s performance review. He found that Complainant did her job but did not perform beyond that which was expected. As such, he rated Complainant “fully successful” for her performance review. ROI at 143-149. The AJ found that Complainant was unable to provide sufficient evidence to demonstrate pretext. As such, Complainant failed to prove discrimination as alleged. The Agency subsequently issued a final order adopting the AJ’s finding that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged. The instant appeal followed. Complainant raised generalized concerns regarding the lack of discovery due to the issuance of summary judgment.
