At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a GS-0592-7 Tax Examining Technician at the Agency’s Small Business/Self-Employed Service Center in Kansas City, Missouri. On October 1, 2017, a Supervisory Tax Examiner became Complainant’s first-line supervisor (S1). At all times relevant to her complaint, a Department Manager was Complainant’s second- line supervisor (S2), and a Program Manager was her third-line supervisor (S3). Complainant is African-American, and her color is light brown. Complainant identified her disability as a speech impediment, stuttering. Complainant averred that she is limited in her ability to speak in front of a crowd, express herself clearly in her meeting, or speak clearly and concisely over the telephone. Complainant alleged that, as a result of her stutter, management believes that she has a learning disability. According to Complainant, she engaged in prior protected activity by filing an EEO complaint in 2015 that named S2 as the responsible management official. Complainant stated that, although S1 was not her supervisor in 2015, S1 was a witness for her EEO complaint. Complainant attended Collection Statute Expiration Date (CSED) program training in October 2016. Complainant stated that, after she attended the training, she told her former supervisor (FS1) that she was still having a hard time understanding the CSED program. According to Complainant, she met with FS1 to discuss CSED on February 3, 2017. Complainant averred that she became anxious during the discussion, which raised her blood pressure, culminating in Complainant being taken to the hospital to be treated for high blood pressure. According to Complainant, on September 21, 2017, S1 emailed her and stated that she would not receive any more “read time” to learn CSED. Complainant averred that, although S1 was not her supervisor at that time, she had explained to S1 that she was having trouble understanding CSED and that it caused her anxiety. Complainant alleged that S1 told her that she had to get certified on the CSED program because she had been fully trained. According to Complainant, she was not the only employee who had trouble understanding the complexity of the CSED program. Complainant alleged that, as of January 2018, at least eight of her coworkers who attended CSED training were not CSED certified and had not been required to work CSED cases in more than four months. S1 stated that all employees had been trained on CSED and would be required to work the CSED program once they became certified. Complainant stated that she was scheduled to take a vacation day on September 29, 2017. Complainant averred that she told FS1 that she might come in to the office that day and that she would call if she did not come in. According to Complainant, she called FS1 to tell him that she was not coming in and to request leave for the already scheduled vacation day. On September 29, 2017, S1 issued Complainant a memorandum stating that Complainant had not completed her time and attendance for the week because she did not input her leave for September 29, 2017. The memo stated that it was a “courtesy” reminder to Complainant to timely and accurately complete her time and attendance. According to S1, when she became the new supervisor of Complainant’s team, she told the team members that she liked to document conversations in emails and memoranda to have a record of what was discussed. On October 4, 2017, S1 forwarded the team an email with a reminder about the housekeeping guidelines for the office. On October 10, 2017, S1 issued Complainant a memorandum for her failure to follow the housekeeping guidelines. In the memorandum, S1 stated that Complainant used an electric blanket during a training on October 3-4, 2017, and that Complainant did not unplug the blanket when asked by the instructor. S1 averred that electric blankets violated the housekeeping guidelines and asked Complainant to remove her electric blanket from the workplace. According to S1, the reason for the memorandum was that Complainant had ignored the instructor’s request to unplug the blanket, on top of the fact that the electric blanket violated the housekeeping guidelines. According to Complainant, a Caucasian coworker (C1) who had Halloween decorations in her cubicle that violated the guidelines was not issued a memorandum for failing to follow the housekeeping guidelines. Complainant averred that C1 had two 36-inch skeletons on the outside of her cubicle wall that S1 asked her to take down because the decorations violated the fire code. Complainant stated that, when she asked C1 whether she had received anything in writing, C1 told her that she had only received a verbal warning. Complainant stated that, on October 24, 2017, S1 gave her a memorandum documenting an Embedded Quality Review System (EQRS) review, which stated that Complainant’s performance had dropped and that her performance appraisal could be affected. Complainant averred that she asked why the review was in a memorandum and that S1 told her that it was her practice to document such matters in memoranda and that S2 was aware of the matter. Complainant stated that she reached out to the union. According to Complainant, S1 later asked for the memorandum back and tore it up. S1 stated that she asked Complainant for the document back in order to be consistent with other supervisors who did not issue memoranda for performance matters after discussing the issue with S2. According to Complainant, on October 31, 2017, S1 approached her to discuss the suspended cases in her inventory. Complainant stated that she tried to explain how previous supervisors had taught her to work these cases but that S1 interrupted her, talking over her and telling her that she should know better and had been in her position long enough to know how to process cases. Complainant alleged that S1 said, “I’m gonna kill you.” Report of Investigation (ROI) at 121. Complainant stated that she was utterly shocked and amazed by S1’s remark and that she did not feel safe and feared for her life. According to Complainant, S1 also said, “You gonna make me kill you” before she left Complainant’s desk. ROI at 122. Complainant stated that, later on October 31, 2017, S1 emailed her and told her to come to her office. Complainant alleged that, in S1’s office, S1 said, “I could kill you” and gave her negative feedback on her work process. ROI at 122. S1 stated that she was talking to Complainant about her inventory on October 31, 2017. According to S1, she told Complainant to ask the Lead Tax Examining Technician (C2) for help and joked that Complainant could say, “[S1] is going to kill me if I don’t close this case.” ROI at 272. S1 averred that she was joking and stated that both she and Complainant laughed. Complainant denied that S1 was joking when she said she would kill her. According to Complainant, she does not have a friendly relationship with S1, and they do not joke with each other. The record contains an October 31, 2017, email from one of Complainant’s coworkers (C3) to a union representative. In the email, C3 stated that she was at her desk and could hear S1 “going off” on Complainant. ROI at 189. According to C3, Complainant did not understand what S1 was talking about, and S1 was “talking to her like a dog…bullying her.” Id. Complainant averred that she reported S1’s threats to kill her to the Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration (TIGTA). TIGTA investigated Complainant’s report that S1 threatened to kill her. S1 stated that she was interviewed by TIGTA on November 3, 2017. On November 20, 2017, TIGTA forwarded its report of investigation to the U.S. Attorney’s Office, which declined to prosecute S1 due to lack of federal interest. S1 stated that, as a result of Complainant’s report to TIGTA, she was given a coaching mentor to work on communicating with her subordinates and was required to take training on communication. According to S2, although she had not observed S1 harass Complainant, she knew that S1 struggled with effective communication. S3 stated that S1’s tone can come off as rude and added that S1 has been counseled for unprofessional behavior. Complainant averred that S1 attempted to provide side-by-side instruction on CSED with her on November 15, 2017. Complainant alleged that she was uncomfortable with the situation after S1 had threatened to kill her. Complainant stated that she was nervous and scared to have S1 sitting behind her. According to Complainant, S1 later issued her a memorandum about the instruction, noting that Complainant appeared uncomfortable and distracted and kept pulling her coat and scarf on and off. The record contains a November 15, 2017, memorandum from S1, which discussed the errors Complainant made, noted that Complainant did not ask for help, and added, “You appeared to be distracted, pulling your coat and scarf on and then taking the coat off and turning your desk fan on, while shuffling your notes on your desk.” ROI at 157. Complainant stated that S1 gave her a mid-year evaluation on January 5, 2018. According to Complainant, S1 told her that her performance had declined in several areas and warned, “One more bad review will kill you.” ROI at 136. S1 stated that she told Complainant, “another error is going to kill your accuracy.” ROI at 272. According to S2, the goal was for employees to reach 93 percent customer accuracy, but Complainant’s customer accuracy was 57. percent from October 1, 2017, through February 15, 2018. On December 14, 2017, Complainant filed an EEO complaint, which she subsequently amended, alleging that the Agency discriminated against her on the bases of race (African-American), color (light brown), disability (speech impediment), and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity (prior EEO complaint) when she was subjected to harassment, including when: 1. Since October 1, 2017, management continually issued her memoranda for work performance issues, required her to work a difficult program not required of all employees, threatened to lower her performance evaluation, yelled at her, talked over her, made belittling remarks to her, and made three threatening remarks to the effect of, “I’m going to kill you”; and 2. On January 5, 2018, during her midyear review, S1 stated, “one more bad review will kill you,” and Complainant continued to receive negative memoranda. At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant timely requested a hearing but subsequently withdrew her request. Consequently, the Agency issued a final decision pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b). The decision concluded that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged. The instant appeal followed. Neither Complainant nor the Agency submitted a statement or brief regarding the appeal.
