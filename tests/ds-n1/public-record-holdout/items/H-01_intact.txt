At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Purchasing Agent at the Agency’s VA Medical Center in Decatur, Georgia. On August 12, 2020, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against her on the basis of disability (anxiety; depression) when: 1. On June 2 and 3, 2020, Complainant was questioned about the leave she had taken under the Family Medical Leave Act (FMLA) and 2. On June 2, 3, and 24, 2020, Complainant was charged Absent Without Leave (AWOL). 2 Complainant stated that she has major depression and anxiety. See Report of Investigation (ROI) at 66. She asserted that on June 3, 2020, her Supervisor questioned her about her FMLA leave which she asserted was “targeting” her due to her disability by marking her AWOL even though she was on FMLA leave. See ROI at 68. She acknowledged that after she told them about her FMLA leave, the Chief told her that the AWOL would be removed. See ROI at 69. The Supervisory Purchasing Agent (Supervisor) stated that the Chief of Prosthetics had him reach out to Complainant to clarify the situation with Complainant’s leave because HR had only provided the FMLA paperwork for January to February 2020 when it was requested. See ROI at 144-45. He asserted that once Complainant had verified that she had approved FMLA leave, her AWOL was removed. See ROI at 147. The Assistant Chief of Prosthetics (Assistant Chief) confirmed that Complainant was never officially marked AWOL and once her FMLA was verified, he instructed the timekeeper to enter in Complainant’s time as FMLA as required. See ROI at 124-25. Complainant’s time sheet records confirm that Complainant was not actually marked AWOL on the dates at issue. See ROI at 157. The Agency’s acceptance letter erroneously listed only June 24, 2020 as the date on which Complainant alleged she was charged AWOL but the investigation was later clarified at Complainant’s request to include June 2 and 3, 2020. See Report of Investigation (ROI) at 31-36. At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant timely requested a hearing. On May 16, 2022, the Agency filed a motion for a decision without a hearing to which Complainant did not respond. The AJ assigned to the case granted the Agency’s motion and issued a decision without a hearing on June 16, 2022. The Agency subsequently issued a final order adopting the AJ’s finding that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged.
