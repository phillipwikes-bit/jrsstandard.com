At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Special Agent at the Agency’s Miami Division in Miami, Florida. 1. On February 9, 2017, an individual management denied Complainant’s request for a Law Enforcement Officer Identification Card (LEO ID Card); and 2. On March 28, 2017, an individual management denied Complainant’s appeal of the decision not to grant her a LEO ID Card. Complainant worked as a special agent (SA) at the time of her retirement. She stated that she decided to retire on August 3, 2016, and sent her retirement package to the Agency on that day, for overnight delivery. On August 5, 2016, while Complainant was on medical leave, three Agency employees came to her home to retrieve her service weapon, complete paperwork, and give her a security briefing. an individual stated that, the week following the Agency employees visiting her home, one of those Agency employees (CW1) returned Complainant’s personally owned weapon (POW). an individual stated that the documentation of her firearms training should have been included with her POW, but that paperwork was not returned when her POW was. an individual stated that she was later informed by an individual that a first-level supervisor (S1) stated that Complainant was not entitled to the firearm training documentation, her retired credentials, a retirement plaque, or a LEO ID. Report of Investigation (ROI) at 11. an individual stated that during the six-months after her retirement, she was in contact with the Agency’s Human Resources Division (HRD) requesting her LEO ID. an individual stated that her request for her LEO ID was denied on February 9, 2017, based on information from the Office of Professional Responsibility (OPR). Complainant appealed the denial of her LED ID to the HRD Assistant an individual, who denied the appeal on March 28, 2017. The an individual stated that Complainant was proposed for removal, prior to her retirement, due to an investigation conducted by OPR. ROI at 89. Complainant’s proposed removal was related to an investigation initiated by Agency’s management. OPR proposed Complainant’s removal from Federal service on charges of False/Misleading Financial Information and Insubordination pertaining to repeated acts of falsified time and attendance documentation and failure to obey her supervisor’s instructions on six occasions. Complainant contacted an EEO counselor on March 22, 2017, and she was issued a Notice of Right to File on May 19, 2017. At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant timely requested a hearing but subsequently withdrew her request. Consequently, the Agency issued a final decision (FAD) pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b). The decision concluded that Complainant failed to prove that an individual subjected her to discrimination as alleged. Report of Investigation file dated November 21, 2017.
