At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Clerk at the Agency’s Florissant Post Office in Jefferson City, Missouri. Supervisor Customer Service (Supervisor) was his first-level supervisor. Report of Investigation (ROI) at 92. 1. On February 2, 2021, he was issued a letter of warning; and 2. On February 27, 2021, his work hours were changed. At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of his right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant timely requested a hearing. The AJ assigned to the case issued a Notice of Proposed Judgment (Notice) indicating his intent to issue a decision without a hearing. Subsequently, the AJ determined that the complaint did not warrant a hearing and issued a decision without a hearing on February 18, 2022, incorporating the reasons provided in the Notice. The AJ found that the Agency provided legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for each of the claims. As to claim 1, Supervisor issued Complainant a Letter of Warning for failing to maintain a regular work schedule, based upon Complainant’s failure to report to work on a day that he was scheduled to work. As to claim 2, Supervisor asserted that Complainant’s duty hours were changed for operational reasons. Specifically, Supervisor said that Complainant was best qualified to process certain mail parcels that did not arrive at the facility until later than his prior start time. Turning to Complainant to establish that the Agency’s reasons were pretext for discrimination, the AJ found that Complainant did not do so. Concerning claim 1, the AJ noted that Complainant claimed that the Letter of Warning was issued because of “personal” reasons and the only reason provided to him was “rules and regulations.” The AJ noted that Complainant asserted that his medical condition was the reason for the Agency’s action because Supervisor “mentioned,” however, Complainant did not explicitly state what Supervisor said or explain how his medical condition was referenced. As such, the AJ determined that this alone was insufficient to demonstrate that the Agency’s reason was pretext for discrimination based on his disability. As to claim 2, the AJ noted that Complainant asserted that Supervisor changed his work hours because he did not follow her instructions. The AJ found that, even if that was correct, that is insufficient to prove pretext. The Agency subsequently issued a final order adopting the AJ’s finding that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected him to discrimination as alleged. The instant appeal followed. Complainant did not provide arguments in support of his appeal. The Agency contends that the AJ’s decision was proper and their decision implementing it should stand.
