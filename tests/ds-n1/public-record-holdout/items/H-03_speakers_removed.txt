At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as an Industrial Specialist, GS 13 at the Agency’s Mid-Atlantic Regional Maintenance Center in Norfolk, Virginia. On June 25, 2021, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against her on the bases of race (African-American), sex (female), and color (Black) when: 1. (Manager) dismissed her report of harassment onboard the USS Cole by the ship Commander, with examples of the harassment including: a. On April 22, 2021, the Commander compared her to the last Project Manager (PM) who was a white male, when he said, “Where have you been? I never went a single day without seeing the PM prior to your arrival.” b. On April 22, 2021, she received derogatory comments from the Commander in a “Reply All” email such as “I hope it is not your intention to allow the contractor to not complete this work. That is unacceptable.” c. When on or about April 26, 2021, she received an email reply from the Commander which told her he never had to have a conversation with someone of her rank or responsibility and asks if it alarms her since it was concerning to him; d. When on July 6, 2021, the Manager issued her a Letter of Expectations based on email feedback from the Commander. 2 Complainant was assigned to work as the Project Manager (PM) for the Maintenance Team of the USS Cole in December 2020, a position which entailed being responsible for expediting product completion deadlines and serving the customer, i.e., the USS Cole and its Commander, and ship forces. See Report of Investigation (ROI) at 176; 613-21. The Commander stated that it was the usual practice for the PM to hold production meetings on the ship each day with the USS Cole’s leadership and respective contractors, which allowed for leaders to proactively socialize maintenance problems and solutions. See ROI at 458. He stated that the Commanding Officer and the PM were expected to work together to unite the maintenance team and ship’s force to complete the maintenance package on time. See ROI at 458. He stated that when Complainant started as the PM, he was upfront about the daily meeting requirement and was frank about the challenges that he and the previous PM had faced. See ROI at 459. He explained that the emails he sent her were to provide feedback about the status of the work being untimely and done with poor workmanship. See ROI at 460. He further explained that in his opinion, Complainant’s referring to any communication issues that led to the job being late or incomplete was due to Complainant’s own refusal to hold daily meetings. See ROI at 460. He stated that people at their level and pay grade were expected to execute at the highest levels and not allow excuses to get in the way of what is right for the ship, which always takes precedence. See ROI at 462. Complainant responded to that email by referring to her protected classes, stating “Please understand that I am a mid-level GS-13 Civil Service Employee with protected status under four categories: black, female, over 40 and disabled.” See ROI at 463. The an individual stated that he issued the Letter of Expectations after this email because he found it to be unprofessional and inappropriate for Complainant to bring up her protected classes because it was not germane to the conversation. See ROI at 435. He further explained that the Letter of Expectations was not disciplinary or a negative action. See ROI at 436. The Agency dismissed a claim that between June 2020 and November 2020, the commander of the USS Ramage yelled at her, stood in the way of timely project completion, and sent her command derogatory comments regarding her performance pursuant to 29 C.F.R. §1614.107(a)(2) for untimely counselor contact. See Report of Investigation (ROI) at 137-39. an individual stated that she felt the Commander’s emails to be demeaning because he was correcting her about something when he is a peer and not a supervisor. See ROI at 180. She asserted that she believed the Commander’s treatment of her and her supervisor’s lack of response to her allegation of harassment had to do with her protected classes because “[t]hey do not do this to people who are not a marginalized group in [her] experience.” See ROI at 182. an individual acknowledged, however, that the only way in which she tried to make her supervisors aware of the alleged harassment was by adding her first-line supervisor (Supervisor) to the email chain with the Commander. See ROI at 181. Both the Coordinator and the an individual stated that to their knowledge, Complainant never actually mentioned that she believed she was being subjected to harassment to anyone in management. See ROI at 421; 434. At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant timely requested a hearing. Over Complainant’s objections, the AJ assigned to the case granted the Agency’s April 1, 2022, motion for a decision without a hearing and issued a decision without a hearing on April 27, 2022. The AJ found that Complainant could not establish a prima facie case with respect to the Letter of Expectations and even if she could, there was no evidence to indicate it was due to discriminatory animus. The AJ further found that all of the alleged incidents of harassment taken together were not sufficiently severe or pervasive to constitute an unlawful hostile work environment. The Agency subsequently issued a final order adopting the AJ’s finding that Complainant failed to prove that an individual subjected her to discrimination as alleged. Complainant appealed but did not make any specific arguments on appeal. In response, an individual contends that the AJ correctly found that there was no evidence beyond Complainant’s bare assertions to indicate any discriminatory animus.
