At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Budget Analyst at the Agency’s Efficiency and Renewable Energy (EERE) Office in Washington, D.C. In the Agency’s July 22, 2021, acceptance of Complainant’s May 14, 2021, amendment request, an individual noted that reprisal only applied to claim 2 as an individual alleged that her protected EEO activity was the instant complaint. 1. on or around February 1, 2021, Complainant was informed by email that the Department’s tentative job offer to Complainant for the position of Budget Director, EERE, had been rescinded; and 2. on May 14, 2021, Complainant was not selected for the position of Budget Director. Complainant worked in the Department’s Office of Energy Efficiency and Renewable Energy (EERE) as a Budget Analyst, in Washington, D.C., from September 10, 2018, until March 10, 2021. Report of Investigation, Part 1 (ROI1) at 279. From approximately October 2019 until March 10, 2021, Complainant also served as the Acting Budget Director. ROI1 at 312. On December 30, 2020, Complainant received a tentative job offer for the position of Budget Director, EERE Office. ROI1 at 153. On January 20, 2021, the Principal Deputy Assistant Secretary (Principal), began her position as the new Principal and Acting Assistant Secretary for EERE. Once in position, the Principal stated that she studied position descriptions for various key positions to ensure that the organization was structured appropriately. ROI1 at 391. She found that many position descriptions did not accurately reflect what was needed. For the Budget an individual position in particular, the Principal noted that the position was “watered down” and did not require specialized budget expertise. The Principal asked the Human Capital (HC) Office to correct the position description, noting the expectation that the Budget Director would have to engage in very in-depth and critical budget formulation and execution-related functions. The Principal was not involved in rewriting the position and noted that she was unsure who in the HC Office handled the revisions. The Principal also noted that the position was misclassified as under Program Management job series when it should have been posted under a Budget Analyst job series. Supplemental Report of Investigation (SROI) at 60. The Principal’s request led HC to recall the Budget Director vacancy for which Complainant had applied. ROI1 at 391. The Principal noted that her review also led the Advanced Manufacturing Office (AMO) an individual position to be recalled and a tentative offer for that position was also rescinded. SROI at 59. Additionally, her review also led to the Federal Energy Management Program Director position being reclassified. SROI at 60. On February 1, 2021, Complainant was informed by email that the Agency’s tentative job offer to Complainant for the position of Budget Director had been rescinded. ROI1 at 185. The letter informed Complainant, in part, the following: Based on expansive platform changes which will heavily impact EERE programs, the Budget Director role in EERE must be updated to reflect the changed priorities of the Administration and the Department, as well as the specialized budget formulation and execution expertise and broader responsibilities of the position to address the anticipated increases in appropriations for EERE programs. Due to the increased technical qualifications required, we must pursue re- classification and re-advertisement of this Senior Executive Service (SES) position. The vacancy announcement will be closed, and the Department will not proceed any further in the hiring process. Your Qualifications Review Board submission will not be considered by the U.S. Office of Personnel Management for certification. However, you remain eligible to apply to competitive SES announcements and you are encouraged to apply for the reformulated budget position when it is re-advertised in the near future. ROI1 at 186. The Budget Director position was later reposted under the Budget Analyst job series with a revised description. On April 23, 2021, Complainant interviewed for the newly re-posted Budget Director position with an interview panel 3 comprising of the Budget Officer and Director for Budget and Planning (Director). The Budget Officer stated that she gave Complainant acceptable ratings for the interview, noting that she found Complainant’s responses, using “industry experience” versus “government experience”, to be lacking. SROI at 31. The Budget Officer did not recommend her for a second-round interview. Id. The an individual stated that based on responses to the standard six questions asked of each candidate, Complainant’s score was lower than the three candidates selected as Best Qualified. SROI at 71. On May 13, 2021, the panel submitted the three Best Qualified candidates to the Deputy Assistant Secretary of Operations (Deputy Assistant), who was the selecting official. SROI at 38. The panel unanimously chose three candidates to submit based on the quality of their responses during their respective interviews. SROI at 72. The Deputy Assistant stated that she made her decision based on the recommendation of the interview panel. SROI at 143-147. The Deputy Assistant spoke with all three Best Qualified candidates and made her final decision based on the panel’s recommendation and her conversations with the candidates. SROI at 146. The Deputy Assistant stated that the Selectee: had a tremendous breadth of experience with Federal budgeting and program reviews. In speaking with [the Selectee] it became evident that he had significant experience in leading a diverse skill set of federal employees, providing strong leadership, developing staff and creating a cohesive team which aligned with the needs for this position. The interview panel was comprised of three individuals, but the third individual had a scheduling conflict and was not present during the interview. SROI at 146. On May 14, 2021, Complainant was informed that she was not selected for the position of Director, Budget Office. At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). When Complainant did not request a hearing within the time frame provided in 29 C.F.R. § 1614.108(f), the Agency issued a final decision pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b). The Agency concluded that Complainant failed to prove that an individual subjected her to discrimination as alleged. The instant appeal followed. Both parties provided appellate briefs.
