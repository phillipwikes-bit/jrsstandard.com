At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Carrier Technician at the Bronx Soundview Station in Bronx, New York. Report of Investigation (ROI), at 10., Complainant suffered an on-the-job injury, which resulted in two arthroscopy knee surgeries and was placed on limited duty. Id. at 81. Complainant stopped reporting for duty shortly after he suffered a second on-the-job injury, when he reportedly turned his ankle and twisted both his knees. Complainant thereafter filed a claim with the Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs (OWCP) concerning his, injury, and received a decision from the OWCP. The OWCP’s decision concluded that the “mechanism of injury is unclear. The evidence does not establish that [Complainant] stepped into a hole in the ground as claimed.” Supplemental Report of Investigation (Supp. ROI), at 194. Thereafter, beginning, Complainant was marked by management has having accumulated 472 hours of Absence Without Leave (AWOL). ROI, at 357. However, according to Complainant, after he was injured for a second time, management failed to turn over his paperwork to the OWCP. Complainant believed that if his OWCP claim had been properly processed he would have received payment and medical treatment for his injury. Complainant believed that his OWCP documentation was suppressed by management in an attempt to force him to work full-time. Complainant however received a letter from the Department of Labor (DOL), noting that the OWCP was in receipt of his claim for a traumatic injury filed. Supp. ROI at 279. The Manager of Customer Service also attested that Complainant’s OWCP claim was submitted to the DOL in 2016 but was denied by DOL for not submitting the appropriate medical documentation. Id. at 168-169. Management subsequently presented Complainant with a Notice of Removal. ROI, at 357. The Notice of Removal noted that Complainant failed to report for duty, through, accumulating 472 hours of AWOL. Id. The Notice of Removal specifically stated that Complainant failed to call-in as required during his absences and failed to provide administratively acceptable documentation to support his absences. Id. According to the Manager of Customer Service, Complainant did not submit proper documentation to justify his absences, so she was directed by Human Resources to charge him with AWOL and issue him the Notice of Removal. Supp. ROI at 168-169. The Manager of Customer Service also attested that Complainant did not respond to communications timely and would submit documentation two to three months late. Id. The Manager of Customer service further explained that Complainant stopping calling and failed to report to work and she made it clear to him that if he did not call or report to work he would be marked as AWOL. Id., Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against him on the basis of disability (physical) when: 1. The on-the-job injury he sustained, was not approved by the OWCP of the DOL; and 2., he was issued a Notice of Removal for being AWOL. On November 9. 2017, the Agency issued a partial/acceptance dismissal of Complainant’s complaint. Therein, the Agency accepted claim 2 for investigation, but dismissed claim 1 for the failure to state a claim, finding that it constituted a collateral attack on the OWCP process. Following the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of his right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant initially requested a hearing. Thereafter, the AJ determined that Claim 1 was improperly framed by the Agency and found that claim 1 should have been framed as follows: Whether Complainant was subjected to disability-based disparate treatment in terms, conditions, and privileges of the Agency’s processing of Complainant’s documentation related to his Workers’ Compensation claim. The AJ also noted that Complainant withdrew his hearing request, and therefore remanded Complainant’s complaint to the Agency for a supplemental investigation. The Agency thereafter completed the supplemental investigation and issued a final decision pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b), concluding that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected him to discrimination as alleged. The Agency initially determined that Complainant failed to establish a prima facie case of discrimination with respect to claims 1 and 2, finding that Complainant did not identify any employees outside his protected class who were treated more favorably. The Agency nevertheless assumed, without finding, that Complainant established a prima facie case of discrimination, but found that the Agency articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions. In so finding, the Agency noted that Complainant never submitted evidence to support his absences, did not respond to communications from management, and submitted documentation three months late, causing confusion. The Agency observed that Complainant was informed that his continued failure to report to work or call-in his absences would lead to discipline. The Agency noted that Complainant responded to management that injury compensation was “playing games” and that his lawyer would address the issue. The Agency found no evidence that management delayed the processing of Complainant’s OWCP claim or took any action that swayed the decisions made by the OWCP.
