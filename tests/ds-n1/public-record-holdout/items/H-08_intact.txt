At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Nuclear Medicine Physician, GS-15, with the Imaging Service for the Veterans Affairs Medical Center in Martinsburg, West Virginia. On March 30, 2017, Complainant contacted an EEO Counselor regarding unlawful discrimination and a hostile work environment. On May 4, 2017, Complainant filed a formal complaint alleging that the Agency subjected her to discrimination and a hostile work environment based on religion (Muslim) and sex (female). On June 21, 2017, the Agency issued a Notice of Partial Acceptance on Complainant’s complaint noting she alleged that she was subjected to a hostile work environment based on religion (Muslim) and sex (female) consisting of 15 separate events. The Agency found four of the events listed were timely raised discrete acts and accepted them for investigation as independently actionable claims of disparate treatment (events 1, 13, 14, and 15). Complainant filed an objection to the Notice of Partial Acceptance. On July 27, 2017, the Agency issued a Response to Disagreement with Notice of Partial Acceptance noting Complainant disagreed with the framing of four of the events listed, and requested to add the basis of retaliation to her complaint. The Agency stated it would make the changes necessary as stated in Complainant’s disagreement letter and issue a new notice of partial acceptance. A Revised Notice of Partial Acceptance was issued on July 27, 2017, framing Complainant’s complaint as alleging she was subjected to a hostile work environment based on religion (Muslim), disability, sex (female), and in reprisal for protected EEO activity as evidenced by fifteen events. The Agency accepted five events for investigation as independently actionable claims of disparate treatment (events 1, 12, 13, 14, and 15) and stated that all the events would be considered as part of Complainant’s overall harassment claim. Complainant filed an amendment to her complaint dated July 26, 2017. Complainant requested the following two events be added to her complaint: on June 30, 2017, the Agency summarily suspended Complainant’s clinical privileges and notified her that it is considering permanently revoking her clinical privileges; and on July 10, 2017, the Agency initiated a summary review of Complainant’s employment as a probationary employee. Complainant stated these two events were a direct continuation of the pattern and practice of harassment previously raised. Further, she noted the harassment had escalated, “with the termination of [her] employment and revocation of her clinical privileges now the apparent goal.” Complainant argued the new claims were like or related to those raising during counseling and should be added to the claims already accepted for investigation. On July 27, 2017, the Agency issued a Notice of Amendment of the EEO Complaint, acknowledging receipt of Complainant’s amendment. The Notice stated that the current accepted claim, as indicated in the Revised Notice of Partial Acceptance letter dated July 27, 2017, is one of hostile work environment consisting of 15 events and five disparate treatment claims. The Notice acknowledged that on July 26, 2017, Complainant requested her complaint be amended to include two additional events based on religion and sex. The Agency found the new claims like or related to those raised in the original complaint. The Agency defined the amended complaint as containing the following claims: Complainant was subjected to a hostile work environment based on religion (Muslim), disability, and sex (female) as evidenced by the following events: 1. In November 2015 and ongoing, Supervisor of Nuclear Medicine (Supervisor), and Chief of Imaging Service (Chief), became uncooperative and aggressive towards the Complainant after learning she was a Muslim and proceeded to change her position by removing her supervisory, leadership, and other duties; and cancelled her supervisor training, which undermined and denigrated her amongst her peers as a physician that's supervised by a technologist. 2. On February 23, 2016, Supervisor failed to respond to the Complainant’s emails regarding patient care. 3. On February 25, 2016, Complainant felt threatened when she asked Supervisor to leave her office after they had a meeting regarding a position disagreement. 4. From June 2016 to December 2016, Chief was amused when Complainant informed her that someone was placing candy wrappers in her inbox and commented “infantile.” 5. In October 2016, Complainant’s performance was underrated by Chief when she used the incorrect scoring system. 6. From December 2015 to December 2016, Supervisor ignored Complainant’s warning of multiple patient care and performance behavior issues. At one point Complainant was forced to apologize for reporting a patient care issue. 7. In December 2016, Complainant was denied pay for performance and her request for a pay panel review was also denied. 8. In January 2017, Chief denied Complainant’s request for reasonable accommodation. 9. On March 28, 2017, Chief embarrassed the Complainant during a meeting when she dismissed her concerns about patient care. 10. On March 30 and 31, 2017, Supervisor communicated in an angry tone “you only want to win. I'm done with you” after Complainant voiced her concerns about appropriate patients released processes. 11. In April 2017, Chief accused Complainant of over-usage of Family Medical Leave Act (FMLA) and taking unauthorized time off. 12. In April 2017, during a Human Resource meeting with Chief, Complainant’s personal medical information was disclosed to the group. 13. On April 24, 2017, Complainant’s credential file was not presented during a meeting which caused a delay in her renewal privileges. 14. On May 22, 2017, Chief initiated a review of Complainant’s work product, manipulated the review, and refused to submit her application for renewal of clinical privileges and credentials to the Executive Committee of Medical Staff. 15. On June 1, 2017, the Acting Chief of Staff handed the Complainant a letter of investigation for her work performance. 16. On June 30, 2017, Senior Management summarily suspended the Complainant’s clinical privileges and notified her of a proposal to permanently revoke her clinical privileges. 17. On July 10, 2017, Senior Management initiated a summary review of Complainant’s employment as a probationary employee. The Agency accepted events 1, 12, 13, 14, and 16 for investigation as timely independently actionable claims of disparate treatment. The Agency noted these events were sufficiently related to the overall pattern of harassment that they would be included for consideration in the analysis of the overall harassment claim (events 1 - 17). Complainant’s complaint was assigned for investigation on August 23, 2017. Investigator 1 conducted an investigation from August 31, 2017, to October 6, 2017. The investigative report was submitted to the Regional EEO Officer on October 9, 2017. On February 15, 2019, the Office of Employment Discrimination Complaint Adjudication (OEDCA) sent the matter for a supplemental investigation to the Office of Resolution Management (ORM). It was noted the supplemental investigation was needed due to the first investigator not using a revised version of event 14; failing to investigate the bases of race, disability, and reprisal; and failure to investigate the additional accepted events in claims (16) and (17). The matter was assigned for investigation on March 4, 2019. Investigator 2 conducted an investigation from March through April 2019. The report was submitted to the Regional EEO Officer on April 19, 2019. On July 10, 2019, Complainant requested a hearing on her complaint. The case was assigned to an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ). On September 11, 2019, the AJ dismissed Complainant’s hearing request and remanded the matter to the Agency for issuance of a final decision. On June 10, 2021, the Agency issued a final decision on Complainant’s complaint. In its decision, the Agency stated that at the conclusion of the investigation, Complainant was notified of the right to request a hearing by an EEOC AJ or an immediate final decision without a hearing. Specifically, the Agency stated that “Complainant acknowledged receipt of that notice on October 27, 2017.” The Agency stated that when Complainant failed to respond to the notice, the file was forwarded to OEDCA for an immediate final decision. 2 Further, the Agency stated the record indicated that Complainant was subsequently terminated from her position effective August 4, 2017. The Agency noted that documentation from ORM shows that Complainant did not follow the “proper channels” she was instructed to follow in order to amend her complaint while it was pending hearing or to initiate a new complaint by calling the EEO counseling line. Thus, it noted ORM did not accept Complainant’s termination claim via amendment or investigate the removal as part of Complainant’s discrimination and harassment claim in this case. The Agency analyzed events 8, 12, 13, 14, and 16 as independently actionable claims and events 1 - 17 as an overall harassment claim. The Agency found Complainant failed to establish that she was subjected to discrimination or harassment as alleged. Thereafter, Complainant filed the instant appeal.
