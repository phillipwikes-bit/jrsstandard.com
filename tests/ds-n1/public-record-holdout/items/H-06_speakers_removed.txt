At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Contract Surveillance Representative, GS-9, at the Agency’s Armed Forces Retirement Home, Washington Campus, in Washington, D.C. Complaint #1: [number removed] On April 5, 2021, Complainant filed Complaint #1 alleging discrimination based on race (African American), color (other than white), sex (female), and in reprisal (for the exercise of Title VII rights and for seeking union protection and reporting behavior and contract performance issues), when: 1. On an unspecified date, she was denied a promotion. 2. On unspecified dates, she was excluded from staff activities. 3. From July 2019 to January 2020, Contract Project Manager, Armed Forces Retirement Home, yelled at her while on site visits, and made comments, such as, “I’m sick of [Complainant],” “[Complainant] gets on my nerves,” and “Watch out for [Complainant].” 4. On January 14, 2020, her supervisor (S1), Chief, Resident Services, GS-14, instructed her to send emails upon her arrival and departure from work. 5. On January 17, 2020, S1 removed her as the Contracting Surveillance Representative for Global Connections to Employment duties. 6. On January 17, 2020, September 30, 2019, and June 2, 2019, S1 issued her Letters of Counseling. 7. In March and July 2020, an individual denied her request for telework and hazard pay. 8. In July 2020, S1 issued her a rating of 1 (Unsatisfactory) for Fiscal Year 2020 after revising her Strategic Goal performance element without notice or her participating in her performance plans. 9. On December 15, 2020, S1 threatened to demote her, removed her Contract Officer Representative duties, and “put [her] in a corner” of the office. 10. On January 11, 2021, S1 placed her on an Opportunity to Improve. 11. On January 21, 2021, she was made aware Human Resources did not process her union dues for six months and refused to provide the union’s correct contact information. 12. In February 2021, Senior Financial Analyst, Armed Forces Retirement Home, sent emails and text messages with rude slurs, sexual graphics, and asking the question, “How’s life on the plantation.” 13. On March 24, 2021, Human Resources Specialist denied her request for tuition reimbursement stating the Agency does not have a tuition reimbursement program. On June 10, 2021, an individual accepted claims 10 and 13 as discrete acts and accepted claims 4 – 10 and 13 as part of the hostile work environment claim. The an individual dismissed claims 1 and 2, for failure to provide clarifying information and dismissed claims 3, 11, and 12 for failure to state a claim, pursuant to 29 C.F.R. §§ 1614.107(a)(7) and .107(a)(1), respectively. The an individual dismissed the discrete portion of claims 4 – 8 due to untimely EEO Counselor contact pursuant to 29 C.F.R. §1614.107(a)(2) but accepted those as part of the hostile work environment claim. The an individual also dismissed Complainant’s alleged basis of reprisal because her filing a complaint against Human Resources office for not posting union representatives contact information did not constitute protected activity within the meaning of the EEO regulations. The an individual further stated that Complainant did not allege that she addressed a discriminatory issue in that complaint against Human Resources office. The an individual investigated the accepted claims from July 22 to September 1, 2021. The an individual indicated that on September 15, 2021, it provided, via certified mail, Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ). 29 C.F.R. § 1614.108(f). The an individual stated that an individual reported she did not receive the mail and thus, on October 14, 2021, it reissued a copy of the report of investigation along with her right to hearing, via Agency’s Secure Access File Exchange filesharing electronic system, to Complainant. Complaint #2: [number removed] On July 9, 2021, Complainant filed Complaint #2 alleging discrimination based on race (African American), disability, sex (female), and in reprisal for prior EEO activity when: 14. On June 7, 2021, she was denied Family and Medical Leave Act leave for her May 2021 surgery. 15. On an unknown date, she was denied her reasonable accommodation request for telework and a handicap parking space. On July 30, 2021, the Agency dismissed claim 15 for raising a matter that had not been brought to the attention of an EEO Counselor, pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.107(a)(2). The Agency investigated claim 14 from August 30 through September 28, 2021. The an individual indicated that on October 14, 2021, the Agency, via its Secure Access File Exchange filesharing electronic system, provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation, including a notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC AJ. The an individual stated that it reissued the report of investigation on October 27, 2021, and again on November 10, 2021, because Complainant did not retrieve the original notices. On January 26, 2022, the Agency, consolidated the complaints and issued a final an individual decision finding no discrimination as alleged. The an individual stated that Complainant failed to make an election to request a hearing by the deadline, i.e., by November 13, 2021 (30-days after October 14, 2021) for Complaint #1, and by December 10, 2021 (30-days after November 10, 2021) for Complaint #2. On March 18, 2022, Complainant appealed the Agency’s decision indicating that the Agency’s final decision was “wrong,” and there was an error in an individual’s processing of her claims. In response to an individual’s appeal, the Agency indicated that an individual’s appeal should be denied as untimely. Specifically, an individual stated that Complainant received the final Agency decision on January 26, 2022, but she did not file an appeal until March 18, 2022, which was beyond the 30-day time limit. In the alternative, stated the Agency, the Commission should affirm its final decision finding no discrimination. The record reveals that on October 6, 2021, Complainant, via EEOC Public Portal, requested a hearing before an EEOC AJ for both complaints and uploaded a copy of a Request for a Hearing Form, dated September 30, 2021, for Complaint #1, which was docketed under Hearing No. 570- 2022-00017X; and a copy of a Request for a Hearing Form, dated October 1, 2021, for Complaint #2, which was docketed under Hearing No. 570-2022-00018X. The record indicates that on January 24, 2022, the AJ issued a Notice of Intent to Sanction for each complaint because the Agency failed to submit the complaint file and/or the report of investigation. On March 24, 2022, the Agency filed a separate response to each Notice of Intent to Sanction, indicating that sanctions against the Agency were not warranted because it was not served with a notice that Complainant filed a hearing request, and it was not on notice of any order or other requirement until March 10, 2022. Specifically, an individual stated that Complainant did not use Request for a Hearing Form that was provided to her when the report of investigation was transmitted to her. In that form, Complainant was informed to choose the Agency, in EEOC Public Portal, as “DoD Office of Secretary/Washington Headquarters Services.” Instead, Complainant used an outdated version of Request for a Hearing Form which did not indicate an option to request a hearing in the EEOC Public Portal and thus no instruction choosing the Agency therein. The an individual stated that Complainant’s hearings were docketed, via EEOC Public Portal, with the Agency as “DoD Armed Forces Retirement Home” on October 6, 2021. At that time, the Agency EEO Program Director (“EEOP Director”) or the Agency’s EEO office did not have access to “DoD Armed Forces Retirement Home” via FedSEP and thus, the Agency never received the October 6, 2021, filings. The an individual indicated that on March 10, 2022, the EEOP Director requested and received access to the “DoD Armed Forces Retirement Home” FedSEP box as a stopgap measure while discussing with individuals at the EEOC (Office of Federal Operations and Washington Field Office) other ways to ensure more effective usage of FedSEP, given the large population, consisting of multiple agencies, serviced by “DoD Office of Secretary/Washington Headquarters Services.” On March 10, 2022, the EEOP Director promptly uploaded a copy of the final Agency decision in FedSEP, and on March 11, 2022, the Agency counsel requested an extension to respond to the AJ’s Notice of Intent to Sanction. The Agency argued that the AJ should dismiss Complainant’s hearing requests because they were not properly filed. The an individual indicated that Complainant did not send a copy of the hearing requests to the Agency’s EEO office as instructed and as such it properly issued a final Agency decision for both complaints. The an individual stated that Complainant appealed the Agency’s decision and the AJ proceeding with a hearing would be inappropriate. Further, an individual stated that the AJ lacked jurisdiction to hear Complainant’s complaints because Complainant filed her hearing requests prematurely before her receipt of the investigative report and before 180 days had elapsed under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.108(h). The an individual indicated that regarding Complaint #1, filed on April 5, 2021, Complainant’s hearing request form was dated September 30, 2021, i.e., before she received the reissued investigative report on October 14, 2021, and before 180 days had elapsed from the filing of the complaint. Regarding Complaint #2, filed on July 9, 2021, an individual stated that Complainant’s hearing request form was dated October 1, 2021, i.e., before she received the investigative report on November 10, 2021, and only 84 days after the filing of a formal complaint. Based on the foregoing, the Agency asked the AJ to withdraw the Notice of Intent to Sanction the Agency and dismiss Complainant’s hearing requests as they were not properly filed and because the AJ lacked jurisdiction. On May 6, 2022, Complainant submitted to the AJ a motion, in part, to consolidate cases, motion for sanctions, and request for a hearing. an individual indicated that she timely filed hearing requests for her complaints and the Agency was on actual notice of the filings. Complainant argued that the Agency failed to respond and therefore sanctions against the Agency were warranted. Specifically, an individual stated that she provided a copy of her October 1, 2021, hearing request of Complaint #2 to the EEOP Director on October 6, 2021, via email, as an attachment. Complainant included a copy of her October 6, 2021, email showing it was sent to the EEOP Director, and a Senior EEO Specialist, stating, “Good Afternoon, Here’s your copy of the request for hearing attached above. The formal complaint investigation was completed. I wanted to timely request a hearing.” The email message reflects Complainant attached a copy of the request for hearing on Complaint #2. On June 30, 2022, the AJ issued an Order of Dismissal of Complainant’s hearing requests for her complaints. Specifically, the AJ stated that since Complainant filed an appeal of the consolidated final Agency decision, the AJ no longer had jurisdiction over the complaints.
