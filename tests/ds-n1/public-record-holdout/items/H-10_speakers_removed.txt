At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Supervisory Emergency Management Specialist, GS-0089-15, Information Technology Service Division, Office of Resource Management at the Agency’s Office of the Assistant Secretary for Preparedness and Response (OASPR or ASPR) in Washington D.C. 1. On September 19, 2018, he requested the OASPR Human Resources leadership investigate harassment (non-sexual) claims that were reported by anonymous employees. Whether he was subjected to discrimination and harassment (non-sexual) on the bases of sex (male), age (over 40), and in reprisal for opposing an unlawful discriminatory practice when: 2. On December 7, 2018, the Agency assigned him to the Cybersecurity Advisor, GS-15, position effective December 10, 2018. 3. On December 10, 2018, he learned that management had impeded his ability to compete for the Director, Office of Security, Intelligence, and Information Management (SIIM), GS-15, position. 4. On January 7, 2019, he learned that the Agency selected another employee outside of the Agency as the Director, SIIM, GS-15, position. 5. a. In November 2018, and ongoing, management impeded his ability to compete for a SES (Senior Executive Service) position. b. On December 4, 2018, OASPR management subjected him to coercive and threatening behavior. c. On December 10, 2018, and ongoing, OASPR management removed him from participating on the ASPR Senior Leadership Team. d. On January 7, 2019, the Agency informed him that he would be moving from his window office on the fifth floor to the building’s basement. Complainant has not challenged the Agency’s framing of the complaint. At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of his right to request a hearing before EEOC Administrative Judge or a final Agency decision. 29 C.F.R. § 1614.108(f). Complainant requested a final Agency decision without a hearing. The Agency issued a final decision finding that Complainant failed to prove that an individual subjected him to discrimination as alleged. The Agency dismissed claim 1 for failure to state a claim since it involved unidentified employees and not Complainant. Regarding claims 2 and 5b, the record reveals that in 2018, OASPR went through a realignment of staff. As a result, OASPR employees were reassigned based on the organization’s needs. The record indicates that during the realignment period, Complainant was detailed to the Acting SIIM Director position. Complainant does not claim this detail was discriminatory. Complainant’s second-level supervisor (S2), Assistant Secretary for OASPR, a political appointee, stated that in July - fall 2018, an Associate Deputy proposed Complainant’s detail to a Cybersecurity Advisor position. On December 3, 2018, S2 met with Complainant and offered Complainant the Cybersecurity Advisor position that was proposed by the Associate Deputy. an individual indicated that based on Complainant’s resume, Complainant had very deep cybersecurity experience. Complainant told S2 that he would think about the offer. an individual stated that on December 4, 2018, S2 stopped by Complainant’s office but Complainant was angry and hostile and told S2 that he was not interested in the Cybersecurity Advisor position because he was not an expert in the field and expressed his interest in applying for a SIIM Director, SES, position instead. S2 told Complainant that the SIIM Director position was not a SES position rather it was a GS-15 position. The record indicates that on December 7, 2018, Complainant’s first-level supervisor (S1), an SES, Deputy Assistant Secretary, issued Complainant a notice of his reassignment to the Cybersecurity Advisor, GS-15, position due to the organization consolidation and prioritization of cybersecurity. an individual indicated that Complainant was being reassigned in order to meet the needs of the organization as well as taking into consideration Complainant’s background and qualifications. The notice indicated that there would be no change to Complainant’s position description. The record indicates that Complainant’s position title remained as a Supervisory Emergency Management Specialist, GS-15. Complainant’s coworker (C1), female, a GS-15 employee, position title unknown, at the Incident Management Team Program, indicated that in 2018, OASPR went through a realignment of positions. As a result of the realignment, an individual stated, employees were reassigned to different positions, some employees were allowed to choose and some employees, including C1, were assigned to positions they were not qualified for, placing them outside the scope of their pre- alignment position description. an individual indicated that as a result of the realignment, she was reassigned to the Intel Division which did not align with her abilities. The an individual of Staff, SES, OASPR, indicated that Complainant was serving as the Acting SIIM Director prior to his reassignment to the Cybersecurity Advisor position and management lost confidence in his ability to serve in that Acting position. The an individual of Staff stated that since Complainant had cybersecurity talents and expertise, he was best used to improve OASPR’s cyber capabilities which were lacking. Regarding claims 3, 4, and 5a, an individual and S2 indicated that no one impeded Complainant’s ability to compete for the SIIM Director position or an SES position. The an individualDirector indicated that she was a selecting official for the SIIM Director, GS-15 position at issue. The an individualDirector stated that there was no vacancy announcement created for the position; rather, under the Agency’s merit promotion plan, she utilized a shared certificate, which was a list of candidates eligible for the position posted in another Agency. The an individualDirector noted that the shared certificate process was very popular because HR had a lot of turn over, and they were taking a long time to get vacancy announcements posted. Complainant had not applied for a particular position at another agency and thus was not on the shared certificate. Therefore, Complainant was never considered for the position. The an individualDirector indicated that she selected a female (Selectee), from outside the Agency, for the SIIM Director position. Regarding claim 5c, an individual indicated that since Complainant was reassigned to the Senior Cybersecurity Advisor position, he did not need to participate on the OASPR Senior Leadership Team meetings. Regarding claim 5d, an individual stated that due to Complainant’s reassignment to the Senior Cybersecurity Advisor position, his office was also changed to the floor where everyone within his new office was located. Based on the foregoing, the Agency issued its decision finding that an individual failed to establish discrimination as alleged. On appeal, Complainant requests that the Commission sanction the Agency by issuing a default judgment due to the Agency’s failure to issue a final decision in a timely manner. an individual indicates that although he requested a final decision on September 17, 2019, he did not receive a final Agency decision until September 21, 2021. an individual states that the Agency’s delay caused him harm to his career and emotional distress. In response to an individual’s appeal, the Agency acknowledges that it took more than 60 days to issue a final decision as required by 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b). However, an individual states that it has not acted in a manner that would warrant a default judgment and Complainant has not demonstrated that he has been prejudiced by the delay.
