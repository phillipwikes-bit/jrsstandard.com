# Serious flags on good AI drafts: are they real?

Three samples, each picked by a fixed order (SHA-256 of set, draft and finding id), read against the decision by the developer. Not independent: a second reader should check them. G = genuine (the draft says something the decision does not support as written), F = false alarm (the decision supports it, or it is a writer's note or a fragment), U = unclear.

**Caution on samples 1 and 2.** They were drawn with a diagnostic script that titled the source "S". A bug found afterwards (fixed 26 November) made a one-letter title count as named in almost every sentence, which changed which flags appear. The verdicts on those flags stand, but the mix of flags may differ from the benchmark's setup. Sample 3 uses the benchmark's setup.

## Sample 1: 30 flags, tracks F (15) and FH (15), code of round 2

G 3, F 22, U 5.

F: a near word-for-word copy marked unsupported (4 items: City Carrier Technician; hired October 2015 at Pay Band IV; clock rings deleted; AD supervisor from June 2018), traced to a bug fixed in round 3 (names and roles left nothing to compare); writer's notes about the excerpt (4); a judge "granting the motion" read as an untraceable approval (3); a role entity read as a different person (3); fair paraphrases read as contradictions (4); fragments (2); other supported (2).
G: "This disparity ... directly resulted from discriminatory intent" (characterisation the decision does not make); "this matter remains unresolved through contradictory accounts" (invented conclusion); "management's deliberate inaction regarding known compensation inequities" (characterisation).
U: "S1 told S2 she saw nothing wrong"; "AD's version differs significantly"; an HR officer's statement on documentation policy; "attributed ... to racial animus on the part of S1"; "multiple coworkers provided testimony corroborating".

## Sample 2: 20 flags, track FH, code of round 3 in progress

G 2, F 16, U 2.

F: "eighty hours ... four hundred eighty hours" read as a different figure (spelled-out hundreds, fixed); a second date in the same statement read as a slip (fixed); "granted LWOP" read as an untraceable approval (fixed); "prevent her from standing" against "unable to stand" (fixed); a yearless date checked against too little text (fixed); "did not realize ... until" with a long gap (fixed); paraphrases ("little concern" for "lack of concern"; "difficult for even experienced employees" for "not even experienced employees could"; "objected ... should have received Excellent" for "contended he unfairly did not receive"); supported sentences read as a different person (2); others supported.
G: "These overlapping supervisory relationships would prove significant ..." (padding the decision does not support); "viewed this action as a failure to respect her professional clinical judgment" (characterisation).

## Sample 3: 20 flags, sealed hold-out FH2 after its single run, final code, benchmark setup

G 7, F 11, U 2.

G: "create substantial questions regarding the factual basis" (analysis); "suggesting supervisory work would impose fewer physical demands" (inference); "Funding constraints and program cancellations, rather than discriminatory intent, explain ..." (analysis); "the 300-day statute of limitations ... (or the extended 45-day period ...)" (legal rule the decision does not state, and the wrong way round); "a pattern of mistreatment intended to undermine her authority and isolate her" (characterisation); "This failure to respond compounds the procedural deficiency" (analysis); "This admission substantially undermines the factual foundation" (analysis).
F: a list of claims read as an approval; four sentences the decision states almost word for word ("Complainant did not submit an affidavit"; coworkers could not confirm the substance; two employees received disciplinary action; SCBPO-1 informed coworkers of the reassignment); "insufficient evidence" for "failed to prove"; "made no reference to" for "conspicuously missing from"; a long supported list of allegations; "previously adjudicated ... on May 25, 2022"; "initially accepted this assignment ..."; "S2 clarified the matter with safety personnel".
U: the bathroom-frequency theory; a long list of performance actions.

## What the samples say together

On drafts close to the source (track F) most serious flags are false alarms. On drafts that add their own analysis (FH2), about a third are real catches of added, unsupported content. The share of statements flagged fell in round 3 (fresh hold-out FH2: 12.3% to 10.8%). A reviewer still has to read each serious flag.
