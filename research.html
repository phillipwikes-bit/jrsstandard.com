Evidence at a glance

The detection study involved 16 independent international experts from 11 countries across five continents, evaluating constructed AI-generated decision records against an author-defined operational reference key that was not independently adjudicated by human experts. Their expert participation supports a defined research finding, not certification of JRS or proof of effectiveness in live organizational records. Read the study and its limitations.

83.9%Mean reviewer agreement with an author-defined operational reference classification on constructed records. Three automated raters reproduced the key; independent human reference adjudication has not been established. Detection subset: 16 independent international experts, 384 graded reads; 95% participant-level CI 72.7% to 95.1%.
36 reviewersCompleted a full 24-record set across 16 countries. This broader completion group must not be confused with the 16-expert detection subset.
58 reviewersGraded at least one record across three studies. A participation count is not an accuracy or validation estimate.

The detection threshold was met on its pre-specified analysis. A separate reviewer-agreement criterion was not met because the confidence-interval lower bounds did not clear the required threshold. Cross-vendor raw agreement does not establish reproducibility. See the study-specific documents for estimators, denominators and limitations.

Separate reviewer-agreement sample

The reliability analysis used 104 determinations from 22 analyzed raters, with 10 multi-rater records. Gwet’s AC1 was 0.739 in the invited group and 0.623 in open enrolment. Both point estimates exceeded 0.61, but neither required confidence-interval lower bound exceeded 0.41. The pre-registered two-part criterion was not met.

These are not additional observations from the 16-expert detection panel. They do not establish individual reviewer dependability or operational validity. See analysis and uncertainty and study design.

International research and practitioner contributions

Different Minds. Fewer Blind Spots. Stronger Evidence.

JRS has been examined through research involving international practitioners, methodological scrutiny, and employment and public-records pilot work.

That work has produced findings, questions, and limitations worth examining.

It also points to a concern shared across professional settings: a consequential decision should not become impossible to understand simply because the original decision-maker is no longer available to explain it.

The research contributions below have different roles, samples and evidentiary limits. International participation and publication do not themselves establish operational effectiveness, institutional adoption or certification.

Research participation

Documented: 16 independent experts from 11 countries contributed 384 graded reads in the defined constructed-record detection study. The separate reliability sample and wider participation totals are not interchangeable.

From research questions to two public initiatives

The international statement above is an invitation to examine a record from different perspectives, not a measured outcome. The free JRS resource library provides practical references for reviewing evidence, chronology and reasoning. The Right to Know Why addresses people affected by decisions. Decisions You Can Defend serves professional reviewers. Neither initiative creates missing evidence or proves that a decision is correct.
