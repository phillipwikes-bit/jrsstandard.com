**The hold-out failed.** The changes in this round raised every development measure (track F good drafts 16 to 24 of 60; changed sentences 42 to 48 of 60), but they did not carry over to these drafts.

**Why, read after the run (post-hoc; not evidence and not used to change anything).** The Haiku P3 drafts are formal, expanded rewrites that pad and generalise ("The complainant's employment status and job classification remained consistent throughout the relevant period"). The serious flags on them come mostly from rules the development drafts rarely triggered: approvals narrated as events ("Supervisor1 approved this subsequent request as well") read as authority claims needing a document (24 findings); sentences opening "Accordingly," or "Consequently," read as the writer's own conclusions (10); and padded sentences whose cited passage does not confirm them as written (37). The inserted negations were missed because no passage was found that the changed sentence restates closely enough to compare.

What this means: on the development drafts, which keep close to the source wording, it now clears 24 of 60, but that is on material it was tuned on; on fresh drafts that rewrite in a different register, it cleared none. A reviewer still has to clear its flags on reworded drafts.
