Documentation reviewability: a practical briefing for institutional reviewers

JRS is a structured pre-finalization method for examining whether a consequential record remains understandable and evidentiary when the original author is unavailable. It is designed for use inside existing professional review workflows.

JRS public practitioner referencePublished 3 November 2026Phillip Wikes, JRS creator

The operational problem

Decision records increasingly contain professionally written or AI-assisted text that appears complete. Presentation quality alone does not establish that the document retains its evidentiary basis. Missing source identification, chronology and reasoning may become apparent only when a later reviewer, investigator or auditor needs to reconstruct the decision.

The JRS framework calls this vulnerability Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR). It concerns the explanatory sufficiency of the record, not the legal merits of the underlying decision.

The five-condition review

A practitioner examines whether the retained record satisfies five distinct conditions.

  1. Reconstructability: A later reviewer can establish what happened and why from the record alone.
  2. Basis identification: The sources or authority supporting material conclusions are identifiable.
  3. Chronology: The sequence of events and decision steps can be followed.
  4. Decision-process traceability: The record shows how evidence and reasoning connect to its conclusion.
  5. Evidentiary sufficiency: The retained material supports subsequent independent review.

These are documentation-review conditions. Satisfying them does not establish that a decision is fair, legally correct or compliant with a particular regulatory regime.

A low-friction existing-workflow application

  1. At the existing pre-finalization review point: Select the completed draft and identify its source documents. Keep the record within authorized organizational systems.
  2. Check the five conditions: Compare material statements against the source material; identify unsupported inferences, missing dates, unresolved conflicts or unexplained reasoning.
  3. Retain the disposition: Record the specific missing evidence or ambiguity, obtain correction where feasible, and refer unresolved issues according to existing approval and escalation rules. An authorized human decides whether the record advances.

JRS does not require replacing a case-management or document-management platform. It does not independently authorize new data collection or transfers.

A short example of the failure pattern

A draft says a supplier's temporary system access was approved and subsequently closed. The available record documents an access request and account activation, but contains no identifiable approval or closure entry. The draft is more definite than its source basis. A reviewer should document those gaps instead of treating fluent wording as proof.

Examine the source-by-source constructed record and reviewer response. The example is entirely fictional, not an observed pilot outcome.

Research evidence and limitations

A constructed-record detection study reports 16 international reviewers assessing 24 constructed records (384 graded judgments), with a mean reviewer-level detection accuracy of 83.9% and a 95% confidence interval of 72.7% to 95.1%.

A separate inter-rater agreement sample produced reported Gwet's AC1 point estimates of 0.739 for invited raters and 0.623 for open-enrolment raters. The predefined reliability criterion was not met because the specified confidence-bound requirements were not satisfied. These results should not be conflated.

The findings do not establish real-world operational effectiveness, validation across regulated decisions, automated Review Engine accuracy or independent certification. See the research summary and methods and interpretation limits.
