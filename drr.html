Evidence and explanation

What is Decision Reconstruction Risk?

Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR) describes the possibility that a completed record cannot independently explain how a consequential decision was reached.

Explore free resourcesRead the standard

The problem is not the quality of the prose

A polished explanation may omit the supporting records, the order of events, the identity of the decision-maker, or the reasoning connecting an observation to the outcome. Those omissions can remain even when the language is clear and professionally written.

DRR concerns the ability to reconstruct a documented decision from preserved materials. It does not determine whether that decision was lawful, fair, accurate or justified.

A constructed illustration

Recorded conclusion: “The supplier-access exception was approved after Security completed its review.”

Available source: A paid vendor invoice. No approval, Security review or decision date appears in the file.

Review issue: The invoice exists, but it does not establish the asserted approval. A later reviewer cannot reconstruct who approved access, on what basis, or when.

The illustration is fictional. It is not a real organization, an observed incident or evidence of software detection performance.

Five conditions for independent review

  1. Reconstructability: Can an independent person follow the material steps?
  2. Identifiable basis: Is each material assertion connected to relevant, locatable evidence?
  3. Chronology: Are event, assessment and decision dates distinguishable?
  4. Reasoning trace: Is the connection from observation to inference and disposition visible?
  5. Sufficiency: Does the record retain the information needed for the defined review?

These questions are accessible summaries. The versioned JRS Codebook controls the formal definitions.
