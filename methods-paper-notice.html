Research document notice

Earlier methods paper: Reliability and Accuracy of JRS (Rungs 1 and 2)

This is an earlier draft methods paper, kept for the historical record. Some of its figures and wording have since been superseded or clarified by the current research record. Read this notice before relying on the draft. The current figures, with their limits, are on Research and Limitations and in the Research Summary.

What has changed since this earlier draft
Reviewer agreement
The draft reports Gwet's AC1 of 0.74 and 0.63 on 10 records and calls this substantial agreement. The current manuscript (29 August 2026) reports 0.739 for invited participants and 0.623 for open enrolment, in a separate reviewer sample of 10 records. It no longer uses the verbal band, and it reports that the pre-registered two-part reliability criterion was not met because neither lower confidence bound reached 0.41.
Agreement between AI models
The draft reports that cross-vendor agreement averaged 84% across 15 constructed records, a figure from an earlier point in a nightly series. The series closed on 21 August 2026. Across 61 recorded runs, 12 June to 21 August 2026, raw agreement ranged from 66.7 to 93.3 percent, mean 85.3 percent, and 82.2 to 93.3 percent across 37 runs at the full 15-record set, as the series stood on 13 August 2026. This is consistency between models, not accuracy. The pre-registered reproducibility criterion is not established, because the required chance-corrected AC1 was not computed.
Reproducibility and reliability conclusions
The draft says its findings support reproducible application and substantial inter-rater reliability. The current research record does not support either statement: the reproducibility criterion is not established and the reliability criterion was not met.
Detection accuracy
The draft treats accuracy as preliminary. The detection result is now reported: on a constructed 24-record corpus, the detection panel matched the reference classification at a mean accuracy of 83.9 percent (95 percent confidence interval 72.7 to 95.1, participant level). This is a result on constructed records, not performance on real records.
What this paper does not establish

The draft concerns the JRS method and the people and models who applied it. It is not evidence about the Review Engine, which is a controlled development candidate. It does not establish psychometric validation of the five conditions or performance on real records.

The archived draft

The draft is unchanged and remains available as a historical document.
