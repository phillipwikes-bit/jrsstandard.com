Chronology integrity

How to Reconstruct Chronology in a Decision Record

A record may contain several dates and still fail to show what happened in what order. Reconstructing chronology requires dated events, source references and a visible path to the decision.

What to distinguish

Date typeReview question
Underlying eventWhen did the documented event occur?
Source creationWhen was the email, note, interview or system log produced?
Knowledge and noticeWhen did the reviewer or decision-maker receive the material?
Approval or actionWhen was the decision made or implemented?
Later reconstructionWas an explanation written after the decision, and is that clear?

A five-step check

  1. Extract the dated assertions without changing their wording.
  2. Associate each important event with an identifiable source.
  3. Identify missing dates, contradictory timestamps and uncertain sequences.
  4. Verify what information was actually available to the decision-maker at the relevant time.
  5. Record unresolved chronology questions and route them for authorized review.

Fictional example

Draft: “The supplier-access extension was approved after the emergency request.”

Check: The request is dated September 11; the access log shows an extension on September 12; the approval record has no date. Without a dated approval, the record does not establish the sequence claimed.

Next step: Locate the actual approval artifact or record the uncertainty. Do not infer a date from the narrative's order.

See the missing-chronology reference and later-reviewer test.

Review boundary. These are educational review questions and fictional illustrations, not determinations about a live record, legal merits or regulatory compliance. Apply organizational procedures and retain all source material in authorized systems. No record upload is requested.
