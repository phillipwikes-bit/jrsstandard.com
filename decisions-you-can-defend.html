Professional initiative · Documentation integrity

The Decisions You Can Defend Initiative

Make decisions you can explain. Keep records you can defend. Retained records should preserve the information an independent reviewer needs to examine a consequential decision.

Documentation review is not legal certification. A reviewable record does not establish that the underlying decision was lawful, correct, fair or legally defensible. JRS remains in operational validation.

The question from the organization's perspective

Can someone who was not present reconstruct the evidence, chronology and reasoning behind a decision years later from the retained record alone?

Examine a constructed record before using the check.

This is the concern examined through Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR). AI-assisted drafting can make an incomplete evidentiary foundation less apparent when prose appears finished.

What a practitioner can examine

Evidence traceability

Is each evaluative conclusion anchored to identifiable information, rather than assumed facts or undocumented conversations?

Chronology and reasoning

Can an independent reader follow the events and see how the cited information supports the stated conclusion?

A constructed record: test the evidentiary foundation

Illustrative example · Not a real investigation

Recorded conclusion: “The policy was applied consistently, and the decision was appropriate.”

What is absent: No policy version, decision chronology, identifiable comparison records, or source-to-conclusion explanation is cited in the excerpt.

A reviewer can identify those gaps without assuming that the decision was wrong. The next task is to examine whether the actual retained record contains the missing support.

Check your review of the fictional record

Identify the documentation gaps before revealing the explanations. No response is recorded or scored.

Question 1: Which claims cannot be reconstructed from this excerpt?

Reveal the explanation for question 1

Answer: Consistent policy application and the appropriateness of the decision, because this excerpt does not identify the governing policy version, comparison records, dated events, or the path from source evidence to conclusion.

Question 2: Does identifying the gap prove the decision was incorrect or legally indefensible?

Reveal the explanation for question 2

Answer: No. The reviewer must look for supporting material elsewhere in the authorized retained record before concluding whether a documentation deficiency remains.

Different perspectives can expose review questions

A second reader may notice an unsupported characterization, a missing event or a gap between source material and conclusion. Different backgrounds can expand the questions asked, but do not substitute for evidence.

What are we not seeing? Examine that question against verifiable sources, chronology and documented reasoning. “Different Minds. Fewer Blind Spots. Stronger Evidence.” expresses an aim for disciplined review, not a measured result from JRS participation.
