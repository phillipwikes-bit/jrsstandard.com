The Decisions You Can Defend Initiative

Make decisions you can explain. Keep records you can defend. Retained records should preserve the information an independent reviewer needs to examine a consequential decision.

Documentation review is not legal certification. A reviewable record does not establish that the underlying decision was lawful, correct, fair or legally defensible. JRS remains in operational validation.

The question from the organization's perspective

Can someone who was not present reconstruct the evidence, chronology and reasoning behind a decision years later from the retained record alone?

Preserve the justification while the supporting information is still available. Decision inputs, contemporaneous notes and firsthand knowledge may become harder to retrieve after a record is finalized. JRS provides structured questions to identify gaps for human review; it cannot recover evidence that was never retained or establish that a decision was lawful or correct.

Examine a constructed record before using the check.

This is the concern examined through Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR). AI-assisted drafting can make an incomplete evidentiary foundation less apparent when prose appears finished.

What a practitioner can examine

Evidence traceability

Is each evaluative conclusion anchored to identifiable information, rather than assumed facts or undocumented conversations?

Chronology and reasoning

Can an independent reader follow the events and see how the cited information supports the stated conclusion?

The professional responsible for the record

A hiring decision may be reasonable, but a reviewer should be able to find the relevant criteria, evaluations and recorded justification before relying on a polished conclusion.

See five illustrated decisions: hiring, termination, benefits, medical authorization and AI governance. These are fictional demonstrations of documentation questions, not findings of wrongdoing or proof that JRS improves outcomes.

A fictional hiring decision: can the organization explain it?

Illustrative example · Not a real investigation

Recorded conclusion: “The applicant was not selected because they were not the right fit.”

What is absent: The excerpt does not identify job-related selection criteria, dated evaluations, comparison materials or the source-to-conclusion rationale.

Why this could matter: If the decision is challenged, the organization may need to locate the actual selection and evaluation records. JRS helps the reviewer flag missing support; it does not determine whether hiring discrimination occurred or establish legal defensibility.

Check your review of the fictional record

Identify the documentation gaps before revealing the explanations. No response is recorded or scored.

Question 1: What about this hiring decision cannot be reconstructed from the excerpt?

Reveal the explanation for question 1

Answer: Whether job-related selection criteria were applied, what each candidate evaluation recorded, and how those records supported the stated reason. The excerpt supplies none of that information.

Question 2: Does the missing explanation establish hiring discrimination or legal liability?

Reveal the explanation for question 2

Answer: No. Reviewers must examine the authorized retained records and applicable requirements. A documentation gap does not itself establish discrimination, liability or an incorrect decision.

Examine the JRS review method

After reviewing the example, examine the seven-point record check and consider whether its questions fit an existing investigation, HR, compliance, internal audit, legal or AI governance review of decision documentation. Public reviewer training is available; no software purchase or new workflow is required. AI governance use concerns the traceability of records and human reasoning, not model auditing or compliance certification.

Different perspectives can expose review questions

A second reader may notice an unsupported characterization, a missing event or a gap between source material and conclusion. Different backgrounds can expand the questions asked, but do not substitute for evidence.

What are we not seeing? Examine that question against verifiable sources, chronology and documented reasoning. “Different Minds. Fewer Blind Spots. Stronger Evidence.” expresses an aim for disciplined review, not a measured result from JRS participation.
