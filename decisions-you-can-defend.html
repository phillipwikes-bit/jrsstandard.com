Review the record, not merely its wording

A final memo can be persuasive without being independently reconstructable. JRS asks practitioners to inspect the documentary basis, chronology and explanation of material assertions before the record is finalized.

This initiative does not certify that a decision is legally defensible. It supplies review questions and free resources that practitioners can apply within their own responsibilities and applicable rules.

A five-step practical review

  1. Identify the decision: Locate the conclusion, responsible role and applicable decision context.
  2. Locate the support: Check that each material assertion cites a relevant, identifiable source, not merely an attached document.
  3. Reconstruct chronology: Distinguish dates of events, analysis, approval, notice and expiry.
  4. Challenge the inference: Identify missing reasoning steps, contrary evidence and alternative explanations.
  5. Preserve disposition: Record what was corrected, requested, escalated or left unresolved, with the responsible human reviewer.

Use within existing professional authority

Human reviewers remain responsible for investigation, analysis, corrections, decision-making and any legal or professional conclusions. These free methods do not authorize the use of the separately developed Decision Spellchecker software on confidential or consequential real records.
