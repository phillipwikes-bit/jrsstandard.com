Professional initiative · Documentation integrity

The Decisions You Can Defend Initiative

Make decisions you can explain. Keep records you can defend. Retained records should preserve the information an independent reviewer needs to examine a consequential decision.

Documentation review is not legal certification. A reviewable record does not establish that the underlying decision was lawful, correct, fair or legally defensible. JRS remains in operational validation.

Reconstruct a fictional decision

You are reviewing completed file TR-204 without speaking to its author. Read the record, form a provisional explanation, then examine the retained sources.

Every person, identifier, policy and event below is fictional. This is a bounded exercise, not an actual investigation or a claim about how often such gaps occur. No answers are collected or scored.

TR-204 · Closed file · Four retained documents

Termination approved

Document 1. Investigator's recommendation, May 12. “The employee missed three mandatory safety briefings. The attendance record and acknowledgement support termination under OP-12, section 4. I recommend termination.”

Document 4. Board minute, May 16.

“File TR-204 reviewed. Termination approved.”

Retained-file index: investigator's report; briefing attendance and acknowledgement; HR cover note and credential record; board minute.

1. Examine the retained sources

Document 2. Briefing attendance and acknowledgement

Attendance entries dated March 3, March 17 and April 7 record three missed mandatory safety briefings. The employee's acknowledgement identifies the briefing requirement. These are the sources cited by the investigator under OP-12, section 4.

Document 3. HR cover note, May 14

“Investigator's report attached. Separately, the access credential expired on April 30. AC-08 requires a current credential for assigned work. Credential record attached for the board's review.”

Keep both documents in view as you return to the recorded approval.

2. Return to the final approval

Document 4 · Board minute · May 16

“File TR-204 reviewed. Termination approved.”

End of retained file. Documents 1 through 4.

What question does this record leave you with?

Pause here and formulate your own question before opening the interpretation.

3. Compare your interpretation

You can follow why the investigator recommended termination. The source record also puts a separate credential issue before the board. The final minute records approval without identifying whether the board adopted the investigator's reasoning, relied on the credential issue, or considered both.

The recommendation and the final approval are different decisions. This file documents one rationale for recommending termination. It does not establish which rationale controlled the board's approval.

The record permits competing reconstructions. The exercise does not reveal a hidden true reason. A reviewer should identify the unresolved connection and seek authorized source verification rather than supply a rationale.

JRS does not determine whether the termination was discriminatory, justified or legally defensible. A documentation gap does not itself establish wrongdoing.

The question from the organization's perspective

Can someone who was not present reconstruct the evidence, chronology and reasoning behind a decision years later from the retained record alone?

Preserve the justification while the supporting information is still available. Decision inputs, contemporaneous notes and firsthand knowledge may become harder to retrieve after a record is finalized. JRS provides structured questions to identify gaps for human review; it cannot recover evidence that was never retained or establish that a decision was lawful or correct.

What a practitioner can examine

Evidence traceability

Is each evaluative conclusion anchored to identifiable information, rather than assumed facts or undocumented conversations?

Chronology and reasoning

Can an independent reader follow the events and see how the cited information supports the stated conclusion?

The professional responsible for the record

A hiring decision may be reasonable, but a reviewer should be able to find the relevant criteria, evaluations and recorded justification before relying on a polished conclusion.

See five fictional decisions: hiring, termination, benefits, medical authorization and AI governance. These are fictional demonstrations of documentation questions, not findings of wrongdoing or proof that JRS improves outcomes.

Examine the JRS review method

After reviewing the example, examine the seven-point record check and consider whether its questions fit an existing investigation, HR, compliance, internal audit, legal or AI governance review of decision documentation. Free reviewer training and investigator guides are available through the public Resources directory; no software purchase or new workflow is required. Different perspectives can expose review questions

A second reader may notice an unsupported characterization, a missing event or a gap between source material and conclusion. Different backgrounds can expand the questions asked, but do not substitute for evidence.

What are we not seeing? Examine that question against verifiable sources, chronology and documented reasoning. “Different Minds. Fewer Blind Spots. Stronger Evidence.” expresses an aim for disciplined review, not a measured result from JRS participation.
