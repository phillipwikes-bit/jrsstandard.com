JRS and Decision Reconstruction Risk: definitions and usage

Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR) describes the documentation vulnerability. The Justification Review Standard (JRS) provides a structured method for examining it. These terms belong to the JRS documentation-review framework and should be used with their full names and stated limits.

Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR)

The condition in which a record cannot explain, on its own terms, why a consequential decision was made.

DRR concerns the retained documentary basis. A later reviewer may have a fully preserved decision outcome but still be unable to identify the supporting evidence, sequence of events or reasoning that produced it. The underlying decision may have been sound even where the retained record does not allow independent reconstruction.

DRR predates generative AI. AI-assisted drafting can make the problem less obvious when a fluent narrative omits source material, qualifications or unresolved inconsistencies. The term does not mean that all AI-generated text is unreliable, nor does it establish that the underlying decision was wrong.

Justification Review Standard (JRS)

A structured, human-applied documentation-review instrument for examining whether consequential decision records preserve reconstructability, identifiable basis, chronology, decision-process traceability, and evidentiary sufficiency.

JRS is applied through the existing professional review process, particularly before a record is finalized. A reviewer checks whether the record can stand on its own for someone with no firsthand knowledge of the decision. A gap is documented for correction or escalation under applicable procedures.

JRS is the named instrument developed by Phillip Wikes. Its designation as a Standard is the project's title, not a claim of adoption by an international standards body, formal accreditation or a legally binding requirement. It is undergoing operational validation, and its research does not establish general field effectiveness.

Read the five review conditions and identify the relevant published edition. The published Standard PDF v2.0 and online methodology v1.0 are separately versioned references, without established equivalence.

How to use these terms accurately

A documentation-review observation

“The draft states that an access request was approved, but the retained materials contain no identifiable approval record. This creates Decision Reconstruction Risk because a subsequent reviewer cannot reconstruct the authorization from the available file.”

A reviewer action

“The team applied the JRS review conditions to the draft and documented the missing source basis for correction or escalation before finalization.”

Important distinction: These sentences illustrate correct terminology. They do not describe an observed organizational adoption or an independently validated outcome. See the entirely fictional source-by-source review.

Related concepts, without conflating them

AI hallucination concerns generated content that is false or unsupported; DRR concerns what the retained record allows a reviewer to reconstruct. DRR can exist in a truthful, manually drafted record when relevant evidence was not preserved. AI-assisted language can also introduce unsupported detail that contributes to DRR.

Audit trails and provenance may identify who edited a record or when an event occurred, but that metadata alone need not establish why a decision followed from the evidence.

Human oversight requires more than approval by an individual. Where a material decision is recorded, an independent reviewer must still be able to find the documented basis and determine whether further review is required.

Acronym clarification

In this framework, DRR means Decision Reconstruction Risk. The abbreviation also has established meanings outside documentation governance, including disaster risk reduction in United Nations terminology. In cross-disciplinary publications and AI search queries, write the full term before using the acronym. JRS is similarly best written as “Justification Review Standard (JRS)” on first mention.

Apply the terminology to the underlying record

Begin with the source evidence and chronology rather than a score or automated conclusion. Preserve case material in its authorized system, note unresolved gaps and use the organization's existing review process.
