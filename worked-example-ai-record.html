When an AI-drafted record sounds complete but its evidence does not

A source-by-source demonstration of how a reviewer can detect missing approval evidence, a weak chronology and unsupported conclusions before a record is finalized. All entities, dates, policies and events are fictional.

JRS public practitioner referencePrepared 3 November 2026Methodology developed by Phillip WikesAI-assisted editorial draft

Purpose and evidence boundary

This exercise illustrates documentation review, not the accuracy of an AI model or the effectiveness of an automated JRS product. The fictional records below were authored solely for training and are not a participant submission, operational pilot result or independently adjudicated research case.

Constructed and unvalidated illustration. Nothing on this page establishes that any actual supplier was granted access, that any policy exists, or that a JRS reviewer or Review Engine produced an empirical result. No identifiable case records should be uploaded to this site.

1. What the retained source notes show

Assume a fictional reviewer receives the following four items and no other source material. The supplied file is the entire evidence available for this exercise.

Source A: Request
11 September 2026. Request SA-104 seeks temporary supplier account access for maintenance during 12 to 18 September. Status: requested, not approved.
Source B: Coordinator note
12 September 2026. A project coordinator writes, “Please prioritize the maintenance request.” The note does not identify the coordinator as the authorized system owner and does not record an approval.
Source C: Access log
12 September 2026. An entry records that an account was activated. The provided extract has no recorded expiration date or closure entry.
Source D: Fictional policy
Policy AC-07 requires recorded system-owner approval and a defined access expiration before access is provisioned. No such approval document is in the supplied file.

2. The polished but unsupported draft

Suppose the following text is proposed as an AI-assisted summary for permanent retention:

“Supplier access was appropriately extended because urgent maintenance was necessary. The responsible owner approved the extension under AC-07, and access was closed within the planned window.”

The sentence appears orderly, but the supplied sources do not establish an approved extension, a responsible owner's authorization, the application of policy AC-07 or the closure of access. “Urgent” is also stronger than the coordinator's instruction to prioritize the request.

3. Review each statement against its basis

Source-to-claim comparison for the constructed record

4. Apply the five JRS review conditions

  1. Reconstructability: A later reviewer cannot reconstruct the authorization and closure history from these four items.
  2. Basis identification: The approval, policy compliance and closure assertions lack an identifiable supporting source.
  3. Chronology: The request and activation dates are supplied, but the approval sequence and termination of access are not established.
  4. Decision-process traceability: The draft supplies a justification that the underlying record does not document.
  5. Evidentiary sufficiency: The retained materials do not support several material conclusions in the proposed summary.

These are illustrative observations under the five-condition methodology, not formal scored results or a prediction about a real reviewer. The full methodological definitions are provided in the review conditions reference.

5. What a defensible reviewer response looks like

Disposition for this exercise: Do not finalize the proposed AI-assisted narrative on the supplied evidence. Seek the approval record, access expiry and closure evidence through the authorized workflow. If any remain unavailable, record the limitation and route the matter for a human determination rather than inventing support.

While verification is pending, a narrower status note could read:

“Request SA-104 was recorded on 11 September 2026 for temporary supplier maintenance access. The available log shows account activation on 12 September. The supplied file does not contain the required system-owner approval, a recorded expiration date or a closure entry. The authorization and closure history requires further verification.”

This example describes only what its fictional source notes establish. It does not conclude that approval never occurred. Absence from the supplied record is not proof of absence everywhere.

Use the method in an existing review process

Identify the proposed conclusion, locate the original sources and match each material statement to its documented basis. Record gaps and refer the draft for correction or escalation within the organization's existing process. Do not replace source verification with fluent rewriting.
