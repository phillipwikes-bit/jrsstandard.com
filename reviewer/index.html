Reviewer Training and Certificate

Six self-paced modules and a companion desk reference for deciding whether a record can still explain its own conclusion, particularly where AI-assisted drafting has contributed to it. Built for compliance, HR, audit, legal, AI governance and investigative reviewers. Module 1 opens with no sign-up.

Written by Phillip Wikes, former Lead Civil Rights Officer, Maryland Commission on Civil Rights, M.S. Negotiation and Conflict Management. The five review conditions taught here have been applied record by record by 58 international reviewers across three studies, including 36 independent experts across 16 countries who each completed a full 24-record set.

Curriculum

Six modules, worked at your own pace

Each module ends in a short applied exercise. Progress saves in your browser, so you can stop and come back. The full set takes most reviewers two to three hours.

Module 01

JRS Review Conditions

The five conditions themselves: reconstructability, basis identification, chronology, decision-process traceability, evidentiary sufficiency. What each one asks and what a failure looks like.

Module 02

Common Documentation Conditions

The patterns that recur across real records: conclusions without a stated basis, chronologies that cannot be followed, and characterizations with no visible source.

Module 03

Secondary Review and Escalation

What a second reviewer needs from a file, when a record should be returned to the drafter, and how to write an escalation that survives being read a year later.

Module 04

AI-Assisted Record Review

Reviewing records that a model helped draft. Polished prose can hide missing evidence and untraceable reasoning, and the same five conditions still apply.

Module 05

Investigation Review

Applying the conditions to investigative files: interview records, evidence handling, and whether the finding can be rebuilt from the file alone.

Module 06

Implementation Sequence

Putting pre-finalization review into an existing workflow without adding a stage, and what to report to leadership.
