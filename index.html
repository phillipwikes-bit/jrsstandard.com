For investigations, HR, compliance, audit, legal and AI governance · JRS™

Can the record explain the decision?

JRS is a human-applied documentation-review instrument. It is designed for investigators, HR professionals, compliance officers, auditors, legal reviewers and AI governance professionals examining whether consequential decision records retain evidence, chronology and reasoning.

Open seven-point record check See five fictional decision examples

The decision may be permanent. The evidence behind it may not be. A later reviewer may have only the retained record, not the firsthand knowledge of the person who made the decision. If the record preserves a conclusion but not its sources, chronology or reasoning, that creates Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR). JRS helps reviewers identify these gaps while supporting information may still be available. It does not recover missing evidence or determine whether the decision was correct.

Apply JRS within an existing authorized review workflow. No new software platform or case-record upload is required.

Five familiar situations · One documentation question

A decision can change someone's life. Can the record explain why?

Select an example. Each fictional excerpt shows a conclusion but omits part of the evidence, chronology or reasoning needed to reconstruct it. See what a later reviewer may need to reconstruct, then examine the JRS review question. Hiring is open as a first example. All five examples are fictional and do not describe actual people or organizations.

Hiring: You didn't get the job.

The recorded explanation

“Not the right fit.”

But where is the evidence explaining this decision?

What is missing? (DRR)

No identifiable selection criteria, interview evaluation or comparison explaining this conclusion appears in the fictional excerpt.

Why could it matter?

If the decision is challenged, the employer may struggle to show from this excerpt whether job-related criteria were applied consistently. A hiring discrimination complaint could require further evidence.

What would JRS flag?

Locate the selection criteria, dated interview records, comparative evidence and decision rationale. If support cannot be verified, document the gap and refer it for authorized review.

An incomplete excerpt does not prove discrimination or establish legal liability. JRS does not supply missing evidence or determine the merits of the decision.

Termination: You lost your job.

The recorded explanation

“Repeated performance problems.”

But where is the evidence explaining this decision?

What is missing? (DRR)

No dated incidents, supporting performance records, applicable policy or explanation of the employee's response is identified in the excerpt.

Why could it matter?

An employment dispute or internal appeal may be harder to evaluate when the records do not show what occurred, when it occurred or how the conclusion was reached.

What would JRS flag?

Examine contemporaneous records, the policy in effect, the relevant chronology, the employee's documented response and the stated reasoning.

Missing documentation alone does not establish that a termination was improper. JRS does not supply missing evidence or determine the merits of the decision.

Benefits: Your assistance was denied.

The recorded explanation

“You do not qualify.”

But where is the evidence explaining this decision?

What is missing? (DRR)

The excerpt does not identify the eligibility rule, dated information, calculation or explanation connecting the facts to the denial.

Why could it matter?

A person may have difficulty understanding the decision or preparing an authorized appeal. A subsequent reviewer cannot readily reconstruct the eligibility determination from the excerpt.

What would JRS flag?

Locate the applicable rule, source information, calculation, event dates and documented connection between the record and the conclusion.

This example does not establish a particular disclosure entitlement or benefit eligibility. JRS does not supply missing evidence or determine the merits of the decision.

Medical: Your treatment request was denied.

The recorded explanation

“Not medically necessary.”

But where is the evidence explaining this decision?

What is missing? (DRR)

No coverage criterion, clinical material considered, review date, accountable reviewer or reasoned application of the criterion is identified in the fictional notice.

Why could it matter?

Without a reviewable explanation, a patient and authorized reviewer may struggle to understand the denial or evaluate an appeal. Care could be delayed while the basis is clarified.

What would JRS flag?

Check whether the retained authorization file identifies the applicable rule, clinical sources, decision chronology and reviewer rationale; flag missing documentation for authorized review.

JRS does not determine medical necessity, treatment appropriateness or insurance coverage. JRS does not supply missing evidence or determine the merits of the decision.

AI governance: The AI-assisted decision was approved.

The recorded explanation

“Human reviewed and approved.”

But where is the evidence explaining this decision?

What is missing? (DRR)

The record excerpt identifies no supporting source version, dated human assessment, reviewer notes or reason the recommendation was accepted.

Why could it matter?

A later audit or governance review may be unable to establish what the responsible human actually examined before approving the recommendation.

What would JRS flag?

Identify source records, assessment version, human reviewer, approval date and reasoning; record unresolved gaps without treating an approval label as evidence of review.

JRS does not audit a model, validate a system or certify compliance. JRS does not supply missing evidence or determine the merits of the decision.

One-minute method demonstration

What Decision Reconstruction Risk looks like in one record.

A polished conclusion is not a substitute for evidence. This entirely fictional supplier-access example illustrates how a reviewer identifies a missing basis, examines chronology and checks the sources before accepting a revised record. It is not an automated assessment or an actual case.

  1. 01 · Draft record

    Conclusion without its basis

    “Supplier access was extended because the request was urgent.”

    No dated request, approving authority, governing rule, or access end date is identified.

  2. 02 · Review the record

    Check before finalization

    Locate the request, approval, applicable policy, event dates and access log. Confirm what each source actually supports, and reconcile the chronology.

    Where a source cannot be located, record the gap for human review rather than inventing support.

  3. 03 · Illustrative revision

    Only after source verification

    “On 12 September 2026, the system owner approved supplier access through 18 September in response to request SA-104 dated 11 September, under access policy AC-07. The approval and end date are recorded in the access log.”

    Use this form only if the underlying sources confirm every stated fact.

Fictional identifiers and dates. SA-104, AC-07, the people and the events are invented solely to illustrate documentation review. A more detailed narrative is not evidence by itself. If the basis is unavailable, the proper disposition is further review or escalation, not an unsupported correction.

Evidence, with its boundaries

What the JRS research establishes.

Detection findings, agreement results and operational effectiveness answer different questions. None should be presented as a substitute for the others.

  • 83.9% mean detection accuracy16 independent international experts from 11 countries across five continents, each assessing 24 constructed records (384 graded judgments). Reviewer-level 95% confidence interval: 72.7% to 95.1%.
  • Separate reliability criterion not met104 analyzed determinations on a separate sample; only 10 records had multiple raters. Gwet’s AC1 was 0.739 (invited) and 0.623 (open enrolment); the prespecified confidence-bound requirement was not met.
  • Field effectiveness not establishedThese constructed-record and reviewer-agreement results do not validate performance on real organizational records, or operation of an automated review service.

The method

Five questions that help expose documentation gaps.

Instead of asking only whether a narrative sounds complete, examine whether a later reviewer can reconstruct events, identify source evidence, follow the chronology and trace the reasoning. The conditions concern the retained record, not the writer or the legal merits of the decision.

01. ReconstructabilityCan a reviewer establish what occurred and why?
02. Basis IdentificationIs the evidence or authority for the decision identifiable?
03. ChronologyAre events and decision steps in an intelligible order?
04. Decision-Process TraceabilityCan the reviewer follow how facts led to the conclusion?
05. Evidentiary SufficiencyDoes the retained record contain enough support for later review?

Accountability

Evidence must remain distinguishable from assurance.

JRS research includes detection findings from constructed records. These findings should not be interpreted as proof of effectiveness in real organizational records or as confirmation of professional accreditation.

Current validation boundary

The research record identifies findings, negative results and unresolved reliability questions. Operational effectiveness remains under validation. JRS does not currently provide public automated record processing, legal advice, or certified conformity assessment.
