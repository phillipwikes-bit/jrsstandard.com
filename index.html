Independent professional reference · JRS™

Can the record explain the decision after its author is gone?

The Justification Review Standard is a human-applied method for checking whether a consequential record preserves enough evidence, chronology and reasoning for someone else to review it independently.

The method

Five conditions for a reviewable record.

JRS examines the record as a retained evidence artifact. The question is whether an independent reviewer can reconstruct the basis and reasoning without depending on the original decision-maker's memory.

01. ReconstructabilityCan a reviewer establish what occurred and why?
02. Identifiable basisIs the evidence or authority for the decision identifiable?
03. ChronologyAre events and decision steps in an intelligible order?
04. Reasoning traceCan the reviewer follow how facts led to the conclusion?
05. SufficiencyDoes the retained record contain enough support for later review?

The conditions identify documentation issues requiring professional review. They do not determine the outcome of an investigation, audit or legal matter. Read the complete methodology.

Accountability

Evidence must remain distinguishable from assurance.

JRS research includes detection findings from constructed records. These findings should not be interpreted as proof of effectiveness in real organizational records or as confirmation of professional accreditation.

Current validation boundary

The research record identifies findings, negative results and unresolved reliability questions. Operational effectiveness remains under validation. JRS does not currently provide public automated record processing, legal advice, or certified conformity assessment.

Development and stewardship

Built around the realities of retained records.

JRS was developed by Phillip Wikes, former Lead Civil Rights Officer at the Maryland Commission on Civil Rights. The instrument focuses on documentation conditions that become consequential when another reviewer must reconstruct a completed decision.
