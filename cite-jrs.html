How to Cite JRS: Standard, Method, and Research

Cite the specific source actually examined. The published Standard PDF, online method and research record have different purposes and version identifiers.

Publication identity and references

SourceIdentity to recordUse and limitation

Suggested plain-language citation format

Use the author, exact title, edition or version, publication year, publisher or site, and the specific page or URL. Examples below are suggested bibliographic descriptions, not claims of formal designation by an external standards authority.

Published PDF: Wikes, P. (2026). Justification Review Standard (PDF Version 2.0). JRS. Available from the published Standard PDF.

Online methodology: Wikes, P. (May 2026). JRS Decision Defensibility Methodology (Online Version 1.0). JRS website.

DRR definition: Wikes, P. Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR): Definition. JRS website. Include the access date used by the reviewer.

Research quotation and evidence controls

  1. For the detection panel, identify the 24 constructed records, 16 reviewers and 384 graded judgments. The reported 83.9% is mean detection accuracy in that sample, not demonstrated operational accuracy.
  2. For reviewer agreement, identify the distinct sample of 22 analyzed raters and 10 multi-rater records. The pre-registered two-part reliability criterion was not met.
  3. Do not combine detection and reliability findings as though they arose from one experiment.
  4. Do not cite unpublished engineering checks as independent real-world operational validation.
  5. Do not treat a professional publication or an editorial recommendation as certification or endorsement of a product.
Version caution. PDF v2.0 and online methodology v1.0 are separately published references. No declaration of equivalence, supersession or compatibility is made here. See publication editions and research findings and limitations.

What this page does not establish

A stable citation route improves reference accuracy and traceability. It does not establish copyright ownership, trade-mark registration, third-party accreditation, methodological validity or operational fitness. The original publication remains controlling for its own content.
