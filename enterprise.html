Method Information · Current Boundary

Information for organizations reviewing the JRS method.

JRS examines whether a decision record preserves the basis, evidence, reasoning, and chronology required for later independent review. The published method is available for study. The Review Engine remains a controlled local-development candidate. It is not a public record-processing service, production offering, or available integration product.

JRS was developed by Phillip Wikes, former Lead Civil Rights Officer, Maryland Commission on Civil Rights. Published research concerns the JRS method. It does not validate the controlled Review Engine or authorize its external use.

What is available publicly

The public site is a method and research platform. It does not provide a sandbox, token-gated API, record intake, technical evaluation access, licensing offer, or acquisition pathway.

Published method

The Standard, Codebook, field guides, training materials, and constructed examples support independent study and manual application.

Research record

Published results, negative findings, and limitations describe evidence about the method. They are not a validation claim for the Review Engine.

Manifest specification

The public schema describes a versioned evidence artifact. It is not an offer of a Manifest-generation service.

Controlled materials

Implementation protocols, benchmark materials, evaluation data, detailed engineering methods, and provenance records are not public-site materials.

Current status. The Engine does not accept public record text. Its configured local-development profile is limited to completed, non-HR supplier-access exception drafts and requires human review.
