## Version A: full length (LinkedIn allows 3,000 characters; this is ~1,780)

```
Tanvi led the employment and industrial-relations arm of a validation study I ran on
documentation quality in AI-assisted records, and she is the reason that arm produced
anything worth publishing.

The work was harder than it sounds. She selected and screened 22 adjudicated employment and
labour matters from public sources across three jurisdictional systems: United States
Supreme Court decisions, Federal Labor Relations Authority decisions, and United Kingdom
Employment Tribunal judgments. For each one she read the employer's record as it appeared in
the decision, applied a five-condition structured review, and recorded her assessment before
looking at how the matter actually resolved. Then she recorded the outcome from the source.
Twenty-two times, one case at a time, on her own time, alongside a full HR role.

What impressed me most was her discipline about sequence. The entire value of that design
depends on the reviewer committing to a judgment before knowing the answer, and it is a
genuinely uncomfortable way to work: you have to be willing to be wrong on the record. She
never once asked me what an outcome had been before she had filed her read. Plenty of
experienced people would have peeked.

The results held up. Records she flagged as incomplete went on to draw an adverse finding in
7 of 9 cases, against 2 of 13 for the records she passed. When the same review was applied
in a different domain by a different practitioner, it showed nothing, and understanding why
those two results differ turned out to be one of the more interesting findings in the whole
programme. Her corpus is what made that comparison possible.

She is also straightforward to work with in a way I did not take for granted. She flagged
ambiguous cases rather than forcing them into a category, told me when a coding rule did not
fit what she was seeing, and kept going through a stretch where the project had no visible
deadline and no reason to prioritise it beyond wanting the work done properly.

She is first author on the resulting article, which is the correct order: the research is
hers.

If you work in HR, employment relations or compliance and you need someone who can hold a
standard steady across dozens of files without drifting, and who will tell you when your
method is wrong rather than quietly working around it, hire her.
```
