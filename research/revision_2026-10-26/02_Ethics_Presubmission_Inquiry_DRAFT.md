**Subject:** Pre-submission inquiry: human-participant study conducted without institutional ethics review

Dear Editors,

We would like to ask, before submitting, whether a study with the following arrangements could be considered by [JOURNAL NAME].

The manuscript reports a controlled study in which 16 professional reviewers in 11 countries classified 24 constructed, AI-generated decision records as supported or unsupported. We measured their accuracy against a pre-specified reference classification, as well as the agreement between raters. The protocol and analysis plan were registered before data collection.

The study was not reviewed by an institutional ethics committee. The authors are independent researchers without an institutional affiliation, so no committee was available to us. The arrangements were as follows:

- All participants were adult professionals who volunteered unpaid and in a personal capacity, and gave informed consent.
- Participants could withdraw at any time before publication.
- Every document reviewed was wholly synthetic. No real case, person or organisation was involved.
- No personal data was collected beyond name, professional title, country and contact address.
- No vulnerable population was involved.
- Responses are reported only in aggregate, and participants are named only with their consent.

We would state all of this plainly in the manuscript's ethics declaration. We would not claim any approval or exemption.

Could you tell us whether a study conducted on this basis is eligible for consideration, and if so, whether you would need any further information or documentation from us?

We would also be grateful for confirmation that the article processing charge is payable only on acceptance, and whether any waiver or discount is available to independent researchers without institutional funding.

Thank you for your time.

Kind regards,

Phillip Wikes, on behalf of both authors
