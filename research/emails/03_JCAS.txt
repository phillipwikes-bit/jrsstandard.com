Subject: Presubmission enquiry: case study on public-records documentation quality

Dear JCAS Editorial Board,

I am writing to ask whether the following would fall within the scope of the Journal of Contemporary Archival Studies as a case study.

We have a 32-case study of whether the documented basis of United States public-records determinations can be independently reconstructed from the record itself. The corpus covers four document classes and two states, issued between 2005 and 2026. In the five cases where a State or City Comptroller had independently audited the same programme, our record-level assessment and the auditor's own finding agreed in all five.

It is a pilot and the manuscript says so. The corpus is non-random, 22 of the 32 cases carry a single reviewer, a blind second read of 10 cases returned 70 percent agreement, and one author developed the review protocol under evaluation and ran the analyses. The design places the protocol against independent government audit findings and an independent blind second read for that reason.

The manuscript is 16 pages, which we believe sits inside your case study length, and an anonymised version is prepared for double-blind review.

Would this be appropriate for consideration, and does the journal accept manuscripts previously posted as a preprint?

Sincerely,
Stacyann Young and Phillip Wikes
