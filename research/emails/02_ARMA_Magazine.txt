To: editor@armaintl.org
Subject: Article proposal: can the basis of a decision be rebuilt from the record?

Dear Editor,

I am writing to propose an article for ARMA Magazine and to ask whether the subject interests you before I write it to your length.

The question is one your readership meets constantly. A record can be retained, accessible, complete and professionally written, and still fail the person who has to review it later, because the evidence behind the conclusion is not on the page. We examined that across 32 United States public-records determinations from 32 distinct sources, four document classes and two states, issued between 2005 and 2026.

The finding that would matter most to records managers is this. In the five cases where a State or City Comptroller had independently audited the same programme, our record-level assessment and the auditor's own finding agreed in all five. A structured read of the record anticipated what a government auditor concluded through a different method.

The practical form is a five-point check that can be applied before a consequential record is finalised, and the article would set out where to place it in an existing review workflow.

I would write this as a practitioner piece at your usual length, without statistical apparatus. Would this suit the magazine?

Sincerely,

Phillip Wikes and Stacyann Young
