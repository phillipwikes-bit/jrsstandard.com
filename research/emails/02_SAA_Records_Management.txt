Subject: Case study enquiry: reconstructability of public-records determinations

Dear Records Management Section,

I am writing to ask whether the following would suit the Records Management case study series.

We have a 32-case study asking a records and information management question: when a consequential determination is later reviewed, does the record itself preserve enough of its evidentiary basis and reasoning for an independent reviewer to reconstruct how the organisation acted? The corpus is 32 publicly available United States public-records determinations and audits from 32 distinct sources, across four document classes and two states, issued between 2005 and 2026.

In the five cases where a State or City Comptroller had independently audited the same programme, our record-level assessment and the auditor's own finding agreed in all five.

It is a pilot and the manuscript says so. The corpus is non-random, 22 of the 32 cases carry a single reviewer, a blind second read of 10 cases returned 70 percent agreement, and one author developed the review protocol under evaluation and ran the analyses. The design places the protocol against independent government audit findings and an independent blind second read for that reason.

The manuscript runs about 5,600 words and would be trimmed to your 5,000. Would the subject be appropriate for the series?

Sincerely,
Stacyann Young and Phillip Wikes
