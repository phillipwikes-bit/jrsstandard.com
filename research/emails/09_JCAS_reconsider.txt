May I put one thing to you, because I think my summary buried it and your reading followed from what I actually wrote.

My enquiry led with the corpus and the audit agreement, which made it sound like a records management study. The paper's frame is archival. It is built on Duranti's separation of reliability from authenticity in Archivaria, and on Yeo's account in The American Archivist of records as persistent representations of activities, which locates a record's value in what it allows a later party to understand rather than in its survival. Our question is Yeo's question put to a test: does a record retain the evidentiary capacity to let someone who was not there rebuild the action it documents, and can that be assessed consistently from the record alone? We also work against ISO 15489's requirement that a record be authentic, reliable, of integrity and usable, and argue that a record can satisfy all four and still fail a later reviewer.

That question seems to us to sit alongside work you have published. Beyond Description asks whether a descriptive instrument adequately represents what it describes and whether a later user can follow it. Tracing Transformations reads a correspondence corpus through the records continuum, which is an argument about evidential capacity persisting beyond the moment of creation. We are asking the same question of a different material, and measuring the answer.

That measurement is the contribution we would offer your readership. Archival science has a precise vocabulary for the qualities a record must hold and comparatively little item-level empirical work testing whether individual records actually hold them. We assessed 32 records against that standard, and in the five cases where a government auditor had independently examined the same programme, our reading and the auditor's finding agreed in all five. The rest of the paper is limitations, including a constructed reliability estimate on ten records that we report without softening.

One further thing, offered as context and not as a complaint, since your reply is not part of it. No referee has yet read this study.
