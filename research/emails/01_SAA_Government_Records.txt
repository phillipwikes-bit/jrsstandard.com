Subject: Case study enquiry: documentation quality in 32 public-records determinations

Dear Government Records Section,

I am writing to ask whether the following would suit the Government Records case study series before I cut it to your length.

We have a 32-case study of whether the documented basis of United States public-records determinations can be independently reconstructed from the record itself. The corpus covers four document classes and two states, issued between 2005 and 2026: New York appellate and trial decisions, New York Committee on Open Government advisory opinions, Connecticut Freedom of Information Commission final decisions, and compliance audits from the New York State and New York City Comptrollers.

In the five cases where a Comptroller had independently audited the same programme, our record-level assessment and the auditor's own finding agreed in all five that the programme could not adequately evidence its public-records administration.

It is a pilot and the manuscript says so. The corpus is non-random, 22 of the 32 cases carry a single reviewer, a blind second read of 10 cases returned 70 percent agreement, and one author developed the review protocol under evaluation and ran the analyses. The design places the protocol against independent government audit findings and an independent blind second read for that reason.

The manuscript currently runs about 5,600 words, so it would need trimming to your 5,000. Before doing that I would value your view on whether accountability and transparency in public-records determinations fits the series.

Sincerely,
Stacyann Young and Phillip Wikes
