ORCID: WHAT IT IS AND WHO DOES WHAT

SHORT ANSWER TO "HOW DO I GET HIS EMAIL TO UPDATE HIS ORCID"

You do not update his ORCID, and you cannot. An ORCID iD is personal and
self-controlled. Only the person it belongs to can create it or change it. That
is precisely why it answers the editor's question: a record you could edit on his
behalf would prove nothing about whether he is a separate person.

You also do not need his email address in order to get his ORCID iD. He creates
the iD himself and sends you the number. Nothing needs looking up.

And you do not need to find his email address at all. You ask him for it on
LinkedIn, which is the channel you already use and where he replied on 28 August.
The message does that.

WHAT AN ORCID iD LOOKS LIKE

A sixteen digit number in four blocks, for example 0000-0002-1825-0097. That
number is all you need from him. It is public by design and safe to put on a
paper.

WHAT EACH OF YOU DOES

HIM
  1. Go to orcid.org and register. Free, about two minutes.
  2. Send you the iD.
That is the whole task. He does not need to fill in a profile for the iD to work
as identification.

YOU
  1. Go to orcid.org and register your own. Neither of you has one recorded
     anywhere in the estate, and the submission package audit already lists ORCID
     as a missing item.
  2. Use a direct personal address, not info@jrsstandard.com.

WHY THE JOURNAL CARES

The editor's objection was that he could not tell whether the two author records
belonged to two people. Two ORCID iDs, each created and held by its own owner,
is the standard answer to that question across scholarly publishing. It is
stronger evidence than any assurance either of you can write in a letter.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT FIX

ORCID confirms identity. It does not confirm affiliation, and it does not speak
to authorship contribution. Those are already stated correctly in the paper and
were not what the editor queried.

ONE THING TO GET RIGHT AT THE SAME TIME

The estate's canonical contact address, info@jrsstandard.com, is a website
contact. It is not a submission address. Every future journal submission needs a
direct personal address for each author. That distinction is what caused this
rejection and it had never been written down anywhere before now.
