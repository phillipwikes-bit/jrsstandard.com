Subject: Presubmission enquiry for the January window: public-records documentation quality

Dear Professor Cary,

I am writing ahead of the January submission window to ask whether the following would fall within the scope of The American Archivist.

We have a 32-case study of whether the documented basis of United States public-records determinations can be independently reconstructed from the record itself. The corpus covers four document classes and two states, issued between 2005 and 2026. In the five cases where a State or City Comptroller had independently audited the same programme, our record-level assessment and the auditor's own finding agreed in all five that the programme could not adequately evidence its public-records administration.

It is a pilot and the manuscript says so. The corpus is non-random, 22 of the 32 cases carry a single reviewer, a blind second read of 10 cases returned 70 percent agreement, and one author developed the review protocol under evaluation and ran the analyses. The design places the protocol against independent government audit findings and an independent blind second read for that reason. The manuscript is approximately 5,600 words with an anonymised version prepared.

Would this be appropriate to submit when the window opens on 1 January, and does the journal accept manuscripts previously posted as a preprint?

Sincerely,
Stacyann Young and Phillip Wikes
