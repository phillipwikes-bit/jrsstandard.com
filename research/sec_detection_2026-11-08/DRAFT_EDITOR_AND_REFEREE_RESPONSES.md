## Referee 1
**R1.1, socioeconomic implications.** We appreciate the distinction between the question experimentally examined and its wider socioeconomic importance. Our empirical contribution concerns expert judgments on evidentiary support in constructed decision records. We will condense the societal implications and explicitly present unmeasured review costs, appeals, equity impacts and institutional resource effects as testable directions for future study, rather than consequences established by these observations.

**R1.2, introduction and evidence of adoption.** We agree that the Introduction should establish the documentation-oversight problem without implying that this study measured sector-wide adoption of generative drafting. We will support any factual deployment statements with verifiable sector evidence, remove numerical results from the opening, and conclude with the study aim and research questions. Where narrowly relevant adoption evidence is unavailable, the language will describe a potential workflow exposure rather than an established prevalence.

**R1.3, prior experiments and research gap.** We appreciate the request to place this work in direct dialogue with prior experimental scholarship. The paper examines a different outcome from general reliance on algorithmic recommendations: it asks whether reviewers can identify missing evidentiary support in the retained record. We will compare that task with the specific designs and outcomes of Alon-Barkat and Busuioc (2023), Green and Chen (2019), Green (2022), Ben-Michael et al. (2025), and further verified audit/reviewer studies, rather than claiming that human oversight has not previously been measured. Sources: https://doi.org/10.1093/jopart/muac007 ; https://doi.org/10.1145/3287560.3287563 ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0267364922000292 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00061 .

**R1.4, explicit hypotheses.** We agree that the quantitative criteria should be explicit in the Methods. We will reproduce the decision thresholds and their actual chronology from the preserved protocol, distinguish prospectively specified decisions from subsequent exploratory analyses, and avoid representing an internal time-stamped file as a publicly deposited preregistration. Evidence: research/DRR_Detection_Validation_Protocol.md; research/OSF_PreRegistration.md.

**R1.5, theoretical framework.** We appreciate the distinction between an analytic frame and a tested economic model. The paper will use agency theory and asymmetric information to explain why reconstructable reasons merit investigation, while confining empirical claims to measured reviewer classifications and agreement. No cost, expenditure or market effect will be attributed to the study.

**R1.6, companion preprint.** We agree that an unreviewed related preprint does not independently corroborate the present finding. We will confine the reference to background context, disclose the shared author and remove combined conclusions based on the two papers.

**R1.7, empirical comparative discussion.** We will organize the Discussion around the observed detection result, between-reviewer variation and limited reliability evidence, and examine for each external comparator which outcome, task, participants and materials differ. We will use “extends”, “differs from”, or “is not directly comparable to” only when the cited study's actual methods and findings support the characterization. Sources: R1.7 and the verified studies named at R1.3.

**R1.8, discussion organization.** We appreciate the suggestion to prioritize analytical synthesis over repeated characterization of the study's strengths. We will merge overlapping subheadings, remove self-appraisal and place design decisions and limitations in the appropriate methodological and concluding sections.

**R1.9, conclusions.** We agree that the observed result supports a narrower conclusion than broad claims about the dependability of individual reviewers. The conclusion will state that the defined panel exceeded the prespecified classification-agreement threshold in a deliberately contrasted constructed corpus, whereas dependable single-reviewer performance and the separate reliability criterion were not established. Operational recommendations will be presented as proposals to evaluate, not interventions this study proved effective.

## Referee 2
**R2.1, registration and deviations.** We appreciate the request for reconstructable study chronology. The repository contains dated protocol records and an author-defined key. We will identify their actual timestamps and public-access status, and distinguish (i) the internal documented plan, (ii) any changes from it, and (iii) exploratory procedures. The three automated reference passes, additional mixed-effects model, smaller reliability set, baseline-label exclusion and exploratory automated runs beginning before 6 July will be reported accurately. We will not retrospectively describe a later public deposit as a prospective OSF registration. Evidence: research/OSF_PreRegistration.md; research/REFERENCE_CLASSIFICATION_SOURCE_REPORT_2026-08-18.md.

**R2.2, reference classification.** We appreciate the concern that reproducibility under an author-defined rule is not identical to independently established ground truth. The intended reference classifications were fixed before the outcome analysis, and three blinded LLM instances reproduced all 24. We will describe this explicitly as **agreement with a predefined operational reference classification**, not human-validated truth. Where model identities, prompts, construction details and human editing records cannot be recovered from original evidence, they will remain disclosed limitations rather than be reconstructed retrospectively. Evidence: research/Verified_Key.md; research/Intended_Key_authorside.md; research/CORPUS_CONSTRUCTION_LOG_VERIFICATION_2026-08-18.md.

**R2.3, class-specific text features.** We agree that constructed grounded and ungrounded records may differ in observable cues beyond documentation support. We will calculate transparent descriptive summaries from the frozen 24-record packet and report these by intended class, without treating these descriptive checks as evidence of causal isolation. Evidence: research/AnswerKey_Verification_Packet.md.

**R2.4, task and platform.** We agree that readers need the exact task to interpret classification agreement. We will provide the preserved prompt/questions, response options, scored mapping, available timing/ordering evidence, collection dates and resubmission rule. We will specify which review instrument applied to the detection panel and distinguish it from the separate five-condition reliability exercise. Any procedure not documented by surviving records will be clearly identified as unavailable. Evidence: research/AnswerKey_Verification_Packet.md; research/closed_aggregates_2026-08-15.json.

**R2.5, sample and reviewer characteristics.** We agree that an international self-selected panel is a defined sample, not a representative population. We will give the inclusion/recruitment process, documented experience and professional grouping where verifiable, the number of invitations and nonstarters, and a clear rationale for the attainable sample, without suggesting that every credential was formally independently verified unless evidence supports that statement. Evidence: research/aie_submission_2026-09-01/01_Blinded_Manuscript.md and original roster/progress records.

**R2.6, intervals and response tendency.** We appreciate the distinction between observed reviewer-level performance and the uncertainty estimator used. We will retain the original Student-t result as originally reported, and add reproducible sensitivity analyses from the retained reviewer data when available. The sensitivity/specificity contrast will be tested on paired reviewer scores if those scores can be reconstructed; otherwise it will remain strictly descriptive. Signal-detection indices will be reported only when their inputs and assumptions can be substantiated. Evidence: research/closed_aggregates_2026-08-15.json; corresponding primary response records to be identified.

**R2.7, exploratory crossed-effects model.** We agree that the observed mean of the 16 reviewer accuracies (83.9%) and an 89.2% conditional prediction with random effects set to zero are different estimands. We will specify the model formula, fit, boundary/singularity diagnostics, software versions and the prediction target; we will not describe the model estimate as unchanged panel accuracy. Evidence: Referee 2 comment 7 and model source/results in the maintained research repository, subject to reproduction.

**R2.8, agreement and denominators.** We appreciate the need to reconcile all denominators at the same decision grain. The preserved output distinguishes 113 originally submitted five-condition determinations and 104 retained after latest-per-rater/record de-duplication; its AC1 results are 0.739 (invited) and 0.623 (open enrollment). We will rerun the condition-level tabulation on the retained rows, report the number of raters per record, provide the companion coefficients and interval procedures, and disclose the three-person sample overlap and the baseline-mode sensitivity. The manuscript will not report 565 five-condition observations as the de-duplicated denominator unless a matching original execution record supports it. Evidence: research/current_reliability_2026-08-18.json; research/compute_ac1_ci.py; Referee 2 comment 8.

**R2.9, automated consistency.** We agree that repeated runs on the same 15 records are dependent and cannot be interpreted as independent observations supporting an artificially narrow confidence interval. We will separate exploratory cross-vendor consistency from the human detection and reliability results, report agreement at a justified unit of analysis, and remove inferences about invariance to drafting tools that were not manipulated in the study. Evidence: E-039 in docs/enterprise-diligence/EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md; Referee 2 comment 9.

**R2.10, review instruments.** We agree that the actual classification rule and five-condition instructions need to be inspectable. The repository contains the 24-record verification packet and public JRS Codebook. We will identify and include the exact material used in the study and distinguish historical study versions from the latest website version, subject to legitimate disclosure and permission constraints. Evidence: research/AnswerKey_Verification_Packet.md; https://www.jrsstandard.com/codebook.html .
