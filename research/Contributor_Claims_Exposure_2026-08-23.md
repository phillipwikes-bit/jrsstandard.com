# Are any research participants owed money, and can any of them sue if the work earns profit

**2026-08-23.** Built from the consent architecture as actually deployed, the recruitment
copy as actually published, and `research/CONSENT_AND_RELEASE_AUDIT_2026-08-13.md`.

**This is a structural risk map, not legal advice, and it is not a substitute for counsel.**
The budget line for a contributor release in `research/Institutional_Stress_Test_2026-08-23.md`
Section 2.3 exists for exactly this question.

---

## 1. The short answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Is anyone owed money today?** | **No.** No promise of payment, royalty, profit share, equity or future consideration was made to any participant, anywhere, at any time |
| **Could anyone sue if you earn profits?** | **Anyone can file. The question is what survives.** One category has a real claim, one has a narrow one, and the rest are weak |
| **Is the real risk damages?** | **No. The real risk is control**: a co-author blocking a paper, or a contributor withdrawing consent and forcing their name off published work |

## 2. Nobody is owed money, and the record proves it

### 2.1 No promise of payment exists anywhere

A repository-wide search of all 71 public pages for `in exchange`, `in return`, `share of`,
`royalt`, `profit`, `equity` and `stake` returns **zero hits in any recruitment, participation
or credit context.** Nothing offered anyone value in return for participating.

### 2.2 The opposite was published, repeatedly

| Surface | Language |
|---|---|
| `research.html:108` | "unpaid and in a personal capacity" |
| `research.html:160` | "Reviewers took part in a personal capacity, unpaid." |
| `reviewer/index.html:178` | "graded records for this work, **unpaid and in a personal capacity**" |
| `acquisition-9f3c2a7d4b.html:116` | "Reviewers take part in a personal capacity, unpaid." |
| Every manuscript | "No participant was compensated." |

**This is the single strongest fact in your position.** A claim for payment, whether framed as
unjust enrichment, quantum meruit or an implied contract, needs a reasonable expectation of
compensation. **A published, repeated, contemporaneous statement that the work is unpaid
defeats that expectation.**

### 2.3 No employment or contractor relationship exists

No participant was directed as to hours, supervised, given equipment, or integrated into a
business. Every participant chose their own timing, worked from their own environment, and
took part in a personal capacity. **There is no wage claim here and no misclassification
question.**

### 2.4 One weakness in the disclosure, and it is worth fixing

**The word "unpaid" appears on the pages that describe the studies, not on the pages where
people actually did the work.**

| Page | "unpaid" or "personal capacity" present |
|---|---|
| `research.html` | **Yes** |
| `reviewer/index.html` | **Yes** |
| `access.html` | **No** |
| `ai-records-pilot.html` | **No** |
| `ai-records-arm-b.html` | **No** |
| `bench-review.html` | **No** |
| `submit-validation.html` | **No** |

**Nobody was told they would be paid, so this is a gap in proof rather than a
misrepresentation.** But the pages a reviewer actually sat on while grading 24 records are the
pages where the statement carries the most weight. **One sentence on each of five pages closes
it**, and it costs nothing.

## 3. Where the real exposure is, ranked

### TIER 1. Co-authors. Real, and it is not about money

**Four manuscripts carry co-authors. Co-authorship in a jointly created work creates joint
rights in that work. None of them has assigned anything.**

| Person | Position | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Ubayet Hossain | Co-author, detection paper. Designed the validation methodology | **Sign-off deferred 2026-08-22.** No assignment |
| Tanvi Pokhriyal | **First author**, employment records paper. Designed the case protocol and conducted **every record review** | Declarations supplied 2026-08-22. **No assignment** |
| Kyle McMullan | Co-author, employment records paper | **Raised objections. No sign-off. No declarations.** No assignment |
| Stacyann Young | First author, public records companion paper | **No assignment** |

**What they can actually do.** Not sue you for a share of licensing revenue. **Object to the
use of a paper they co-own**, decline to be associated with a commercial product, or withhold
sign-off. `CONSENT_AND_RELEASE_AUDIT_2026-08-13.md` gap 2 states it exactly: **"co-authored
papers carry shared copyright and cannot be sold as solely owned works. A consent tick is not
an assignment."**

**Why this matters commercially rather than legally.** A licensee taking exclusive rights will
ask who owns the validation evidence. **Today, part of the answer is: jointly, with four people
who have signed nothing.**

**Tanvi is the one to think about carefully and it is not a legal point.** She designed the
protocol and personally conducted every record review in that study, as an unpaid volunteer,
while travelling. If that paper becomes a component of a commercial licence, **she has the
strongest equitable position of anyone in the programme.** She has already declared no
financial interest in JRS, which helps you considerably and which she volunteered without
being pressed. **Do not let the first time she hears about commercialisation be from a press
release.**

### TIER 2. Reviewers and raters. Narrow, and it turns on one sentence

The consent control on `contributor.html` reads, verbatim:

> How may your contribution be used? One answer covers all of it: whether you are named in the
> paper, whether your credited name and the review work you contributed may keep appearing in
> **publications and materials about this study**, and whether those permissions travel with
> the work if it passes to a **successor organization**. Nothing here touches anything you own
> outside this study, and **you can change or withdraw it at any time** by writing to
> info@jrsstandard.com.

**Three things follow, and they cut in different directions.**

**(a) The scope is publication, not product.** It authorises use in "publications and
materials about this study." **It does not mention licensing, commercial products, revenue, or
embedding in a vendor platform.** A licensing deal that reproduces reviewer contributions
inside a commercial product sits outside what that sentence describes.

**(b) The successor clause helps a sale and not a licence.** Permissions travel "if it passes
to a successor organization." **That covers selling the programme. It does not obviously cover
you personally earning licensing revenue while the contributors remain unpaid volunteers.**

**(c) The permission is revocable at will, and this is the operative constraint.** "You can
change or withdraw it at any time." **A revocable licence cannot support an exclusive
perpetual grant to a licensee.** Any exclusivity clause you sign that recites the validation
evidence as licensed property is, as to that evidence, built on permissions any contributor
can end with one email.

**Realistic claim value: low. Realistic disruption: real.** The likely event is not a lawsuit.
It is a contributor reading about a commercial licence, feeling their volunteer work was
monetised without notice, and withdrawing. **Under your own published policy you would then
have to remove them from the Registry and delete or anonymise their record on request**, and
your published panel figures move.

### TIER 3. Unjust enrichment and quantum meruit. Weak

Requires an expectation of payment. Defeated by Section 2. **Weak on these facts, and the
weakness is documented and contemporaneous rather than reconstructed.**

### TIER 4. Attribution and reliance claims. Weak, and deliberately so

Four published disclaimers do real work here, per the consent audit:

- **"Not certification, not accreditation, not a credential"** on the certificate, the training
  and `index.html`. **Blocks a reliance claim** by anyone who received an honor or certificate.
- **"Not legal advice, HR guidance, or compliance determination."**
- **"It does not guarantee that any decision is correct, lawful, or will survive challenge."**
- **Publicity is never a condition of anything**, provable from separate stored flags.

**Recognition was given, not traded.** Nobody was told a certificate was consideration for
their work, and the certificate says on its face that it is not a credential.

## 4. The structural weakness underneath all of it

`CONSENT_AND_RELEASE_AUDIT_2026-08-13.md` Section 1: **"There is no signature anywhere. Nobody
signs anything."** Every permission is a tick plus a stored boolean, against wording on a live
page you can edit, **with no stored copy of the terms as they read on the day each person
ticked.**

**If the wording changes, you cannot prove what any given person saw.** That is gap 1 and it is
the cheapest fix in the whole programme: **store a terms version string on every consent row.**

**And your best-drafted instrument has been sent to almost nobody.** Gap 4: the contributor
confirmation was issued to 20 and confirmed by 0 at the time of that audit. **The permissions a
buyer most wants are the ones not yet collected.**

## 5. What to do, in order, before any revenue

| # | Action | Cost | What it closes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Store a terms version identifier on every consent row** | An hour | Gap 1. Converts "he ticked something" into "he ticked v1.3 on this date" |
| 2 | **Snapshot `privacy.html` and the consent wording to a dated immutable copy today** | 30 minutes | Proves today's terms in a year. Do this before you change anything |
| 3 | **Send the contributor confirmations** | A day | Gap 4. The instrument exists and is unused |
| 4 | **Draft a short commercial-use notice and licence, and send it to the 36 completers** | $1,000 to $2,500 with counsel | Tier 2. Converts a revocable publication consent into a durable commercial licence, **with an honest offer to opt out** |
| 5 | **Get written co-author consent to commercial use, separate from authorship sign-off** | Attorney time | Tier 1. Two different permissions. **Do not bundle them into one ask** |
| 6 | **Add "unpaid, personal capacity" to the five task pages that lack it** | 20 minutes | Section 2.4 |
| 7 | **Tell Tanvi, Hossain, Kyle and Stacyann directly, before any announcement** | An email each | Not a legal control. The one that actually prevents the problem |

**Item 4 deserves a specific caution.** Going back to 36 volunteers to ask for broader rights
**after** commercialisation begins reads very differently from asking before. **Ask now, while
revenue is $0 and the request is transparently about doing it properly.** Some will decline.
Their contributions are already counted in the aggregate under the terms they accepted, and a
clean "no" from a few is worth far more than an assumed yes from all.

---

## 6. Bottom line

**Nobody is owed money and nobody has a strong damages claim.** The record on non-payment is
unusually clean: published, repeated, contemporaneous, and never contradicted.

**The exposure is not a bill. It is a veto.** Four co-authors hold joint rights in the papers
that constitute your validation evidence, and 36 contributors hold revocable publication
permissions that were never framed as commercial. **Neither group can take your money. Either
group can complicate the asset you are trying to license**, and a licensee's counsel will find
both in diligence.

**Every one of these is cheap to fix at $0 revenue and expensive to fix at the first dollar.**
