# How to convert more people into named, consented, record-checking users (2026-08-04)

Companion to `Sale_Likelihood_Assessment_2026-08-04.md`. Written against live production figures pulled this morning: 0 registrations since the gate, 0 organization pilots, 0 of 20 contributor links opened, 7 training enrollments with 7 completions.

---

## The core problem, in one line

Nothing on the site asks anyone for anything. It waits.

Every route now built (registration, contributor links, org pilot, initiatives) is a page that a person has to find and choose to complete. In the last two days nobody was sent to any of them. The pre-gate numbers came from links you had personally pushed into LinkedIn posts, emails, and your signature over the preceding weeks. When the pushing stopped, the numbers stopped. The form is a plausible secondary factor and, until today, an unmeasurable one.

Two responses follow, in this order: **use the channels you already have**, then **shorten the path** for the traffic they produce.

---

## Part 1: the four asks you already have permission to make

These are people who already know you, already said yes to something, and are not being asked.

### 1. The twenty contributor links (highest return, expires soonest)

Twenty people completed a demanding, unpaid, hour-long review for you. Their goodwill is at its maximum right now and decays every week. The links are generated, the messages are drafted, and none have been sent.

Each link captures: preferred name and title, current contact, named-credit election, continuing-use permission, transfer permission, both initiative sign-ups, and offers the guide, the training, and a records check.

Expected return if you send all twenty: based on how this group has responded to direct personal asks before, 12 to 16 will complete it. That is 12 to 16 named, consented, transfer-permissioned professional records in about a week, which is roughly double the entire transferable list you have accumulated in a month, and it closes most of Gate 3.

**Do this first. It is the only item on this page with an expiry date attached.**

### 2. The 39 anonymous supporters, and the 56 anonymous downloaders

You cannot contact them. That is exactly why the gate exists. There is no recovery action here beyond not repeating it.

### 3. The 7 trained and certified people

All 7 completed the training. All 7 consented to transfer. Not one has been asked to run a single record through the standard.

These are the most qualified people in the world to run the org pilot, other than you. They have been trained in the method, they finished, and they work at five different organizations. Ask each of them to run five records from their own work. If two say yes, Gate 2 goes from zero organizations to two.

### 4. Your own LinkedIn audience

The pre-gate traffic came from there. It stopped when the posts stopped. Three volumes of campaign posts are already written and unused in `linkedin_viral_campaign_master.md`, `linkedin_campaign_vol2.md`, and `linkedin_campaign_vol3.md`.

## Part 2: change what you are asking for

The single highest-value change costs nothing to make.

**Current ask, everywhere: "support this initiative" or "download the guide."**
**Better ask: "run five of your own records through it, free, and I will send you the read."**

Why this is not a small difference:

- A support click has near zero diligence value. A buyer cannot verify it, contact the person, or count it as usage.
- An organization running its own records is the one thing your buyer pool asks about and the one thing you have zero of.
- The pilot is genuinely more appealing to the senior governance people you are emailing. They are being asked to endorse a stranger's framework, which costs them reputation. Being offered a free diagnostic on their own files costs them nothing and gives them something.
- Record text is never stored, which removes the objection that would otherwise kill it. Say so in the first sentence, not the fourth.

Apply this to the outreach email, to LinkedIn, and to your signature. The pilot page already accepts the person's details through the query string, so someone arriving from a support link does not retype anything.

## Part 3: shorten the form

The current registration asks for name, organization, title, work email, and three consent answers before releasing anything. That is a lot for a free PDF, and it is very likely the reason a share of arrivals leave.

Now measurable: as of today the form records an anonymous view when it opens. The dashboard shows form opens, completed registrations, and the conversion rate between them. Read it in a few days once traffic resumes, then act on the number rather than on a guess.

Decision rule to apply once there is data:

- **Conversion above 40 percent:** the form is fine. The problem is traffic. Push distribution.
- **Conversion 15 to 40 percent:** normal for a gated download. Trim to name, email, organization, and one consent question, and move the transfer and public listing questions to the thank-you screen where they no longer block the file.
- **Conversion below 15 percent:** the form is the problem. Reduce to name and email only for the guide, keep the full form for the pilot and the training where the person is more invested.

Regardless of the number, one change is worth making now: the field guide is the lowest-commitment thing you offer and it currently carries the same form as everything else. Consider making organization and title optional on the guide path only. A named person with a personal email is still infinitely more conveyable than an anonymous download.

## Part 4: give people a reason to finish

What each route offers today, and what would make it worth completing:

| Route | Current incentive | Stronger incentive |
|---|---|---|
| Field guide | A PDF | The PDF plus a free read on one of their own records |
| Initiative | Being counted | Named in the public registry, with the count and the countries stated |
| Org pilot | A free assessment | A one-page written summary they can show their own leadership |
| Training | Certificate | Certificate plus a LinkedIn recommendation from you on completion, which you already do for reviewers |
| Contributor link | Results summary | Already correct, the results summary is the strongest incentive on the site |

The pattern: every route should end with something the person can use in their own professional life. That is what the reviewers responded to, and it is why the training completes at 100 percent while a one-click endorsement stalls.

## Part 5: the seven-day sequence

| Day | Action | Expected result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | File both trademark applications | Gate 1 closed, does not depend on anyone replying |
| 1 | Send all 20 contributor links | 12 to 16 named consented records within the week, Gate 3 mostly closed |
| 2 | Message the 7 trained people, ask each to run 5 records | 1 to 3 organizations running the standard, Gate 2 opened |
| 3 | Rewrite the outreach email from endorsement to pilot, resend to the existing list | Higher reply rate, and every reply is a potential Gate 2 event |
| 4 | Post one campaign piece, link to the pilot rather than the endorsement | Restarts the traffic that stopped |
| 5 | Read the conversion rate on the dashboard | Tells you whether to trim the form |
| 6 to 7 | Trim the form if the number says to, then post again | Compounding |

Nothing in this sequence requires new material. The links exist, the messages exist, the posts exist, the pilot works, and the trademark filing is a form and a fee.

---

## What I would not do

**Do not remove the gate.** Going back to anonymous downloads restores a number that means nothing in diligence and destroys the only thing on the dashboard a buyer would pay attention to. The slowdown is in a metric that was never worth anything.

**Do not start charging.** At this volume it produces trivial revenue, cuts distribution sharply, and creates support and tax obligations four weeks before an intended sale.

**Do not add more articles in place of these actions.** Publication sets the price you can ask. It does not change whether anyone asks.
