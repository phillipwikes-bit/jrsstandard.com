# Target journal for the detection paper: decision and reasoning (2026-08-05)

**Pick: AI and Ethics (Springer Nature), with an SSRN or OSF preprint posted first.**

---

## Why this one

**1. It is hybrid, so publication does not require money.** This is the deciding factor and it is verified. On acceptance, authors of AI and Ethics choose between the subscription route and open access. The subscription route carries no article processing charge. Every other credible venue on the shortlist is Gold open access, which means a mandatory APC on acceptance, in the range of roughly 1,700 to 2,500 US dollars for comparable titles. Your own record already verified that Journal of Responsible Technology is Gold OA with an APC on acceptance. Choosing a Gold venue converts an acceptance into an invoice.

**2. The scope is an exact match, not an approximate one.** The journal's stated scope covers accountability in automated decision-making systems and transparency in algorithmic processes. That is the paper's subject, in the journal's own words.

**3. It is already reserved for this paper in your plan.** Your record from July assigned Journal of Responsible Technology to the Rungs 1-2 paper and held AI and Ethics for the detection paper specifically to avoid venue collision. Submitting both papers to the same journal weakens both. That decision was correct and still holds.

**4. It reviews empirical work of this size.** Manuscripts are peer reviewed by at least two specialists. The journal publishes applied and empirical governance work rather than requiring large-scale trials, which is what a 16-reviewer panel study needs.

**5. Your own prior estimate was 45 to 55 percent with a null secondary.** Nothing in the refreshed data changes that band. The detection result got slightly stronger, the secondary comparison got weaker, and those roughly cancel for acceptance purposes.

## Post the preprint first, this week

SSRN or OSF, before submission. Reasons:

- It produces a citable DOI immediately, with no rejection risk. Given your timeline that matters more than it normally would.
- It timestamps the work as yours, which is worth something in an IP sale.
- Springer Nature permits preprint posting prior to submission.

The one check to run yourself before posting: confirm on the journal's submission guidelines page that preprint posting is acceptable for this title, since the detection figure is already published on your own site and a preprint makes that disclosure formal rather than incidental.

## What raises the odds before you submit

Three things, in order of effect, all within your control this week:

**1. Get Ubayet's sign-off in writing.** He is the methodology co-author and the paper reports his analysis plan, coefficients, and floors. A reviewer who sees a model-validation professional named on a paper with pre-registered thresholds reads the whole manuscript more favourably. His sign-off is also required by your own earned-authorship policy before his name goes on it.

**2. Lead the cover letter with the null.** Editors at ethics and governance journals treat a visible, correctly-handled null as a credibility signal. Say in the first paragraph that the primary detection result clears its pre-registered threshold and that the secondary randomized comparison is reported as an underpowered null with a power specification attached. Reviewers who find the null themselves react worse than reviewers who were told.

**3. State the limitations before the reviewers do.** The constructed corpus, the self-selected panel, the interim reliability on 10 records, and the unmet secondary lower-bound criterion are all already written into the manuscript. That is the correct choice and it should stay.
