Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, "Detecting Decision Reconstruction Risk in AI-Assisted Documentation: A Record-Level Review Standard and Its Pre-Registered Validation," for publication in AI and Ethics.

Most AI governance work addresses model risk: bias, robustness, and system behavior. Our paper addresses a complementary and under-examined problem that arises after a model has assisted with drafting and lives in the record it helped produce. AI-assisted drafting can make a record read as complete and well supported while the evidence that would justify its conclusions never appears on the page. We name this property Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR): the condition in which a record cannot, on its own terms, let an independent reviewer reconstruct the basis for a consequential decision. We argue DRR matters to two parties at once, the person the record describes and the institution accountable for it, and we introduce the Justification Review Standard (JRS) as a workflow-independent, record-level control for detecting it.

The paper contributes a governance construct, a review method, and a pre-registered validation program. It already reports two complete supporting results: a cross-vendor reproducibility test in which three AI systems from three different providers applied the review to the same records with 84 percent mean pairwise agreement, and an inter-rater reliability test in which independent expert reviewers reached substantial chance-corrected agreement (Gwet's AC1 of 0.74). It then sets out a pre-registered detection study against an independently verified answer key and a randomized comparison that isolates the value of the standard against unaided expert judgment. Consistent with the study's analysis plan, we report the design and the supporting results in full and we do not state the primary detection and randomized-comparison results until the pre-registered sample and thresholds are met.

We believe the paper fits the journal's focus on the ethics and governance of AI in practice. It is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved it. Author identities are provided; a competing-interest statement is included, noting that the first author created the standard and that the study was pre-registered to constrain that interest. We are happy to submit either as Original Research or as a Perspective, depending on the editors' preference for the reflective opening.

Thank you for considering our submission.
