# Submission correspondence: Journal of Responsible Technology

*How this journal actually takes submissions, and the two pieces of correspondence you need. Read the mechanics note first: there is no address you email a finished manuscript to. Doing so is how predatory journals operate, and a JoRT-shaped legitimate journal does not work that way. Sending it correctly is part of protecting the paper's standing in due diligence.*

## Submission mechanics (verified 2026-07-27)

- **Publisher:** Elsevier. **Journal:** Journal of Responsible Technology (ISSN 2666-6596). **Model:** Gold open access (an Article Processing Charge applies on acceptance). **Review:** double-blind.
- **You submit through the Editorial Manager portal, not by email.** From the journal's ScienceDirect page ("Submit your article") you reach Editorial Manager, create an account, and upload: (1) the anonymized manuscript, (2) the separate title page, (3) the cover letter. The cover letter below is pasted or uploaded there; it is the "letter to the editor" in this workflow.
- **Editor-in-Chief:** Prof. Marina Jirotka, University of Oxford, Department of Computer Science.
- **Optional presubmission inquiry by email:** a short note to the Editor-in-Chief asking whether the paper is in scope is legitimate academic practice and is the one genuine "email to the publisher" step. Oxford Computer Science addresses follow the format firstname.lastname@cs.ox.ac.uk, which gives **marina.jirotka@cs.ox.ac.uk**. Confirm it on her department page (https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/marina.jirotka/) before sending; if you would rather not cold-email the Editor, skip the inquiry and go straight to Editorial Manager, where the cover letter reaches her anyway.

Guide for authors: https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/journal-of-responsible-technology/publish/guide-for-authors

## A. Presubmission inquiry (email, optional)

**To:** marina.jirotka@cs.ox.ac.uk *(confirm on the department page above)*
**Subject:** Presubmission inquiry: reliability and reproducibility of a record-level review standard

Dear Professor Jirotka,

I am writing to ask whether a manuscript we are preparing would be within the scope of the Journal of Responsible Technology before we submit it through Editorial Manager.

The paper reports the first two stages of a pre-registered evidence program for a record-level documentation review method, the Justification Review Standard. It addresses a problem in responsible technology practice: AI-assisted drafting increasingly produces administrative records that read fluently while omitting the reasoning and evidence a later reviewer would need to reconstruct the basis for a decision. We name that failure Decision Reconstruction Risk. The manuscript presents cross-vendor reproducibility of the review method across three independent AI models (84 percent agreement on 15 records) and inter-rater reliability among independent human panels (Gwet's AC1 of 0.74 for experts and 0.63 for trained reviewers, both meeting a pre-registered floor). We are explicit that these are reliability and reproducibility findings, not claims of accuracy or effectiveness, which later stages test.

If this fits the journal's aims, we will submit the full manuscript through Editorial Manager. I would be grateful for your view.

With thanks,

Phillip Wikes

## B. Cover letter (upload with the Editorial Manager submission)

Dear Professor Jirotka and Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Reliability and Reproducibility of a Record-Level Review Standard: Rungs 1 and 2 of a Staged Evidence Program," for publication in the Journal of Responsible Technology.

AI-assisted drafting is changing the records that organizations rely on to justify consequential decisions. A record can now be fluent and still fail to show why a decision was made, because the reasoning, chronology, or evidence behind the conclusion is absent. We call that failure Decision Reconstruction Risk, and the manuscript reports the first empirical evidence for a method built to detect it, the Justification Review Standard.

The paper contributes two pre-registered results. First, three AI models from different vendors, applied to the same constructed records, returned the same review determination on 84 percent of 15 records, a cross-vendor reproducibility signal. Second, independent panels of expert and trained human reviewers reached substantial chance-corrected agreement, Gwet's AC1 of 0.74 and 0.63 respectively, with the trained-reviewer result meeting a floor fixed before the data were seen. We are deliberate about what the evidence does not show: it establishes consistent application, not accuracy, value, or effectiveness, and we frame the results as one stage of a staged program rather than as validation of the method.

We believe the work fits the journal's focus on the responsible development and governance of technology, in particular the accountability of AI-assisted documentation. The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved it. A declaration of competing interest is included on the title page: the first author created the standard and has a commercial interest in its adoption, and the study was pre-registered to constrain that interest. We have prepared the manuscript for double-blind review, with author-identifying information confined to the separate title page.

Thank you for considering our submission.

Sincerely,

Phillip Wikes, on behalf of both authors
Corresponding author
info@jrsstandard.com
