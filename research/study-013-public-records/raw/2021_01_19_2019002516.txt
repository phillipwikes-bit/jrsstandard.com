                                           DECISION

Complainant filed an appeal with the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC or
Commission), pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.403(a), from the Agency’s January 16, 2019, final

For Claim A, the Agency found that Complainant did not establish a prima facie case of

                                  ANALYSIS AND FINDINGS

Standard of Review

As this is an appeal from a decision issued without a hearing, pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b),
the Agency's decision is subject to de novo review by the Commission. 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(a).
See Equal Employment Opportunity Management Directive for 29 C.F.R. Part 1614 (EEO MD-
110), at Chap. 9, § VI.A. (Aug. 5, 2015) (explaining that the de novo standard of review “requires
that the Commission examine the record without regard to the factual and legal determinations of
the previous decision maker,” and that EEOC “review the documents, statements, and testimony
of record, including any timely and relevant submissions of the parties, and . . . issue its decision
based on the Commission’s own assessment of the record and its interpretation of the law”).

As a general rule, no new evidence will be considered on appeal unless there is an affirmative
showing that the evidence was not reasonably available prior to or during the investigation. See
EEO MD-110 at Chap. 9, § VI.A.3. On appeal, Complainant provides a copy of her ePAR and
additional documents related to her ePAR, which she received on December 26, 2018. We note
that the record shows Complainant’s repeated attempts to obtain her ePAR, which the Agency
finalized on December 7, 2018. We find that Complainant has shown that this new evidence was
not available during the investigation, which concluded on September 27, 2018. Accordingly, the
Commission will consider this new evidence on appeal.

Disparate Treatment

Generally, claims of disparate treatment are examined under the analysis first enunciated in
McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green, 411 U.S. 792 (1973). Hochstadt v. Worcester Found. for
Experimental Biology, Inc., 425 F. Supp. 318, 324 (D. Mass.), aff’d, 545 F.2d 222 (1st Cir. 1976).
For Complainant to prevail, she must first establish a prima facie case of discrimination by
presenting facts that, if unexplained, reasonably give rise to an inference of discrimination, i.e.,
that a prohibited consideration was a factor in the adverse employment action. Furnco Constr.
Corp. v. Waters, 438 U.S. 567 (1978); McDonnell Douglas, 411 U.S. at 802 n.13. Once
Complainant has established a prima facie case, the burden then shifts to the Agency to articulate
a legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason for its actions. Texas Dept. of Community Affairs v.
Burdine, 450 U.S. 248, 253 (1981). If the Agency is successful, the burden reverts back to
Complainant to demonstrate by a preponderance of the evidence that the Agency’s reason(s) for
its action was a pretext for discrimination. At all times, Complainant retains the burden of
persuasion, and it is her obligation to show by a preponderance of the evidence that the Agency
acted on the basis of a prohibited reason. St. Mary’s Honor Ctr. v. Hicks, 509 U.S. 502 (1993);
U.S. Postal Service v. Aikens, 460 U.S. 711, 715-716 (1983).
                                                6                                     2019002516

In its final decision, the Agency found that Complainant failed to establish a prima facie case of
reprisal discrimination for claim A. The Agency noted that while Complainant engaged in
protected activity when she filed the instant EEO complaint, since the incident “occurred as of the
date of filing the complaint, there is no evidence that S1 or S2 were aware of the filing prior to
taking any alleged adverse action.” However, we find that this is not correct. S1 stated that he
learned of Complainant’s EEO activity in early 2018, and S2 stated that he learned in January
2018. ROI at 207,226. S1 signed Complainant’s ePAR on November 26, 2018. In addition, S2
stated that when Complainant’s “complaint was sent to the command,” he was advised “not to take
further action,” and that he completed his section of the ePAR after he received legal advice. ROI
at 227. As such, we find that S1 and S2 were aware of Complainant’s EEO activity when the
Agency delayed, and eventually issued, Complainant’s ePAR.

However, we find that there is insufficient evidence in the record regarding the Agency’s delay in
issuing Complainant’s ePAR. In reviewing the record, we find that the available evidence does not
reveal who was responsible for the delay in issuing Complainant’s ePAR, or the reason(s) for the
delay.

A Contracting Officer’s Representative (COR) provided his affidavit on August 8, 2018, and he
stated that Complainant’s ePAR was not yet complete because they were pending information to
support the “negative comments” in the ePAR. ROI at 257-8. However, we note that COR did not
state who was expected to provide this additional information; when this information was
requested; or any reason for a delay in providing this information. The additional information
attached to the ePAR was the Record of Counseling, issued to Complainant on November 27,
2017, and statements from staff members to support the counseling. We note that there is no
evidence showing the reason for the delay in providing this documentation to COR by August
2018, when it was available in November 2017.

When S1 provided his affidavit on August 8, 2018, he stated that he had “no input” on
Complainant’s ePAR. ROI at 207,218. However, S1 signed the ePAR on November 26, 2018, and
the ePAR included a statement that Complainant’s “unprofessional behavior” led to “a written
counseling by me [S1].” There is nothing in the record to explain why S1 did not act on
Complainant’s ePAR for at least eight months.

In addition, S2 stated in his affidavit that once Complainant’s “complaint was sent to the command
[he] was advised by legal counsel not to take further action until directed to do so. Once the
Credentialing Department sent [him] the prepared ePAR and [he] received legal advice, [he]
completed [his] section as necessary.” ROI at 227. In her affidavit, Complainant stated that MSP
stated that she was waiting for S2 to finish Complainant’s ePAR, and that she had requested that
S2 correct his “accusations and false statements,” or provide evidence of his negative reviews.
ROI at 165-6. Complainant added on appeal that she had this conversation with MSP in February
2018. We find that S2’s role in Complainant’s ePAR is unclear since he did not sign Complainant’s
ePAR, and that additional information is needed to determine how S2’s actions could have
contributed to the delay.
                                                 7                                      2019002516

We find that a supplemental investigation is needed to obtain additional information regarding the
delay in issuing Complainant’s ePAR. Specifically, we note that MSP’s statement is an important
part of the investigation due to her direct involvement in the early stages of preparing
Complainant’s ePAR. We also find that additional information is needed from COR, S1, and S2.
Further, we note that other management officials signed Complainant’s ePAR, and based on the
limited information in the record, it is unclear how they may have delayed the ePAR, and if other
unnamed officials were also involved in the processing of Complainant’s ePAR. We shall not rule
on the other claims at this time, because we find that all claims are best considered on their merits
at one time.

                                          CONCLUSION

We VACATE the Agency’s final decision finding no discrimination, and REMAND the complaint
to the Agency for further processing, in accordance with our ORDER herein.

                                              ORDER

Within 90 days from the date this decision is issued, the Agency shall complete a supplemental
investigation on Complainant's claim regarding the delay in the issuance of her ePAR and issue a
new final decision on all the claims in Complainant’s complaint.

   1. The EEO Investigator shall fully investigate Complainant's claim regarding the delay in
      the issuance of her ePAR. The Agency shall ensure that the EEO Investigator obtains all
      pertinent evidence needed to address Complainant's claim including, but not limited to,
      sworn (or affirmed) affidavits from MSP, COR, S1, S2, and other relevant officials. The
      EEO Investigator will ask the witnesses to provide detailed information regarding their
      knowledge of, and involvement in, the issuance of Complainant’s ePAR, including
      timeframes and reasons for their actions. The EEO Investigator will also obtain any
      documentary evidence regarding the issuance of Complainant’s ePAR. Complainant shall
      have the opportunity to provide a rebuttal affidavit.

   2. Once the supplemental investigation is completed, the Agency shall issue a new final
      decision, with appeal rights to the Commission's Office of Federal Operations, that will
      address all the accepted claims in Complainant’s complaint.

The Agency is further directed to submit a report of compliance in digital format as provided in
the statement entitled “Implementation of the Commission's Decision.” The report shall be
submitted via the Federal Sector EEO Portal (FedSEP). This report must include a copy of the
supplemental investigation and new final agency decision.
