                                           DECISION

On June 11, 2018, Complainant filed an appeal with the Equal Employment Opportunity
Commission (EEOC or Commission), pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.403(a), from the Agency’s
June 7, 2018, final decision concerning her equal employment opportunity (EEO) complaint
alleging employment discrimination in violation of Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (Title
VII), as amended, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e et seq., and the Age Discrimination in Employment Act of
1967 (ADEA), as amended, 29 U.S.C. § 621 et seq. For the following reasons, the Commission
VACATES the Agency’s final decision and REMANDS the matter for a supplemental
investigation.

                                      ISSUE PRESENTED

The issue presented is whether the record is adequately developed to allow the Commission to
determine if the Agency subjected Complainant to a hostile work environment on the bases of
race, sex, age, and reprisal for protected EEO activity.

                                        BACKGROUND

At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Mail Handler at the
Agency’s Kansas City Processing and Distribution Center (P&DC) in Kansas City, Missouri.

Final Agency Decision

The Agency issued a “Final Agency Decision -- Consolidated Case” on June 7, 2018. The Agency
found that it properly dismissed claim (4), and it “endorsed and incorporated by reference” its
January 12, 2016, decision.

Further, the Agency concluded that Complainant established prima facie cases of reprisal with
respect to claims (1), (2), and (3). The Agency also concluded, however, that Complainant did not
establish her hostile-work-environment claim. In that regard, the Agency found that Complainant
did not establish that the incidents at issue were based on her protected status. The Agency
determined that there was no evidence that management officials singled out Complainant or
treated her unfairly due to her protected classes. Instead, according to the Agency, “the record is
clear that various management officials listened to her complaint, told her to write down her
contentions, spoke with [CW1], followed up with his manager to ensure that he was informed that
his actions were not appropriate, and warned him to stay away from her.” With respect to claim
(1), the Agency found that the employees who Complainant alleged received more favorable
treatment were not similarly situated to her or were not treated better under similar circumstances.
With respect to claims (2) and (3), the Agency found no basis from which to infer that
discriminatory or retaliatory animus motivated management’s actions.

The Agency further found that the incidents at issue were not sufficiently severe or pervasive to
create an abusive or hostile environment. It stated that “Complainant largely complains of isolated
incidents, such as one incident when [CW1] stood next to her, one time when he came into her
work area, and one day when she was allegedly sent home early and charged with sick leave.” In
addition, the Agency found that there was no basis for imputing liability to the Agency.

Finally, the Agency found that Complainant did not establish her claim of sexual harassment with
respect to claim (1). The Agency concluded that Complainant did not show that CW1’s actions
on February 20, 2015, were of a sexual nature or were based on her sex. The Agency further
concluded that Complainant did not demonstrate that CW1’s actions on that date were sufficiently
severe or pervasive to constitute harassment. Noting that the parties settled Complainant’s 2012
EEO complaint, the Agency stated that there was no evidence in the current record that the alleged
prior harassment actually occurred or that CW1’s conduct on February 20, 2015, “was similar to
a prior act of a sexual nature, such that an inference of an unwelcome sexual advance is warranted.”
The Agency ruled that there was no basis for imputing liability to the Agency and that Complainant
did not prove that the Agency had discriminated against her as alleged.

                                    STANDARD OF REVIEW

As this is an appeal from a decision issued without a hearing, pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b),
the Agency’s decision is subject to de novo review by the Commission. 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(a).
See Equal Employment Opportunity Management Directive for 29 C.F.R. Part 1614, at Chapter 9,
§ VI.A. (Aug. 5, 2015) (explaining that the de novo standard of review “requires that the
Commission examine the record without regard to the factual and legal determinations of the
previous decision maker,” and that EEOC “review the documents, statements, and testimony of
record, including any timely and relevant submissions of the parties, and . . . issue its decision
based on the Commission’s own assessment of the record and its interpretation of the law”).

                                  ANALYSIS AND FINDINGS

To establish a claim of harassment, Complainant must show that: (1) she is a member of a
statutorily protected class; (2) she was subjected to unwelcome verbal or physical conduct
involving the protected class; (3) the harassment complained of was based on the protected class;
(4) the harassment had the purpose or effect of unreasonably interfering with the work environment
and/or creating an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment; and (5) there is a basis for
imputing liability to the employer. Humphrey v. U.S. Postal Serv., EEOC Appeal No. 01965238
(Oct. 16, 1998); 29 C.F.R. § 1604.11. The harasser’s conduct should be evaluated from the
objective viewpoint of a reasonable person in the victim’s circumstances. Enforcement Guidance
on Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., EEOC Notice No. 915.002 (Mar. 8, 1994) (Enforcement
Guidance on Harris). The evaluation “requires careful consideration of the social context in which
particular behavior occurs and is experienced by its target.” Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Servs.,
Inc., 523 U.S. 75, 81 (1998).

Complainant has the ultimate burden of persuading the fact finder by a preponderance of the
evidence that discriminatory animus created a hostile work environment. Frank R. v. Dep’t of
Justice, EEOC Appeal No. 012015`118 (Oct. 18, 2019) (citing Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing
Prods., Inc., 530 U.S. 133, 141 (2000); McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green, 411 U.S. 792, 802-
803 (1973)). During the investigative stage of the federal administrative process, the agency has
an obligation to develop an adequate investigative record. 29 C.F.R. § 1614.108. “An appropriate
factual record is one that allows a reasonable fact finder to draw conclusions as to whether
discrimination occurred.” Id. § 1614.108(b). The investigator must conduct a thorough
investigation, “identifying and obtaining all relevant evidence from all sources regardless of how
it may affect the outcome.” EEO MD-110, Chap. 6 § V.D.

In this case, we find that the record is not adequately developed for a fact finder to determine
whether the Agency subjected Complainant to a discriminatorily hostile work environment. The
record does not contain Complainant’s February 24, 2015, written statement or affidavits from a
number of relevant witnesses. Complainant submitted a handwritten note from CW2, but the
record does not contain an affidavit from CW2 describing what she witnessed on February 20,
2015. The record also does not contain affidavits from CW1, AMO1, AMO2, LRM, AS, or S1.
                                                 8                                     0120182189

Further, there was no investigation into Complainant’s allegations, raised in her December 8, 2015,
complaint (number 1E-641-0033-15), of ongoing harassment. The Agency dismissed the
complaint claiming it stated the same claim as the claim then pending before the EEOC AJ, and
the AJ granted the Agency’s motion to consolidate the complaints. Complainant, however,
ultimately withdrew her request for a hearing, and there was no investigation into or discovery of
evidence relevant to the December 2015 allegations.

The final decision before us on appeal incorporates the Agency’s January 12, 2016, decision. To
the extent that the instant decision continues to hold that the two complaints state the same claim,
the decision is mistaken. Complaint number 1E-641-0033-15 raises allegations of harassment
occurring after the matters covered by the investigation of the allegations raised in complaint
number 1E-641-0015-15. To the extent that the final decision accepts the consolidation of the two
complaints, the Agency issued the decision without conducting an appropriate investigation. The
record before us contains insufficient evidence regarding Complainant’s claims that CW1
continued to harass her, that two investigators told her in August 2015 that she was harassing her
co-worker, and that two Agency officials discriminatorily offered her a transfer that would result
in a loss of seniority. Those allegations, considered in light of Complainant’s overall harassment
claim, require investigation.

We find that the record before us contains insufficient information for us to issue a determination
on the merits of Complainant’s complaints and that further investigation is necessary.
Accordingly, we will remand this matter to the Agency for a supplemental investigation.

                                         CONCLUSION

Based on a thorough review of the record and the contentions on appeal, including those not
specifically addressed herein, we VACATE the Agency’s final decision and REMAND the matter
for a supplemental investigation, consistent with this decision and the Order below.
