                    DECISION ON REQUEST FOR RECONSIDERATION

Complainant timely requested that the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC or
Commission) reconsider its decision in EEOC Appeal Nos. 0120182412 and 2019003093
(December 11, 2019). 2 EEOC regulations provide that the Commission may, in its discretion,
grant a request to reconsider any previous Commission decision issued pursuant to 29 C.F.R. §
1614.405(a), where the requesting party demonstrates that: (1) the appellate decision involved a
clearly erroneous interpretation of material fact or law; or (2) the appellate decision will have a
substantial impact on the policies, practices, or operations of the agency. See 29 C.F.R. §
1614.405(c).

During the relevant time, Complainant worked as a Supervisory Examiner – Field Examiner
Manager, for Fair Lending and Enforcement, in the Agency’s Southeast Region, located in Dallas,
Texas. On May 27, 2014, believing that he was subjected to discrimination and harassment,
Complainant filed a formal complaint based on race (African-American), national origin
(Nigerian-Ibo), age (55), sex (male), and reprisal.

The complaint, amended on November 25, 2014, was comprised of sixteen claims regarding
numerous issues, including: promotion without an increase in pay; non-selection for promotions;
ratings; removal from assignment; denial of leave; negative comments in his performance
appraisal. Following an investigation, Complainant requested a final decision from the Agency.

On April 20, 2015, the Agency issued a decision finding no discrimination. Complainant appealed
the decision to the Commission. On November 16, 2017, the Commission issued a decision
vacating the Agency’s decision and remanding the matter to the Agency for a supplemental
investigation and new decision. See EEOC Appeal No. 0120160005 (Nov. 16, 2017).
Specifically, the Commission found that the record was not adequately developed with respect to
claims (2), (6), and (10).

In compliance with our order, the Agency issued a new decision on June 13, 2018. The Agency
concluded that Complainant was neither sexually harassed nor was his non-selection
discriminatory. Regarding claim (2), the Agency reasoned that Complainant was not eligible for
a salary increase until the Agency adopted a supervisory premium. Complainant was not chosen
for the position, claim (6), due to the Selectee’s stronger management experience. Complainant
failed to establish he was “significantly superior” and the Agency’s reasons were pretext for
discriminatory animus. As for claim (7), while the conduct was unwelcome, Complainant did not
prove that it was due to one of his protected bases. Lastly, in claim (10), Complainant himself
admitted that his qualifications were comparable to the Selectee’s, but not significantly superior.
Complainant appealed the decision to the Commission.

In our December 11, 2019 decision, the Commission affirmed the Agency’s finding of no
discrimination. See EEOC Appeal Nos. 2019003093 and 0120182412. Complainant did not meet
his burden in showing pretext, with respect to the disparate treatment claims (Claims (6) and (10)).
See id. The incident in claim (7) was not connected to any of Complainant’s protected bases. See
id. The Commission concluded that the alleged harassment was related to Complainant’s work and
the management of his performance, rather than his race, national origin, age, sex or prior EEO
activity. See id. Additionally, we addressed the issue of official time, initially raised in
Complainant v. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, EEOC Appeal No. 2018000042 (Feb. 9,
2019). Although Complainant requested 40 hours of official time to work on his appeal, the
Commission found that Agency’s reduction, to four hours due to the office workload and goals,
was reasonable. See id. Complainant filed the instant request.

Complainant argues, in his request, that the “Commission decision was overly friendly to the
Agency” and the Commission “made a material error” when it failed to hold the Agency
accountable for creating a hostile work environment.” Complainant contends that the Agency
failed to provide an “impartial and appropriate record”, and the Commission should have
sanctioned the Agency for such failure. Regarding the promotions, Complainant asserts that the
Commission compared “apples to oranges” rather than “apples to apples”, that he should have
been compared to all Field Managers in the Southeast Region. As for the issue of official time,
Complainant argues that the Agency provided incorrect information during the supplemental
investigation as part of a “cover-up”.

In response, the Agency notes that Complainant’s request seeks to challenge not only the
December 11, 2019 appeal decision, but EEOC Appeal No. 2019001482 and “allegations from
earlier complaints” as well. The Agency argues that Complainant’s request seeks to reopen the
investigation of his complaints and provide him with the chance to re-litigate his claims.

A “request for reconsideration is not a second appeal to the Commission.” Equal Employment
Opportunity Management Directive for 29 C.F.R. Part 1614 (EEO MD-110) (rev. Aug. 5, 2015),
at 9-18; see, e.g., Lopez v. Dep't of Agriculture, EEOC Request No. 0520070736 (Aug. 20, 2007).
Here, we find that Complainant is simply attempting to raise arguments which were previously, or
could have been, raised on appeal. He has not shown that our previous decision involved a clearly
erroneous interpretation of material fact or law. Complainant’s mere disagreement with our
decision is insufficient grounds for granting a request.

After reviewing the previous decision and the entire record, the Commission finds that the request
fails to meet the criteria of 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c), and it is the decision of the Commission to
DENY the request. The decision in EEOC Appeal Nos. 0120182412 and 2019003093 remains
the Commission's decision. There is no further right of administrative appeal on the decision of
the Commission on this request.

                COMPLAINANT’S RIGHT TO FILE A CIVIL ACTION (P0610)

This decision of the Commission is final, and there is no further right of administrative appeal
from the Commission’s decision. You have the right to file a civil action in an appropriate United
States District Court within ninety (90) calendar days from the date that you receive this decision.
If you file a civil action, you must name as the defendant in the complaint the person who is the
official Agency head or department head, identifying that person by his or her full name and
official title. Failure to do so may result in the dismissal of your case in court. “Agency” or
“department” means the national organization, and not the local office, facility or department in
which you work.

                            RIGHT TO REQUEST COUNSEL (Z0815)

If you want to file a civil action but cannot pay the fees, costs, or security to do so, you may request
permission from the court to proceed with the civil action without paying these fees or costs.
Similarly, if you cannot afford an attorney to represent you in the civil action, you may request the
court to appoint an attorney for you. You must submit the requests for waiver of court costs or
appointment of an attorney directly to the court, not the Commission. The court has the sole
discretion to grant or deny these types of requests.
