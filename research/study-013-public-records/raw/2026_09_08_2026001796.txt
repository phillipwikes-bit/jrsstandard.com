             DECISION ON REQUEST FOR RECONSIDERATION

Complainant timely requested that the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission
(EEOC or Commission) reconsider its decision in EEOC Appeal No. 2025000720
(March 4, 2026). EEOC Regulations provide that the Commission may, in its
discretion, grant a request to reconsider any previous Commission decision issued
pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(a), where the requesting party demonstrates that:
(1) the appellate decision involved a clearly erroneous interpretation of material fact
or law; or (2) the appellate decision will have a substantial impact on the policies,
practices, or operations of the agency. See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c).

At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a
Customer Service Representative at the Agency’s District Office in Joplin, Missouri.

On September 29, 2021, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging that the
Agency discriminated against her on the bases of disability (physical) and reprisal
for protected EEO activity for improperly disclosing Complainant’s confidential
medical information and also mentioning her EEO activity when it was not
necessary. Complainant initially requested a hearing but later withdrew her request
and therefore, the Agency issued a final decision, concluding that the Agency had
committed a per se violation of the Rehabilitation Act by disclosing her medical
information. Pursuant to that finding, the Agency awarded Complainant
compensatory damages. Complainant appealed the Agency’s decision on
compensatory damages. On appeal, the Commission determined that the Agency
had improperly determined the award of compensatory damages without considering
supporting evidence from Complainant. See Arleen L. v. Social Sec. Admin., EEOC
Appeal No. 2022003617 (Aug. 22, 2024). The Commission therefore vacated the
Agency’s first decision on compensatory damages and remanded for a supplemental
investigation into Complainant’s entitlement to compensatory damages. See id.

During the supplemental investigation, Complainant requested compensatory
damages in the amount of $40,000. The Agency issued a second decision, awarding
Complainant $2,000 in compensatory damages. Complainant appealed that second
decision and on appeal, the Commission affirmed the Agency’s award of
compensatory damages in the amount of $2,000. See Arleen L. v. Social Sec.
Admin., EEOC Appeal No. 2025000720 (March 4, 2026).

Complainant filed a request for reconsideration, reiterating that an award of $2000
is not adequate to compensate her for the harm that she suffered. The Agency filed
a brief in response, urging denial of Complainant’s request for reconsideration.

We reject Complainant’s arguments in her request for reconsideration as they
amount to nothing more than reiterating her previous arguments on appeal. We
emphasize that a request for reconsideration is not a second appeal to the
Commission. See Equal Employment Opportunity Management Directive for 29
C.F.R. Part 1614 (EEO MD-110), Chap. 9, § VILA (Aug. 5, 2015). Rather, a
reconsideration request is an opportunity to demonstrate that the appellate decision
involved a clearly erroneous interpretation of material fact or law, or will have a
substantial impact on the policies, practices, or operations of the Agency.
Complainant has not done so here.

While we acknowledge that objective evidence is not necessary in order to support
an award of compensatory damages, the lack of any evidence aside from a
complainant’s own statement can affect an award of damages. See Soo C. v. Dep’t
of State, EEOC Appeal No. 2019000659 (Aug. 19, 2020); Levi P. v. U.S. Postal
Serv., EEOC Appeal No. 012015113 (Nov. 9, 2017) (finding that the lack of
documentary evidence presented by the complainant did not provide any basis to
modify an agency’s award of compensatory damages). Moreover, to the extent
Complainant argues that the award should be increased because the Agency’s
actions were deliberate, we emphasize that compensatory damages are only intended
to remedy the harm caused by the discriminatory event, not to punish the Agency
for the discriminatory action. See Complainant v. U.S. Postal Serv., EEOC Appeal
No. 0120133316 (Sept. 4, 2014).

After reviewing the previous decision and the entire record, the Commission finds
that the request fails to meet the criteria of 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c), and it is the
decision of the Commission to deny the request. The decision in EEOC Appeal No.
2025000720 remains the Commission's decision. There is no further right of
administrative appeal on the decision of the Commission on this request.

        COMPLAINANT’S RIGHT TO FILE A CIVIL ACTION (P0124)

This decision of the Commission is final, and there is no further right of
administrative appeal from the Commission’s decision. You have the right to file a
civil action in an appropriate United States District Court within ninety (90)
calendar days from the date that you receive this decision. If you file a civil action,
you must name as the defendant in the complaint the person who is the official
Agency head or department head, identifying that person by their full name and
official title. Failure to do so may result in the dismissal of your case in court.
“Agency” or “department” means the national organization, and not the local office,
facility or department in which you work.

                    RIGHT TO REQUEST COUNSEL (Z0815)

If you want to file a civil action but cannot pay the fees, costs, or security to do so,
you may request permission from the court to proceed with the civil action without
paying these fees or costs. Similarly, if you cannot afford an attorney to represent
you in the civil action, you may request the court to appoint an attorney for you.
