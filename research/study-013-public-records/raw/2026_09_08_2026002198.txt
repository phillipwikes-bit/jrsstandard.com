             DECISION ON REQUEST FOR RECONSIDERATION

Complainant timely requested that the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission
(EEOC or Commission) reconsider its decision in EEOC Appeal No. 2025000743
(March 26, 2026). EEOC Regulations provide that the Commission may, in its
discretion, grant a request to reconsider any previous Commission decision issued
pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(a), where the requesting party demonstrates that:
(1) the appellate decision involved a clearly erroneous interpretation of material fact
or law; or (2) the appellate decision will have a substantial impact on the policies,
practices, or operations of the agency. See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c).

At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Claims
Specialist at the Agency’s Lumberton Field Office in Lumberton, North Carolina.
On April 2, 2024, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency
subjected her to numerous incidents of discrimination and harassment based on her
sex (female), race (American-Indian/Alaskan Native), national origin
(Hispanic/Latino), and religion (spiritually/Mexican culture) with respect to
training, assignment of duties, denial of premium pay requests, and leave, among
other things, that ultimately culminated in being terminated during her probationary
period on May 10, 2024.

After completion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy
of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an
Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge, or a final
decision from the agency. When Complainant did not request a hearing within the
time frame provided in 29 C.F.R. § 1614.108(f), the Agency issued a final decision
pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b). The decision concluded that Complainant did
not prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged.

Complainant appealed and on appeal, in a decision dated March 26, 2026, the
Commission affirmed the Agency’s final decision finding no discrimination.

Complainant filed the instant request for reconsideration, arguing among other
things, that the appellate decision erred because it accepted without question the
statements of the Agency management officials without considering their credibility.
Complainant also argued that the appellate decision erred in dismissing a claim
because Complainant later filed a grievance concerning that same matter, insisting
that filing a grievance was not an automatic abandonment of the EEO process.
Complainant also reiterates her argument raised on appeal that her termination was
retaliatory. 2 In response, the Agency filed a brief urging denial of Complainant’s
request for reconsideration.

We reject Complainant’s arguments in her request for reconsideration as they
amount to nothing more than reiterating her previous arguments on appeal. We

emphasize that a request for reconsideration is not a second appeal to the
Commission. See Equal Employment Opportunity Management Directive for 29
C.F.R. Part 1614 (EEO MD-110), Chap. 9, § VILA (Aug. 5, 2015). Rather, a
reconsideration request is an opportunity to demonstrate that the appellate decision
involved a clearly erroneous interpretation of material fact or law, or will have a
substantial impact on the policies, practices, or operations of the Agency.
Complainant has not done so here. Contrary to Complainant’s argument in her
request for reconsideration, the appellate decision did not conclude that filing a
grievance was an automatic abandonment of the EEO process. Rather, the appellate
decision dismissed the claim because to do otherwise would be to permit
Complainant to proceed in two different forums in the same matter, which is not
allowed. See Tressa L. v. Agency for Int’l Dev’t, EEOC Appeal No. 2024001610
(stating that the adjudication of a case in another forum is tantamount to an election
of remedies). To the extent Complainant assigns error to the appellate decision for
accepting the management officials’ statements, we note that because Complainant
did not request a hearing, the Commission did not have the benefit of an AJ’s
credibility determinations and could only evaluate the facts based on the evidence
presented. See Liz M. v. Dep’t of Defense, EEOC Appeal No. 0120150805 (April
5, 2017). The fact that Complainant clearly disagreed with the Agency’s reasonsias
not sufficient to establish any discriminatory animus. It is well established that mere
disagreement with the Agency’s actions is not sufficient to establish discrimination.
See Eryn M. v. Dep’t of the Army, EEOC Appeal No. 2022000871 (April 10, 2023).
Moreover, there is no evidence in the record indicating that any of the alleged
incidents of harassment were due to a protected class and therefore, Complainant's
claim of unlawful harassment must fail. See Stuart M. v. Dep’t of Veterans Affs.,
EEOC Appeal No. 2025001043 (Aug. 27, 2025).

After reviewing the previous decision and the entire record, the Commission finds
that the request fails to meet the criteria of 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c), and it is the
decision of the Commission to deny the request. The decision in EEOC Appeal No.
2025000743 remains the Commission's decision. There is no further right of
administrative appeal on the decision of the Commission on this request.

                    RIGHT TO REQUEST COUNSEL (Z0815)

If you want to file a civil action but cannot pay the fees, costs, or security to do so,
you may request permission from the court to proceed with the civil action without
paying these fees or costs. Similarly, if you cannot afford an attorney to represent
you in the civil action, you may request the court to appoint an attorney for you. You
must submit the requests for waiver of court costs or appointment of an
attorney directly to the court, not the Commission. The court has the sole
discretion to grant or deny these types of requests. Such requests do not alter the
time limits for filing a civil action (please read the paragraph titled Complainant’s
Right to File a Civil Action for the specific time limits).
