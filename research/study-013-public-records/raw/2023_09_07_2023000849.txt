                    DECISION ON REQUEST FOR RECONSIDERATION


Complainant timely requested that the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC or
Commission) reconsider its decision in Sherman K. v. Department of Veterans Affairs, EEOC
Appeal No. 2022001496 (November 8, 2022). EEOC Regulations provide that the Commission
may, in its discretion, grant a request to reconsider any previous Commission decision issued
pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(a), where the requesting party demonstrates that: (1) the
appellate decision involved a clearly erroneous interpretation of material fact or law; or (2) the
appellate decision will have a substantial impact on the policies, practices, or operations of the
agency. See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c).

During the relevant time, Complainant worked as a Project Manager, GS-14, at the Agency’s
Office of Construction and Facility Management (CFM), Office of Design and Construction
(ODC) in Washington D.C. On October 5, 2020, Complainant filed a formal complaint alleging
that the Agency subjected her to hostile work environment harassment based on age (60) and in
reprisal for prior protected EEO activity when:

Following an investigation, Complainant requested a final Agency decision. The Agency then
issued a final decision finding that Complainant failed to establish her claims of unlawful
discrimination or retaliation as alleged. Complainant appealed.

In EEOC Appeal No. 2022001496, the Commission affirmed the Agency’s final decision finding
no discrimination or unlawful retaliation. Our prior decision found that, considering the totality
of the record, there were conflicts and tensions with the Regional Director’s management style
that left Complainant feeling aggrieved and that there may have been a “fractured relationship”
between the new Regional Director and seasoned project managers, such as Complainant. Our
prior decision determined that the preponderance of the evidence did not establish that the
Regional Director was motivated by discriminatory or retaliatory animus. Therefore, the prior
decision affirmed the Agency’s final decision finding no discrimination or retaliation.

In the instant request for reconsideration, nothing that Complainant has submitted supports a
determination that the prior decision affirming the Agency final decision was in error.
Complainant challenges whether the prior decision considered her arguments and evidence.

After reviewing the previous decision and the entire record, the Commission finds that the
request fails to meet the criteria of 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c), and it is the decision of the
Commission to DENY the request. The decision in EEOC Appeal No. 2022001496 remains the
Commission's decision. There is no further right of administrative appeal on the decision of the
Commission on this request.

               COMPLAINANT’S RIGHT TO FILE A CIVIL ACTION (P0610)

This decision of the Commission is final, and there is no further right of administrative appeal
from the Commission’s decision. You have the right to file a civil action in an appropriate
United States District Court within ninety (90) calendar days from the date that you receive
this decision. If you file a civil action, you must name as the defendant in the complaint the
person who is the official Agency head or department head, identifying that person by his or her
full name and official title. Failure to do so may result in the dismissal of your case in court.
“Agency” or “department” means the national organization, and not the local office, facility or
department in which you work.

                          RIGHT TO REQUEST COUNSEL (Z0815)

If you want to file a civil action but cannot pay the fees, costs, or security to do so, you may
request permission from the court to proceed with the civil action without paying these fees or
costs. Similarly, if you cannot afford an attorney to represent you in the civil action, you may
request the court to appoint an attorney for you. You must submit the requests for waiver of
court costs or appointment of an attorney directly to the court, not the Commission. The
court has the sole discretion to grant or deny these types of requests.
