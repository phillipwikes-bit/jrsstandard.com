On September 20, 2024, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging
discrimination by the Agency based on race (Asian), national origin (Chinese), and
sex (female). ROI at 10-12.

By letter dated October 31, 2024, the Agency accepted Complainant’s complaint for

      On March 14, 2024,2 [Complainant was] terminated from [her] position of

After an investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report
of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal
Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ), or a final
decision from the agency. Complainant initially requested a hearing before an AJ.
Subsequently, on May 21, 2025, Complainant withdrew her request for a hearing
and requested a final agency decision. On August 19, 2025, an EEOC AJ remanded
the matter to the Agency to issue a final decision within 60 days of receipt of the
AJ’s Order due to Complainant’s withdrawal of her hearing request.

The Agency issued a final decision on December 18, 2025. The final decision
dismissed Complainant’s complaint for untimely EEO Counselor contact. Final
Agency Decision (FAD) at 3. The final decision then proceeded to address the
merits of the complaint and found no discrimination. The final decision found that
management articulated a legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason for Complainant’s
removal. Specifically, the final decision set forth that Complainant failed to report
to USCIS that she was residing with her mother who did not have lawful immigration
status. Finally, the final decision found that Complainant failed to establish that the
Agency’s articulated reason was pretext for discrimination.

Agency improperly dismissed her complaint for untimely EEO Counselor contact.

Complainant, on appeal, requests sanctions because the Agency was delayed in

imposition of a sanction. Jocelyn R. v. Dep’t of Def., EEOC Appeal No.
0120152852 (Mar. 11, 2016) (declining to sanction an agency that issued a final
decision more than four months after complainant’s request for a final decison).

To prevail in a disparate treatment claim, a complainant must satisfy the three-part
evidentiary scheme fashioned by the Supreme Court in McDonnell Douglas Corp.
v. Green, 411 U.S. 792 (1973). A complainant must initially establish a prima facie
case by demonstrating that she was subjected to an adverse employment action under
circumstances that would support an inference of discrimination. Furnco Constr.
Co. v. Waters, 438 U.S. 567, 576 (1978). Proof of a prima facie case will vary
depending on the facts of the particular case. McDonnell Douglas, 441 U.S. at 804
n.14.    The burden then shifts to the agency to articulate a legitimate,
nondiscriminatory reason for its actions. Texas Dep’t of Cmty. Affairs v. Burdine,
450 U.S. 248, 253 (1981). To ultimately prevail, a complainant must prove, by a
preponderance of the evidence, that the agency’s explanation is a pretext for
discrimination. Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., 530 U.S. 133, 143
(2000); St. Mary’s Honor Ctr v. Hicks, 509 U.S. 502, 519 (1993).

The Agency articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for Complainant’s
removal. The record contains a copy of Complainant’s notice of termination dated
March 14, 2024. Therein, the Director of the San Bernardino Filed Office sets forth
that:

The record supports the reasoning set forth in Complainant’s termination letter.
Complainant attended the USCIS Ethics and Integrity Course on September 19,
2023. ROI at 300. This training set forth in pertinent part that USCIS employees
must report without delay “[c]onduct that is contrary to the USCIS mission such as
engaging in a personal, financial, or business relationship…with a person that you
know or should know does not have a lawful status in the United States.” ROI at
446. The training provided contact information for an employee to report such
misconduct to USCIS or DHS’s Office of Inspector General. ROI at 448, 450.
Finally, the record contains a memorandum dated February 15, 2024 from the
Agency’s Office of Security and Integrity to the Agency’s Director, San Bernardino
Field Office. ROI at 190-191. The memorandum set forth that Complainant’s
mother continued to reside with her in the United States after the expiration of her
visa. ROI at 191. According to the memorandum, Complainant disclosed this
information during a polygraph examination with CBP in August 2023. Id.

Complainant failed to establish, by a preponderance of the evidence, that the
Agency’s articulated reason for her removal was pretext for discrimination.
Complainant asserts that she disclosed during a polygraph examination with CBP in
August 2023, prior to the September 2023 CSIS Ethics training, that her mother was
residing with her and that her mother did not have lawful immigration status. We
find that this assertion is not sufficient to establish pretext.

AFFIRM the Agency’s final decision finding no discrimination.

                          RECONSIDERATION (M1125)

The Commission may, in its discretion, reconsider this appellate decision if
Complainant or the Agency submits a written request that contains arguments or
evidence that tend to establish that:
        1. The appellate decision involved a clearly erroneous interpretation of
           material fact or law; or
        2. The appellate decision will have a substantial impact on the policies,
           practices, or operations of the agency.
Requests for reconsideration must be filed with EEOC’s Office of Federal Sector
(OFS) within thirty (30) calendar days of receipt of this decision. If the party
requesting reconsideration elects to file a statement or brief in support of the request,
that statement or brief must be filed together with the request for
reconsideration. A party shall have twenty (20) calendar days from receipt of
another party’s request for reconsideration within which to submit a brief or
statement in opposition. See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405; Equal Employment Opportunity
Management Directive for 29 C.F.R. Part 1614 (EEO MD-110), at Chap. 9 § VII.B
(Aug. 5, 2015).

Complainant should submit their request for reconsideration, and any statement or
brief in support of their request, via the EEOC Public Portal, which can be found at
https://publicportal.eeoc.gov/Portal/Login.aspx.
