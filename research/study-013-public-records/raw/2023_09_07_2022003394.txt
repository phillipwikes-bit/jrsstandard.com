On June 6, 2022, Complainant filed an appeal with the Equal Employment Opportunity
Commission (EEOC or Commission), pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.403(a), from the Agency’s

The Agency did not file a response to the Notice. On March 25, 2022, Complainant filed a
response to the Notice that included 12 exhibits (Complainant’s Response). In her response,
Complainant argued that the Notice “essentially relies on the Agency’s proffers in finding that

3. However, citing to Commission precedent, the AJ stated that the Notice did nothing more than
acknowledge that the Agency had met its “not onerous” burden of proffering legitimate,
nondiscriminatory reasons for the acts in question. The Notice then explained why it appeared
that Complainant could not satisfy her burden of establishing that the proffered legitimate,
nondiscriminatory reasons were pretext for discrimination or otherwise demonstrating that her
race, sex, age, or EEO activity motivated any of the acts in question to any extent.

Complainant’s Response also contended that two statements from Supervisor 1 that referenced
Complainant’s EEO complaint were evidence that Complainant’s EEO activity motivated the acts
at issue. The AJ found that even when evaluating those statements in a light most favorable to
Complainant, such statements did not suggest any discriminatory animus because they merely
acknowledged that Complainant filed an EEO complaint. Again, citing to Commission precedent,
the AJ stated that a supervisor’s mere acknowledgment that an employee has filed an EEO claim,
without more, did not evidence retaliation-based discriminatory animus.

According to the AJ, Supervisor 1’s alleged statement, “No more than the EEO complaint you
filed is putting a strain on working relationships,” was stated after Complainant complained about
the strained relationships that she believed Analyst’s grievance alleging a hostile work
environment had caused. See ROI at 427. In that context, observed the AJ, the statement could
not reasonably be interpreted to be anything but an acknowledgment that Complainant had filed
an EEO complaint and a reminder to Complainant that other employees possessed the same right
to file grievances and/or EEO complaints that Complainant did.

The AJ noted that when Supervisor 1 was questioned during a management directed investigation
about what was broken in the workplace, her response included the following:

“I think there is a brokenness between me & [Complainant] in that she filed a formal complaint
without ever giving me the opportunity to talk over her concerns prior to filing a complaint.
Another example of our relationship is I tried to talk to her about [a named employee’s] retirement
and she stated she did not want to talk to anyone in the organization. She jumps the chain of
command, ex. she contacted [another named employee] about her hierarchy in the org structure.”
ROI at 315.

According to the AJ, Supervisor 1’s reference to Complainant’s EEO complaint in the foregoing
response could not reasonably be interpreted to be anything but Supervisor 1 identifying an
example of the breakdown in communication between she and Complainant. At most, asserted
the AJ, Supervisor 1’s response conveyed animus towards Complainant’s unwillingness to
communicate with her. The AJ added that Supervisor 1’s response did not suggest any animus
towards Complainant’s filing of an EEO complaint. Rather, observed the AJ, Supervisor 1’s
response merely acknowledged Complainant filed an EEO complaint. The AJ determined that
even when viewed in a light most favorable to Complainant, Supervisor 1’s response could not
establish retaliation-based discriminatory animus.

The AJ stated that the other efforts within Complainant’s Response to establish that the Agency’s
proffered legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons were pretext for discrimination or to otherwise
demonstrate Complainant’s race, sex, age, or EEO activity motivated any of the acts in question
to any extent amounted to no more than unsupported allegations and speculation. Citing to
Commission precedent, the AJ asserted that unsupported allegations and speculation were
insufficient to create a genuine issue of material fact.

                                 CONTENTIONS ON APPEAL

In her Appeal Statement, among other things, Complainant contests the AJ’s decision. She
reiterates her allegations, arguing that the AJ erred when he determined that the Agency's proffers
were legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for the acts in question. Complainant asserts that a
hearing is required to evaluate the legitimacy of the Agency's proffers to ensure that Complainant
has a fair and reasonable opportunity to examine and cross-examine witnesses. Indeed, asserts
Complainant, the Agency's proffers in this case have not been subjected to cross-examination or
any examination for that matter.

According to Complainant, the delay in placing her in the 461st AMXS P&A position indicated
pretext and proof that the Agency planned to assign her to the 459th Deputy WSSC position.

Complainant also asserts that her 2017 appraisal was lower because “[Supervisor 1] meant to ruin
her reputation and opportunity for advancement because she is a Black female in her fifties and
because of protected activity.”

Complainant argues that the directive on overtime, Supervisor 1 directing the work and addressing
Complainant’s employees, and Supervisor 1 directing Analyst to report to Deputy Chief, proved
per se retaliatory animus. She cites her previous appraisals (all issued before her time working
with Supervisor 1), management’s discussions with her regarding work assignments and
responsibilities, and the proximity in which those discussions occurred to the filing of her EEO
complaints as evidence of pretext.

In response, among other things, the Agency asserts that it successfully articulated legitimate
nondiscriminatory rationales for its decisions in each complaint at issue, and Complainant had not
set forth any supported justification to overturn the AJ’s decision or the Agency’s Final Order.
According to the Agency, Complainant was not placed in the Deputy WSSC Chief position
because management decided that Complainant would be placed in a P&A Chief position, which
was in accordance with its inherent authority to assign work and manage its employees. The
Agency states that Complainant’s SF-50 documenting her transfer from the P&A Chief in the
559th AMXS to 561st AMXS was not completed until June 2018 due to administrative error, not
malicious intent.

                                  ANALYSIS AND FINDINGS

The Commission’s regulations allow an AJ to grant summary judgment when he or she finds that
there is no genuine issue of material fact. 29 C.F.R. § 1614.109(g). An issue of fact is “genuine”
if the evidence is such that a reasonable fact finder could find in favor of the nonmoving party.
Celotex v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 322-23 (1986); Oliver v. Digital Equip. Corp., 846 F.2d 103, 105
(1st Cir. 1988). A fact is “material” if it has the potential to affect the outcome of the case. In
rendering this appellate decision, we must scrutinize the AJ’s legal and factual conclusions, and
the Agency’s final order adopting them, de novo. See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(a)(stating that a
“decision on an appeal from an Agency’s final action shall be based on a de novo review...”); see
also Equal Employment Opportunity Management Directive for 29 C.F.R. Part 1614 (EEO-MD-
110), at Chap. 9, § VI.B. (as revised, August 5, 2015)(providing that an administrative judge’s
determination to issue a decision without a hearing, and the decision itself, will both be reviewed
de novo).

In order to successfully oppose a decision by summary judgment, a complainant must identify,
with specificity, facts in dispute either within the record or by producing further supporting
evidence and must further establish that such facts are material under applicable law. Such a
dispute would indicate that a hearing is necessary to produce evidence to support a finding that the
agency was motivated by discriminatory and/or retaliatory animus.

Upon careful review of the AJ’s decision, the Agency’s final order and the evidence of record, we
find that the AJ correctly determined that Complainant failed to establish that the Agency subjected
her to discrimination, a hostile work environment or unlawful retaliation as alleged. In making
this finding, we note that Complainant has not presented any arguments on appeal that would
contradict the AJ’s conclusions or the Agency’s final order adopting it.

Disparate Treatment Based on Race, Age, Color, Sex, and Reprisal

A claim of disparate treatment based on indirect evidence is examined under the three-part analysis
first enunciated in McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green, 411 U.S. 792 (1973). For Complainant to
prevail, he or she must first establish a prima facie case of discrimination by presenting facts that,
if unexplained, reasonably give rise to an inference of discrimination, i.e., that a prohibited
consideration was a factor in the adverse employment action. McDonnell Douglas, 411 U.S. at
802; Furnco Construction Corp. v. Waters, 438 U.S. 567 (1978). The burden then shifts to the
Agency to articulate a legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason for its actions. Tex. Dep’t of Cmty.
Affairs v. Burdine, 450 U.S. 248, 253 (1981).
                                                  13                                2022003394

Once the Agency has met its burden, Complainant bears the ultimate responsibility to persuade
the fact finder by a preponderance of the evidence that the Agency acted on the basis of a prohibited
reason. St. Mary’s Honor Ctr. v. Hicks, 509 U.S. 502 (1993).

Assuming arguendo that Complainant established her prima facie case, we find that the Agency
has articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions. Regarding Claims 1, 9 and 18,
Complainant was not placed in the Deputy WSSC Chief position for which she was recommended
because management in its discretion to assign employees in the organization decided that
Complainant would best remain as a P&A Chief. See ROI at 166. The SF-50 to complete
Complainant’s permanent assignment was subsequently completed, realigning her position as
P&A Supervisor from 559th Aircraft Maintenance Squadron (559 AMXS) to 561 AMXS. Supp.
ROI at 54.

Regarding Claim 2, Complainant did not receive a SF-50 documenting her transfer from the P&A
Chief of 559 AMXS to 561 AMXS until June 2018 due to administrative oversight. See ROI at
514-15 and Supp. ROI at 256.

Regarding Claims 3, 4, 10 and 11, Complainant received the performance appraisal comments and
ratings at issue because her supervisors believed that her work performance warranted such
comments and ratings. See ROI at 516-17 and Supp. ROI at 221-23 and 266-68.

Regarding Claim 5, Supervisor 1 questioned Complainant’s use of overtime because she did not
believe Complainant’s use of overtime on March 20, 2017 was efficient. See ROI at 71 and 506.

Regarding Claim 6, Supervisor 1’s March 21, 2017 email regarding overtime was to inform her
entire staff of the new overtime rules she was imposing to reduce the number of overtime hours
her staff was taking. See ROI at 98 and 506.

Regarding Claim 7, Supervisor 1 directed Analyst to report to Deputy Chief and for all direct
communications between Analyst and Complainant to cease because Analyst had alleged that
Complainant was creating a hostile work environment for her and an investigation into Analyst’s
allegations was necessary. See ROI at 506-07.

Regarding Claim 8, Supervisor 1 spoke directly to Complainant’s employees regarding the
performance of their duties because Complainant was failing to ensure that her employees
produced quality work product in a timely manner. See ROI at 507.

Regarding Claim 12, Supervisor 1 informed Complainant that she believed that all employees
should be rated a 5 in element 7 unless there was an example of the employee engaging in unsafe
acts. See Supp. ROI at 136-37, 223, and 335-36.

Regarding Claim 13, Chief directed that the award any affected employee received be changed
because he believed that the employee’s work performance warranted the increased award. See
Supp. ROI at 138-39 and 248-49.
                                                14                              2022003394


Regarding Claim 14, Chief directed Complainant to conduct her performance appraisal feedback
sessions in his presence because at that time, Complainant was the subject of an ongoing hostile
work environment investigation. See Supp. ROI at 249-50.

Regarding Claim 15, Chief and Complainant had a heated conversation because after a
performance appraisal feedback sessions with Complainant’s employee, Complainant indicated
that she would not perform the tasks that Chief was assigning to her. See Supp. ROI at 250-51.
Regarding Claim 16, Supervisor 2 directed Complainant to ban an employee from Building 47
because that employee had been in a verbal altercation with another employee that resulted in the
issuance of a No Contact Order and Supervisor 2 believed the ban from Building 47 was necessary
to enforce the No Contact Order. See Supp. ROI at 140 and 271.

Regarding Claim 17, Supervisor 2 issued Complainant a Notice of Proposed Suspension for 7 days
and suspended Complainant for 3 days because he believed that Complainant had failed to follow
her supervisors’ instructions. See Supp. ROI at 44, 169-70, 272-73, and 77-78.

Regarding Claim 19, Chief and Deputy Chief had direct communications with Complainant’s
subordinate employees and assigned work to such employees because Complainant was not
providing effective leadership for her team. See Supp. ROI at 253-54 and 339-40.

Regarding Claim 20, Supervisor 2 directed Complainant to report to Deputy Director’s office on
July 10, 2018, reassigned Complainant to a nonsupervisory position, directed Complainant to
vacate her office, and issued 5 No Contact Orders to Complainant because following investigation,
the Agency concluded Complainant had created a hostile work environment for her subordinate
employees and determined that these actions were necessary to ensure the hostile work
environment did not continue. See Supp. ROI at 274-76.

Regarding Claim 21, Deputy Director offered Complainant a nonsupervisory GS-12 Analyst
position outside 561 AMXS because Complainant requested reassignment to a position outside
561 AMXS and the GS-12 position was considered equivalent to the NH-3 position Complainant
held at that time. See Supp. ROI at 292.

Management has provided legitimate non-discriminatory reasons for the challenged actions. We
now turn to Complainant to establish that the Agency’s reasons were pretexts for discrimination.

Complainant did not dispute management’s explanations, and she presented no evidence linking
her race to the alleged management actions. In an attempt to show pretext, however, Complainant
argued that when she told Supervisor 1 that the actions taken subsequent to Analyst filing a
grievance alleging a hostile work environment were causing strained relationships, Supervisor 1
responded, “No more than the EEO complaint you filed is putting a strain on working
relationships.” See ROI at 427. According to Complainant, that statement revealed retaliatory
animus.
                                                  15                                2022003394

However, as the AJ correctly asserted, the alleged statement conveyed nothing more than that
employees were entitled to file both grievances and EEO complaints when they believed it was
necessary to do so. Therefore, the alleged statement did not support the existence of any retaliatory
animus. See Notice of Intent to Issue a Decision without a Hearing at 8.

Complainant did not show that any of the challenged management actions were based on any
purported prior EEO activity. Having carefully reviewed the record, we find that the management
actions were taken entirely for reasons other than Complainant’s protected bases. As such,
Complainant’s claims fail because she did not establish that she was subjected to unlawful
discrimination or retaliation. The Commission recognizes that ordinary managerial and
supervisory duties include assuring compliance with agency policy and procedures, monitoring
subordinates, scheduling the workload, scrutinizing and evaluating performance, providing job-
related advice and counsel, taking action in the face of performance shortcomings, and to otherwise
manage the workplace. Erika H. v. Dep’t of Transp., EEOC Appeal No. 0120151781 (Jun. 16,
2017). We find that many of the allegations stated in Complainant’s complaint fall within these
types of management prerogatives, and Complainant has not shown how she was treated
differently than others who were similarly situated and outside of any protected group to which
Complainant belongs.

Regarding Complainant’s hostile work environment claim, we find that, under the standards set
forth in Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17 (1993), Complainant’s claim of a hostile work
environment must fail. See Enforcement Guidance on Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., EEOC
Notice No. 915.002 (Mar. 8, 1994). A finding of a hostile work environment is precluded by our
determination that Complainant failed to establish that any of the actions taken by the Agency
were motivated by discriminatory or retaliatory animus. See Oakley v. United States Postal
Service, EEOC Appeal No. 01982923 (Sept. 21, 2000).

                                          CONCLUSION

Based on a thorough review of the record and the contentions on appeal, including those not
specifically addressed herein, we AFFIRM the AJ’s decision and the Agency’s final order adopting
it.

                                 RECONSIDERATION (M0920)

The Commission may, in its discretion, reconsider this appellate decision if Complainant or the
Agency submits a written request that contains arguments or evidence that tend to establish that:
       1. The appellate decision involved a clearly erroneous interpretation of material fact or
          law; or
       2. The appellate decision will have a substantial impact on the policies, practices, or
          operations of the agency.
                                                   16                                2022003394

Requests for reconsideration must be filed with EEOC’s Office of Federal Operations (OFO)
within thirty (30) calendar days of receipt of this decision. If the party requesting reconsideration
elects to file a statement or brief in support of the request, that statement or brief must be filed
together with the request for reconsideration. A party shall have twenty (20) calendar days
from receipt of another party’s request for reconsideration within which to submit a brief or
statement in opposition. See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405; Equal Employment Opportunity Management
Directive for 29 C.F.R. Part 1614 (EEO MD-110), at Chap. 9 § VII.B (Aug. 5, 2015).

Complainant should submit his or her request for reconsideration, and any statement or brief in
support of his or her request, via the EEOC Public Portal, which can be found at

https://publicportal.eeoc.gov/Portal/Login.aspx

Alternatively, Complainant can submit his or her request and arguments to the Director, Office of
Federal Operations, Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, via regular mail addressed to
P.O. Box 77960, Washington, DC 20013, or by certified mail addressed to 131 M Street, NE,
Washington, DC 20507. In the absence of a legible postmark, a complainant’s request to
reconsider shall be deemed timely filed if OFO receives it by mail within five days of the expiration
of the applicable filing period. See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.604.

An agency’s request for reconsideration must be submitted in digital format via the EEOC’s
Federal Sector EEO Portal (FedSEP). See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.403(g). Either party’s request and/or
statement or brief in opposition must also include proof of service on the other party, unless
Complainant files his or her request via the EEOC Public Portal, in which case no proof of service
is required.

Failure to file within the 30-day time period will result in dismissal of the party’s request for
reconsideration as untimely, unless extenuating circumstances prevented the timely filing of the
request. Any supporting documentation must be submitted together with the request for
reconsideration. The Commission will consider requests for reconsideration filed after the
deadline only in very limited circumstances. See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.604(c).
