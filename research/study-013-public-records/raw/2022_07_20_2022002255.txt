                    DECISION ON REQUEST FOR RECONSIDERATION

Complainant timely requested that the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC or
Commission) reconsider its decision in EEOC Request No. 2021003448 (Feb. 16, 2022). EEOC
regulations provide that the Commission may, in its discretion, grant a request to reconsider any
previous Commission decision issued pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(a), where the requesting
party demonstrates that: (1) the appellate decision involved a clearly erroneous interpretation of
material fact or law; or (2) the appellate decision will have a substantial impact on the policies,
practices, or operations of the agency. See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c).

During the period at issue, Complainant worked as a Special Agent at the Agency’s Los Angeles
Field Division Office in Los Angeles, California.

On November 30, 2018, Complainant filed a formal complaint alleging that the Agency
subjected him to discrimination on the bases of race (Arabic), national origin (Middle Eastern),
sex (male), age (57), and in reprisal for prior protected EEO activity when, on September 28,
2018, Complainant learned that his request for an exemption from mandatory law enforcement
retirement was not submitted to Agency Headquarters for review. After an investigation, the
Agency issued a final decision finding no discrimination was established. Complainant filed an
appeal.

In EEOC Appeal No. 2020003002 (April 29, 2021), we vacated the Agency’s final decision
reasoning that the record was devoid of evidence that the Agency provided Complainant with the
right to request a hearing. Thus, we remanded the matter to the Agency and ordered it to notify
Complainant of his hearing rights.

The Agency requested reconsideration of EEOC Appeal No. 2020003002 and asserted that due
to an administrative oversight, the documents related to Complainant’s receipt of the ROI and
request for a final decision were inadvertently omitted from the uploaded appeal documents.
EEOC Request No. 2021003448 (Feb. 16, 2022). In response, we denied the Agency’s request
for reconsideration, but reconsidered its initial decision on its own motion. In addressing the
merits of the complaint, OFO affirmed the Agency’s final decision finding no discrimination.
We found that the Agency’s witnesses articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for the
disputed action which Complainant failed to establish, by a preponderance of the evidence, were
pretext for discrimination. The decision provided rights to request reconsideration because the
merits of the complaint were addressed for the first time. Id.

Complainant requested reconsideration of EEOC Request No. 2021003448. Complainant
reiterates arguments raised below, such as he did not receive the memorandum regarding his
exemption from mandatory retirement in order to make corrections.

We find that EEOC Appeal No. 2021003448 addressed Complainant’s argument that he did not
receive the memorandum in order for him to make corrections. Specifically, the decision stated:

       Upon review, we find that Complainant has not established that the Agency’s
       reasons were a pretext for unlawful discrimination. In so finding, we note that
       multiple management officials attested that Complainant’s initially submitted
       memorandum contained multiple factual errors about his experience and career.
       The record contains the original memorandum submitted by Complainant and an
       edited version wherein supervisors noted the alleged factual errors regarding
       Complainant’s work experience…Complainant has not asserted that the factual
       errors identified by management are untrue. He does, however, dispute his
       supervisor’s statements regarding being given an opportunity to correct the
       memorandum. Complainant steadfastly maintains that he was never provided
       with the memorandum prior to his retirement date.

       However, Complainant did not request a hearing before an EEOC AJ, and
       therefore, we do not have the benefit of an AJ’s credibility determinations
       regarding the witnesses in the case. EEOC Request No. 2021003448.

We remind Complainant that a "request for reconsideration is not a second appeal to the
Commission." Equal Employment Opportunity Management Directive 110 for 29 C.F.R. Part
1614 (Aug. 5, 2015), Chapter 9. To the extent Complainant alleges that we erred in
reconsidering our initial decision on our own motion, we find that no abuse of our discretion in
reconsidering the decision. See EEOC Management Directive 110 for 29 C.F.R. Part 1614 (rev.
Aug. 5, 2015) Ch. 9, VII(A).

After reviewing the previous decision and the entire record, the Commission finds that the
request fails to meet the criteria of 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c), and it is the decision of the
Commission to deny the request. The decision in EEOC Appeal No. 2021003448 remains the
Commission's decision. There is no further right of administrative appeal on the decision of the
Commission on this request.


               COMPLAINANT’S RIGHT TO FILE A CIVIL ACTION (P0610)

This decision of the Commission is final, and there is no further right of administrative appeal
from the Commission’s decision. You have the right to file a civil action in an appropriate
United States District Court within ninety (90) calendar days from the date that you receive
this decision. If you file a civil action, you must name as the defendant in the complaint the
person who is the official Agency head or department head, identifying that person by his or her
full name and official title. Failure to do so may result in the dismissal of your case in court.
“Agency” or “department” means the national organization, and not the local office, facility or
department in which you work.


                          RIGHT TO REQUEST COUNSEL (Z0815)

If you want to file a civil action but cannot pay the fees, costs, or security to do so, you may
request permission from the court to proceed with the civil action without paying these fees or
costs. Similarly, if you cannot afford an attorney to represent you in the civil action, you may
request the court to appoint an attorney for you. You must submit the requests for waiver of
court costs or appointment of an attorney directly to the court, not the Commission. The
court has the sole discretion to grant or deny these types of requests.
