                                  DECISION

Complainant filed a timely appeal with the Equal Employment Opportunity
Commission (EEOC or Commission) from the Agency's decision dated
February 2, 2024, dismissing his complaint of unlawful employment
discrimination in violation of Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (Title
VII), as amended, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e et seq. Upon review, the Commission
finds that Complainant's complaint was properly dismissed for failure to
state a claim.

                             ISSUES PRESENTED

The issue presented before the Commission is whether Complainant's
complaint was properly dismissed pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.107(a)(1),
for failure to state a claim.

                               BACKGROUND

At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as
an Assistant Watch Commander at the Agency’s facility in Pearl Harbor,
Hawaii.

On May 2, 2022, Complainant filed a formal complaint alleging that the
Agency subjected him to hostile workplace discrimination on the bases of
race (African-American) and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity when:

   1. On December 22, 2021, Complainant’s second-level supervisor
   informed him that all Joint Base Pearl Harbor Hickam Annexes Police
   Officers will be moved to another shift; Complainant’s second-level
   supervisor stated that Complainant’s third-level supervisor had
   thrown the movement directive in his lap and that he had no control
   over the decision; Complainant’s second-level supervisor disregarded
   Complainant’s notice of the active EEO settlement agreement
   (executed on November 23, 2013, in settlement of DON Case No. 14-
   61449-00165) and failed in his due diligence efforts to confirm the
   settlement agreement; and

   2. On January 3, 2022, Complainant’s second-level supervisor sent
   an e-mail to Complainant indicating that Complainant was being
   moved from third shift to first shift; and Complainant’s second- and
   third-level supervisors purposefully breached the EEO settlement
   agreement when they decided to move Complainant to a different
   shift.

The record reflects that previously on November 23, 2013, the parties had
entered into a settlement agreement that states, in pertinent part, as
follows:

   “The Agency will return Employee to third shift subject to the
   operational needs of the department.”

The record further reflects that by letter dated May 31, 2022, the Agency
accepted the complaint pursuant to Commission regulations and
investigated the EEO complaint from August 2, 2022 through October 12,
2022. A copy of the Investigative File was sent to Complainant’s Attorney
Representative on November 22, 2022 and advised Complainant of the
right to request a final agency decision, or a hearing and decision by an
Administrative Judge (AJ) from the EEOC.
                                      3                          2024002447

On December 2, 2022, Complainant requested hearing. The case was
docketed and assigned to an AJ from the EEOC’s Los Angeles District
Office. On March 2, 2023, the AJ issued a Notice of Proposed Dismissal
pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.107 and 109 due to the Commission’s lack
of jurisdiction to address what the AJ determined is a settlement breach
claim. On March 23, 2023, Complainant filed his response. On April 11,
2023, the Agency filed its response, and on April 20, 2023, Complainant
filed a reply. On January 3, 2024, the AJ issued a Dismissal Order
dismissing the complaint in its entirety, pursuant to 29 C.F.R.
§1614.107(a)(1). The AJ specifically provided the following explanation:

   Complainant’s accepted claims in this matter both involve his move
   from third shift to first shift, and specifically refer to a breach of
   settlement claim relating to his placement on third shift. In both his
   submissions, Complainant asserts his claims raise new claims;
   however, a review of Complainant’s April 11 and April 20, 2023,
   responses make clear that his claims arise solely from the breach of
   the November 23, 2013 settlement agreement executed between the
   parties, and raise the same claim as identified in the settlement
   agreement. Complainant does not raise any claims unrelated to this
   agreement. Complainant should have pursued his breach claim
   pursuant to the administrative process explained in 29 C.F.R.
   1614.504. Allegations of breach settlement are not properly in this
   forum. See Anthan v. Dep’t of Trans., EEOC Appeal No. 01A50295
   (Apr. 6, 2006). Pursuant to 29 C.F.R. 1614.107(a), claims alleging
   matters previously raised are appropriate for dismissal. Here, the
   claims and issues are identical. Therefore, pursuant to 20 C.F.R.
   1614.107(a)[(1)], I hereby DISMISS Complainant’s claims.

The Agency adopted the AJ’s decision.

                         CONTENTIONS ON APPEAL

Complainant through counsel reargues his position that Complainant’s claims
constitute new claims. Specifically, counsel asserts, “The allegations in this
case involve new and subsequent causes of action that transcend a breach
of the settlement agreement.” In response, the Agency notes that because
Complainant failed to adhere to the procedures under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.504,
his claims have been appropriately dismissed by the AJ. The Agency
requests that we affirm its final decision.

                           STANDARD OF REVIEW

The Agency’s decision to dismiss a complaint is subject to de novo review by
the Commission, which requires the Commission to examine the record
without regard to the factual and legal determinations of the previous
decision maker and issue its decision based on the Commission’s own
assessment of the record and its interpretation of the law. 29 C.F.R. §
1614.405(a). The Commission should construe the complaint in the light
most favorable to the complainant and take the complaint’s allegations as
true. See Cobb v. Department of the Treasury, EEOC Request No. 05970077
(March 13, 1997). Thus, all reasonable inferences that may be drawn from
the complaint’s allegations must be made in favor of the complainant.

                                 ANALYSIS

The regulation set forth at 29 C.F.R. § 1614.107(a)(1) provides, in relevant
part, that an agency shall dismiss a complaint that fails to state a claim. An
agency shall accept a complaint from any aggrieved employee or applicant
for employment who believes that he or she has been discriminated against
by that agency because of race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age or
disability. 29 C.F.R. §§ 1614.103, .106(a). The Commission's federal sector
case precedent has long defined an "aggrieved employee" as one who
suffers a present harm or loss with respect to a term, condition, or privilege
of employment for which there is a remedy. Diaz v. Dep’t of the Air Force,
EEOC Request No. 05931049 (Apr. 21, 1994).

EEOC Regulation 29 C.F.R. § 1614.107(a)(1) provides that prior to a request
for a hearing, the Agency shall dismiss a complaint that states the same
claim that is pending before or has been decided by the agency or
Commission.

The AJ properly dismissed Complainant’s complaint for raising matter(s) that
are both identical and directly related to those contained within the
November 23, 2013 settlement agreement. Specifically, Complainant’s
claims (above) regarding shift change are inextricably linked to the previous
settlement in this matter and should have been raised by Complainant as a
settlement breach claim pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.504. As noted by the
AJ, if Complainant believed that the Agency had failed to comply with the
terms of the settlement agreement, he should have [first] notified the EEO
Director, in writing, of the alleged non-compliance, within 30 days of when
he knew, or should have known of the alleged non-compliance.
                                      5                           2024002447

Complainant could have then filed an appeal with the EEOC alleging non-
compliance with the settlement agreement. Complainants may appeal within
thirty (30) days if they do not agree with the Agency’s determination, or
within thirty-five (35) days if the Agency does not issue a decision. See 29
C.F.R. § 1614.504 generally; and EEO Management Directive (MD-110)
Chapter 10, Section II.3(a), (b).

                                CONCLUSION

Accordingly, the Agency's final decision dismissing Complainant's complaint
is affirmed.

                    STATEMENT OF RIGHTS - ON APPEAL
                      RECONSIDERATION (M0124.1)

The Commission may, in its discretion, reconsider this appellate decision if
Complainant or the Agency submits a written request that contains
arguments or evidence that tend to establish that:
      1. The appellate decision involved a clearly erroneous interpretation of
         material fact or law; or
      2.     The appellate decision will have a substantial impact on the
           policies, practices, or operations of the agency.

Requests for reconsideration must be filed with EEOC’s Office of Federal
Operations (OFO) within thirty (30) calendar days of receipt of this
decision. If the party requesting reconsideration elects to file a statement or
brief in support of the request, that statement or brief must be filed
together with the request for reconsideration. A party shall have
twenty (20) calendar days from receipt of another party’s request for
reconsideration within which to submit a brief or statement in opposition.
See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405; Equal Employment Opportunity Management
Directive for 29 C.F.R. Part 1614 (EEO MD-110), at Chap. 9 § VII.B (Aug. 5,
2015).

Complainant should submit their request for reconsideration, and any
statement or brief in support of their request, via the EEOC Public Portal,
which can be found at https://publicportal.eeoc.gov/Portal/Login.aspx
                                       6                           2024002447

Alternatively, Complainant can submit their request and arguments to the
Director, Office of Federal Operations, Equal Employment Opportunity
Commission, via regular mail addressed to P.O. Box 77960, Washington, DC
20013, or by certified mail addressed to 131 M Street, NE, Washington, DC
20507. In the absence of a legible postmark, a complainant’s request to
reconsider shall be deemed timely filed if OFO receives it by mail within five
days of the expiration of the applicable filing period. See 29 C.F.R. §
1614.604.

An agency’s request for reconsideration must be submitted in digital format
via the EEOC’s Federal Sector EEO Portal (FedSEP). See 29 C.F.R. §
1614.403(g). Either party’s request and/or statement or brief in opposition
must also include proof of service on the other party, unless Complainant
files their request via the EEOC Public Portal, in which case no proof of
service is required.

Failure to file within the 30-day time period will result in dismissal of the
party’s request for reconsideration as untimely, unless extenuating
circumstances prevented the timely filing of the request. Any supporting
documentation must be submitted together with the request for
reconsideration.         The Commission will consider requests for
reconsideration filed after the deadline only in very limited circumstances.
See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.604(f).
