             DECISION ON REQUEST FOR RECONSIDERATION

Complainant timely requested that the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission
(EEOC or Commission) reconsider its decision in EEOC Appeal No. 2025000633
(Feb. 17, 2026). EEOC Regulations provide that the Commission may, in its
discretion, grant a request to reconsider any previous Commission decision issued
pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(a), where the requesting party demonstrates that:
(1) the appellate decision involved a clearly erroneous interpretation of material fact
or law; or (2) the appellate decision will have a substantial impact on the policies,
practices, or operations of the agency. See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c).

The record reflects the following chronology of events.

In a final decision, dated April 9, 2024, the Agency found that Complainant was
subjected to discrimination based on sex (female) and race (African American)
regarding three non-selections. As part of its remedial order, the Agency ordered a
supplemental investigation into Complainant’s entitlement to compensatory
damages.

Following a supplemental investigation, the Agency issued a final decision awarding
Complainant $30,000.00 in non-pecuniary compensatory damages.

Complainant appealed to the Commission and requested an increase in the award to
$178,825.00.

In our initial decision, we found that the Agency’s award of $30,000.00 was
appropriate and held that Agency’s decision regarding compensatory damages
would remain in place. EEOC Appeal No. 2025000633.

Complainant filed the instant request for reconsideration. Therein, Complainant
requests an award of compensatory damages between $85,000.00 to $125,000.00.
Complainant asserts that our initial decision did not consider the basis of reprisal or
her being denied a reasonable accommodation regarding the compensatory damages
award. Complainant reiterates arguments raised below that the $30,000.00 award in
non-pecuniary damages is inadequate because it does not reflect the nature, severity,
and duration of the harm.

In response, the Agency’s asserts that we should deny Complainant’s request for
reconsideration. The Agency asserts that Complainant fails to meet the criteria for
a request for reconsideration and that the award of $30,000.00 was proper. The
Agency asserts that even if the basis of reprisal was addressed with respect to the
three non-selections, this would not entitle Complainant to additional relief. The
Agency also asserts that Complainant’s accepted claims in the instant matter did not
include a denial of a reasonable accommodation claim.

A request for reconsideration is not a second appeal. See Equal Employment
Opportunity Management Directive for 29 C.F.R. Part 1614 (EEO MD-110), Chap.
9 § VII.A (Aug. 5, 2015); see, e.g., Lopez v. Dep’t of Agric., EEOC Request No.
0520070736 (Aug. 20, 2007).

A request for reconsideration is an opportunity to demonstrate that the appellate
decision involved a clearly erroneous interpretation of material fact or law, or will
have a substantial impact on the policies, practices, or operations of the Agency.
Complainant has not done so here.

The initial decision properly found that $30,000.00 was an appropriate award in the
instant matter. To the extent that Complainant is alleging harm from a denial of a
reasonable accommodation, this matter was not an accepted claim in this instant
complaint. In addition, we concur with the Agency that while Complainant may
have initially raised the basis of reprisal with respect to the three non-selections, a
finding on this additional basis would not have altered her award of compensatory
damages. 2 Based on the foregoing, our prior decision properly set forth that the
Agency’s final decision regarding compensatory damages would remain in place.

After reviewing the previous decision and the entire record, the Commission finds
that the request fails to meet the criteria of 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c), and it is the
decision of the Commission to deny the request. The decision in EEOC Appeal No.
2025000633 remains the Commission's decision. There is no further right of
administrative appeal on the decision of the Commission on this request.

        COMPLAINANT’S RIGHT TO FILE A CIVIL ACTION (P0124)

This decision of the Commission is final, and there is no further right of
administrative appeal from the Commission’s decision. You have the right to file a
civil action in an appropriate United States District Court within ninety (90)
calendar days from the date that you receive this decision. If you file a civil action,
you must name as the defendant in the complaint the person who is the official
Agency head or department head, identifying that person by their full name and
official title. Failure to do so may result in the dismissal of your case in court.
“Agency” or “department” means the national organization, and not the local office,
facility or department in which you work.

                    RIGHT TO REQUEST COUNSEL (Z0815)

If you want to file a civil action but cannot pay the fees, costs, or security to do so,
you may request permission from the court to proceed with the civil action without
paying these fees or costs. Similarly, if you cannot afford an attorney to represent
you in the civil action, you may request the court to appoint an attorney for you. You
must submit the requests for waiver of court costs or appointment of an
attorney directly to the court, not the Commission. The court has the sole
discretion to grant or deny these types of requests. Such requests do not alter the
time limits for filing a civil action (please read the paragraph titled Complainant’s
Right to File a Civil Action for the specific time limits).
