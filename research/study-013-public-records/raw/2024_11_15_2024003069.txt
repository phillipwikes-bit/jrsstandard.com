                             ISSUE PRESENTED

Whether Complainants’ requests for reconsideration of EEOC Appeal No.
2020000512, et al. (Mar. 27, 2024) meet the criteria detailed in 29 C.F.R.
§ 1614.405(c).

                                BACKGROUND

At the time of events giving rise to these complaints, Complainants worked as
Foresters at the Agency’s Southern Research Station in Fort Smith, Arkansas
and Oxford, Mississippi, respectively.

On June 10, 2016, and June 17, 2016, 2 Complainants filed separate EEO
complaints alleging that the Agency discriminated against them on the basis
of religion (Christian) when management failed to accommodate their religious
beliefs by denying them a training exemption and directing them to complete
mandatory LGBT training by September 30, 2016. 3

At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainants with
a copy of the report of investigation and notice of their right to request a
hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative
Judge (AJ). Complainants timely requested a hearing. The AJ ultimately
consolidated Hearing Nos. 490-2018-00072X and 490-2018-00070X.

Over Complainants’ objections, the AJ granted the Agency’s motion for a
decision without a hearing, as the AJ found that Complainants did not establish
that the Agency’s training either promoted LGBT ideals or sought to change
Complainants’ beliefs. Therefore, the AJ reasoned that the Agency was not
required to provide an exemption that would excuse Complainants from a
training that merely instructed employees on how to comply with federal law.

The Agency subsequently issued a final order adopting the AJ’s finding that
Complainants failed to prove that the Agency subjected them to discrimination
as alleged. Complainants appealed the Agency’s final order to the
Commission, arguing, in relevant part, that in lieu of requiring them to attend
the training, the Agency could have reasonably accommodated them by
allowing them to sign a document acknowledging that they had read and
understood the expansion/ruling of including LGBT individuals under the
definition of “sex.”

In EEOC Appeal No. 2020000512 (Mar. 27, 2024), the Commission affirmed
the Agency’s final order. While Complainants had argued that the Agency
could have reasonably accommodated them by allowing them to sign a
document acknowledging that the law had expanded to cover LGBT
individuals, the Commission found that such a bare acknowledgement would
have been inadequate to prevent the Agency from being in violation of federal
law. The Commission ultimately concluded that even assuming arguendo that
attending such a training could be said to burden Complainants’ religious
beliefs, the Agency established that it would have been an undue hardship to
provide a religious exemption for the training, as doing so would have laid the
Agency open to potentially violating federal law and regulations. The instant
request followed.

                         CONTENTIONS ON REQUEST

In requesting reconsideration, Complainants reiterate their prior contention
that the Agency could have simply posted the change in the EEOC law and
required an acknowledgement and understanding verification signature.
Complainants reiterate that this religious accommodation would have been
completely acceptable. However, the Agency unlawfully “required employees
to view a video labeled ‘Cultural Transformation’ that was intended to validate,
approve, and celebrate the LGBT lifestyle.”

The Agency opposes the appeal and emphasizes that                 requests for
reconsideration are not second appeals to the Commission.

                           STANDARD OF REVIEW

EEOC regulations provide that the Commission may, in its discretion, grant a
request to reconsider any previous Commission decision issued pursuant to
29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(a), where the requesting party demonstrates that: (1)
the appellate decision involved a clearly erroneous interpretation of material
fact or law; or (2) the appellate decision will have a substantial impact on the
policies, practices, or operations of the agency. See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c).

                                  ANALYSIS

We have reviewed the various arguments raised by Complainants in the
instant requests for reconsideration. However, we can find no basis to disturb
the Commission’s prior decision, as Complainants largely present contentions
that were previously raised and considered. The Commission emphasizes that
a request for reconsideration is not a second appeal. See Equal Employment
Opportunity Management Directive for 29 C.F.R. Part 1614 (EEO MD-110),
Chap. 9 § VI.A (Aug. 5, 2015); see, e.g., Lopez v. Dep’t of Agric., EEOC
Request No. 0520070736 (Aug. 20, 2007). Rather, a reconsideration request
is an opportunity to demonstrate that the appellate decision involved a clearly
erroneous interpretation of material fact or law, or will have a substantial
impact on the policies, practices, or operations of the Agency. Complainants
have not done so here.

                                CONCLUSION

After reviewing the previous decision and the entire record, the Commission
finds that the requests fail to meet the criteria of 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c),
and it is the decision of the Commission to deny the request. The decision in
EEOC Appeal No. 2020000512, et al., remains the Commission’s decision.
There is no further right of administrative appeal on the decision of the
Commission on these requests.
