                                    DECISION

On July 21, 2022, Complainant filed an appeal with the Equal Employment
Opportunity Commission (EEOC or Commission), pursuant to 29 C.F.R. §
1614.403(a), from the Agency’s June 17, 2022, final decision concerning her
equal employment opportunity (EEO) complaint alleging employment
discrimination in violation of Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (Title VII),
as amended, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e et seq. and the Age Discrimination in
Employment Act of 1967 (ADEA), as amended, 29 U.S.C. § 621 et seq. For
the following reasons, the Commission VACATES the Agency’s final decision
and REMANDS the matter for a supplemental investigation.

                                ISSUE PRESENTED

The issue is whether the Agency properly issued a final decision (FAD)
concluding that Complainant failed to prove that she was subjected to
discrimination regarding an awarded salary increase on the bases of her age
(59 [DOB: March 30, 1962]) and reprisal (instant complaint).

                                BACKGROUND

At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant, 59 (YOB
1962) worked as a Human Resources (HR) Generalist at the Agency’s
Bellingham Customer Care Center in Troy, Michigan. Report of Investigation
(ROI) at 70 and 92. According to Complainant, she had been detailed into her
position for ten months prior to her selection, which became effective July 17,
2021. She stated that when she was offered a five percent (5%) salary
increase, she told the HR Business Partner (Business Partner [age 58]) that
she was eligible for an 8% increase. ROI at 71.

Complainant initiated EEO contact on August 31, 2021. On November 17,
2021, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency
discriminated against her on the bases of age (59) and reprisal for prior
protected EEO activity under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964
the Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967 when on or about July 17,
2021, Complainant was awarded a 5% salary increase when selected for an
HR Generalist position, while a peer selected for the same position was
awarded an 8% salary increase.

The basis of Complainant’s reprisal allegation is the instant complaint. ROI at
72.

The Agency conducted an investigation into the complaint. The investigation
revealed that Complainant’s coworker (Coworker [YOB: 1975]) was 46 at the
time of her promotion effective on July 17, 2021. ROI at 98. Both Complainant
and Coworker were selected and promoted to the position of HR Generalist
HQ position, occupation code 0201-0140, on the Customer Care Team. ROI at
35, 92, and 98. An email was shared with Complainant that included the Form
50s of Coworker and Complainant evidencing their promotions to the position
of HR Generalist. See ROI at 33-8. The email revealed that Coworker received
an 8% raise, while Complainant only received a 5% raise.

On June 13, 2015, the Postal Service issued a policy that governs promotional
pay increases. That policy provides, “promotional pay increases will be
determined based on 2 factors: distance from positions maximum salary, and
the number of grades being promoted.” ROI at 86. The Postal Service’s policy
does not provide for any other justification for promotional pay increases. The
policy also contains an explanatory attachment that sets forth what
promotional pay raise a candidate can receive under postal policy as follows:

Promotional pay increase policy determined based on the following two (2)
factors:

  a. Distance from maximum salary level of new grade (Employees will
     receive a minimum promotional salary increase of 3%, unless the
     increase takes their salary above the maximum of the new grade):
        • >20% = 3% up to 10% increase (inc)
        • 10%-20% = 3% up to 8% inc
        • < 10% = 3% up to 5% inc


  b. And, total number of grade levels being promoted (if applicable):
       • If 3 grades = 3% inc

Complainant’s Form 50 provides in the remark section “salary increase process
under pay promotion policy effective 6/13/15. Level 19 promoted to level 21.
Present increase 5% applied to current salary of $90,868.” Complainant’s
Appeal Brief at 21. Coworker’s Form 50 contains the following statement in
the remark section “salary increase process and promotional policy effective
6/13/15. Level 19 promoted to level 21. Percentage increase of 8% applied to
current salary of $86,337.” Complainant’s Appeal Brief at 20.

As both Complainant and Coworker’s pre-promotion salaries were between
10% to 20% of the top of the pay grade for EAS-21, and they fell within the
3% to 8% range for raise upon being promoted from their EAS-19 positions
to the EAS-21 position of HR Generalist. ROI at 86-7 and Complainant’s Appeal
Brief at 18. Complainant’s and Coworker’s respective ages were readily known
to the Responsible Management Officials as it was reflected on their Form 50s.

When Complainant was notified that she received the promotion, she was told
that she would receive a 5% raise. According to Complainant, Business Partner
told Complainant that she did not have a budget for an 8% raise and that she
would have to get approval from a named individual. Business Partner also
allegedly stated that Complainant did not have enough experience. ROI at 71.

However, Complainant stated that she had been doing the job fully and
successfully, she possessed a bachelor’s degree in business administration
and had three years’ experience at the Troy Customer Care Center. Thus, she
had higher education, more human resource experience, and more experience

in the Customer Care Center than Coworker. ROI at 71; also compare ROI at
93-7 (Complainant’s resume) to ROI at 100-04 (Coworker’s resume).
According to Complainant, Coworker’s education was limited to a high school
certificate in cosmetology and licensing in cosmetology. ROI at 103.
Complainant asserted that she worked in Human Resources for 32 months as
a labor relations specialist from July 3, 2004 – March 30, 2007 as part of a
detail assignment. ROI at 95.

Complainant also asserted that the amount of training the respective
candidates received while in detail reflected their readiness to assume the
position. According to her, Coworker received more training than Complainant
when she was detailed into the position of HR Generalist. Coworker allegedly
received 1 hour on and 1 hour off the clock 1 on 1 training by an HR Generalist
for 30 days when she was detailed into the position for a total of 60 hours of
training. Complainant’s 1 on 1 training added up to less than 2 hours. ROI at

According to Business Partner, although she initially requested a 5% salary
increase for Coworker, her manager (Manager), approved an 8% increase
based on Coworker’s HR knowledge and experience. ROI at 77, 79, 92, and
100. Business Partner stated that she also offered a 5% salary increase to the
last person she selected for the position of HR Generalist (YOB: 1976) in 2019,
some two years prior. ROI at 78-9 and 83.

At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with
a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing
before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge
(AJ). When Complainant did not request a hearing within the time frame
provided in 29 C.F.R. § 1614.108(f), the Agency issued a final decision (FAD)
pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b). The decision concluded that
Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination
as alleged.

                           STANDARD OF REVIEW

As this is an appeal from a decision issued without a hearing, pursuant to 29
C.F.R. § 1614.110(b), the Agency's decision is subject to de novo review by
the Commission. 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(a).            See Equal Employment
Opportunity Management Directive for 29 C.F.R. Part 1614, at Chapter 9,
§ VI.A. (Aug. 5, 2015) (explaining that the de novo standard of review
“requires that the Commission examine the record without regard to the
factual and legal determinations of the previous decision maker,” and that
EEOC “review the documents, statements, and testimony of record, including
any timely and relevant submissions of the parties, and . . . issue its decision
based on the Commission’s own assessment of the record and its
interpretation of the law”).

                                  ANALYSIS

Reprisal

At the onset, we note that the record does not reflect a basis for concluding
that Complainant was subjected to reprisal in this case. Complainant has failed
to meet any of the four elements required to establish a prima facie case of
reprisal. See Nida R. v. Dep’t of Def., EEOC Appeal No. 0120152884 (Apr. 22,
2016); see also EEOC Enforcement Guidance on Retaliation and Related
Issues, § II.C.2, n. 154 (Aug. 25, 2016) (citing Henry v. Wyeth Pharm., 616
F.3d 134, 148 (2d Cir. 2010)). Furthermore, “[t]he cases that accept mere
temporal proximity between an adverse employment action as sufficient
evidence of causality to establish a prima facie case uniformly hold that the
temporal proximity must be ‘very close’ [in time].” Clark County Sch. Dist. v.
Breeden, 532 U.S. 268 (2001) (citing to O’Neal v. Ferguson Constr. Co., 237
F.3d 1248, 1253 (C.A.10 2001); Richmond v. ONEOK, Inc., 120 F.3d 205, 209
(C.A.10 1997) (finding a three-month period insufficient); Hughes v.
Derwinski, 967 F.2d 1168, 1174-1175 (finding a four-month period
insufficient).

The record is devoid of any evidence that Complainant engaged in protected
activity prior to August 31, 2021, when she initiated EEO contact in the instant
complaint or July 17, 2021, when the alleged action occurred. There is also
no evidence that management, including Business Partner, was aware that
Complainant had contacted an EEO counselor before her salary increase
determination was made. Notably, Business Partner did not become aware of
the instant complaint until on or about September 17, 2021.
                                    6                             2022004043

ROI at 80-1. Complainant herself identified her protected activity as the
instant complaint. ROI at 72.

Inadequately Developed Record

We find that Complainant has established a prima facie case of discrimination
based on age to the extent that she is over age 40, having been born in 1962.
ROI at 71. Complainant was also subjected to adverse action because she was
awarded a five percent salary increase on or about July 17, 2021. ROI at 76.
Coworker, a peer who was outside of Complainant’s protected age class being
some 13 years younger, received an 8% salary increase; 3% more than what
Complainant received. See e.g., Orval T. v. Dep’t of Energy, EEOC Appeal No.
0120161688, at *3 (Sept. 14, 2018) (setting forth required elements to
establish a prima facie case of age discrimination). See also, Hammersmith v.
Soc. Sec. Admin., EEOC Appeal No. 01A05922, at *4 (Mar. 6, 2002) (“While
there is no bright-line test for what constitutes ‘substantially younger,’ that
term has generally applied to age differences in excess of five years.”).

Complainant indicated that she had been given a reason by Business Partner
that she then proceeded to show was pretextual. Complainant has raised
serious allegations indicating that she was subjected to disparate treatment
based on that adverse action. The following reflects the information from a
chart that compared the peers’ relevant experience and qualifications. See
Complainant’s Appeal Brief at 8.

In regard to education, Complainant has a Bachelor of Arts (BA) in Accounting
and Business Management, while Coworker has High School Certificate in
Cosmetology. ROI at 96 and 103. Complainant had 10 months experience as
an Acting HR Generalist. In comparison, Coworker had 11 months. ROI at 71,
78, and 102. Concerning their years of Postal experience, Complainant had 27
years and 11 months and Coworker had 26 years and 7 months. ROI at 92
and 98. Complainant did not have any “net experience as Field Recruiter” while
Coworker had 9 months. ROI at 100 and 102. Complainant had 32 months of
“net experience as Labor Relations Specialist.” ROI at 95. In comparison,
Coworker had none. Lastly, Complainant had 42 months of experience working
in HR positions and Coworker had 20 months.

However, we determine that the Agency has failed to provide legitimate
nondiscriminatory reasons for the challenged management actions because
the investigative record with respect to the allegation that formed the basis of
Complainant’s age discrimination allegation regarding her lower salary raise
in this case was not adequately developed. Specifically, Manager, who
                                    7                             2022004043

approved an 8% salary raise for Coworker and only a 5% salary raise for
Complainant, did not provide testimony. Notably, the record includes the EEO
Investigator’s note that Manager did not return the affidavit questionnaire that
was sent to her. 2 ROI at 63 and 65.

Since Manager was not interviewed as part of the investigation, it is
Complainant’s word against the statements included in the Agency’s FAD.
Given Complainant’s assertions of a discriminatory intent by Business Partner
and Manager which could be true, we find that the investigation was
inadequate in this case.
                               CONCLUSION

Therefore, after a careful review of the record, including Complainant's
arguments on appeal and evidence not specifically discussed in this decision,
the Commission VACATES the Agency's final decision and REMANDS the
matter to the Agency in accordance with this decision and the Order below.
