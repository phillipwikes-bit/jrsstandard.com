                    DECISION ON REQUEST FOR RECONSIDERATION

Complainant timely requested that the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC or
Commission) reconsider its decision in EEOC Appeal Nos. 0120182203 and 2019000587
(December 11, 2019). EEOC regulations provide that the Commission may, in its discretion, grant
a request to reconsider any previous Commission decision issued pursuant to 29 C.F.R. §
1614.405(a), where the requesting party demonstrates that: (1) the appellate decision involved a
clearly erroneous interpretation of material fact or law; or (2) the appellate decision will have a
substantial impact on the policies, practices, or operations of the agency. See 29 C.F.R. §
1614.405(c).

During the relevant period, Complainant worked as a GS-0105-11 Claims Specialist (Claims
Representative) in the Agency’s Lake May, Florida Field Office, which is part of the Agency’s
Atlanta, Georgia Region. Complainant had previously worked as a GS-12 Technical Expert in the
Agency’s Clarksburg, West Virginia Field Office, which is part of the Agency’s Philadelphia,
Pennsylvania Region.

On February 24, 2017, Complainant filed an EEO complaint (Agency No. ATL-17-0300-SSA)
alleging that the Agency discriminated against her based on disability when:

       1. From August 17, 2015 through February 2017, she was denied a reasonable
          accommodation;

       2. On November 23, 2016, she received a rating of 3 in all areas of her Performance
          Assessment Communications System (PACS) appraisal; and

       3. On December 21, 2016, she learned that she should have received an informational
          appraisal when she transferred to the Lake May Field Office.

On May 14, 2017, Complainant filed another EEO complaint (Agency No. ATL-17-0527-SSA)
alleging the Agency discriminated against her based on disability and in reprisal for prior EEO
activity when:

       4. On February 27, 2017, management required her to attend a mandatory PACS
          evaluation meeting regarding her work performance;

       5. From February 27 through March 23, 2017, she was subjected to a hostile work
          environment characterized by performance meetings, her request for reasonable
          accommodation being denied, and meetings about her workload; and

       6. In the spring of 2017, she was denied a reasonable accommodation.

At the conclusion of both of the investigations, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of
the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment
Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). When Complainant did not request a hearing
within the time frame provided in 29 C.F.R. § 1614.108(f), the Agency issued a consolidated final
decision on Agency Nos. ATL-17-0300-SSA and ATL-17-0527-SSA pursuant to 29 C.F.R. §
1614.110(b) on May 4, 2018, finding no discrimination. Appeal No. 0120182203 followed.

On February 14, 2018, Complainant filed another formal EEO complaint (Agency No. ATL-18-
0230-SSA) alleging that the Agency discriminated against her based on disability when:

       7. On October 31, 2017, she received a rating of 3 in all of her PACS appraisal;

       8. Between November 21, 2017, and January 2018, she was denied a reasonable
          accommodation; and

        9. Beginning on December 29, 2017, she was subjected to a hostile work environment
            characterized by written remarks and written directives.
At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report
of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC AJ.

When Complainant did not respond a hearing within the time frame provided in 29 C.F.R. §
1614.108(f), the Agency issued a final decision on Agency No. ATL-18-0230-SSA pursuant to 29
C.F.R. § 1614.110(b), on September 18, 2018. The decision concluded that Complainant failed to
prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged. Appeal No. 2019000587
followed.

In EEOC Appeal Nos. 0120182203 and 2019000587, the Commission affirmed the Agency’s final
decision finding no discrimination.

In her request for reconsideration, Complainant repeats the same arguments made during her
original appeal. We emphasize that a request for reconsideration is not a second appeal to the
Commission. See EEO MD-110, Ch. 9, § VII.A. Rather, a reconsideration request is an
opportunity to demonstrate that the appellate decision involved a clearly erroneous interpretation
of material fact or law, or will have a substantial impact on the policies, practices, or operations of
the Agency. Complainant has not done so here.

After reviewing the previous decision and the entire record, the Commission finds that the request
fails to meet the criteria of 29 C.F.R. § 1614.405(c), and it is the decision of the Commission to
deny the request. The decision in EEOC Appeal No. 0120182203 and 2019000587 remains the
Commission's decision. There is no further right of administrative appeal on the decision of the
Commission on this request.

                COMPLAINANT’S RIGHT TO FILE A CIVIL ACTION (P0610)

This decision of the Commission is final, and there is no further right of administrative appeal
from the Commission’s decision. You have the right to file a civil action in an appropriate United
States District Court within ninety (90) calendar days from the date that you receive this decision.
If you file a civil action, you must name as the defendant in the complaint the person who is the
official Agency head or department head, identifying that person by his or her full name and
official title. Failure to do so may result in the dismissal of your case in court. “Agency” or
“department” means the national organization, and not the local office, facility or department in
which you work.

                            RIGHT TO REQUEST COUNSEL (Z0815)

If you want to file a civil action but cannot pay the fees, costs, or security to do so, you may request
permission from the court to proceed with the civil action without paying these fees or costs.
Similarly, if you cannot afford an attorney to represent you in the civil action, you may request the
court to appoint an attorney for you. You must submit the requests for waiver of court costs or
appointment of an attorney directly to the court, not the Commission. The court has the sole
discretion to grant or deny these types of requests.
