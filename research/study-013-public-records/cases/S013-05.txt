Investigative record summary (background section of a published federal-sector EEO appellate decision; outcome text removed).

Complainant worked as a Human Resources (HR) Specialist, GS-0201-14, in the Agency’s HR Office at its Headquarters in Washington, D.C. On November 2, 2017, Complainant filed an EEO complaint in which she alleged that Agency discriminated against her on the bases of race (African-American), sex (female), and age (69) when: 1. Beginning on August 10, 2017, and continuing until her retirement in November 2017, S2 harassed Complainant by displaying antagonistic, rude, and abrupt behavior towards her, excluding her from important leadership meetings and communications, embarrassing her by criticizing her in front of her coworkers, and showing favoritism toward men, White women, and younger employees. At the conclusion of the ensuing investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the investigative report (IR) and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ). In accordance with Complainant’s request, the Agency issued a final decision pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b). The decision concluded that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged. Position Reclassification Complainant averred that from August 2015 until December 2016, she performed the duties of Chief of the Corporate Recruitment and Career Programs Division, and that when the position was abolished in January 2017 pursuant to a reorganization, she continued to perform those duties until she retired in November 2018. She claimed that while she served in this capacity, she planned, directed, administered and coordinated the Agency’s nationwide recruitment and outreach programs; managed a $4.7 million budget; and conducted mid-year and annual performance evaluations. IR 196-98, 211. According to Complainant's immediate supervisor at the time, a Supervisory HR Specialist (S1), the HR Headquarters Operations Branch was comprised of two divisions: Corporate Recruiting/Career Programs and Classification/Staffing. The Headquarters Operations Chief was a GS-15. The two division leads were GS-14s. Complainant was the lead for the Corporate Recruitment and Career Programs Division. IR 258-59. S2 averred that he did not take over as Acting Director until January 2017, and consequently, his predecessor would have had responsibility for supervising Complainant prior to 2017. IR 253. He further averred that he was a coworker of Complainant’s prior to being appointed as Acting Director and that his race was black. IR 814-15. Complainant alleged that she requested a position reclassification on April 18, 2017, June 22, 2017, and again on July 9, 2017. She further averred that she made the request through one of her coworkers (CW1) who was serving in an acting capacity, and that on August 10, 2017, CW1 informed her that S2 would not allow him to reclassify her position. IR 203, 212. CW1 stated that he never received a reclassification request from Complainant and that he was the one who had initiated the request for Complainant’s position description to both Complainant and S2 due to the fact that the duties of the position had changed. IR 273. CW1 affirmed that he, rather than Complainant had initiated a request that Complainant’s position description be rewritten in order to more accurately reflect the duties that she was currently performing. However, the position description was for the GS-14 level, not the GS-15 level. CW1 stated that he was not aware that the request for reclassification was for a GS-15 position. IR 272-74, 276, 282, 324, 326. S1 and S2 both averred that they could not recall Complainant ever performing GS-15-level work. IR 234-35, 258-59. S1 stated that Complainant declined the opportunity to act as a GS-15 as part of a rotation of three employees. IR 259. S1 also stated that he knew of no HR employees who had received a grade increase based upon the reclassification of a position description, and that on April 18, 2017, Complainant did submit a request to have her position description updated for her replacement when she retired in November 2017. IR 237, 259-61, 264-65. The Deputy Director of the Office of HR Management (DD), who was Complainant’s first-line supervisor until December 2016, also stated that there had been a GS-15 Chief of the Corporate Recruitment Career Programs Division until December 2016 but that the position had been abolished pursuant to a reorganization. She further noted that Complainant did not perform the duties of a GS-15 Division Chief. IR 286-88. Complainant maintained between December 2016 through August 2017, several individuals were automatically reclassified to higher-level positions based on an accretion of duties without the positions being advertised, and that she should have been compensated at the GS-15 level, noting that a white female coworker (CW2) and other coworkers were being paid GS-15 salaries. IR 198, 201, 203, 212. In addition to CW2, Complainant identified three other individuals whose positions S2 allegedly approved for reclassification. According to Complainant: a white female (CW3) was reclassified to a GS-13 HR Specialist position on December 25, 2016; a Hispanic female under 40 (CW4 ) was reclassified to a GS-13 HR Specialist position on December 26, 2018; and a white male (CW5) was reclassified to a GS-15 Management and Program Analyst position on August 20, 2017. IR 198. CW2 stated that she had been a GS-15 Supervisory HR Specialist since May 1, 2017, and that while she had worked in the same HR Office as Complainant, she was in a different division and had never been a team lead. IR 857-58. CW3 averred that Complainant was her team leader until Complainant retired in November 2017 and that she had never held a team leader position. IR 845-47. CW4 affirmed that she was Caucasian, that she was in a different division than Complainant, and that she had never held a team lead position. IR 827-28. CW5, a white male GS-15 Program & Management Analyst, averred that he had served as a team lead between October 2013 and April 2016 at Easter Federal Lands in Sterling, Virginia, and that while he was involved in performance evaluations for the three employees under him, he had no disciplinary authority over them. CW5 also declared that he was not promoted into his position; rather he applied and was selected for the job from his previous position. IR 839-41. Complainant identified other employees who she believed were promoted via reclassification, but none of these individuals were given such promotions or had otherwise served as team leaders, and none were promoted to GS-15. IR 797, 802-03, 808-09, 833-34, 851-52, 863-64, 869-70, 879-80, 889-90, 907-08. S2 affirmed that any requests for desk audits were management-initiated, that CW3 and CW4 were granted reclassifications, and that he did not recall any requests for reclassification to the GS-15 level. IR 237-38. In response to Complainant’s assertion that he had approved the reclassification of 12 other employees but not of any African-Americans, S2 averred that he was not the selecting official in any of those actions but had recommended or concurred on some of them. S2 pointed out that each HR action at the GS-14 or GS-15 level required concurrence by the Executive Director. IR 248-49. The DD confirmed that HR actions involving GS-14s and GS- 15s required approvals at levels higher than S2, usually the Associate Administrator, S2’s immediate supervisor (S3). IR 288-89. Harassment S2’s Behavior Towards Complainant Complainant averred that every time S2 interacted with her, he was rude, abrasive and abrupt. According to Complainant, S2 did not want to meet with her, hear her input, or provide her with any support. She also stated that S2 did not exhibit similar behavior with non-African-American or non-female subordinates. IR 205. She identified several incidents that occurred between November 2016 and March 2017 which she claimed typifies S2’s behavior toward her. Complainant averred that S2 and another colleague (CW6) verbally attacked her at a staff meeting held on November 17, 2016, causing her to feel “totally humiliated.”. IR 205-06. Next, Complainant claimed that S2 accused her failing to properly implement a program without first asking for background information at a program meeting held on December 22, 2016. IR 206. Third, Complainant alleged that S2 had asked her to prepare extensively for a meeting scheduled for February 9, 2017, but at that meeting, he only asked her three questions about one aspect of her program and did not ask about her other programs, projects, assignments, or staffing, causing her to waste considerable time and effort. She averred that this reflected a “bait and switch” tactic on the part of S2 in which he would announce the subject of a meeting ahead of time and switch topics when the meeting was actually convened. IR 206. Fourth, Complainant claimed that between February and March 2017, S2 failed to relay crucial information about a Summer intern program to S3, the Associate Administrator, for more than three months, which damaged her relationship with S3. IR 205-07. S2 affirmed that Complainant’s accusations were false and without merit. He pointed out that as the acting director, he had minimal personal interactions with Complainant. But he did recall an incident that occurred in May or June 2017 in which he questioned Complainant about a sole source acquisition request where the HR Office had been audited and investigated due to questionable practices surrounding acquisition transactions. IR 238. S1 averred that at a meeting with Complainant held on May 10, 2017, Complainant shared that she had a “history” with S2. S1 also averred that after another meeting, Complainant shared with him that she felt frustrated that she had prepared materials for specific topics that S2 requested when the topics actually discussed at the meeting turned out to be different, and that her efforts were waisted as a result. IR 262. The DD averred she once spoke with Complainant about her work situation and was aware that Complainant was not happy in that situation, but that Complainant never told her that she was being harassed by S2. IR 290. Exclusions from Meetings & Communications Complainant initially claimed that on January 20, 2017, S2 excluded her from email lists for HR leadership that she had previously been on and had not given her a reason for having her removed from those lists. IR 208. CW1 stated that Complainant was excluded from leadership meetings and communications on a regular basis after the DD departed from the Agency but could not recall specific details. IR 275. Next, Complainant alleged that on April 4, 2017, she was excluded from a developmental and rotational selection meeting, and that this had a direct impact on her ability to do her job since several of her employees had applied for rotational and developmental assignments. She stated that as a result of the exclusion, she had to adjust her staff’s workload, reassign and reprioritize projects, and adjust to new work schedules in order to continue to function as a high-performing team. IR 214. Third, Complainant claimed that on May 3, 2017, S2 excluded her from HR email updates at the Administration and Department levels, which hindered her ability to keep her staff informed of what was going on and created additional steps in the process, delaying internship conversions and having budgetary consequences. IR 208-09, 214-15. Fourth, Complainant averred that on May 16, 2017, July 7, 2017, and August 4, 2017, she made requests to be included on the vacancy approved list, and that on all three occasions, S2 failed to respond. She stated that S2’s failure to respond to her request made it much more difficult to fill the 23 vacancies for the Professional Development Program. IR 215. Fifth, Complainant claimed that on May 17, 2017, S2 excluded her from a meeting on relocation that was critical for her to attend, given her program and budgetary responsibilities. IR 209. Sixth, Complainant alleged that on July 18, 2017, S2 failed to inform her that the Professional Development Program’s ceiling had been lowered, which necessitated a revaluation of which division offices would sponsor participants and adversely affected the conversion of interns to full-time positions. IR 215. In addition, Complaint averred that on November 1, 2017, S2 excluded her from an email regarding internship conversions, S2 did not give her a list of proposed positions that needed approval at the department level, which continued to make it difficult to fill the 23 Professional Development Program vacancies that were still pending, and that S2 failed to initiate a discussion with her regarding her performance plan. IR 215-216. S2 responded that Complainant and other team leaders regularly attended the weekly HR leadership team meetings to ensure that they were receiving important program information. IR 239. Regarding the January 20, 2017 incident, S2 averred that he could not recall the incident and pointed out that Complainant was not a direct report since she reported to S1, and that he, S2, did not manage the email distribution list. IR 240. Concerning the April 4, incident, S2 affirmed that the purpose of that meeting was to discuss developmental and rotational selection within the HR Office, and that he did not recall the Corporate Recruiting team sponsoring any rotational assignments. IR 241. With respect to the vacancy approval list, S2 averred that he could not recall the incidents nor was he able to locate any email messages from Complainant requesting to be included on the vacancy approval list. IR 241-42. As to the May 3 incident, S2 referenced an email chain between himself, S1, and the Acting Executive Director seeking concurrence for a permanent placement of a Professional Development Program participant. IR 240. With regard to the July 18, 2017, S2 responded that he had represented S3 at a leadership meeting during which the proposed ceiling numbers were discussed, and that he was directed not to disclose the information about the ceiling because the proposal had not been finalized. IR 242. Regarding the November 1, 2017 incident, S2 stated that the filling of those positions did not fall within the purview of the Corporate Recruitment team. IR 242. S1 affirmed that although he could not recall these incidents, it was his understanding that group email distributions across the HR Office would be limited to division chiefs, including himself, with further dissemination as appropriate. IR 262- 63. Favoritism & Other Evidence of Discriminatory Motivation When asked by the EEO investigator in Questions (14) and (35) of her affidavit why she believed that her race, sex, and/or age were factors in how she was treated, Complainant answered as follows: A14: S2 has a history of discriminating against African-American females and males in the Office of Human Resources, Headquarters, and the Atlanta field office. He also has a history of being hostile and antagonistic towards me personally. He has regularly been rude and abrupt in our interactions and had taken steps to undermine my authority while I served as chief. This includes excluding me from important leadership meetings, denying me information needed to perform my duties, unfairly criticizing me in front of my coworkers which undermined my authority and caused me tremendous embarrassment. In contrast, S2 constantly showed favoritism toward males, white females, and younger employees. This favoritism and bias are most readily demonstrated by the many reclassifications S2 authorized for non-African American, non-female employees. *** A35: S2 has treated me differently than he treated his non-African-American, non- female and younger staff members, including those similarly situated to me. He also treated non-African-American, non-female, and younger staff members differently with respect to promotions and reclassifications. Based on his actions with respect to my performance of GS-15 level duties, how long he left me in that role, and how he responded to my requests for reclassification, it is evident that he was satisfied using my skills and expertise to perform GS-15 duties without giving me a corresponding promotion to the GS-15 supervisory level. He did not want an older African American female officially in a position of power, supervision, or authority. *** On multiple occasions, S2 also openly expressed his biases against African Americans. On at least one occasion approximately two years ago, S2 stated to me that he was not happy with the Atlanta office and that the Atlanta office was “too black,” and if it were up to him, he would shut the office down. He also told me that he no longer wanted to recruit from Historically Black Colleges and Universities. On another occasion, S2 told the Former Classification and Staffing Branch (CCSB) Chief, an African-American woman that he did not want to hire anyone who looked like her, referring to her race. IR 203-04, 213. The CCSB, who left the Agency in May 2016, affirmed that S2 did not treat black and white employees equally, and that S2 followed a practice that the CCSB referred to as the “vanilla-chocolate swirl promotion rule” under which a black employee would not be promoted unless a white employee was promoted at approximately the same time. The CCSB stated that this had happened to her when she was promoted to GS-14 in 2010. She stated that she could not be promoted until a white coworker was promoted to GS-14. She also stated that this practice had continued until she left the Agency in 2016. In addition, the CCSB averred that she was prevented from promoting a black female currently in a career ladder position from GS-12 to GS-13. IR 298- 99, 302. Beyond the issue of promotions, the CCSB averred that S2 would tell her during one-on- one meetings that the black employees were incompetent and could not write or communicate properly. IR 302. In her affidavit, the CCSB stated: Q17: Complainant alleges S1 told you not to hire anyone who looked like you. Is this accurate? I was told behind closed doors and without any witnesses that I could not fill a position in my branch that looked like me. You can interview them but we won’t hire them. A18: I was not an [Agency] employee at that time and I was not aware [S2] was hostile, antagonistic, rude and abrupt towards Complainant after May 29, 2016. However, before I left in 2016 and while at [the Agency], I was aware of his dislike for Complainant. S2 would not include her in meetings for HR issues that would affect both of our teams such as action processing, coverage, and he would make unkind or unprofessional comments overtures (I can’t stand her, she annoys me). I witnessed that he would use a short and abrupt tone with her – give short or curt answers, close the door, ask me to stay after a meeting and tell her that she could go now – in a very uncomfortable way – not exactly friendly. A19: S2 has a soft spot for white women in lower level positions. He is quick to meet with and provide opportunities, details, promotions, pay for college classes for white women. Additionally, he prefers to hire white males in leadership positions and will promote upon entry for these employees but not for blacks; however, if he finds out that his new white employee or hire is married to or dating a black person, that white employee quickly falls out of favor with him.
