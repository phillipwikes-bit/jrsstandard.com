Investigative record summary (background section of a published federal-sector EEO appellate decision; outcome text removed).

At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant, 59 (YOB 1962) worked as a Human Resources (HR) Generalist at the Agency’s Bellingham Customer Care Center in Troy, Michigan. Report of Investigation (ROI) at 70 and 92. According to Complainant, she had been detailed into her position for ten months prior to her selection, which became effective July 17, 2021. She stated that when she was offered a five percent (5%) salary increase, she told the HR Business Partner (Business Partner [age 58]) that she was eligible for an 8% increase. ROI at 71. Complainant initiated EEO contact on August 31, 2021. On November 17, 2021, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against her on the bases of age (59) and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 the Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967 when on or about July 17, 2021, Complainant was awarded a 5% salary increase when selected for an HR Generalist position, while a peer selected for the same position was awarded an 8% salary increase. The basis of Complainant’s reprisal allegation is the instant complaint. ROI at 72. The Agency conducted an investigation into the complaint. The investigation revealed that Complainant’s coworker (Coworker [YOB: 1975]) was 46 at the time of her promotion effective on July 17, 2021. ROI at 98. Both Complainant and Coworker were selected and promoted to the position of HR Generalist HQ position, occupation code 0201-0140, on the Customer Care Team. ROI at 35, 92, and 98. An email was shared with Complainant that included the Form 50s of Coworker and Complainant evidencing their promotions to the position of HR Generalist. See ROI at 33-8. The email revealed that Coworker received an 8% raise, while Complainant only received a 5% raise. On June 13, 2015, the Postal Service issued a policy that governs promotional pay increases. That policy provides, “promotional pay increases will be determined based on 2 factors: distance from positions maximum salary, and the number of grades being promoted.” ROI at 86. The Postal Service’s policy does not provide for any other justification for promotional pay increases. The policy also contains an explanatory attachment that sets forth what promotional pay raise a candidate can receive under postal policy as follows: Promotional pay increase policy determined based on the following two (2) factors: a. Distance from maximum salary level of new grade (Employees will receive a minimum promotional salary increase of 3%, unless the increase takes their salary above the maximum of the new grade): • >20% = 3% up to 10% increase (inc) • 10%-20% = 3% up to 8% inc • < 10% = 3% up to 5% inc b. And, total number of grade levels being promoted (if applicable): • If 3 grades = 3% inc • If 4 or 5 grades = 5% inc • If 6+ grades = 8% inc. ROI at 87. Complainant’s Form 50 provides in the remark section “salary increase process under pay promotion policy effective 6/13/15. Level 19 promoted to level 21. Present increase 5% applied to current salary of $90,868.” Complainant’s Appeal Brief at 21. Coworker’s Form 50 contains the following statement in the remark section “salary increase process and promotional policy effective 6/13/15. Level 19 promoted to level 21. Percentage increase of 8% applied to current salary of $86,337.” Complainant’s Appeal Brief at 20. As both Complainant and Coworker’s pre-promotion salaries were between 10% to 20% of the top of the pay grade for EAS-21, and they fell within the 3% to 8% range for raise upon being promoted from their EAS-19 positions to the EAS-21 position of HR Generalist. ROI at 86-7 and Complainant’s Appeal Brief at 18. Complainant’s and Coworker’s respective ages were readily known to the Responsible Management Officials as it was reflected on their Form 50s. When Complainant was notified that she received the promotion, she was told that she would receive a 5% raise. According to Complainant, Business Partner told Complainant that she did not have a budget for an 8% raise and that she would have to get approval from a named individual. Business Partner also allegedly stated that Complainant did not have enough experience. ROI at 71. However, Complainant stated that she had been doing the job fully and successfully, she possessed a bachelor’s degree in business administration and had three years’ experience at the Troy Customer Care Center. Thus, she had higher education, more human resource experience, and more experience in the Customer Care Center than Coworker. ROI at 71; also compare ROI at 93-7 (Complainant’s resume) to ROI at 100-04 (Coworker’s resume). According to Complainant, Coworker’s education was limited to a high school certificate in cosmetology and licensing in cosmetology. ROI at 103. Complainant asserted that she worked in Human Resources for 32 months as a labor relations specialist from July 3, 2004 – March 30, 2007 as part of a detail assignment. ROI at 95. Complainant also asserted that the amount of training the respective candidates received while in detail reflected their readiness to assume the position. According to her, Coworker received more training than Complainant when she was detailed into the position of HR Generalist. Coworker allegedly received 1 hour on and 1 hour off the clock 1 on 1 training by an HR Generalist for 30 days when she was detailed into the position for a total of 60 hours of training. Complainant’s 1 on 1 training added up to less than 2 hours. ROI at 72. According to Business Partner, although she initially requested a 5% salary increase for Coworker, her manager (Manager), approved an 8% increase based on Coworker’s HR knowledge and experience. ROI at 77, 79, 92, and 100. Business Partner stated that she also offered a 5% salary increase to the last person she selected for the position of HR Generalist (YOB: 1976) in 2019, some two years prior. ROI at 78-9 and 83. At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). When Complainant did not request a hearing within the time frame provided in 29 C.F.R. § 1614.108(f), the Agency issued a final decision (FAD) pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b). The decision concluded that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged.
