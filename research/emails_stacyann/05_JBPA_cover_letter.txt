Dear Editors,

We submit "Documentation Quality in 32 Public-Records Determinations: A Two-State Case Study" for consideration as a Research Article.

The study examines whether the documented basis of a public-records determination can be independently reconstructed from the record itself. The corpus is 32 publicly available United States determinations and audits from 32 distinct sources, across four document classes and two states, issued between 2005 and 2026. Each case was assessed from the source, with a contemporaneous note recording the reason, before the documented outcome was consulted.

In the five cases where a State or City Comptroller had independently audited the same programme, the record-level assessment and the auditor's own finding agreed in all five. Assessments differentiated document classes according to how far each source exposed the underlying basis. They were not associated with appellate outcome, and we report that null directly, because reconstructability and appellate disposition appear to measure different properties.

We note your symposium on null results. This paper reports a null, a wide confidence interval on a ten-case blind second read, and a secondary pre-registered criterion that was not met, all stated plainly in the text. We would rather submit it to a journal that treats those as findings.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all data are publicly available with citations given for every case.

Sincerely,

Stacyann Young and Phillip Wikes
