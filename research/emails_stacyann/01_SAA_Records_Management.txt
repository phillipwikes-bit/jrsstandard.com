Subject: Case study enquiry: reconstructing the basis of public-records determinations

Dear Records Management Section,

I am writing to ask whether the following would suit the Records Management case study series.

We have a 32-case study of United States public-records determinations, drawn from 32 distinct public sources across four document classes and two states, covering decisions issued between 2005 and 2026. It asks a records management question directly: when a consequential determination is reviewed later, does the record itself preserve enough of its evidentiary basis and reasoning for an independent reviewer to reconstruct how the organisation acted?

The headline result is external. In the five cases where a State or City Comptroller had independently audited the same programme, our record-level assessment and the auditor's own finding agreed in all five that the programme could not adequately evidence its public-records administration. The assessment also separated document classes according to how far each source exposed the underlying basis, and it was not associated with appellate outcome, which distinguishes documentation quality from legal result.

The material is all publicly available and each case carries a public citation. The manuscript would be prepared to your 5,000-word limit.

Would the subject be appropriate for the series?

Sincerely,

Stacyann Young and Phillip Wikes
