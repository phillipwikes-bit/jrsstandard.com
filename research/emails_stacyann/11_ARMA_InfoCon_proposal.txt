Subject: Session proposal: can your records explain the decisions they document?

Dear Programme Committee,

I would like to propose a 60-minute session and would welcome a view on whether it fits the programme.

TITLE
When the Record Cannot Explain the Decision: Evidence from 32 Public-Records Cases

THE PROBLEM THE SESSION ADDRESSES
A record can be retained, accessible, complete and professionally written, and still fail the person who has to review it later, because the evidence behind the conclusion is not on the page. Retention schedules do not catch this. Neither does a completeness check.

WHAT THE SESSION PRESENTS
We assessed 32 United States public-records determinations from 32 distinct sources, four document classes and two states, issued between 2005 and 2026, asking a single question: could an independent reviewer rebuild the basis for the decision from the record alone?

In the five cases where a State or City Comptroller had independently audited the same programme, our record-level assessment and the auditor's own finding agreed in all five. A structured read anticipated what a government auditor concluded by a different method.

WHAT ATTENDEES TAKE AWAY
A five-point check that can be applied to a consequential record before it is finalised, guidance on where to place it in an existing review workflow, and the honest limits of what a pilot of this size establishes.
