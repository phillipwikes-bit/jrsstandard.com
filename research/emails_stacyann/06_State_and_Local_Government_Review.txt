Subject: Presubmission enquiry: documentation quality in state and local public-records determinations

Dear Professor Scicchitano,

I am writing to ask whether the following would fall within the scope of State and Local Government Review.

We have a 32-case study of state and local public-records determinations in New York and Connecticut, drawn from 32 distinct public sources across four document classes and covering decisions issued between 2005 and 2026. It asks whether the documented basis of a determination can be independently reconstructed from the record itself once the original decision-maker is no longer available.

The result most relevant to your readership is a convergence with state oversight. In the five cases where the New York State Comptroller or the New York City Comptroller had independently audited the same programme, our record-level assessment and the auditor's own finding agreed in all five that the programme could not adequately evidence its public-records administration. The assessment also differentiated document classes by how far each exposed the underlying basis, and was not associated with appellate outcome, which separates documentation quality from legal disposition.

The sources are New York appellate and trial decisions, New York Committee on Open Government advisory opinions, Connecticut Freedom of Information Commission final decisions, and Comptroller compliance audits. All are publicly available and every case carries a citation.

The manuscript is approximately 5,600 words.

Would this be appropriate for consideration?

Sincerely,

Stacyann Young and Phillip Wikes
