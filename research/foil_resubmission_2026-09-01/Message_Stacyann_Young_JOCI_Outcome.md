# Message to Stacyann Young: JOCI outcome and where the paper goes next

*Draft for Phillip to send. She is first author; the decision below is hers to make with him, not his to report to her.*

---

Stacyann,

The Journal of Civic Information declined our paper this morning. It was a desk decision by the editor, David Cuillier, about two and a half hours after submission. His reason in full: he reviewed the manuscript and determined it is not a good fit for the journal, and he wished us well in finding a suitable one.

I want to be precise about what that does and does not mean, because the speed of it can read worse than it is.

No reviewer was assigned. Nobody criticised the work. The 32 cases you selected and screened, the contemporaneous basis notes you recorded blind to the outcome, the agreement with the independent audits, the construct and discriminant analyses and the blind second read were not evaluated at all. This was a scope screen. There is nothing in it to fix, and I do not think we should change the paper in response to it.

What it does tell us is how the paper reads to an editor: he saw a documentation-quality instrument being tested through public-records cases, and judged the instrument to be the centre. That is useful information about where to send it next.

The suggestion I have been given is to go to the Records Management Journal next, then Information Polity, then Archives and Records, with Government Information Quarterly only after a heavier reframing. The reasoning is that our actual question, whether an independent reviewer can rebuild the basis for a decision from the record alone, sits closer to records and information governance than to civic information. I think that reasoning is sound.

Two things I am not going to decide on my own.

The first is the journal. You are first author and you lead the public-records framing, so the target is yours to pick as much as mine.

The second is the title. The proposal is to retitle it "Decision Reconstruction Risk in Public Records: A Documentation Quality Review of 32 Cases". I have reservations and I would rather say them than quietly go along. It leads with my construct and puts your empirical contribution second, which inverts our byline. It also leads with a construct that our companion paper explicitly says this work does not validate, which I think invites a question we do not need to invite. The current title claims less and stands on its own evidence. But it is your call as much as mine, and if you think the construct framing gets it read, say so.

One practical note: I have not verified the Records Management Journal's actual submission requirements yet, because the publisher's site blocks automated access. Before we build anything I will read their aims and scope, article types, abstract format and reference style directly. We have already been caught twice this week by assuming a journal's requirements instead of reading them.

Nothing is being changed until you weigh in.

Phillip
