# The JRS Organizational Pilot Package: designation messages (2026-08-04)

Generic, ready-to-send messages offering a named professional designation plus twenty five free record checks. Three audiences: organizations that want a pilot, training completers, and selected research panel experts.

---

## Recommended title, and why

**Use `JRS Lead Diagnostic Specialist` as the default.**

Reasoning against the five options you listed:

| Title | Verdict |
|---|---|
| JRS Lead Practice Architect | Reads as internal consulting jargon. "Architect" is heavily used and carries no institutional weight outside software. |
| JRS Master Practitioner | Clean and short, but "Master Practitioner" is the standard vocabulary of coaching and NLP certification mills. It will hurt you with the audit and legal audience. |
| JRS Senior Governance Fellow and Risk Strategist | Two titles joined by "and" reads as invented. "Fellow" also implies an appointment by an institution with a membership body, which invites a question you cannot answer yet. |
| JRS Senior Practitioner for Global Accountability and Procedural Precision | Far too long. Nobody will put this on a LinkedIn headline, which is the whole point of offering it. |
| **JRS Lead Diagnostic Specialist** | **Best.** Short enough for a headline, "Diagnostic" describes what the person actually did, "Lead" signals they ran it rather than attended it, and "Specialist" claims competence without claiming rank the programme cannot confer. |

Two practical notes on conversion. Short titles convert better because the recipient can picture it on their profile immediately. And a title tied to an action they performed (`ran a diagnostic on twenty five records`) survives scrutiny, where a title tied to nothing does not, which matters for exactly the compliance and audit people you are targeting.

**Suggested ladder, so the designation means something:**

| Records run | Designation |
|---|---|
| 1 to 4 | JRS Field Specialist |
| 5 to 19 | JRS Lead Diagnostic Specialist |
| 20 or more | JRS Lead Diagnostic Specialist, eligible for named co-authorship |

**Package name: the JRS Organizational Pilot Package.** Plain, describes what it is, and works in a forwarded email to a manager.

**On record count:** twenty five is the right number and is now live on the pilot page. It is enough for a pattern to be visible in the results, small enough to finish in one sitting, and it sets the twenty record co-authorship threshold as a reachable target rather than a distant one.

## Message 1: to an organization contact who wants a pilot

Subject: The JRS Organizational Pilot Package, twenty five records, no cost

Dear [Name],

Your record text is never stored. I want that to be the first thing you read, because it is usually the first question.

I am inviting a small number of organizations to run the Justification Review Standard on their own records, and I would like [Organization] to be one of them.

Here is what it is. You paste up to twenty five of your own records, redacted, one at a time. Each is assessed against five conditions that together test whether the basis for the decision can be reconstructed from the record itself, by someone who was not there. You get the read on each record immediately, on screen. Each record is assessed in memory and discarded. Nothing you paste is written to any database and none of it is used to train anything. Only the count and the pattern of results is kept.

It takes about half an hour. You can stop after one.

What you get:

- **An executive summary, one page, written for internal leadership.** How your records routed, where the five conditions held and where they did not, the share of the reasoning that could not be reconstructed, and three specific steps that follow from your result. It downloads at the end and contains no record text.

- **A personal reference from me whenever you need one**, for a role, a client, a board, or a credential. Tell me what you want it to speak to.

If you run twenty or more records, there is one further offer. That is subject to the ordinary conditions of earned authorship: you would contribute to the writing, review the manuscript, and approve it before submission. It is a real offer, not a courtesy line.

You choose what is public. Nothing names your organization unless you tell me it may.

## What you need to know before sending

**The co-authorship offer creates a real obligation.** If several people run twenty records, you owe several papers, or you owe a single multi-site paper with several co-authors, which is the better outcome and worth planning for now. The honest version of the offer is the one written above: authorship is earned by contributing to the writing, which is also your existing authorship policy.

**The designation is yours to grant.** It is not accredited by anyone, and nobody is claiming otherwise in the wording above. Keep it that way. The moment the copy implies external accreditation, the audit and legal audience stops trusting the rest of it.

**Consent is captured by the pilot page itself**, so these messages do not need to ask for it. The page requires contact consent, and asks the transfer and public-naming questions as forced choices before any record is run.
