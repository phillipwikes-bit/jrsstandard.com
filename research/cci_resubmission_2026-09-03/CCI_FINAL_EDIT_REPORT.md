## A. Changes made

Six. Each is a full-sentence replacement; nothing was altered for variety alone.

**1. Paragraph 6, Decision Reconstruction Risk.**
"In employment matters **that gap matters** because..." → "In employment matters **this is consequential** because..."
The paragraph opened "We call that gap Decision Reconstruction Risk" and then used "that gap" again eight words later.

**2. Paragraph 8, McDonnell Douglas.**
"...established a burden-shifting framework **under which, where that framework applies,** an employer may articulate..." → "...established a burden-shifting framework. **Where it applies,** an employer may articulate..."
The original stacked two forms of the same qualifier. The split keeps the legal restraint, which is the reason the qualifier is there, and removes the stumble. The hyperlink on the case name is untouched.

**3. Paragraph 10, the illustrative file.**
"**The underlying history may include** emails, attendance records..." → "**Consider a file where the underlying history includes** emails, attendance records..."
The paragraph previously opened on a definite noun phrase with no antecedent, so the reader met "the underlying history" of a file that had not been introduced.

**4. Paragraph 46, JRS.**
"...**runs inside** existing HR, compliance, investigations, audit and legal **workflows and asks** whether..." → "...**applies within** existing HR, compliance, investigations, audit and legal **review processes. It asks** whether..."
"Runs inside ... workflows" reads as a software capability claim. "Applies within ... review processes" is accurate for a review discipline and is not promotional. The split also breaks a 38-word sentence.

**5. Paragraph 49, closing section.**
"...is not whether AI should write employment records. **It is whether** the organization has a control..." → "...is not whether AI should write employment records **but whether** the organization has a control..."

**6. Paragraph 51, closing section.**
"...the issue **may no longer be** an individual employee's wording. **It may be whether** the organization's records..." → "...the **question may shift from** an individual employee's wording **to whether** the organization's records..."

Changes 5 and 6 address one problem. The article used the same two-sentence "not X. It is Y" shape three times: paragraphs 5, 49 and 51. The construction is doing real work in paragraph 5, which is the strongest of the three and was kept. Two of three uses within four paragraphs of each other read as a tic.
