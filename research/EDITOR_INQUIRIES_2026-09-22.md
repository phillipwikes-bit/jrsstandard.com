## Part 1. The retitled abstract

### Title options, first author's choice

1. **Documentation Quality in 32 Public-Records Determinations: A Two-State Case Study**
2. **When the Record Cannot Explain the Decision: 32 Public-Records Determinations in New York and Connecticut**
3. **Can the Basis Be Rebuilt? A Case Study of 32 Public-Records Determinations**

Option 1 is the least likely to be filed as an instrument paper, which is what both prior
editors did. Option 3 stays closest to the current title.

### Abstract, reframed

The change is order, not content. The corpus and the audit convergence lead; the protocol
moves to Methods. Nothing was added and no figure moved.

> This study examines 32 publicly available public-records determinations and audits drawn
> from 32 distinct sources, issued between 2005 and 2026 across four document classes and
> two United States jurisdictions. Each case was assessed for whether an independent
> reviewer could reconstruct the documented basis of the determination from the record
> alone. The assessment and a short note giving its reasons were recorded from the source
> before the documented outcome was consulted.
>
> Five cases were programmes that a State or City Comptroller had independently audited. In
> all five, the record-level assessment and the auditor's own finding agreed that the
> programme could not adequately evidence its public-records administration. Assessments
> also differed across document classes according to how far each source exposed the
> underlying basis, and were not associated with appellate outcome, which is consistent
> with reconstructability and appellate disposition being different properties.
>
> The study is a pilot. The corpus is small, non-random and limited to two jurisdictions.
> Twenty-two of the 32 cases carry a single reviewer, and a blind second read of 10 cases
> returned 70 percent agreement. The study does not establish that applying the review
> improves documentation, reduces adverse findings, or performs equivalently outside the
> jurisdictions and document classes sampled.

stated scope, and revealed scope is what has rejected this paper twice.

The manuscript is approximately 5,780. It would need a genuine cut, not a trim.

## Part 3. Why every inquiry carries the same two disclosures

**The competing interest.** One author developed the protocol under evaluation and ran the
analyses. An editor who finds that at submission weighs it far more heavily than one told at
the outset. It is also answerable: the audit convergence and the blind second read are the
parts that do not rest on the protocol author's judgement, and the inquiry says so in the
same breath.

**The preprint question.** One sentence, asked four times, settles the Zenodo decision
without guessing. It costs nothing to ask and it removes a risk that cannot be undone later.
