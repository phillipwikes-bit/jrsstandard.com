```
Hi Tanvi,

Two things, and the second one is genuinely your call rather than mine.

First: thank you for offering to add more cases. You do not need to. The pilot closed at
22 on 29 July and 22 is enough. Adding cases now, after the analysis is written, would
weaken the paper rather than strengthen it, because a reviewer cannot tell a corpus that

stop where you are with my thanks. Nothing is outstanding from you on the case set.

Second, the paper has changed shape, and for the better.

Your 22 employment matters are now analysed alongside Stacyann Young's 32 public-records
determinations as a single study of 54 records. The two of you built your corpora
independently, in different domains, on different jurisdictions, without conferring, and
that independence turns out to be the strongest thing about the paper. Your set showed a
clear association between an incomplete read and an adverse finding. Hers did not, and she
had already explained why: published freedom-of-information cases reach publication because
a legal question was live, so the outcomes in her corpus are not a fair test.

When the two are pooled, the association holds across 54 records, and a formal test shows
your result and hers do not actually differ from each other in a statistically meaningful
way. Your set had the cleaner base rate and the statistical power; hers had the reference
material. Neither paper could have shown that alone.

Now the two decisions.

Authorship order. I have you as second author, after Stacyann and before Ubayet Hossain,
with me last as senior author. Stacyann is first because her corpus is larger and carries
three of the paper's four evidence lines. Your set carries the fourth, which is the
outcome association, and it is the result the paper leads on in its abstract. If you think
that order misreads the contribution, say so and we will discuss it properly. I would
rather have that conversation now than after submission.

The limitation, which I want you to see before anyone else does. The paper states plainly
that you recorded both the reads and the outcomes for your cases, and that this means the
reads were not independent of the person assigning the outcome. That is a real weakness in
the design and it is mine, not yours: I set the protocol up that way. It is disclosed in the
methods section, again in the limitations, and the fix is written into the design for the
next study, which puts reads and outcomes in different hands.

I am telling you directly because it sits close to your name on a paper you are second
author of, and you should not discover it in a reviewer's comments. If you would rather not

argument.

Your written observations on what you noticed across these cases, the documentation
features that tended to come before a challenge, are still very welcome and would go in the
discussion in your own voice. They are not required for submission.

Thank you for the care you put into this. Twenty-two adjudicated matters read one at a time
```
