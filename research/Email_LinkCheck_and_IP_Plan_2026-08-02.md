The email says support takes **"one click... only a second, commits you to nothing."**

That was true before today. It is not true now. Support now requires a short form: name, organization, title, email, and a consent box. That change is the entire point, but the sentence promising a one-click action will read as a bait-and-switch to anyone who clicks expecting one.

## Part 3: is the current plan the best way to increase IP value?

**Short answer: the plan is reasonable but it is optimizing the wrong axis, and one change to this exact email would fix the biggest gap.**

### What the plan currently is
Submit as many articles as possible before the end of August, then increase IP value, then sell.

### Why that is not the highest-value sequence

Your own action plan already states the conclusion: *the jump in sale probability comes from the demand-side gates, not from more artifacts.* Articles are artifacts. Going from one published paper to four raises the price a buyer might pay, but it barely moves the probability that any buyer appears, because the three things a buyer's counsel actually checks are still open:

1. **Trademarks unregistered.** JRS and DRR are common-law only.
2. **Zero organizations using it.** No customer evidence of any kind.
3. **Contributor rights unassigned.** Twenty-plus reviewers, no signed assignment.

None of those is fixed by publishing.

### The four moves that actually move value, ranked by return per effort

**1. File the trademark applications. Today, not after the articles.**
Cost is a few hundred dollars per class at the USPTO and you can file yourself. This is the single highest return available and it has been outstanding since July. A buyer acquiring JRS is principally acquiring the category name; unregistered, that is a weak thing to convey. A pending application is itself a listed asset and takes minutes to initiate. Registration takes 8 to 12 months, which is precisely why filing cannot wait until after a sale conversation starts.

**2. Fold an IP assignment request into the emails already going out.**
You have an open, warm channel to every reviewer right now, for the name and title verification and the results summary. Adding a short assignment consent to that same message costs one paragraph and closes Gate 3 while people are still engaged and feel good about the work. Once the study closes and everyone disperses, chasing twenty signatures cold is a different and much worse job.

**3. Change what this email asks for.**
This is the most valuable observation in this document.

Your email is going to senior governance leaders and it asks them for **an endorsement**. An endorsement is a click. It has almost no diligence value: a buyer cannot bank it, cannot verify commitment behind it, and will not price it.

The same email, to the same people, could ask for **a pilot**. One paragraph, something like:

> If this is useful to your team, I would welcome the chance to run it on a small set of your records, five to ten, at no cost. You would get a written read on whether those records hold up, and I would get the field evidence the standard needs.

You already have pilot.html and enterprise.html built for exactly this. One organization saying yes, even informally, even a five-record pilot with a short letter confirming it happened, is worth more in a diligence conversation than all four articles combined, because it converts "a framework with papers" into "a framework someone used."

You are already spending the outreach. The ask is the only thing that needs to change.

**4. Keep publishing, but as the credential rather than the strategy.**
The articles are worth finishing. They make you citable, they support the price, and they hold independent value for you regardless of whether a sale ever happens. Just do not expect them to produce a buyer.

### What the registration gate deployed today contributes

It stops the leak. From now on every guide download and every supporter becomes a named, consented, transferable record rather than an anonymous tick. That accrues quietly and requires nothing further from you. It does not by itself create demand.

### The honest ordering

1. File the marks this week.
2. Add the assignment request to the emails already scheduled.
3. Change the ask in this email from endorsement to pilot, and send it to the same list.
4. Finish and submit the articles.
5. Then open buyer conversations.

Steps 1 through 3 cost a few hours in total and address all three open gates. Step 4, as currently planned, addresses none of them.
