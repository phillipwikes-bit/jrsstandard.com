## 2. The direct answer on impact

**The downloads and the support clicks carry very little weight in a sale. The enrollments carry nearly all of it, and they are the number you should be quoting.**

**Why 56 downloads counts for little.** A download is anonymous. It proves a file transfer occurred, not that a professional read it, applied it, or works anywhere in particular. A buyer's diligence cannot verify it, cannot contact those people, and cannot receive them in an assignment. Fifty-six is also a small number in absolute terms, so even at face value it does not demonstrate traction.

**Why 39 support clicks counts for even less.** A one-click endorsement costs the giver nothing and is therefore worth close to nothing as evidence. The exception is the 2 named public supporters, who are genuinely worth more than the 37 anonymous ones combined, because a name can be verified and cited.

**Why the 7 enrollments are the real asset.** Every one of them is a named person, from a named organization, who completed the full training and consented to having their record transferred. That is the only line in your entire dashboard that is verifiable, contactable, and legally conveyable. Seven is a small number. Seven real is worth more in diligence than fifty-six anonymous, because a buyer can do something with it.

The 100 percent completion rate is a genuine signal in its own right. Free training normally sees completion in the single digits to low tens of percent. Seven of seven finishing says the material holds attention. Small sample, but a real finding and one you can state honestly.

**One caution.** The "5 organizations" figure means five organizations are represented among your enrollees. It does not mean five organizations have adopted JRS. Do not let those two claims blur in a buyer conversation, because a diligence question would expose it immediately and cost you credibility on everything else.

## 3. Should you stop giving it away free?

**No paywall. Put up a registration wall instead, and do it now rather than after the study closes.**

**Why charging is the wrong move at this scale.** You are seeing about 3.5 downloads a day. Introducing a price typically cuts free-to-paid conversion by 90 percent or more. At a plausible price you would be looking at a handful of sales a week, which is trivial revenue, while destroying the distribution that is currently building your citation base and your international framing. Paid products also create obligations you are winding down from: support requests, refunds, payment processing, and tax handling.

**The actual problem is not that the guides are free. It is that 56 downloads produced zero captured identities.** Your training already does this correctly. It captures name, organization, contact consent, and transfer consent, which is why those 7 records are worth something and the 56 are not. The guides do not.

**The fix:** gate the Investigator Field Guides behind the same short registration the training uses, capturing name, organization, and the transfer consent tier. Keep the guides free. Change what you receive in exchange from nothing to a consented contact record.

**The cost, stated honestly:** you will lose downloads. A registration step commonly cuts completion by a third to a half. You would trade roughly 3.5 anonymous downloads a day for perhaps 2 named ones. Given that you are trying to build a transferable asset in about four weeks, that trade strongly favours capture over reach.

**Timing.** Do this now, not after the study closes. Your traffic is currently driven by article submissions and the LinkedIn campaign, which is the highest-volume window you are likely to get. Gating after that window means gating an empty channel.

## 4. What I would change on the page and the funnel

1. **Gate the three Investigator Field Guides** behind the existing enrollment form, or a shortened version of it. Reuse the consent tiers already built, since they work.
2. **Add the transfer-consent tier to the guide form**, matching the training. This is what makes the resulting list conveyable rather than merely a mailing list.
3. **Convert anonymous supporters into named ones.** 37 of your 39 supporters are anonymous. A single follow-up asking whether they are willing to be listed by name would likely convert several. Named supporters are citable in a buyer conversation and anonymous counts are not.
4. **Stop leading with the download number.** In any buyer or diligence material, lead with: 7 trained and certified professionals across 5 countries and 5 organizations, all transfer-consented, 100 percent completion. That is a smaller number and a much stronger claim.
5. **Do not build a paid certification now.** It is the one thing people genuinely pay for, but it requires ongoing administration, and starting a service business four weeks before you intend to sell works against you.

## 5. Bottom line

The downloads and clicks are close to noise for valuation purposes. The 7 consented, completed enrollments are the only genuine asset in the dashboard, and the highest-value change available is to convert future guide downloads into more records of exactly that kind.

Keep the material free. Charge for it in identity and consent rather than money, and make that change while the traffic still exists.
