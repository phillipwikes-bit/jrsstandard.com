## 4. What he is actually offering, and what he gets from it

Stated plainly so the trade is visible:

**What Phillip gets.** A banking and risk audience the programme does not currently reach.
A genuinely strong conceptual frame that makes JRS the third of three moments rather than a
standalone claim, which is a stronger position than arguing for it alone. An author with a
distribution channel and a third book on exactly this material.

**What Serhii gets.** Association with a validated evidence programme at the moment his
third book, *The Automation Decision*, is in play. A joint piece serves that launch. **That
is not a problem, but it should be visible rather than assumed**, and it is the reason the
sequencing below matters: a piece that publishes before the detection article has JRS
lending credibility with nothing published behind it.

**Unverified.** No independent check has been run on his position, his employer, or the
book. He is a LinkedIn contact who wrote one very good letter.

## 5. Recommendation

**A fifth paper, not a fifth author on an existing one.**

- **Subject:** the three-moment governance timeline. Pre-deployment delegation, runtime
  escalation, post-hoc reconstruction. His frame, not JRS's.
- **Lead:** Serhii. It is his subject, his audience and his book. Phillip contributes the
  third moment as a bounded named section, which is the same shape as Kyle's 6.4 and Hekim's
  Section V, and is consistent with the standing rule that **co-authorship is earned by
  writing, not granted for an exchange.**
- **Venue:** a banking and risk publication, which does not collide with the *Journal of
  Business Ethics*, *Corporate Compliance Insights* or the *Journal of Civic Information*.
- **Timing: after the detection article publishes.** Two reasons, one of them a hard
  constraint. Part of the wider programme is still blinded
  (`research/IP_SALE_TRACKER.md:155`), so design details cannot be discussed until it
  closes. And a joint piece that runs before the detection results are out means Phillip
  is lending credibility rather than citing evidence.

**Do first:** verify him. Confirm the bank role, confirm the two prior books and that *The
Automation Decision* is real and dated. The Ekhmenin lesson is that a well-produced approach
is not evidence of anything on its own.

**Do not:** add him to the Business Ethics paper, offer him anything on the CCI article,
which is locked and Hekim's, or let a joint piece cite his framework and JRS as mutual
validation.
