## Instructions

1. Read each record on its own terms. Judge whether its conclusions can be traced to evidence written in the record. Do not judge whether the decision itself was right, and do not use outside knowledge.
2. What a documentation gap is. A gap exists when the record states a material conclusion but does not write down the evidence it rests on: no source, no fact, no document, or a reason that only repeats the outcome. A gap is about what the record shows, not whether the decision was right.
3. Overall judgement. GROUNDED (no gap identified): every material conclusion is traceable to identifiable evidence in the record. UNGROUNDED (gap identified): at least one material conclusion is not. UNCERTAIN (cannot tell): say why in the rationale. Leaving it blank means not yet judged.
4. Material conclusions. A material conclusion is a statement that, if wrong, would change the decision or its defensibility. List up to three, a few words each.
5. Supporting passages. For each conclusion, copy the exact words from the record that support it (copy and paste; do not paraphrase). Choose "stated link" if the record says the conclusion rests on that evidence, "plain link" if the evidence plainly concerns the same item, or "no supporting passage".
6. If UNGROUNDED, name the conclusion that has no basis. Under "Documentation gaps found", list each gap you see in your own words, one per line, and copy the exact passage that shows it into a supporting passage field where you can.
7. Answer the four questions: Q1 is a rule or policy cited, and do facts show its conditions were met; Q2 does the stated reason give a separate basis or only repeat the outcome; Q3 is one event given two different dates (a next, previous or revised event is a different event); Q4 are opposite outcomes about the same item or separate items.
8. Dispute. If the record, the questions or these instructions seem flawed or unclear for this record, choose "yes" and say why. A dispute is recorded with your answers; it is not an error.
9. Rationale. A few sentences on how you decided. Note the minutes you spent if you can.
10. Save your answers with the "Download answers" button (CSV or JSON) and send the file back. Nothing is saved automatically. Your answers become the human reference only when the completed file is imported; they are kept alongside, never over, the earlier AI-proposed labels.
