Hi Gabi,

I want to ask you something I should have offered sooner.

The paper I have been building out of this work, the one on records that can no longer explain the decisions behind them, does not just mention you. Its spine is yours. "Stand on business." The insistence that a record has to be understandable to the person it affects, not only to the file. The idea that a decision, a policy, or a principle is only as resilient as the range of people who pressure-tested it, which is the reason the reviewer panel is international and multilingual in the first place. Those are your ideas, and they carry the normative weight of the paper.

So I am not writing to thank you again. I am writing to invite you to be a co-author.

I want to be precise about what that means, because I hold authorship to a strict standard and I would not offer it as a courtesy. Co-authorship is earned by doing the work. Here is the part I would like to be yours: a short section, currently titled "The record and the person it describes," on documentation accessibility as an accountability property, why a record the affected person cannot read or follow fails the same test a court would apply, and why linguistic and cultural range strengthens review rather than complicating it. I have written a shared framing paragraph to build on; the fuller treatment should be yours, in your voice, from your experience with civil rights records and bilingual intake. I would also like your hand on the Introduction that tells your story. Your name goes on the paper when that section does, and once you have reviewed and approved the manuscript.

Two honest notes so there are no surprises. First, you are also one of the reviewers whose reads are part of the study's data, so the paper states plainly that your authorship rests on your writing and analysis, not on your own reviews; that keeps everything clean if anyone ever asks. Second, the opening still calls you "my mentee, Gabi," which was right when I was the only author; tell me how you would prefer to be described now that the paper is partly yours, and we will change it together.

If you are open to this, I will send you the working draft with your section flagged, and we will shape the rest together. If the timing is wrong, that changes nothing between us, and your recognition as a named panel reviewer stands regardless.

You taught me the standard this paper is trying to hold documentation to. It would mean a great deal to have your name on it.

With respect and appreciation,
Phillip
