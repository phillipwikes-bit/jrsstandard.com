🔍 I spent my career reviewing records after the fact as Lead Civil Rights Officer with the Maryland Commission on Civil Rights. One pattern kept appearing: the decision was defensible, the record was not.

⚠️ I call this Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR): when a consequential record can no longer explain, on its own terms, how or why a decision was reached.

⚡ Generative AI makes DRR harder to spot. Polished prose hides missing evidence, unsupported statements, and untraceable reasoning. The document looks finished. Its justification may not be.

🛡️ THE JUSTIFICATION REVIEW STANDARD
A practical framework for HR, compliance, legal, audit, AI governance, and investigative teams to test whether AI-assisted records hold up under scrutiny, before they are final.

Five review conditions:
🔎 Reconstructability: can the conclusion be rebuilt from the record?
❓ Basis identification: is the source of each claim visible?
⏳ Chronology: can the sequence be followed?
🧩 Decision-process traceability: is it clear how it was reached?
⚖️ Evidentiary sufficiency: could a stranger judge the evidence?

📊 EMPIRICAL VALIDATION
• Detection: in a blind study against a verified key, 16 independent experts identified unreconstructable records at 83.9% accuracy (95% CI 72.7 to 95.1), clearing a threshold fixed before the data.

• Reliability: substantial inter-rater agreement, Gwet's AC1 0.739 experts, 0.624 trained. Interim: wide intervals on 10 records.

• AI consistency: three vendors applying the same conditions to the same 15 records agreed 86.7% on the latest nightly run, range 82.2 to 93.3 over 37 runs.

• Global panel: 54 international reviewers have graded records for this work, unpaid and in a personal capacity. 33 completed a full 24-record set, across 16 countries and 5 continents.
