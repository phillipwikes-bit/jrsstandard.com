# LinkedIn profile blueprint: review and revision (2026-08-05)

The architecture is sound. Headline, Featured, About, Experience, Projects, Publications is the right order, and pointing Featured at the pilot and the Registry is correct.

The copy has eight claims that will not survive a diligence check, and three of them are the kind that end a buyer conversation rather than slow it down. Every one is fixed below with replacement text you can paste.

---

## Part 1. What has to change, and why

### Critical, fix before anything goes live

**1. The podcast entry describes an episode that does not exist.**

The draft lists a Publication titled *"Examining Decision Defensibility with Phillip Wikes (23:45)"* with a description of an in-depth discussion of your civil rights background and the JRS framework.

The study record says otherwise, and this has already been corrected once. The episode is *"July 6th, 2026"* of the Dewey Publications Podcast, hosted by Peter Broida. **You were not a guest. JRS is mentioned, and the site is listed in the episode notes under resources for investigators.** The "23:45" timestamp was removed from the prospectus in July because the published episode runs 18 minutes, so that timestamp cannot be right, and the exact spoken words remain unverified and must not be quoted.

Listing yourself as the subject of an interview you did not give is the single most damaging item in this draft. An auditor checks a podcast link in ninety seconds.

**2. "Multi-institutional" is not accurate.**

Reviewers took part **in a personal capacity, unpaid, and not on behalf of their employers**. That is stated in the study consent and in the paper. "Multi-institutional" tells a reader that institutions participated as institutions, which would imply approvals nobody gave. Use "international".

**3. The Medium article is not published.**

*The Evidentiary Deficit in AI-Assisted Record-Keeping* is a draft. It was offered to Dr Gabriela Bar, who declined on capacity grounds, and then to Hekim Colpan, who has not replied. A Publications entry for an unpublished draft is a fabricated credential. Publish it on Medium first, then list it, which you can do this week without anyone's permission.

### Material, fix before outreach

**4. The country count is wrong, and it is attached to the wrong group.**

The draft says the initiatives brought together "institutional supporters across 10 countries and 5 continents."

Two errors. The count is now **11 countries** for the reviewer panel. And that figure describes **reviewers**, not supporters. Registry supporters are a different and much weaker set: 39 recorded, across 7 countries, of whom **2 are named**. Presenting reviewer geography as supporter geography is exactly the blur a buyer's counsel is looking for.

**5. The reliability figure needs its qualifier.**

AC1 0.74 is real and it clears the pre-registered floor of 0.61. It rests on **10 records against a target of about 26**, the confidence interval is wide, and the plan's secondary lower-bound criterion is not met. On a public profile, one clause fixes it: "in interim testing on 10 records."

**6. CEP Magazine is scheduled, not published.**

*"When the Record Cannot Speak for Itself"* was accepted for the **November** issue and is with copy editors, with markup expected late September. Say "forthcoming, November 2026." Also, Corporate Compliance Insights is a different publisher and there is nothing in the record placing you there. Remove it.

**7. "Scientifically validated" overclaims.**

Detection is established. Consistency of application is established. The randomized test of whether the method improves on expert judgment returned a **null**. "Validated" invites a reader to check, and what they will find is a paper that says otherwise in plain terms. "Under independent validation" is both accurate and more credible.

**8. The entity and the dates.**

If "JRS™ Advisory" is not a registered entity, do not list it as an employer. An invented company on a profile is a diligence problem out of proportion to its benefit. Use your own name as an independent practice. Separately, confirm the December 2025 start date against your own records before publishing it; nothing in the study record establishes it.

Minor: "Spearheaded" is on your banned list. "Open-access pilot environments" is plural; there is one.

### Experience

**Title:** Founder and AI Governance Advisor
**Entity:** Justification Review Standard (JRS), independent practice
**Dates:** [confirm start month] to Present

> Independent practice focused on Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR): the risk that a consequential decision cannot later be reconstructed and defended from the documentation itself. Work covers record-level control design, AI documentation defensibility, and validation research.
>
> **Framework design.** Created the Justification Review Standard, a five-condition review that tests whether an AI-assisted record can be independently reconstructed, understood, and defended before it becomes permanent.
>
> **Validation research.** Designed and ran an international detection study in which experienced professionals reviewed a fixed corpus of constructed records against a verified answer key: 32 reviewers, 768 graded reads, 11 countries, 5 continents. Panel detection accuracy 83.9 percent, clearing a threshold set before any data were examined. Separately, three AI systems from three vendors applied the same review to the same records with 84 percent agreement, which measures consistency of application rather than accuracy. Inter-rater reliability reached Gwet's AC1 of 0.74 among expert reviewers in interim testing on 10 records.
>
> **Open initiatives.** Founded The Right to Know Why and The Decisions You Can Defend, with an International Registry of Supporters open to practitioners and organizations.
>
> **Practitioner tools.** Operates a free, zero-retention diagnostic that lets HR, legal, audit, and compliance teams run their own records against the five conditions. Record text is never stored. Also publishes a free field guide in three editions and a free six-module reviewer training with certification.

**Project 2: Organizational Pilot Programme**

## Part 3. The one addition worth making

Nothing in the draft says the standard is **free and open**, and that is your strongest differentiator against a reader who assumes this is a product pitch. Add one line to About:

> The standard, the field guide, the training, and the diagnostic are free to use. The validation data and the analysis plan are published.

That sentence does more for institutional credibility than any adjective in the original draft.

## Part 4. Closing statement, revised

The original ends: *"every visitor sees JRS as an established, scientifically validated open standard."*

Accurate version, and the one to work toward:

> An openly published standard with a completed international detection study, cross-vendor consistency testing, interim reliability results, a scheduled compliance-press feature, and free practitioner tools in active use.

Every clause there is checkable, which is the point.
