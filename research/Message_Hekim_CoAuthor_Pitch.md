Subject: An invitation, with your name first

Dear Hekim,

I have been wanting to write this one for a while. When you completed the full review set and then offered to stay involved in the analysis, it stood out. Most people who finish a study move on. You asked to build on it. That instinct, plus the exact shape of your expertise, is why I am writing.

I have an article ready for a governance and compliance audience: "The Evidentiary Deficit in AI-Assisted Record-Keeping." It argues that AI-assisted records increasingly cannot explain the decisions they document, and that this is a governance and accountability failure before it is ever a litigation problem. The United States side, discovery and evidentiary sufficiency, is solid. What the piece needs to be genuinely strong is the part that is your home ground: how the EU AI Act's record-keeping, logging, and technical-documentation obligations for high-risk uses, and the GDPR's accountability principle, apply to records produced with everyday AI tools. That is precisely the terrain you work in every day as an ISO 42001 auditor, and it is not terrain I can write with your authority.

So I would like to put your name first. This is not honorary. It reflects that the European governance frame, Section V and the oversight and checklist sections, should be led by the person who actually operationalizes those obligations. I would be the co-author on the US civil-rights and records side.

A few things I want to be plain about, because you deserve a clean ask:

- The draft is attached. It is a starting point, not a finished text. The governance sections are yours to rewrite, cut, or expand. I would rather the article carry your judgment than my phrasing.

Both publish contributed pieces from practitioners like us, and both suit your profile better than an academic law review would.

If the subject interests you but the timing is hard, tell me and I will hold it. If you would rather contribute a named section than take the lead byline, that works too. And if it is not for you, a plain no costs nothing and changes nothing about my respect for the work you already did on the panel.

Either way, thank you. The review you completed was real and careful, and this invitation is a direct result of that.
