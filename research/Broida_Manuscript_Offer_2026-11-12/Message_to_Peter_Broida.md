**Subject:** A short manuscript for the EEO manual, if it is useful

Peter,

In July you mentioned the JRS site and the Investigator Field Guide on the podcast and in News and Case Alert, and you sent my work on to Dewey's investigation-book authors. You also told me I had something to offer the civil service world. I have kept at it since.

CEP Magazine ran my article "When the Record Cannot Speak for Itself" on November 1, and Corporate Compliance Insights published a piece I co-wrote in September. I also worked with co-authors on three small studies. One looked at 20 published employment adjudications. One asked whether 32 public-records determinations could be reconstructed from their files. In the third, 16 reviewers from 11 countries classified AI-drafted decision records.

The attached manuscript, "Independent Reconstruction of the Investigative Record," came out of that work. I wrote it with the Report of Investigation section of the EEO Counselors' and Investigators' Manual in mind. It runs three pages and sets out a source check an investigator can run on a drafted ROI before it goes out. The check asks whether a later reader could find the evidence behind each material statement, follow the chronology, and tell the investigator's synthesis from the parties' accounts. It is meant to sit alongside what the manual already says about the ROI. The research behind it is early and limited, and the manuscript says so.

If you or the manual's authors can use it, in a future edition or as a companion reference, you are welcome to it at no charge. If it doesn't fit, that's fine, and you don't owe me a reply.

Thank you again for July.
