# Reply to Hekim Colpan: answers to the eleven publication questions

*He accepted and asked eleven questions before starting work. Every one is reasonable, several are the questions a careful co-author should ask, and answering them cleanly is what turns a maybe into a working collaboration. Answer all eleven in order, in writing, so he has something to hold. Nothing here commits you to anything you cannot deliver. Send from info@jrsstandard.com. No long dashes.*

*One note for you, not for him: he asked these in a specific order, and questions 5 through 9 are all about control and rights. Answer those without hedging. A co-author who has to negotiate for approval rights on his own work will disengage, and his questions signal he has been in a bad collaboration before.*

---

Dear Hekim,

Thank you, and I am glad you are in. Your questions are the right ones and I would rather settle all of them now than discover a disagreement halfway through a draft. Answers in your order.

**1. Intended venue.** Corporate Compliance Insights is the primary target. It publishes contributed practitioner pieces of roughly 1,200 to 1,800 words for exactly this audience, compliance, governance, and legal risk, and two-author governance pieces are routine there. The article is currently about 1,800 words, which sits inside their range.

**2. Website or independent publication.** Independent publication, not the JRS website. I would not ask you to put your name on something that only appears on my own site. If it is accepted, it lives at the publisher. I would then link to it from jrsstandard.com the way anyone links to a published piece, with no republication of the full text without your agreement.

If Corporate Compliance Insights passes or goes quiet, my proposed order is ISACA, either the Journal or the ISACA Now blog, which fits your ISO/IEC 42001 auditor profile directly, and then IAPP Privacy Perspectives for the data protection angle. I would not submit to a peer-reviewed law journal or an attorney-gated venue, because neither of us is bar-admitted and those venues expect a legal-credentialed lead author. I would rather place it honestly than reach.

**3. Audience and date.** The audience is practising compliance officers, in-house counsel, internal audit, and AI governance leads, on both sides of the Atlantic. On timing, I am aiming to submit within two to three weeks of having a version we are both satisfied with. I am not going to put a publication date on it, because that is the outlet's decision, and I would rather give you a real answer than a confident one.

**4. Type of review.** Editorial review, not peer review. Corporate Compliance Insights is a professional publication with editors, so expect editorial suggestions rather than referee reports. It is a thought-leadership piece grounded in law and standards, not an academic article, and we should write it as such.

On legal review, we are not offering legal advice and the piece should say so. If you want the European legal analysis reviewed by a qualified lawyer before submission, say so and we will arrange it rather than rely on our own reading.

**5. Approval rights.** Yes, without qualification. Neither of us submits, publishes, or amends anything the other has not seen and approved in full: title, abstract, author order, biographies, references, disclosures, graphics, and any JRS-related material. If an editor requests changes, they come back to both of us before we accept them. If we cannot agree on a change, we do not make it.

**6. Author order.** Yes. Hekim Colpan and Phillip Wikes, in that order, and I do not intend to change it. You are carrying the European legal and standards analysis, which is the part of this article that makes it worth publishing, and the order should say so.

**7. Copyright and licensing.** We hold copyright jointly as co-authors. Most outlets of this kind take a non-exclusive right to publish, which leaves the underlying rights with us. Whatever licence the outlet actually asks for, I will send you the exact terms before I sign anything, and I will not agree to an exclusive assignment of copyright without your written agreement. If a venue demands full assignment, that is a reason to choose a different venue.

**8. Reuse of your own contribution.** Yes. You retain the right to refer to, quote, republish, translate, and adapt your own contribution, including in your professional work, your own writing, and your own presentations, subject only to whatever the publisher's terms require of both of us. Attribution to the original article is the only thing I would ask in return, and I will do the same.

**9. Commercial and promotional use.** Yes, and I want to be precise here because it touches my own project. Any use of the article, or of your contribution to it, in commercial, promotional, certification, training, or marketing material requires your prior approval. That covers JRS material specifically. Ordinary linking and citation, saying the article exists and pointing to it, does not require approval each time. Anything beyond that does.

That approval requirement is not tied to me personally. It binds anyone who may hold the JRS materials in future, so it does not lapse if ownership of them ever changes hands.

**10. Citation style and sources.** The outlet's house style governs, and Corporate Compliance Insights uses inline hyperlinks rather than formal footnotes. I suggest we draft with full citations for our own record, then convert to whatever the outlet requires.

Yes to primary sources for the European analysis. Cite the EU AI Act, the GDPR, DORA, and ISO/IEC 42001 by article, clause, or provision rather than to secondary commentary, and where a regulator has published guidance, cite the guidance. The United States sections do the same with the statutes and with McDonnell Douglas. If a claim cannot be supported by a primary source, we either support it or take it out.

**11. Personal capacity.** Yes, and it will be stated plainly in your biography and in a disclosure line, in whatever wording you prefer. You are contributing in a personal professional capacity, not on behalf of your employer, and nothing in the article should read as your employer's position. Draft that line yourself and I will use your words.
