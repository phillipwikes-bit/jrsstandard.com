At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Full-time Mail Handler Equipment Operator at the Agency’s Morgan Processing and Distribution Center facility in New York, New York. On November 18, 2021, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against her on the bases of sex (female), disability (physical), and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity under Title VII and the Rehabilitation Act when: 1. On June 25, 2021, and ongoing, she was denied overtime work within her limitations; 2. On July 19, 2021, she was verbally abused by a coworker and no action was taken; 2 3. Beginning on August 1, 2021, and ongoing, she was micromanaged and assigned to either the rewrap section or city manual section and/or confronted about her job duties; and 4. On August 31, 2021, her request for a temporary change of schedule was denied. After its investigation into the complaint, the Agency indicated that, on April 5, 2022, it transmitted to Complainant and her non-attorney representative a copy of the report of investigation (ROI) and notice of her right to request a hearing (Notice) before an EEOC AJ. On April 5, 2022, the Agency emailed Complainant and her representative, stating that a copy of the final ROI was available for download for a period of 30 days and requesting that the parties send an email confirming receipt. CF at 98-103. The record contains an April 5, 2022, message reflecting that the message was relayed to Complainant and her representative but that the destination server did not send a delivery notification. CF at 97. The Agency initially accepted the following for investigation: “On July 20, 2021, you were verbally abused by a supervisor and no action was taken.” CF at 60. Complainant clarified during the investigation that this incident occurred on July 19, 2021, that it was a coworker who verbally abused her, and that, when she reported it to supervisors, nothing was done about it. CF at 205-08. Also on April 5, 2022, the Agency provided Complainant and her representative 3 with a copy of documents that were submitted after the completion of the investigative file, asking that they be added to Complainant’s copy of the recently submitted file. CF at 85-96. The documents consist of an Acting Supervisor’s investigative affidavit, completed on March 28, 2022, and the Acting Supervisor’s Litigation Hold Notice, which was signed on March 31, 2022. CF at 86-95. In the affidavit, the Acting Supervisor stated that he had no organizational relationship to Complainant during the relevant time and that he was not at the facility on July 20, 2021. CF at 86-93. On May 17, 2022, the Agency again emailed Complainant and her non- attorney representative, notifying them that an electronic copy of the ROI would be available for download for a period of 30 days. CF at 82-83. The record contains a May 17, 2022, message reflecting that the message was relayed to Complainant and her representative but that the destination server did not send a delivery notification. CF at 84. On May 31, 2022, Complainant mailed a hearing request to the Agency and to the Commission’s New York District Office. CF at 40-42. The Agency stamped the mailing envelope and the hearing request form as received on June 6, 2022. CF at 40, 42. Complainant included a May 28, 2022, letter with her hearing request, in which she stated that, after receiving the Acting Supervisor’s affidavit in April 2022, she wanted to refute his response that he was not present on the date of the incident and requested a copy of his Employee Everything Report from the Agency. CF at 41. According to Complainant, on May 18, 2022, she called an EEO Services Analyst to refute the Acting Supervisor’s statement, and the EEO Services Analyst told her that her case was closed on May 1, 2022. CF at 41. Complainant averred that she asked how her case could be closed if she and her representative had not been notified or received a copy of the ROI. CF at 41. Complainant stated that the EEO Services Analyst told her that she could request a hearing but should attach a letter stating that she found out her case was closed on May 18, 2022. CF at 41. It appears that these documents were provided to Complainant and her representative via first class mail. CF at 85. On June 2, 2022, the Agency issued a final decision. The Agency stated that it was issuing the decision pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b) because Complainant did not request a hearing within the time frame provided in 29 C.F.R. § 1614.108(f). The decision concluded that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged. On June 10, 2022, the EEOC’s New York District Office received Complainant’s hearing request, which was docketed as Hearing No. 520- 2022-00326X. On June 15, 2022, Complainant filed the instant appeal. On July 6, 2022, the Agency provided Complainant and her representative with documents that were submitted after the completion of the investigative file, asking that they be added to Complainant’s copy of the recently submitted file. CF at 1-10. The document is an updated June 7, 2022, affidavit from the Acting Supervisor. CF at 2-10. The Acting Supervisor stated that, during the timeframe of the complaint, his organizational relationship with Complainant was Acting Supervisor. CF at 2. The Acting Supervisor stated that he was not present when the coworker verbally abused Complainant on July 19, 2021, and that he did not know if Complainant or anyone acting on her behalf reported any harassment. CF at 4-9. On November 29, 2022, an EEOC AJ issued an order dismissing Complainant’s hearing request, noting that the Agency had already issued a final decision, which Complainant had appealed.
