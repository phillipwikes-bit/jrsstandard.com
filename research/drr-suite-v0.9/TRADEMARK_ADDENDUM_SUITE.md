# Trademark addendum: the test suite and a conformance mark

**Prepared 2026-10-02. Filing preparation, not legal advice.** This extends `TRADEMARK_FILING_DOSSIER_JRS_DRR.md`, whose section 5 inputs are still open. The owner files and pays; Claude cannot file.

## 1. The suite changes the evidence for one class
- The dossier's section 0 finding still stands: no commerce has been transacted.
- Suite v0.9 is the first product built to be licensed. A signed beta, even at no fee, is evidence of the service being rendered under the mark. Whether a no-fee beta supports a Section 1(a) claim is an attorney question (*Requires human review*).
- Until a paid licence exists, **1(b) intent to use** stays the defensible basis for Class 042, as the dossier recommends.

## 2. Two different kinds of mark (do not merge them)
| | Service mark (already in the dossier) | Certification mark (new; only if a conformance mark is wanted) |
|---|---|---|
| What it says | "This service comes from JRS" | "This product met the JRS DRR test" |
| Who uses it | The owner, on the standard, training and suite | Licensed vendors, on their products; **never the owner on its own goods or services** (TMEP 1306) |
| Must file | The mark and its classes | The mark, plus a copy of the **certification standard** (thresholds, test method and retest rules) |
| Ready now | Yes, once the section 5 inputs are given | **No.** v0.9 has no thresholds, and the README says no mark is offered at v0.9 |

**Recommendation (judgment):**
- File the service marks first, as the dossier lays out.
- Decide on a certification mark only at v1.0, once thresholds are published.
- Use a distinct wording or logo for it (for example, "JRS DRR TESTED"; wording to be cleared). That way the owner's own use of "JRS" never conflicts with the rule that a certifier may not use its certification mark.
