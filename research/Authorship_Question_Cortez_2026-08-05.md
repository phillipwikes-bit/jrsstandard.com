# Would adding Gabi Cortez as an author raise the odds? (2026-08-05)

Short answer: **no meaningful effect on acceptance, and the reasoning behind the proposal has two factual problems worth fixing before it goes anywhere near a manuscript.**

There is a legitimate route to putting her on the paper. It runs through her contribution, not through the narrative.

---

## Problem 1: it would not make the author line international

Gabi Cortez is in the United States, at the Maryland Commission on Civil Rights. You are in the United States. Adding her produces a two-author line from one country.

The author line is already cross-national: you in the US and Ubayet Hossain in India. Adding a second US author does not strengthen an internationalism argument. It dilutes it slightly, by making the team look more domestic relative to the panel it recruited.

## Problem 2: Ubayet was not recruited for authenticity

Your own study record states why he is on the paper: he **designed the reliability and validation framework**, the reference-panel design, the chance-corrected agreement statistics, and the pre-registered decision floors. He is an author because he built the analytical machinery the paper reports.

Writing that he was recruited to increase the authenticity of the results would misstate his role, and it would understate it. "We brought in a model-validation professional so the findings would look credible" is a weaker claim than "the analysis plan, coefficients, and acceptance floors were designed by a model-validation professional before any data were examined." The second is true and it is the stronger sentence.

A referee who reads the first version starts wondering what else was arranged for effect.

---

## What actually drives acceptance

Author identity is not among the top factors for this paper. Referees at AI and Ethics assess the manuscript: the question, the design, the analysis, the honesty of the limitations. A third author with no institutional affiliation moves that very little. My estimate of the effect is **zero to plus three points**, which is inside the noise of the 70 to 75 percent figure.

There is also a small offsetting risk. Gabi is V-AI-08. Her reads are among the 16 that produce the 83.9 percent result. An author who is also a study subject requires a dual-role disclosure, and some referees treat that as a weakness rather than a strength. Manageable, but it is a cost rather than a gain.

## The internationalism argument is already in the paper, and it is already doing its work

Section 2.1 makes the methodological case that a standard validated in one jurisdiction and one language has been tested against a narrow slice of reality, which is why the panel was recruited across countries, languages, and legal traditions. Methods now names all 11 countries. The Acknowledgments credit that argument to Gabi by name, with her permission.

That argument does not become more persuasive because its originator is on the byline. It is persuasive because the study actually did it.

## The legitimate route, if you want her on the paper

Authorship follows contribution. Your own policy, recorded 2026-07-11, says completing reviewers are named contributors and are not offered co-authorship, with co-authorship reserved for people doing substantial analysis or writing. Gabi has a real claim under that policy, because the Section 2.1 argument is hers. What is missing is the writing.

The path is the one that was set out when she was first invited:

1. She rewrites Section 2.1 in her own voice.
2. She reads and approves the full manuscript.
3. She chooses her author line and how she is described.
4. The dual-role disclosure goes in: she is V-AI-08, a data-contributing reviewer, and her authorship rests on her writing, not on her reads. She took no part in scoring or analysis.

If she does those things, she is an author on the merits and the paper is honest. If she does not, she stays in the Acknowledgments, credited by name for the argument, which is what she has already said she prefers.

**She declined once already**, on 2 August, preferring reviewer status for now. Asking again is fine. Asking again with the reason being that it helps the paper look more international is not, and she would likely notice.
