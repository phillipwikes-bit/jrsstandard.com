# Competitive content review: workforce discipline documentation (2026-11-03)

*Scope: published guidance competing for the same reader, a healthcare compliance or HR professional documenting discipline. Sources were found through search, and full texts were not read, so findings rest on search-result excerpts. Purpose: sharpen "Testing employment files before they become evidence: Lessons for healthcare".*

## Where this article is different (keep and lead with these)
1. **Evidence, not only advice.** Competitors assert what good documentation looks like. This article reports what adjudicators did with real files: 20 decisions, a defined protocol, and disclosed limits. None of the sources reviewed presents original outcome data.
2. **A method that works backward**, from the adjudicator's decision to the file, instead of forward from a drafter's checklist.
3. **A test and a measure.** Five checkpoints, Ready / Needs work / Gap, and committee indicators. Competitors stop at "document consistently".
4. **Repair rules.** Dated addenda and no backfilling. SHRM warns against building a file after the decision; this article also says how to fix a file honestly.
5. **No hype.** The field uses "bulletproof"; this article states its limits. CT editors favor that.

## Content-strategy recommendations
- **In the pitch**, contrast the article with the field in one line: practical guidance usually says *what* to document; this article shows, from adjudicated cases, *which* gaps lose cases and how to test for them. (Already close to the "works backward" sentence.)
- **Offer a worksheet supplement** (the five checkpoints on one page) as an online extra. It matches the template-heavy formats readers already use.
- **Follow-up articles** using the banked material: proportionality (SSA, Douglas factors); the first 72 hours after a hotline report (sequencing plus preservation); investigation documentation (Carson).
