Subject: An invitation to co-author a legal article on AI, records, and accountability

Hi Gabriela,

I am reaching out because I have nearly finished a legal article that I would like to carry your name, and I want to be direct about why.

The article is "The Evidentiary Deficit in AI-Assisted Record-Keeping." The draft is attached. It argues that AI-assisted records increasingly read as complete while losing the evidence that would let anyone reconstruct and defend the decision behind them, and it sets out the litigation and regulatory exposure that follows.

The piece is strong on the US side: discovery, the McDonnell Douglas burden-shifting analysis, ESI obligations. What it needs to be complete is the data-protection and European accountability frame, and you are my first choice.

You are not one option among several. Among the people working where data-protection law and AI accountability meet, you are a reference point: Vice President for Global Privacy at the Future of Privacy Forum, formerly of the European Data Protection Supervisor in Brussels, where you were part of the litigation team appearing before the Court of Justice of the European Union and took part in the Article 29 Working Party, and a scholar whose doctoral work is on the rights of the data subject. Few people have shaped, litigated, and taught data-protection accountability at that level.

Every major shift in technology eventually calls for a legal mind who insists that progress cannot outrun the rights of the person a decision is made about: a Brandeis moment for the algorithmic age. That is the tradition your work sits in, preserving the fundamental right to know why when automated systems affect people's careers, homes, and livelihoods. Very few people in the world stand where data-protection law, enforcement, and AI accountability meet in practice. You are one of them, and on this question, to my mind, you are the definitive voice of your generation. Yours is the name that belongs on the accountability half of this piece.

Section V opens that frame: the GDPR accountability principle and the record-keeping and logging expectations that high-risk AI now carries. I left it deliberately as a skeleton, because the precise contours are yours to draw. You have spent your career on the rights of the data subject and on how those rights are actually adjudicated and enforced, and that is exactly the instinct this section needs. When practitioners want to understand what accountability asks of the record itself, I want them to arrive at your analysis.

If this article is to have a conscience on the accountability question, I would like it to be yours. That is why you are the lead author, and why your name comes first on the attached draft: the accountability analysis is the anchor of the piece, and on that ground your expertise is without equal. The American litigation material sharpens the argument; the framework that gives it lasting weight is yours.

On placement, I think the natural home is the Oxford Business Law Blog: citable, international, and read by exactly the audience we want. JD Supra or a data-protection outlet is a strong second option and reaches the privacy community you are already central to. I am glad to defer to your judgment on venue.

A word on authorship so there is no ambiguity: co-authorship is earned by contribution, so your name goes on when your section does, and there is no obligation here at all. If the timing or the fit is not right, no problem, and I will be grateful you considered it.

Would you be open to it? If so, expand Section V as you see fit, and we will shape the rest together.
