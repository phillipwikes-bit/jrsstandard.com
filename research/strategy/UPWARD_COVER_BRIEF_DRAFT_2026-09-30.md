# WHEN A PERSONNEL RECORD CANNOT EXPLAIN ITSELF
### A two-page brief for executive and board review

**Prepared by Phillip Wikes · Justification Review Standard · jrsstandard.com**
**DRAFT 1, 2026-09-30. NOT APPROVED FOR RELEASE.**

---

## The problem in one paragraph

Organizations are now using AI assistance to draft performance reviews, written
warnings, investigation summaries and termination memos. The resulting documents are
well organized and professionally worded. A number of them cannot show how they reached
their conclusions. The evidence that supported the decision may exist somewhere in
emails, attendance data, prior feedback and manager notes, but the record that will be
produced in litigation or to a regulator does not connect its conclusions to any of it.
It asserts. It does not demonstrate.

## Why routine review does not catch this

A reviewer reads a record for whether it is clear, consistent and professional. These
records are all three. **Fluency is what AI assistance reliably supplies, and fluency is
what a reviewer uses as a proxy for substance.** The failure is invisible at the point
of review and becomes visible only when someone who was not present, and who has no
interest in the outcome, reads the file cold and asks what supports each conclusion.

That reader is usually opposing counsel, an agency investigator or an auditor. By then
the people who knew the answer have moved on, and their recollection is not evidence.

## The pattern that no single review can see

The harder exposure does not sit inside any one file. **It appears across files.**

AI assistance reuses phrasing. "Cultural fit." "Executive presence." "Struggles with
change." Read in one file, any of these is ordinary management language. Read across a
dozen files, clustering among employees who share a protected characteristic, the same
phrases read very differently to someone holding the entire set.

**Nobody drafted that pattern deliberately. The tool produced it, because producing
consistent language is what the tool is for.** An organization reviewing one file at a
time cannot see it. Discovery produces the whole set at once, and the first person to
read them side by side is the one bringing the claim.

This is the difference between defending an individual decision and defending a pattern.

## What a defensible record contains

Five properties. They are not new law and they are not a product. They are what a
reader with no prior knowledge needs in order to assess whether a conclusion is
supported.

1. **Reconstructability.** The path from documented evidence to conclusion is traceable
   from the record itself, without the author's recollection.
2. **Basis identification.** Each material characterization is tied to an identified
   source rather than asserted.
3. **Chronology.** The sequence of events, prior interventions and the review period are
   followable from the record alone.
4. **Decision-process traceability.** Who reviewed the matter, what threshold applied,
   and whether responsive or mitigating information was considered before finalization.
5. **Evidentiary sufficiency.** The aggregate: the record stands on its own, such that an
   independent reviewer with no prior knowledge could assess whether the conclusion is
   supported.

**A ten-minute test any executive can run without consulting anyone.** Take one
consequential personnel record from the last twelve months. For each conclusion in it,
point to the evidence in the file that supports that conclusion. Not the sentence
asserting it. The evidence.

## What this brief does not claim

Stated plainly, because a brief that overreaches is the same failure it describes.

- It does not claim that any framework is legally required, or that meeting these
  conditions establishes compliance with any statute, regulation or standard.
- It does not claim to prevent a lawsuit, an enforcement action or an adverse finding.
- It does not offer legal advice. **Whether any particular record is defensible is a
  question for counsel.**
- The five conditions are published openly and are at an early stage of formal
  validation. Supporting research, including a pre-registered criterion that was **not**
  met, is published in full rather than summarized favourably.

## NOTES FOR PHILLIP. NOT PART OF THE BRIEF. DELETE BEFORE ANY CIRCULATION.

**On the absence of an ask.** There is no call to action, no contact invitation, no
product and no pricing. This is the mechanism, not modesty. A General Counsel forwarding
this to a chief executive must not appear to be forwarding a vendor's material. The
moment a request appears, the forward stops happening and the document is worthless.

**On the near-absence of statistics.** One qualified reference to a failed criterion,
and nothing else. A founder's own study invites a methodology argument, and that is an
argument the forwarder loses in front of their boss even when the study is sound. The
reader can verify the core argument against their own files in ten minutes, which is
stronger than a cited interval. The full figures are held in
`UPWARD_COVER_CLAIM_REGISTER_2026-09-30.md` for the second conversation.

**On naming the failed criterion in a marketing-adjacent document.** Deliberate. It is
the single most credible sentence available, it pre-empts the hostile reader, and to
this audience it signals that the rest of the document can be trusted.

**On the ten-minute test.** It is the loss-aversion trigger and the locus-of-control
move in one move. The reader discovers the gap themselves, privately, with nobody
present, and owns the finding.

**Three acceptance tests, to be run before release.**

1. **Forward test.** Could a GC send this to a CEO with a one-line note and not be
   embarrassed?
2. **Claim register test.** Does every factual statement trace to the register?
3. **Hostile read test.** Does opposing counsel find anything overstated?
