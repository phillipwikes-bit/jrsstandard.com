## 4. What a reviewer will attack, and the answer

**"Your reviewers scored their own outcomes."** Concede immediately. It is disclosed in
Section 3.2 and again in Section 6, and Section 7 makes role separation the first
requirement of the confirmatory design. Do not defend it.

**"The audit cases are circular: a document that exists because auditors found a failure
will read as a gap."** This is the sharpest available objection and the paper must not
dodge it. The answer is that Section 4.1 reports concordance between two instruments, not
prediction of an outcome, and that it is reported alongside a discriminant result and a
predicted null which a circular measure would not produce. **If a reviewer insists, offer
to drop Section 4.1 entirely.** The pooled criterion result, the construct evidence and the
discriminant evidence all stand without it.

**"You pooled a significant result with a null."** The Woolf test is the answer, and it was
run before the pooling was written up rather than after: Q = 2.550 on 1 df, p = 0.110. The
domains are statistically consistent. Say so and show the per-domain rows, which are in the
Section 4.4 table.

**"Post-hoc note coding."** Conceded in the text itself. It is labelled construct evidence
rather than a confirmatory test, and the contemporaneous notes make it checkable by anyone
re-reading the case set.
