## Notes on the choices

**"That's completely fine."** First line, before anything else. He deferred, and the reply
should confirm the deferral was received as normal rather than as a problem.

**The date correction is the substance of the message.** He was sent a manuscript and a
deadline in the same envelope. Left alone, that reads as a clock on his review, and it also
would have deemed an authorship sign-off from silence, which the earned-authorship policy
forbids. Correcting it yourself, unprompted, is both the honest position and the single
strongest signal you are not pressing him.

**The KPMG offer is made first, not waited for.** Employer exposure is the live failure mode
on this programme: Kyle McMullan raised exactly that class of objection three days ago.
Offering the personal-capacity wording before Hossain has to ask removes the awkwardness of
asking, and it costs the paper nothing.

**No date is requested.** Asking "when do you think?" converts a deferral into a commitment
he may not be able to make if a firm review sits behind it.

**The declarations are flagged now but not demanded.** He knows what is coming at the end,
so nothing lands as a surprise late.

**Zero em-dashes, per CLAUDE.md III.7.**
