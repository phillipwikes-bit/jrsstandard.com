## A note on Section 2 before you read it

You asked for a roster of every supporter and participant by name, title, organisation and
country. **That table is not in this briefing, and the reason is the single most valuable
thing in it.**

The programme's own rule is that a person's name is never printed on silence alone. Of 42
contributors, **23 have returned a naming election and 19 have not**. Those 19 default to
anonymous. Separately, the comparison study runs on a neutral recognition track precisely so
that no document ever shows which participant was in which arm; a single table naming
everyone side by side would undo that.

**Give your coach the aggregate picture instead.** It is more persuasive anyway: "58
reviewers across 16 countries, 36 of whom completed a full structured review" is a stronger
claim than a list of names your coach cannot verify. If a specific introduction becomes
useful later, that is a one-person conversation with that person, not a spreadsheet.

**This restraint is itself the product.** Anyone can build a compliance tool. Very few
people demonstrably run a research programme that refuses to name its own participants
without consent. That is the credential.

## 1. Plain-English Executive Summary: What He Actually Built

**The problem, in one sentence.** When AI drafts a workplace record, the text reads as
finished while the evidence behind it quietly disappears, so a decision that was correct at
the time can no longer be proved two years later when a court, regulator or auditor asks.

**What Phillip built.** A structured review, applied *before* a record is finalised, that
asks one question: could an independent person rebuild how this conclusion was reached from
the record alone? It is a checklist and a discipline, not software. It requires no new
system, no vendor, and no IT project. He named the underlying risk **Decision Reconstruction
Risk**, which is now the phrase the work travels under.

**Why that framing matters commercially.** He is not selling AI. He is selling
*defensibility*, the thing an HR director, general counsel or head of audit is actually
afraid of. That market exists regardless of which AI vendor wins.

## 3. Probability-Ranked Strategic Pathways

Rankings are reasoned judgment against his verified position, not measured fact.

### Path B: High-Ticket Independent Consulting and Advisory, **60 to 70%**
**Ranked first.**

**Why it wins.** Everything required already exists: the method, the evidence, the
publication, the network, and a named risk category. Nothing has to be built and nobody's
permission is needed. Revenue can start inside 90 days.

**What he is selling:** a documentation-defensibility review for HR, legal, compliance and
audit functions, priced per engagement, delivered inside existing workflows.

**The credential that closes deals:** thirteen years assessing discrimination complaints
under federal HUD and EEOC frameworks. He has personally read the files that failed. That is
not a consultant's anecdote, it is operator experience, and buyers can tell the difference.

**What holds it below 70%:** solo delivery caps revenue at his own hours, and there is no
institutional brand behind him. Both are fixable with associates, not with more research.

### Path C: Senior / Fractional Roles and Advisory Boards, **45 to 55%**

**Why it is real.** AI governance roles are being created faster than qualified people
exist, and almost every applicant is technical. He is the opposite: a governance and
evidence person who understands what a regulator asks for. Advisory board seats are
realistic now; a fractional Head of AI Governance is realistic within a year.

**The specific asset:** the ISO/IEC 42001 co-author relationship and the Big Four
model-validation co-author give him credible references inside the exact institutions that
hire for these roles.

**What holds it down:** these roles often screen for prior corporate seniority rather than
research output, and a public-sector background can be read as unfamiliar with commercial
pace. Board seats frequently pay little at first.

### Path A: B2B Software IP Licensing / Enterprise Platform Partnerships, **15 to 25%**
**Ranked last, and the gap is deliberate.**

**Why it is the weakest despite feeling like the biggest.** Platform vendors of that scale
license standards that are *already adopted by their customers*, not standards seeking
adoption. The pipeline shows **zero organisational pilots** to date. Without enterprise
deployments, there is nothing for a platform to license, and the conversation stalls
regardless of how good the method is.

**What would move it.** Three to five named enterprise deployments. Path B produces exactly
those, which is why B is the route to A rather than an alternative to it.

**One material risk to name.** The trademarks are unregistered. If licensing is ever the
goal, filing is a prerequisite, and a third party has already produced material using the
JRS name in their own product title.

**Recommended sequence: B now, C in parallel, A in 18 to 24 months if B produces
deployments.**

## 4. Proof Points and Market Tailwinds

**Proof the method works, not just that it exists:**
- **83.9% detection accuracy** by 16 independent experts across 11 countries, reading blind.
- **Five out of five agreement** with independent government auditors who examined the same
  agency records using their own methodology and no knowledge of this study.
- **Reliability above a threshold set before the data was seen**, the discipline most
  practitioner frameworks skip.
- **A published null result.** In one corpus the method showed nothing, and he published that
  alongside the explanation. **A coach should understand this is an asset:** people who
  publish their own negative findings are the ones regulators and buyers trust.

**Market tailwinds:**
- The EU AI Act's high-risk documentation obligations, with deadlines now confirmed for
  December 2027 and August 2028, giving organisations a defined runway during which they
  need exactly this.
- GDPR Article 5(2) accountability already requires demonstrable compliance today.
- US discovery and employment burden-shifting frameworks create the same pressure without
  any AI-specific rule.

**The competitive moat, stated plainly.** Three things almost never appear in one person:

1. **Thirteen years of civil-rights enforcement**, he has read thousands of consequential
   records after the fact and knows precisely how they fail.
2. **A Master's in Negotiation and Conflict Management**, he can sit with a general counsel
   and an HR director who disagree and move them, which is most of what this work is.
3. **A validated research programme**, he can prove his claims, which almost no independent
   consultant in this space can.

Competitors have one or two. The combination is the moat, and it cannot be hired quickly.

## 5. Three Questions for the Coach

**1. "My strongest asset is 58 practitioners in 16 countries who have already used my
method. What is the fastest ethical route from that goodwill to three paying engagements,
given most of them are individuals rather than budget-holders?"**
*Goes to the real bottleneck: reach is proven, conversion is not.*

**2. "I have a compliance-magazine article, four submission-ready papers and thirteen years
of civil-rights enforcement. Which single credential should lead in the market, and which am
I overweighting?"**
*He is likely leading with the research, which impresses academics. The enforcement
background may be what buyers actually pay for.*

**3. "If I take a fractional or advisory role for income stability, does that strengthen the
consulting practice by giving me an institutional affiliation, or does it stall it? How
would I structure it so it does the first?"**
*The genuine strategic fork, and the one most likely to be got wrong by drifting into a job
rather than choosing one.*
