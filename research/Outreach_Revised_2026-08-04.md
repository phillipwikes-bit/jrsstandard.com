Dear [Name],

I am facilitating The Decisions You Can Defend Initiative, an international effort focused on protecting one of an organization's most underrated assets: documentation that still supports a consequential decision long after the decision was made.

Every consequential decision is eventually questioned. The only uncertainty is whether the record will still be able to explain and defend it.

A strong record survives complaints, audits, investigations, regulatory review, and litigation. A weak one becomes the organization's greatest vulnerability. The difference is rarely the decision itself. It is whether the reasoning behind it can still be reconstructed from the documentation.

## 3. LinkedIn About section

I spent much of my career reviewing records after the fact as a Lead Civil Rights Officer with the Maryland Commission on Civil Rights. I kept seeing the same pattern: the decision was often defensible, but the record was not.

I call this Decision Reconstruction Risk (DRR): the vulnerability created when a consequential record can no longer explain, on its own terms, how or why a decision was reached.

Generative AI makes DRR harder to spot. Automated tools generate polished prose that often masks missing evidence, unanchored statements, and untraceable reasoning. The draft looks complete, but the underlying justification has dissolved.

To address this, I developed the Justification Review Standard (JRS), a practical diagnostic framework for HR, compliance, legal, audit, and investigative teams to evaluate whether an AI-assisted record can withstand long-term scrutiny before becoming permanent.

JRS evaluates five core criteria:
• Can the conclusion be reconstructed?
• Is the evidentiary basis identifiable?
• Can the chronology be followed?
• Can the reasoning be traced?
• Is the supporting evidence sufficient?
