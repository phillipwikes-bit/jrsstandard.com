## What she actually said, and why it deserves a straight answer

Three distinct points, all reasonable:

1. **"I am not involved in conducting investigations."** If true in the literal sense, JRS may genuinely not be for her. It reviews records made by people who decide things.
2. **"India has its own legal and procedural frameworks."** Correct, and she knows them better than you do.
3. **"I would like to understand its practical relevance to my work before considering it further."** This is not a brush-off. It is the exact question a serious person asks, and it deserves a real answer including "it may not be relevant."

**The temptation is to argue her into a fit. Do not.** She is a credible person who asked a precise question. The reply that earns respect is the one that concedes the parts she is right about.

## The one honest hook, framed as a question, not a claim

**Do not tell an Indian advocate how Indian law works.** Ask.

Her field is workplace sexual harassment. In that field there is a body that conducts an inquiry and produces a written finding. **Whether that record can later explain why the finding was reached is exactly what JRS measures**, and it matters most to the person who was complained about or the person who complained, neither of whom sees the deliberation.

**Frame it as: does this problem exist in the matters you support? You would know and I would not.**

That is honest, it is genuinely her expertise, and if the answer is no, you have lost nothing and she will respect the asking.
