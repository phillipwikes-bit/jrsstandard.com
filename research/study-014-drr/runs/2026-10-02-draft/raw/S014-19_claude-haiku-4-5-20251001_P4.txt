# CASE FILE SUMMARY

## Employment Information
Complainant was employed by the Agency as a Mail Handler (Modified Assignment - Limited Duty), PS 4/P, at the Church Street Station in New York, New York.

## Timeline of Events

**June 11, 2019:** Agency Health & Resource Management (HRM) informed Complainant that her most recent medical note on file was dated January 18, 2011, and requested she submit an updated note from her doctor with her medical restrictions. (ROI, Affidavit A, at 16, Bates No. 21)

**June 15, 2019:** The American Postal Workers Union, AFL-CIO, filed a Step 2 grievance against the Agency alleging that Complainant performing Clerk Craft duties in her Modified Assignment violated the collective bargaining agreement (CBA) because this crossed crafts. (ROI, Exhs. 4, 7, and Affidavit C, at 3, ¶ 15, Bates Nos. 180, 207, 146)

**June 21, 2019:** The HRM request for an updated medical note was repeated. (ROI, Affidavit A, at 16, Bates No. 21)

**July 2, 2019:** Complainant's doctor issued a letter stating: "Above mentioned patient physical requirements are lifting, pushing/pulling reaching above shoulder not to exceed 20lbs [sic.], standing and walking average time 20 minutes…."

**July 5, 2019:** Complainant came to work with the July 2, 2019 doctor's letter. From July 5, 2019 through July 31, 2019, she was not allowed to work. (ROI, Affidavit A, at 3, ¶ 15, Bates No. 92)

**July 22, 2019:** HRM Manager wrote Complainant that the Agency was reviewing her Modified Assignment (Limited Duty) to determine if a revision was necessary after an arbitration award on limited duty "non-APWU Clerk craft employees performing clerk craft duty assignments." He informed her that management would perform a search for adequate, available work and, if found, she would be offered a revised Modified Assignment (Limited Duty) within her July 2, 2019 medical restrictions.

**July 25, 2019:** HRM Manager presented Complainant an Offer of Modified Assignment (Limited duty) effective August 1, 2019, to the position of Modified Handler (Modified Assignment – indefinite duration) at the James A. Farley Retail Local Operation Center. The duties were within the restrictions set forth in the July 2, 2019 doctor's letter. (ROI, Bates Nos. 25-26; Ex. 8, at 2, Bates No. 199)

**August 1, 2019:** Complainant returned to work.

**After July 31, 2019:** Complainant submitted a revised doctor's note. (ROI, Affidavit C, at four, ¶ 15, Bates No. 147)

## Key Statements

**Manager of Customer Services (S2):** 
- Stated the request for an updated medical note was made so the Agency could draft a new Offer of Modified Assignment for Complainant that was within her current medical restrictions and did not cross crafts. (ROI, Affidavit C, at 3, ¶ 15, Bates No. 146)
- According to S2, HRM found the July 2, 2019 updated doctor's letter to be inadequate because the limitations were unclear, and he asked Complainant to submit another updated doctor's letter. (ROI, Affidavit C, at four, ¶ 15, Bates No. 147)
- S2 told Complainant not to return to work until she submitted an updated doctor's note describing her medical restrictions. (ROI, Affidavit A, at 3, ¶ 15, Bates No. 92)

**Supervisor of Customer Services (S1):**
- Echoed that the request for an updated medical note was made so the Agency could draft a new Offer of Modified Assignment that was within Complainant's current medical restrictions and did not cross crafts. (ROI, Affidavit B, at 3, ¶ 15, Bates No. 138)
- Attested that S2 told him HRM found the updated doctor's letter to be inadequate. (ROI, Affidavit C, at four, ¶ 15, Bates No. 147)

**HRM Manager:**
- Stated that S2 decided not to allow Complainant to work.
- Stated he was not aware why Complainant was not allowed to work in her limited duty assignment between July 5, 2019 and July 31, 2019.
- Learned of S2's decision after it was made.

**Complainant:**
- Does not contest that she performed Clerk Craft duties.
- Refused to submit a doctor's note with her current medical restrictions, believing it was unnecessary. (ROI, Affidavit A, at 3, ¶ 15, Bates No. 92)
- Expected that when she brought in the updated note, she would be allowed to work.
- Erroneously alleged this period started on July 4, 2019, which was a paid holiday for her.

## Final Agency Decision (FAD)

The Agency found no discrimination.

**Criteria Applied:**
- Whether Complainant was an individual with a disability
- Whether Complainant was qualified to perform the essential functions of her Mail Handler (unmodified) job with or without reasonable accommodation
- Whether Complainant submitted the medical documentation management requested in order to offer her a new modified assignment within her medical limitations
- Whether Complainant proved discrimination under the theory of disparate treatment

**Decision-Maker:** The Agency (in the FAD)

**Responses Considered:**
- The Agency assumed, without finding, that Complainant was an individual with a disability.
- The Agency found she was not qualified because she was unable to perform the essential functions of her Mail Handler (unmodified) job with or without reasonable accommodation.
- The Agency reasoned that Complainant did not submit the medical documentation management requested in order to offer her a new modified assignment within her medical limitations.
- The Agency concluded Complainant did not prove discrimination under disparate treatment, noting that Complainant did not identify any comparative employees who were treated differently.

## Appeal

Complainant requested a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ). When Complainant later withdrew the request, the AJ ordered the Agency to issue a FAD. Complainant subsequently appealed the FAD. In reply, the Agency argued that the FAD should be affirmed.
