# Investigation Record Summary

## Background

At the time of the events giving rise to these complaints, both Complainants worked as Emergency Management Specialists (Regional and National Tribal Government Liaisons) for the Agency's First Responder Network Authority. They worked remotely, one from Pauma Valley, California, and the other from Thousand Palms, California. Both joined the Agency in October 2015 at the Pay Band IV level.

Beginning June 13, 2019, each Complainant filed a formal EEO complaint. They alleged discrimination and harassment based on race (Native American), national origin (Native American), and sex, and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity. During the hearing process, Complainants withdrew their sex discrimination claims. The remaining claims are summarized below.

## Claim 1: Pay Discrimination Since Hire

Complainants allege that they have been subjected to pay discrimination since being hired, as follows:

- **(a) Hiring negotiations.** Complainant 1 negotiated his salary before being hired. He was told the Agency was obligated to offer him only 70% of his previous wages and that he could "take it or leave it." He alleges non-Natives were not treated this way during hiring. Complainant 2 claims the Agency failed to account for changes in her prior salary or teaching wages and offered only a salary commensurate with her current tribal salary.
- **(b) Pay band placement.** Complainants performed the same work as peers hired at Pay Band V, but they were hired at Pay Band IV and paid at the lowest level.
- **(c) April 2018 review.** Around April 2018, the Executive Director, Public Safety Advocacy Directorate ("Executive Director"), directed Complainants' supervisor ("Supervisor 1") to conduct a review addressing wage and pay band disparities. After the review was completed, the Executive Director and a Senior Director took no action and failed to acknowledge that it had occurred.
- **(d) May 31, 2018 call.** In a telephone conversation, the Senior Director claimed he was unaware of the review. Complainants later learned from Supervisor 1 that he was "very well aware" of it.
- **(e) June 15, 2018 inquiry.** Complainants asked the Deputy Chief Administrative Officer about hiring and direct hiring authorities. They were told the Agency "does not have personnel policies and practices." The Deputy Chief Administrative Officer also failed to inform them of their right to pursue the EEO process on their allegations of discriminatory pay.
- **(f) July 2018.** Complainants' new supervisor ("Supervisor 2") asked the Executive Director and Senior Director about the April 2018 review. They denied any knowledge of having committed to it.
- **(g) January 2019.** The Executive Director acknowledged to Supervisor 2 that he had received the review but said he felt no obligation to act on it. He then instructed Supervisor 2 to "tamper any escalation of [human resources (HR)] issues for the tribal team." In a later email he backpedaled and claimed Supervisor 2 had misunderstood his instructions.

**Note on framing of Claim 1.** The Agency accepted claim 1 with subparts (a) through (g). However, one of the Administrative Judges (AJs) did not list the subparts in the Case Management Order. That AJ instead consolidated them and described claim 1 as including the allegation that management officials took no remedial action after reviewing wage and pay band disparities. Subparts 1(c) through 1(g) are therefore treated as falling under that allegation.

## Claim 2: Exclusion from Senior Leadership Positions

Complainants allege they were precluded from applying for senior leadership positions because their pay was discriminatorily set at Pay Band IV. The Agency made those positions available only to Pay Band V employees.

## Claim 3: Reorganization

In June 2018, Complainants were allegedly "buried deeper" in the organization through a reorganization. They allege this was intended to provoke them to quit and to "limit any HR escalations from [the] tribal team."

## Claim 4: Separate Strategic Plans

On an ongoing basis since at least June 2018, Complainants were tasked with creating separate strategic plans. They allege none of their non-Tribal peers were required to do so.

## Claim 5: Hostile Treatment by Contractor Officials

On multiple occasions between June 2018 and February 2019, Complainants and other Agency officials repeatedly told senior leadership that Tribal team members were being subjected to hostile and discriminatory treatment by officials of an Agency contractor. Senior leadership allegedly condoned the treatment and continued to place the team in hostile situations with contracting officials.

## Claim 6: Unequal Access to Resources

Complainants allege that, on an ongoing basis, they were denied equal access to the resources needed to perform their duties in the same manner as their peers, as follows:

- **(a)** They were forced to coordinate all their activities through a separate, discriminatory process to access resources and their non-federal shareholders.
- **(b)** They were forced to engage with contracting staff and Tribal shareholders through a "gatekeeper."
- **(c)** The Agency limited the types of meetings in which they were allowed to participate.
- **(d)** An Agency Contracting Officer's Representative was required to participate in daily engagements between the contractors and Complainants.

## Claim 7: May 5, 2019 Tweet

On May 5, 2019, the Agency allegedly failed to acknowledge the accomplishments of Complainants and other Tribal team members. It issued a tweet thanking local, state, and federal workers for their dedication and service but made no reference to the Tribal team.

## Procedural History

After the investigations concluded, Complainants received copies of the investigative files and requested hearings before an EEOC Administrative Judge. The two complaints were assigned to different AJs in the Commission's Washington Field Office.

Complainants' shared attorney moved to consolidate the complaints for joint processing before each AJ. The Agency opposed the motions. One AJ denied the motion, and the other did not address it.

The AJs then issued separate summary judgment decisions. Each found that no discrimination or unlawful retaliation had been established. The Agency's final actions implemented the AJs' decisions. Both Complainants then filed the instant appeals.
