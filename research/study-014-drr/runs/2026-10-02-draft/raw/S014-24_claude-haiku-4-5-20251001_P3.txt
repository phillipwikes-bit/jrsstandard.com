# INVESTIGATIVE CASE FILE REPORT

## COMPLAINANT INFORMATION AND EMPLOYMENT BACKGROUND

At the time of the events giving rise to this complaint, the Complainant was employed as a Travel Specialist, classified at the GS-6 grade level, within the Agency's Office of Infectious Disease (OID). This office operates under the broader organizational structure of the National Center for HIV/AIDS, Viral Hepatitis, STD and TB Prevention (NCHHSTP), located in Atlanta, Georgia. The Complainant's employment file and position responsibilities are documented in the Report of Investigation (ROI) at page 49.

During the relevant timeframe, the Complainant reported to multiple supervisory authorities. Her first-level supervisor (S1) held the position of Supervisor Program Specialist, classified at the GS-12 grade level. The S1 supervisor is an African American female. Additionally, the Complainant was supervised by a Management Officer, identified as a Caucasian male, and a Deputy Director, identified as a male of unspecified racial or ethnic background. These overlapping supervisory relationships would prove significant in the development of the allegations that follow.

## ALLEGED HOSTILE WORK ENVIRONMENT AND SUPERVISORY MISCONDUCT

### Incident of October 28, 2013

According to the Complainant's account, on October 28, 2013, during a workplace meeting, the S1 supervisor made several derogatory and threatening statements. Specifically, the Complainant alleges that S1 instructed her to leave the Agency, stating that upper management did not favor her. The Complainant further contends that S1 indicated the Management Officer was deliberately working against her interests. Most significantly, the Complainant claims that S1 directed her to withdraw her union membership, using profane language and asserting that union affiliation would prevent career advancement. This account is documented in the ROI at page 127.

However, the S1 supervisor categorically denied making these statements. The S1 supervisor further denied that any such meeting or incident transpired. The denial places this matter in direct contradiction, with no corroborating witnesses identified to support either account.

### Christmas Party Incident

The Complainant also reported an interaction with the Business Services Support Specialist regarding her absence from the office Christmas party. According to the Complainant's testimony, the Support Specialist questioned why she was not attending the event and inquired whether she celebrated Christmas. The Complainant explained that she practices the Jehovah's Witness faith and therefore does not participate in Christmas celebrations. This testimony appears in the ROI at pages 129-30.

The Complainant describes the Support Specialist's non-verbal response as particularly offensive. The Complainant asserts that the Support Specialist's facial expression conveyed disgust, characterized as looking at her "as if she had a disease," and that the Support Specialist rolled her eyes in response to this religious disclosure. The Complainant experienced this reaction as dismissive and disrespectful of her religious beliefs.

However, the Support Specialist provided a contrary account, stating she had no recollection of any interaction with the Complainant on the referenced date and maintaining that the incident, as described by the Complainant, never occurred.

### Alleged Coworker Comments of January 9, 2014

The Complainant further alleged that on January 9, 2014, she overheard a conversation between two coworkers—identified as C1 and C2—in which disparaging comments were made about her. According to the Complainant, C1 remarked to C2 that the Complainant thinks "she is cute because she is built like a Black girl" and characterized the Complainant as "bad news and not your friend." The Complainant reported that such comments were repeated on multiple occasions, occurring in hallways and in the office elevator. These allegations are documented in the ROI at pages 131-32.

The Complainant's account stands in stark contrast to the denials offered by both coworkers. Coworker C1 maintained that she had no knowledge of the Complainant's identity and categorically denied making any such comments. Coworker C2 similarly asserted that she did not recall C1 making such a comment to her. Without corroborating evidence or witness testimony, this matter remains unresolved through contradictory accounts.

## ALLEGATIONS OF SEXUAL HARASSMENT BY THE DEPUTY DIRECTOR

The Complainant raised the most serious allegations against the Deputy Director, describing a pattern of unwanted sexual conduct spanning multiple years, with origins in 2006 and continuing through the investigation period. These allegations encompass physical contact, verbal advances, and alleged retaliatory employment actions.

### Physical Contact Allegations

The Complainant testified that the Deputy Director engaged in inappropriate physical conduct during official workplace settings. Specifically, the Complainant alleged that the Deputy Director would frequently stare at her chest. Additionally, the Complainant claimed that on two separate occasions during staff meetings, the Deputy Director placed his hand on her left leg and, in her words, "rolled his hand down" her leg. These incidents are detailed in the ROI at pages 133-35.

The Complainant described her response to the first instance of physical contact, stating that she "scooted over" to create distance between herself and the Deputy Director. However, when this conduct repeated at a second staff meeting, the Complainant reported that she left the meeting prematurely. The Complainant further stated that she verbally communicated her discomfort to the Deputy Director, informing him that his physical contact with her leg during meetings was causing her emotional distress and was unwelcome.

Beyond the leg contact during meetings, the Complainant also alleged that the Deputy Director regularly entered her work cubicle and placed his hands on her shoulders, engaging in what appeared to be shoulder massage motions. The Complainant further contended that the Deputy Director would lean in close proximity to her while directing her attention to her computer monitor, thereby invading her personal space in a manner she found uncomfortable and inappropriate.

### Unwanted Social Advances

The Complainant reported that the Deputy Director extended social invitations to her, specifically requesting lunch and/or dinner engagements. The Complainant consistently rejected these advances, refusing to accept the Deputy Director's social invitations.

### Alleged Retaliatory Actions Following Rejection of Advances

The Complainant maintained that following her rejection of the Deputy Director's social overtures, he engaged in retaliatory workplace conduct designed to punish her refusal. The Complainant outlined several specific forms of alleged retaliation. First, the Deputy Director allegedly assigned her additional job duties beyond her normal responsibilities. Second, he purportedly refused to approve her requests for professional training and development opportunities. Third, he allegedly assigned her duties that fell outside the scope of her position description as a Travel Specialist. Fourth, the Complainant reported that the Deputy Director engaged in aggressive verbal conduct, yelling and cursing at her in his office regarding the proper processing of employee travel preparations. This account is documented in the ROI at page 53.

The Complainant also described volatile behavioral episodes by the Deputy Director. The Complainant testified that the Deputy Director would summon her to his office and engage in loud, angry venting while throwing objects such as pens and pencils across the room. According to the Complainant's account, these outbursts occurred with sufficient frequency that coworkers became aware of the pattern. The Complainant explained that she discussed the Deputy Director's behavioral pattern with several colleagues who had become aware of her distress. The ROI at page 154 documents these allegations.

### Initial Reporting and Lack of Response

The Complainant indicated that she reported the Deputy Director's harassing conduct to the Agency Director shortly after the behavior commenced. However, the Director's response was dismissive—the Complainant reported that the Director "shook his head in disbelief" rather than initiating any remedial action. The Complainant testified that this lack of responsiveness resulted in the harassment continuing unabated. The ROI at page 134 reflects this account.

The Complainant further maintained that she lodged complaints with her first-level supervisor (S1), the Management Officer, and a Consultant employed by the Agency. According to the Complainant's account, all three recipients of her complaints failed to take any meaningful action, effectively ignoring her reports of harassment.

However, both the S1 supervisor and the Management Officer denied that the Complainant had ever reported the Deputy Director's conduct to them. They maintained that they were unaware of any allegations regarding the Deputy Director's harassment of the Complainant.

### Corroborating Witness Testimony

Despite the management denials, multiple coworkers provided testimony corroborating significant elements of the Complainant's allegations. The Senior Budget Analyst provided detailed testimony that the Complainant would visit her office on an almost weekly basis to express frustration about the Deputy Director's conduct. The Senior Budget Analyst specifically noted that the Complainant complained about the Deputy Director violating her personal space and making her uncomfortable through social invitations and inappropriate remarks. The Senior Budget Analyst further testified that she had personally observed the Deputy Director engaging in verbal tantrums and yelling, directing this aggressive conduct at the Complainant as if she were a "soundboard" for his emotional discharge. The Senior Budget Analyst additionally attested that the Complainant visited her office on several occasions in a state of distress, crying over the Deputy Director's behavior. This testimony appears in the ROI at page 208.

The Team Lead similarly provided testimony that the Complainant approached him on multiple occasions to discuss the Deputy Director's inappropriate conduct. The Team Lead specifically recalled that the Complainant described inappropriate physical touching that had occurred during a workplace meeting. The Team Lead further testified that the Complainant had informed him that she had scheduled a meeting with the Management Officer to address the Deputy Director's conduct. The Team Lead noted that the Complainant subsequently reported to him that the Management Officer had shown little concern for addressing her harassment allegations. The Team Lead stated that he expressed his skepticism to the Complainant, noting that her efforts with the Management Officer appeared unlikely to produce any meaningful resolution. This account is documented in the ROI at page 205.

The Resource Management Team Lead provided additional corroborating testimony, describing herself as having frequently served as a "comforting ear" to the Complainant. This witness testified that the Complainant repeatedly expressed her extreme discomfort with the Deputy Director's conduct. The Resource Management Team Lead stated that she had frequently observed the Deputy Director in the Complainant's work cubicle on numerous occasions. While this witness did not directly observe any explicitly inappropriate physical contact or verbal statements, she noted that she found the Deputy Director's frequent encroachment upon the Complainant's personal space to be unusual and noteworthy. The ROI at page 223 contains this testimony.

The Public Health Advisor Supervisor testified that the Complainant had mentioned that the Deputy Director had touched her leg and had extended dinner invitations. Significantly, the Public Health Advisor Supervisor disclosed that she herself had been subjected to harassment by the Deputy Director in the past but had chosen not to file a complaint. The Public Health Advisor Supervisor explained that she feared being the first woman to come forward against the Deputy Director, particularly given his standing and favorable reputation within the Agency. This fear-based silence reflects a pattern of witness reluctance that may have enabled the continuation of the Deputy Director's conduct. The ROI at page 199 documents this testimony.

Additional witnesses, including another Team Lead and two other employees, provided testimony confirming that the Complainant had informed them of the Deputy Director's harassing behavior.

## PERFORMANCE EVALUATION AND DISCIPLINARY ACTIONS

### Mid-Year Performance Review and Performance Assistance Plan

The Complainant challenged the legitimacy of her mid-year performance evaluation and the associated disciplinary actions that followed. The Complainant alleges that the S1 supervisor improperly assigned her an unsatisfactory performance rating of 2.0 (out of a numerical scale) and subsequently placed her on a Performance Assistance Plan (PAP). These allegations appear in the ROI at pages 157-58.

The Complainant attributed the allegedly improper evaluation to racial animus on the part of the S1 supervisor. The Complainant reported that the S1 supervisor made comments to her, including "Stop trying to act Black," "Are you sure you're not Black?" and "Don't worry, we all know you're Black." These statements appear in the ROI at page 139. The Complainant interpreted these comments as evidence that the S1 supervisor harbored racial bias that influenced the performance evaluation.

The Performance Assistance Plan, dated May 4, 2014, outlined specific performance deficiencies. According to the PAP documentation found in the ROI at pages 335-40, the S1 supervisor documented that the Complainant had failed to respond to customers regarding their travel tickets within acceptable timeframes and had failed to edit documentation to ensure the accuracy and completeness of travel reports. The PAP set forth specific performance expectations that the Complainant was required to meet.

### Performance Improvement Plan

Following the PAP period, the Complainant was placed on a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) by the S1 supervisor on September 22, 2014. The PIP documentation, found in the ROI at pages 341-44, indicated that the Complainant was rated as having "Achieved Unsatisfactory Results" in her Customer Service rating. The S1 supervisor specifically documented that the Complainant had failed to document and resolve customer complaints without requiring assistance from her peers. The S1 supervisor further noted that the Complainant did not complete assigned tasks in a timely manner and failed to maintain effective communication with customers regarding the status of their travel arrangements.

The S1 supervisor provided concrete examples of the Complainant's alleged performance deficiencies. One example involved the Senior Federal Agency Travel Administrator (FATA) requesting that the Complainant correct a travel voucher for an employee. According to the documentation, the Complainant required continuous assistance from her colleagues to complete this task, which fell within her assigned job responsibilities.

### Counseling Memorandum

On October 16, 2014, the Complainant received a Counseling Memorandum from the S1 supervisor. The memorandum, documented in the ROI at pages 345-346, addressed the Complainant's conduct during the meeting in which the PIP was presented on September 25, 2014. The S1 supervisor documented that the Complainant spoke in a loud and rude tone throughout the entire meeting. The supervisor further characterized the Complainant's conduct as verbal abuse, noting that the Complainant was unable to control her temper. The Complainant allegedly made statements indicating that she believed she was being retaliated against.

The memorandum also documented that the Complainant complained about the S1 supervisor, specifically asserting that the S1 supervisor had permitted her peers to call her a "wigger," a derogatory term combining racial and ethnic slurs. The Complainant's complaint suggested that she felt unsupported and potentially ostracized by her supervisor.

However, a Support Services Specialist observed the situation from a different perspective. According to the Support Services Specialist's testimony in the ROI at page 202, the Complainant was operating under extreme emotional distress due to the S1 supervisor's treatment. The Support Services Specialist specifically recalled an incident in the office copy room during which the Complainant entered in a visibly distressed state, shaking so intensely that the Support Services Specialist thought she might need to call emergency medical services. The Support Services Specialist stated that the Complainant discussed the harassment she was experiencing from the S1 supervisor and disclosed the frequency with which the harassment was occurring.

### Supervisory Transfer

On December 12, 2014, the Complainant was informed that she would be transferred to another team, thereby removing her from the S1 supervisor's direct supervision. According to the Complainant's account, documented in the ROI at pages 146-47, she was advised that this transfer would provide her with a new start and an opportunity for a fresh beginning. The Complainant noted that following the transfer, she observed a significantly more positive work environment. However, the Complainant subsequently received her end-of-year performance evaluation from the S1 supervisor, which bore the unsatisfactory rating of 2.0. The evaluation is documented in the ROI at pages 148-49.

## FORMAL COMPLAINT AND ALLEGATIONS SUMMARY

On April 8, 2014, the Complainant filed a formal Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) complaint alleging that the Agency had discriminated against her and subjected her to harassment on the bases of multiple protected characteristics. These protected characteristics included race (identified as Caucasian), national origin (identified as German and Persian), sex (female), religion (identified as Jehovah's Witness and Jewish), and reprisal for engaging in prior protected EEO activity.

The formal complaint enumerated eleven distinct alleged incidents or patterns of discriminatory conduct, spanning from October 2013 through January 2015. These allegations encompassed supervisory misconduct by the S1 supervisor, alleged sexual harassment by the Deputy Director, alleged coworker harassment, alleged religious discrimination, and alleged retaliation through performance evaluations and disciplinary actions.

## AGENCY INVESTIGATION AND FINAL DECISION

### Investigation Process

Following the filing of the formal complaint, the Agency initiated a comprehensive investigation. Upon completion of the investigation, the Complainant was provided with a copy of the Report of Investigation and was notified of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). In accordance with the Complainant's request for an administrative hearing, the Agency issued a Final Agency Decision (FAD) pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b).
