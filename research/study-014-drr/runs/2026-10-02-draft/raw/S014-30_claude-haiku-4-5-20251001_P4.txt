# CASE FILE SUMMARY

**COMPLAINANT INFORMATION:**
- Position: Legal Assistant (Court), GS-0986-08
- Employing Agency: Orlando Immigration Court, Orlando, Florida
- Complainant Age: 49

**ALLEGED BASES FOR DISCRIMINATION:**
Race (African-American), sex (male), age (49), and reprisal based on prior protected EEO activity under Title VII, the ADEA, and the Rehabilitation Act

**COMPLAINT 2 (EOI-2016-00061):**
Filed November 5, 2015

**EVENTS GIVING RISE TO COMPLAINT:**
October 22, 2015 – Complainant received a mid-year review from S1 (Hispanic, female, Supervisory Legal Assistant) that:
- Negatively and falsely characterized his professionalism and customer service
- Complainant was not permitted to give evidence refuting the mid-year review
- Review was conducted in a conference room rather than S1's office
- S2 (Caucasian, male, Court Administrator) was present in the conference room

**MID-YEAR REVIEW DETAILS:**

*Rating:* "At a successful level or higher"

*Critical Performance Elements and Comments by S1:*
- Customer Service: Needed to communicate with attorneys and return telephone calls
- Establish, Update, Maintain and Process Record of Proceedings and Case Records: Needed to keep up with motions, not hold them, and not sign nor let orders sit overnight on desk
- Legal and Technical Support: Needed to be a team player by limiting cell phone and internet usage
- S1 also wrote that Complainant should continue his hard work and strive for excellence

*Uncontroverted Fact:* EOIR employees do not receive a rating of record at their mid-year review

**EVIDENCE PRESENTED BY COMPLAINANT:**

Complainant pointed to a September 2015 letter from a defendant to an immigration judge complimenting the judge's handling of a case for nearly five years and expressing "thankfulness… to your direct assistant, who was always very friendly and correct in handling my affairs" (Report of Investigation (ROI) Complaint 1, Bates No. 10 and Bates Nos. 90–91, ¶¶ 24–27, Complainant's affidavit). Complainant had started assisting the judge in June 2014 when hired by EOIR.

Complainant alleged that on October 22, 2015, S2 refused to accept this letter, explaining it "failed… as creditable" evidence.

**COMPLAINANT'S REBUTTAL:**

In his rebuttal investigatory affidavit (made after reviewing S1's, S2's, and a third management official's affidavits), Complainant countered that these affidavits contained lies and inconsistent statements.

**AJ FINDINGS:**

Decision-Maker: EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ)

Criteria Applied: Title VII, ADEA, Rehabilitation Act

*On the Rating:* Complainant unfairly did not receive a rating of Excellent or Outstanding at mid-year review; however, Complainant only contested the assessment of his Customer Service.

*On the Letter Evidence:* Assuming Complainant tried to submit the letter, it would not have changed his rating since none is given at the mid-year review, nor would it have refuted that he needed to better communicate with attorneys and return telephone calls, since it only concerned his interaction with a defendant in court.

*On the Conference Room Location:* Mid-year reviews are typically given in a first line supervisor's office. The AJ found that management chose the conference room to assure privacy, not to harass or discriminate. Complainant acknowledged the conference room gave privacy.

*On S2's Presence:* Typically, mid-year reviews are given with only the evaluator (S1) and person receiving it present. The AJ found that S2 was present because he had a responsibility to closely monitor things between Complainant and S1 since Complainant's misconduct with S1 was abusive and caustic, and his disciplinary issues. Complainant's rebuttal that the affidavits had lies and inconsistent statements did not rebut S1 and S2's explanations for why S2 was present.

**DISPOSITION:**

The AJ granted the Agency's motion for summary judgment and found no discrimination without a hearing. The Agency adopted this decision in its final order.

**APPEAL ARGUMENTS:**

*Complainant's Arguments:*
- S2 admitted treating him differently (cites portions of S2's investigatory affidavit acknowledging reviews are normally done in the evaluator's office with only the evaluator and person being evaluated present)
- Complainant got different treatment on these things
- Raises question regarding equal justice where Agency counsel and AJ are white
- Argues genuine issues of material fact entitle him to a hearing, citing S2's admission that he was treated differently

*Agency's Response:*
The Agency argues its final order should be affirmed.

**STATUS:**
Appeal pending following the AJ's decision.
