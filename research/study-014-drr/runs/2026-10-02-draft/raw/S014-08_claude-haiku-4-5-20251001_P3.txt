# OFFICIAL CASE FILE DOCUMENT
## Investigation Record and Administrative Decision Summary

---

## I. BACKGROUND AND EMPLOYMENT CONTEXT

This case involves a formal Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) complaint filed by a Counseling Psychologist and Licensed Marriage and Family Therapist who was employed at the Agency's Health Clinic located in Baumholder, Germany. At the time the events giving rise to this complaint occurred, the Complainant held a professional clinical position responsible for providing mental health services, including marriage and family counseling, to the clinic's patient population.

The period covered by this investigation spans from September 2014 through January 2017, representing a significant period during which the Complainant experienced various workplace challenges and filed multiple formal complaints with different Agency offices and oversight bodies, including an Administrative Grievance filed in November 2014 and subsequent complaints to the Regional Health Command-Europe Inspector General (IG) office in October 2016.

---

## II. ALLEGATIONS AND COMPLAINTS

### A. General Workplace Environment Concerns (September 2014 - Present)

The Complainant alleged that beginning in September 2014 and continuing throughout her employment, management failed to provide her with necessary professional development and training opportunities. She asserted that she was subjected to what she characterized as demeaning treatment, including derogatory comments, unjustified interrogations, and excessive criticism of her work performance. The Complainant further claimed to experience non-verbal forms of hostility, including negative facial expressions from supervisory and managerial personnel.

Additionally, the Complainant alleged that her professional authority was systematically undermined through various mechanisms. She contended that coworkers and other employees were encouraged to bypass the established chain of command, and that her clinical decisions regarding patient care were frequently overruled without appropriate justification. According to her account, these actions made it impossible for her to effectively execute the duties and responsibilities of her position as a clinical mental health professional.

The Complainant further alleged that she was required to expend considerable energy defending herself against what she characterized as false portrayals and claims made by supervisors and other employees. She asserted that Agency officials used their positions of authority to disparage her professional reputation and to isolate her within the workplace. She claimed that despite her work efforts, her contributions were neither recognized nor appropriately rewarded. The Complainant reported these concerns through her supervisory chain on multiple occasions, yet alleged that the Agency failed to conduct thorough investigations or to address and remediate the underlying causes of her concerns.

### B. Client Assignment and Scheduling Issues

The Complainant alleged that on unspecified dates, other employees unilaterally rescheduled her clients with alternative providers without her knowledge or consent. As a result of these actions, when patients requested to continue their therapeutic relationship with the Complainant, they were informed that such arrangements were not permitted. This action effectively removed the Complainant's caseload and professional responsibilities without formal clinical review or documented clinical justification.

### C. October 5, 2016 Staff Meeting Incident

During a staff meeting held on October 5, 2016, the Complainant alleged that a Captain made clinical decisions regarding her patients without soliciting or considering her input, despite her being the primary treating clinician for these patients. The Complainant viewed this action as a failure to respect her professional clinical judgment and authority.

### D. October 12, 2016 Meeting with Command Staff

On October 12, 2016, the Complainant met with the Lieutenant Colonel (identified as Deputy Chief of Clinical Services and Chief Nursing Officer) and the Lieutenant (identified as Commander of Baumholder and Kaiserslautern Clinics) to discuss her request for reassignment from the Baumholder Team. During this meeting, the Complainant alleged that she felt personally attacked, primarily through what she perceived as disrespectful tone of voice and aggressive demeanor from both officials.

### E. November 12, 2016 Performance Evaluation

The Complainant challenged the accuracy and truthfulness of a performance evaluation issued on or about November 12, 2016. She asserted that the evaluation failed to fairly and accurately characterize her work performance based on her understanding of her actual job performance.

### F. December 21, 2016 Caseload Reduction

Upon returning to work on December 21, 2016, following a two-day sick leave absence (December 19-20, 2016), the Complainant discovered that two clients had been removed from her schedule and reassigned to other providers. The Complainant further alleged that despite an acknowledged shortage of behavioral health providers within the office due to holiday staffing shortages, staff members refused to engage her to assist in covering cases or addressing the documented provider shortage.

### G. Staff Integration Issues

The Complainant alleged that the Non-Commissioned Officer-In-Charge (NCOIC) brought a new staff member to the clinic and proceeded to introduce this individual to all providers and other staff members within the office. However, despite the fact that the NCOIC was conversing with the Complainant directly outside her office at the time, the new staff member was not introduced to the Complainant. The Complainant claimed not to know the identity of this new staff member as a result of this alleged slight.

### H. Investigation and Legal Representation Issues

In October 2016, the Complainant contacted the Regional Health Command-Europe Inspector General regarding allegations of discrimination and harassment. On December 6, 2016, the Sergeant First Class, representing the IG office, contacted the Complainant to discuss her complaint. The Complainant indicated that her legal counsel would be in Germany during the week of January 9, 2017, and she desired to coordinate a meeting with the IG office. Her counsel subsequently emailed the Sergeant First Class seeking clarification regarding the purpose and nature of the proposed meeting. However, the Sergeant First Class did not respond to this communication, and the anticipated meeting did not occur.

### I. Developmental Counseling and Reassignment

On January 27, 2017, the Complainant received developmental counseling regarding a temporary reassignment that had been implemented on January 4, 2017, to the Landstuhl Regional Medical Center (LRMC).

---

## III. PRIOR ADMINISTRATIVE ACTION

The Complainant's initial concerns regarding workplace conditions were previously addressed through the Agency's Administrative Grievance process. On November 28, 2014, the Complainant filed a formal Administrative Grievance that included many of the claims subsequently included in the present EEO complaint. In response to this grievance, on January 30, 2015, the Complainant received a notice of decision stating that she had been granted her requested remedy, which consisted of formal mediation to work through her concerns and improve the work environment. The Administrative Grievance was closed following this resolution.

---

## IV. INVESTIGATION FINDINGS AND AGENCY RESPONSES

### A. Training and Professional Development

Regarding the Complainant's allegations concerning denial of training opportunities, the Commander of the Baumholder, Landstuhl, and Kaiserslautern Clinics stated that the Complainant had expressed her desire to participate in a non-military professional development session regarding suicide and military personnel. The Commander indicated that while the Agency could not fund her participation in what he characterized as personal research, he offered to provide her the time necessary to participate, though without financial support.

In a separate request, the Lieutenant stated that while he had approved the Complainant's request to attend a Suicide Prevention training scheduled in Vicenza, Italy, no funds were available for attendance. The Lieutenant further noted that the training was ultimately cancelled, rendering the funding issue moot.

### B. Client Transfer Incidents and September 20, 2016 Email

On September 20, 2016, the Complainant sent an email to the Supervisory Psychologist and Chief of Behavioral Health alleging that a Nurse Practitioner had informed two of her patients that they were being transferred to another provider for treatment, and had informed another patient that the Complainant was no longer working at the clinic and could not see him.

During the investigation, the Social Services Assistant noted that the Complainant had previously stated that she worked exclusively with marriage and family counseling patients, which limitation had led to some patient transfers and restricted the patient population she could serve. The Nurse Practitioner similarly confirmed this restriction in her professional scope of practice.

The Lieutenant Colonel reported that he had investigated the client transfer situation and resolved the matter. In one instance, he determined that a transfer had been unintentional, and the client was reassigned back to the Complainant's care.

### C. October 5, 2016 High-Risk Meeting and ICE Complaints

On October 5 and 7, 2016, two separate Interactive Customer Evaluation (ICE) complaints were submitted by two different patients of the Complainant's regarding her standard of care to the Inspector General office.

On October 5, 2016, during a standard High-Risk meeting, a Captain discussed clinical concerns regarding two of the Complainant's patients. Immediately following this meeting, the Complainant spoke with the Captain. The Captain asserted that the Complainant had been aggressive during their conversation, had recorded the discussion without her consent, and had spoken loudly about the two patients in an open space within earshot of other patients. The Captain denied making any changes to patient care decisions as a result of the discussion.

The Captain characterized the October 5 meeting as a standard coordination of care discussion that routinely occurred with all providers. The Social Services Assistant confirmed that High-Risk meetings were conducted to enable various providers to coordinate appropriate patient care, and that all providers were expected to contribute their clinical perspectives. She further stated that no provider was being told how to conduct their clinical work.

Following this meeting, the Complainant spoke with the Lieutenant Colonel, expressing that she no longer wished to participate in High-Risk meetings and requesting reassignment from the Baumholder Team.

### D. October 12, 2016 Reassignment Meeting

During the October 12, 2016 meeting with the Lieutenant Colonel and Lieutenant to discuss the Complainant's request for removal from the Baumholder Team, both officials noted that while the Complainant repeatedly alleged that the work environment was hostile, she was unable to provide specific examples or detailed instances when pressed for such information.

During this meeting, the Complainant asserted that both the Lieutenant Colonel and another Colonel had been hostile toward her. Both officials denied conducting themselves in an aggressive manner, stated that they did not wish to continue the conversation if she felt uncomfortable, and offered to invite a neutral third party to be present during future discussions to ensure a more objective environment.

### E. Inspector General Investigation and Report

The IG issued its formal report on November 15, 2016, based on the complaints submitted on October 5, 7, and 13, 2016. The IG investigation concluded that there was insufficient evidence to support the Complainant's allegations of harassment or discrimination by Agency personnel.

However, the IG report did identify that the Complainant had created a disruptive work environment within the Baumholder Behavioral Health Clinic. The report noted that the Complainant's interactions with other behavioral health staff members were contentious in nature. The primary recommendation issued by the IG was to reassign the Complainant out of the Baumholder Health Clinic, citing concerns that her continued assignment to the clinic would result in ongoing conflict with other team members, which demonstrably impacted patient care and significantly decreased morale throughout the clinical unit.

Notably, the IG report also found that the Complainant had violated German law by unlawfully recording a conversation without the other party's consent on November 15, 2016.

### F. Performance Evaluation of November 12, 2016

The Lieutenant Colonel issued a performance evaluation to the Complainant on or about November 12, 2016, based on fifteen standard performance metrics. The Lieutenant Colonel documented that the Complainant received eleven "Exceed" ratings and four "Met" ratings across these metrics. The areas marked as "Met" specifically related to collaboration with staff. The Lieutenant Colonel provided detailed examples to support his ratings, noting that for the period from May 2016 through October 31, 2016, there was minimal collaboration with staff.

Despite the four "Met" ratings, the Complainant ultimately received an overall "Excellent" rating on the performance evaluation. The Lieutenant, serving as the Complainant's second-line rater, concurred with the evaluation and its conclusions.

### G. December 21, 2016 Client Reassignments

Regarding the Complainant's allegation that two clients were removed from her schedule upon her return from sick leave on December 21, 2016, the Lieutenant Colonel, the Colonel, and the Chief of Behavioral Health all attested during the investigation that they were unaware of any issues or incidents occurring on that particular date. No documentation was provided establishing the factual basis for the alleged client reassignments.

### H. New Staff Member Introduction Incident

Concerning the allegation that the NCOIC failed to introduce a new staff member to the Complainant despite speaking with her outside her office, the investigation revealed that the Complainant acknowledged she did not leave her office to introduce herself to the new employee, nor did she inform the NCOIC or any other staff member of her concern regarding the lack of introduction.

### I. Developmental Counseling of January 27, 2017

The Chief of Behavioral Health stated that the developmental counseling provided to the Complainant on January 27, 2017, regarding her temporary reassignment was standard in nature and contained nothing negative. The reassignment itself had been implemented on January 4, 2017, to the Landstuhl Regional Medical Center.

## V. ADMINISTRATIVE HEARING AND FINAL DECISION

Following the completion of the EEO investigation, the Complainant was provided with a copy of the investigation report and was notified of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). The Complainant timely requested such a hearing.

On October 18, 2021, the Agency submitted a Motion for a Decision Without a Hearing, contending that the investigative record was sufficiently developed to permit resolution of the matter without further proceedings. The Complainant submitted a response to this motion.

On January 31, 2022, the Administrative Judge issued his formal decision. The AJ determined that the investigative record was adequately developed and contained sufficient evidence to permit a decision on the merits. After making all reasonable inferences in favor of the Complainant, as required by EEO regulations, the AJ concluded that there were no material facts in dispute that would preclude summary disposition.

Regarding the substantive merits of the Complainant's discrimination claim, the AJ determined that the Agency had articulated legitimate, non-discriminatory reasons for each of the employment actions challenged by the Complainant. The AJ further determined that the Complainant had failed to present sufficient evidence to demonstrate that the Agency's stated reasons were pretextual or were merely a cover for unlawful discriminatory motivation.

With respect to the Complainant's hostile work environment claim, the AJ determined that the Complainant had failed to establish the severe and pervasive nature of the alleged conduct necessary to sustain a legal claim for unlawful hostile work environment harassment. The AJ characterized the Complainant's complaint as primarily reflecting her dissatisfaction with her coworkers and management, as well as her expression of personal grievances, rather than demonstrating unlawful workplace conduct.

The Administrative Judge granted the Agency's Motion for a Decision Without a Hearing. When the Agency failed to issue a final administrative action, the Administrative Judge's decision became the Agency's final administrative action by operation of law, in accordance with 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110.
