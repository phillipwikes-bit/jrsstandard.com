# FINDINGS

## Overview
This investigative report examines five claims of age discrimination and harassment filed by Complainant, a Level 1 City Carrier at the Agency's Meadow Mesa Station facility in North Las Vegas, Nevada, alleging discriminatory treatment based on his age (born 1973). The investigation produced documentary evidence, affidavits, and statements from involved parties regarding events occurring between August 30, 2022, and November 1, 2022.

## Claim 1: Emergency Placement on August 30, 2022

**Complainant's Account:**
Complainant stated that he requested his supervisor (S1) sign or initial a union time request. When S1 indicated she would do so later and instructed him to return to work, Complainant questioned why he was being held to policy while S1 was not. The Postmaster (PM) subsequently intervened and, after an alleged confrontational exchange in the locker room involving raised voices and physical intimidation, demanded Complainant's badge and ordered him to vacate the premises. Complainant was placed on Emergency Placement (EP) in off-duty status without pay. Complainant asserted his age was a factor, claiming he was subjected to overburdened work assignments and constant unfavorable comparisons to younger carriers.

**Agency's Account:**
PM stated he observed Complainant engaging in time-wasting practices and failing to follow instructions in accordance with Agency policy, which precipitated the EP placement. PM denied the intimidation allegations, asserting he maintains distance from employees due to COVID precautions. S1 confirmed she instructed Complainant multiple times to deliver his route, noting he became argumentative with PM and failed to comply with her directives. S1 stated she had no knowledge of alleged locker room conduct. Both PM and S1 indicated that union steward consultation was unavailable at the time of departure and Complainant was instructed to contact the union after leaving the premises.

**Investigative Findings:**
Complainant provided no specific examples of similarly situated younger employees treated more favorably under comparable circumstances. The evidence established that management observed time-wasting conduct and directive non-compliance prior to the EP placement. While Complainant disputed the characterization of events, the factual predicate for the disciplinary action—failure to follow instructions—remained substantially undisputed.

## Claim 2: Suspension on September 14, 2022

**Complainant's Account:**
Complainant alleged he was suspended for failing to fasten his seatbelt before starting the vehicle engine and for exiting the vehicle while the engine remained running. Complainant stated he requested a union steward but was denied. Complainant asserted the discipline violated Agency policy as outlined in Handbook M-41, Section 812, which requires seatbelts be worn while the vehicle is in motion, not necessarily before engine ignition.

**Agency's Account:**
S2 stated he observed Complainant exit the mail truck and walk in front of it while the engine was operating—a safety violation. S2 affirmed that Agency policy mandates seatbelt use at all times while operating a vehicle. S2 indicated that due to the immediate EP placement, union contact was required to occur off postal property. S1 stated she was not present during the incident and had no direct knowledge of the occurrence.

**Investigative Findings:**
The record reflects that a Notice of 14-Day Suspension was issued on September 22, 2022, citing the August 30, 2022, failure to follow instructions as the basis for suspension rather than the seatbelt incident. The seatbelt incident served as grounds for the September 14 EP placement. Complainant did not assert that age was a contributing factor to this incident and provided no comparative examples of younger employees treated more favorably. The safety violation itself—operating a vehicle without a seatbelt fastened—was substantiated and remained undisputed.

## Claim 3: Bathroom Confrontation on September 20, 2022

**Complainant's Account:**
Complainant alleged that S2 followed him into the bathroom on two separate occasions on September 20, 2022, yelled at him, and threatened disciplinary action. Complainant attributed this conduct to his age, theorizing that supervisors subjected him to heightened scrutiny due to increased bathroom frequency associated with aging.

**Agency's Account:**
S2 denied raising his voice and stated that if he followed Complainant, it was due to performance concerns. S2 indicated that rather than conducting bathroom business, Complainant spent extended periods in the locker room and appeared to be accessing his locker. S1 stated she had no knowledge of incidents occurring in the bathroom.

**Investigative Findings:**
The accounts of the bathroom incidents directly conflict. The investigation produced no corroborating evidence of the alleged yelling or threats. Complainant offered no specific comparable examples of younger employees who engaged in similar extended locker room activities yet escaped supervisory scrutiny. The assertion that age-related physiological changes prompted discriminatory treatment remained speculative and unsupported by evidence of differential treatment of younger employees.

## Claim 4: Seatbelt Confrontation on September 24, 2022

**Complainant's Account:**
Complainant stated that S2 yelled at him for failing to fasten his seatbelt before starting the engine. Complainant asserted that S2 misinterpreted Agency policy, citing Handbook M-41, Section 812, which requires seatbelts be fastened before the vehicle is placed in motion, not necessarily before engine start. Complainant further stated he contacted the National Association of Letter Carriers (NALC) office, which confirmed his interpretation. Complainant asserted that S1 subsequently told him to follow supervisor instructions and fasten his seatbelt before starting the engine.

**Agency's Account:**
S2 stated he initially believed seatbelts must be fastened before engine start. S2 indicated he thereafter contacted safety personnel and was informed that no written Agency policy specified this requirement. S2 stated he communicated this information to Complainant, who was then permitted to proceed with his route. S1 confirmed that S2 requested her assessment of Complainant's conduct; she observed no violation. S1 indicated she subsequently directed Complainant to follow supervisor instructions regarding seatbelt fastening, though she expressed uncertainty as to whether the requirement was uniformly applied across all carriers.

**Investigative Findings:**
The evidence reveals disagreement regarding the correct interpretation of the seatbelt policy and confusion among supervisors regarding its application. However, S2 clarified the matter with safety personnel, and Complainant was afforded the opportunity to continue his route. Complainant did not assert that age was a factor in this incident and provided no examples of younger employees treated differently regarding policy interpretation or supervisory directives.

## Claim 5: Escort from Premises on November 1, 2022

**Complainant's Account:**
Complainant presented S1 with a doctor's note restricting him from overtime due to repetitive motion pain in his right shoulder. Complainant alleged S1 refused to honor the medical restriction and demanded he continue delivering until his route was completed. When Complainant expressed concern that working overtime contradicted medical guidance, S1 demanded his badge and he was escorted from the premises. Complainant was placed on EP, effective November 2, 2022. Complainant attributed this action to age, asserting that as an older worker, he recovers more slowly from injuries and should not be subjected to tasks contradicting medical restrictions.

**Agency's Account:**
S1 stated that Complainant indicated, based on union guidance, that he could return undelivered mail and was not required to follow management directives. S1 asserted she requested three times that Complainant finish his route. S1 indicated she submitted Complainant's medical documentation to Occupational Health Management (OHM) for guidance. S1 stated that OHM advised her that Complainant could complete his route that day and that OHM would provide guidance on whether the doctor's note constituted a limited duty job offer or required formal light duty request submission to the PM.

**Investigative Findings:**
The evidence indicates a dispute regarding the applicability and priority of the medical restriction relative to work directives. S1 followed established protocol by submitting the medical documentation to OHM, which advised that Complainant could complete his route pending further guidance on the formal classification of the restriction. Complainant did not dispute that he refused to follow S1's instructions but asserted the instructions were unsafe given medical constraints. Complainant provided no examples of younger employees with medical restrictions who were treated more favorably or exempted from work assignments pending OHM review. While disability concerns may be implicated, Complainant did not raise disability as a basis for his EEO complaint.

## Summary of Investigation Results

Across all five claims, the investigation established:

1. Complainant was observed engaging in conduct the Agency characterized as policy violations and work performance deficiencies.
2. Complainant did not dispute the underlying factual predicates for disciplinary actions (time-wasting, failure to follow instructions, seatbelt violations, refusal to complete assigned work).
3. No documentary evidence of age-related comments by management was identified.
4. Complainant provided no specific examples of similarly situated younger employees who received more favorable treatment under comparable circumstances.
5. Management's stated justifications for each action were grounded in workplace conduct, safety policy, and work performance rather than age-related considerations.
