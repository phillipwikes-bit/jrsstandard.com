**Case Summary**

**Parties/Position:** Complainant, a Mail Handler at the Agency's Incoming Mail Processing & Distribution Center in Linthicum, MD, was removed from employment (removal effective September 2015) for improper conduct and violations of the Agency's zero-tolerance policy and the joint statement on violence and behavior in the workplace. He alleged discrimination based on race, gender, and genetic information (size, under GINA).

**Incident (Aug. 19, 2015):** After a meeting about mislabeled containers, Complainant reportedly called his second-line supervisor (Manager) "sorry" and/or "a sorry ass," did not promptly leave the work floor when instructed, and repeatedly called Manager a "Bitch" and used profanity while being escorted to the conference room and out of the building. He punched a hole in a wall during the escort. Complainant disputed the wording, saying he said "This is bullshit," and did not recall his exact words while being escorted, but acknowledged taking "a swipe" at the wall.

**Agency Actions:** Complainant was placed on Emergency Placement status. A Pre-Disciplinary Interview was held Aug. 24, 2015, with a union representative present, where removal was indicated. On Aug. 31, 2015, Supervisor recommended removal to the Labor Manager. The removal notice cited profanity, failure to follow instructions, and punching a wall, and noted prior discipline: a 10-day suspension (Failure of Obedience to Orders) and a 14-day suspension (Workplace Violence Zero Tolerance Policy violation).

**Procedural History:** The Agency accepted and investigated the complaint. Complainant requested an EEOC hearing. The Agency moved for a decision without a hearing on April 24, 2017; Complainant did not object. The AJ granted the motion and issued a decision on August 21, 2020.

**AJ Findings:**
- The Agency articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons: confrontational, disrespectful, and violent conduct violating the Zero Tolerance Policy.
- Complainant offered no evidence that race or gender played a role.
- On the GINA claim, he failed to establish a prima facie case: he did not show he was entitled to statutory protection, that he suffered a qualifying adverse action, or an inference of discrimination based on genetic information.
- Complainant did not prove discrimination by a preponderance of the evidence regarding the Notice of Removal.

**Outcome:** The Agency issued a final order adopting the AJ's finding of no discrimination.
