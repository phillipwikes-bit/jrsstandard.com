# Case-File Summary

## Parties and Background
- Two Complainants worked as Emergency Management Specialists (Regional and National Tribal Government Liaisons) at the Agency's First Responder Network Authority. They worked remotely from Pauma Valley, California and Thousand Palms, California.
- Both began Agency employment in October 2015 at the Pay Band IV level.

## Complaints
- Starting on June 13, 2019, each Complainant filed a formal EEO complaint. They alleged discrimination and harassment based on race (Native American), national origin (Native American), and sex, and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity.
- The Complainants withdrew their sex discrimination claims during the hearing process (record footnote 3).

## Claims (as alleged by Complainants)
1. **Pay discrimination since hire**
   - (a) Complainant 1 said he negotiated salary before hiring and was told the Agency was only obligated to offer 70% of his previous wages and he could "take it or leave it." He alleged non-Natives were not treated this way. Complainant 2 claimed the Agency failed to account for changes in her prior salary or teaching wages and offered only a salary commensurate with her current tribal salary.
   - (b) Complainants did the same work as peers hired at Pay Band V but were hired at Pay Band IV and paid at the lowest level.
   - (c) In or around April 2018, the Executive Director, Public Safety Advocacy Directorate, directed Supervisor 1 to review wage and pay band disparities. Complainants allege that the Executive Director and a Senior Director took no action afterward and failed to acknowledge the review.
   - (d) In a May 31, 2018 telephone conversation, the Senior Director said he was unaware of the review. Complainants say Supervisor 1 later told them he was "very well aware" of it.
   - (e) On June 15, 2018, Complainants asked the Deputy Chief Administrative Officer about hiring and direct hiring authorities. They were told the Agency "does not have personnel policies and practices." They also allege he failed to tell them of their right to pursue the EEO process on pay.
   - (f) In or around July 2018, Supervisor 2 asked the Executive Director and Senior Director about the April 2018 review. They denied any knowledge of committing to it.
   - (g) In January 2019, the Executive Director acknowledged to Supervisor 2 that he had received the review but felt no obligation to act on it. Complainants allege he instructed Supervisor 2 to "tamper any escalation of [human resources (HR)] issues for the tribal team," then backpedaled in a later email, claiming Supervisor 2 had misunderstood.
2. Complainants were precluded from applying to senior leadership positions because those positions were open only to Pay Band V employees, which they attribute to discriminatory pay-setting at Pay Band IV.
3. In June 2018, a reorganization "buried deeper" Complainants in the organization to provoke them to quit and to "limit any HR escalations from [the] tribal team."
4. On an ongoing basis since at least June 2018, Complainants were tasked with creating separate strategic plans, while non-Tribal team peers were not.
5. On multiple occasions between June 2018 and February 2019, Complainants and other Agency officials told senior leadership that Tribal team members faced hostile and discriminatory treatment from officials of an Agency contractor. Senior leadership allegedly condoned this and kept placing the team in hostile situations with contracting officials.
6. Ongoing denial of equal access to resources, in that:
   - (a) Complainants had to coordinate all activities through a separate, discriminatory process to reach resources and non-federal shareholders.
   - (b) They had to work with contracting staff and Tribal shareholders through a "gatekeeper."
   - (c) The Agency limited the types of meetings they could attend.
   - (d) An Agency Contracting Officer's Representative was required to join daily engagements between contractors and Complainants.
7. On May 5, 2019, the Agency issued a tweet thanking local, state, and federal workers but did not reference the Tribal team, which Complainants allege failed to acknowledge the team's accomplishments.

**Note on claim 1 (record footnote 4):** The Agency accepted claim 1 with subparts (a) through (g). One AJ's Case Management Order did not list the subparts. That AJ treated them as consolidated, with claim 1 including the allegation that management took no remedial action after reviewing wage and pay band disparities. The record states that subparts 1(c) through 1(g) fall under that allegation.

## Procedural History
- After the investigations, Complainants received copies of the investigative files and requested hearings before an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ).
- The two complaints were assigned to different AJs in the Commission's Washington Field Office.
- Complainants' shared attorney moved to consolidate the complaints before each AJ. The Agency opposed. One AJ denied the motion, and the other did not address it.
- The AJs issued separate summary judgment decisions finding that no discrimination or unlawful retaliation was established.
- The Agency's final actions implemented the AJs' decisions.
- Both Complainants then appealed.
