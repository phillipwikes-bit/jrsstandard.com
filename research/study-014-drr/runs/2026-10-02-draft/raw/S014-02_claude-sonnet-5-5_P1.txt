**Case Summary**

**Parties/Background:** Complainant, a Carrier Technician at the Agency's Garland-South Station (Garland, Texas), filed an EEO complaint on April 17, 2021, alleging disability discrimination (physical: neck and shoulder) under the Rehabilitation Act.

**Accepted Claims (Nov. 2020 – Apr. 2021):**
1. Continued questioning about work restrictions and no work assistance (from ~Nov. 7, 2020).
2. Required to do the walking portion of her route (from ~Nov. 9, 2020).
3. Yelled at and embarrassed before coworkers (Dec. 23, 2020).
4. Required to submit a new CA-17 and not paid for time missed to complete it (Dec. 23, 2020).
5. Moved off Route 74 and required to case and carry Route 23 (Dec. 23, 2020).
6. Called to the supervisor's desk to discuss medical documents (Feb. 1, 2021).
7. Harassed and referred to the District Reasonable Accommodation Committee (Mar. 23, 2021).
8. Leave request not granted and charged AWOL (Apr. 2, 2021).
9. Modified assignment changed start time to 10:00 am, with no mail cart provided (~Apr. 15, 2021).

**Dismissed Claim:** The Agency dismissed a separate claim that Complainant was attacked by a dog on the job and not properly compensated for missed time, as a collateral attack on the OWCP process (29 C.F.R. §1614.107(a)(1)). The dismissal was affirmed because the EEO process cannot be used to challenge OWCP adjudicatory actions (citing *Janiece H. v. U.S. Postal Serv.*, EEOC (Jan. 8, 2016)).

**Core Allegations:** Complainant contends the Manager of Customer Service harassed her because of her disability and forced her to work outside her medical restrictions. She specifically argues that the Manager marked her AWOL despite a leave request previously approved by the Supervisor, and that the Agency denied her a mail cart.

**Procedural History:** After the investigation, Complainant received the Report of Investigation and hearing rights, and she requested an EEOC AJ hearing. The AJ denied the hearing as a sanction for Complainant's failure to submit the required Preliminary Case Information and to respond to an Order to Show Cause, and remanded the matter to the Agency. The Agency issued a final decision under 29 C.F.R. §1614.110(b), finding Complainant did not prove discrimination as alleged.
