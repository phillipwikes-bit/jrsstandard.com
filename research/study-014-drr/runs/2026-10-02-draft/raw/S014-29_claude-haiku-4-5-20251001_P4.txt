# CASE FILE SUMMARY

## COMPLAINT AND INVESTIGATION INITIATION
On September 4, 2015, Complainant, a Mail Handler at the Agency's Incoming Mail Processing & Distribution Center in Linthicum, Maryland, filed a complaint alleging improper conduct, violation of the zero-tolerance policy, and violation of the joint statement on violence and behavior in the workplace. The Agency accepted the complaint for investigation.

## INCIDENT OF AUGUST 19, 2015

### Alleged Conduct
The investigative record reflects the following incidents occurring on August 19, 2015:

- Following a meeting with mail handlers regarding mistaken labels on containers, Complainant called Manager "sorry" and/or "a sorry ass." (ROI at 19-24, 78, 85, 89-90)
- Complainant did not immediately leave the work floor when Manager instructed him to do so. (ROI at 21, 78)
- After Manager threatened to call the police, Complainant walked with Supervisor to the conference room. (ROI at 78)
- In the conference room, Complainant said, in reference to Manager, "He's a Bitch! He's a Bitch!" (ROI at 78-79, 84, 90)
- On the way to the work floor to retrieve his belongings, Complainant yelled, "Manager is a Bitch—Bitch Ass!" (ROI at 79, 84, 90)
- Complainant punched a hole in the wall and said, "[Manager] is a Bitch—Fuck Him!" as he was escorted out of the building. (ROI at 80, 90)

### Complainant's Version
Complainant stated that he instead said, "This is bullshit," on the way to the conference room with Supervisor. (ROI at 23) He did not remember exactly what he said to himself while being escorted out of the building but acknowledged taking "a swipe" at a wall. (ROI at 23-24)

### Immediate Action
As a result of the confrontation, management placed Complainant on Emergency Placement status.

## PRE-DISCIPLINARY INTERVIEW
On August 24, 2015, Complainant attended a Pre-Disciplinary Interview with his union representative, Supervisor, and Supervisor 2 to discuss the August 19, 2015 incident. (ROI at 25, 79) At that meeting, Supervisor explained that the Agency would be recommending Complainant's removal. (ROI at 79)

## DISCIPLINARY ACTION
On August 31, 2015, Supervisor recommended removing Complainant to the Labor Manager. (ROI at 83-86) Supervisor issued the removal notice citing three bases: (1) Complainant's use of profanity; (2) failure to follow instructions; and (3) punching a hole in a wall. Supervisor also noted that Complainant had received prior discipline, including a 10-day suspension for Failure of Obedience to Orders and a 14-day suspension for a violation of the Workplace Violence Zero Tolerance Policy. The Notice of Removal was issued on September 11, 2015.

## POST-INVESTIGATION PROCEDURES
Following the investigation's conclusion, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of his right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant timely requested a hearing.

## ADMINISTRATIVE JUDGE DECISION
The AJ assigned to the case granted the Agency's April 24, 2017 motion for a decision without a hearing, which Complainant did not object to, and issued a decision without a hearing on August 21, 2020.

### Findings

**Legitimate, Nondiscriminatory Reasons for Termination:**
The AJ found that the Agency had legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for Complainant's termination, based on Complainant's confrontational, disrespectful, and violent conduct in the workplace, which violated the Agency's Zero Tolerance Policy.

**Race and Gender Discrimination:**
The AJ concluded that Complainant had not proffered any evidence to counter the Agency's legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons to suggest his race and/or gender played any role in the Agency's decision to terminate him.

**Genetic Information Discrimination (GINA):**
As to Complainant's assertion that he was discriminated against based on his size in violation of GINA, the AJ found that Complainant cannot establish a prima facie case of genetic information discrimination because he failed to show that: (1) he is entitled to protection under the statute; (2) he suffered an adverse employment action; and (3) the adverse employment action presents an inference of discrimination on the basis of his genetic information.

**Overall Conclusion:**
After viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to Complainant, the AJ held that Complainant did not prove, by a preponderance of the evidence, that he was discriminated against on the basis of race, gender, or genetic information when he was issued a Notice of Removal on September 11, 2015.

## FINAL ORDER
The Agency subsequently issued a final order adopting the AJ's finding that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected him to discrimination as alleged.
