# Investigation Record Summary

## Background

At the time of the events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Library Director, NF-1410-IV, at the Agency's Joint Base Andrews in Prince George's County, Maryland. Her complaint originally included a claim of reprisal for prior EEO activity. She withdrew that claim during a conference before the EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ).

## Claims

1. On May 31, July 3, and July 16, 2018, Complainant's first-level supervisor (S1), a Force Development Flight Chief, GS-0301-13, claimed to have given her verbal counseling. No documentation was generated in her personnel file.
2. On October 18, 2018, S1 gave Complainant a Letter of Reprimand.
3. On February 12, March 14, March 21, April 2, and April 3, 2019, S1 accepted incorrect statements from Complainant's subordinates.
4. On April 25, 2019, Complainant received a Notice of Proposed Removal signed by S1.
5. On or about June 28, 2019, Complainant was mailed a Decision to Remove letter, dated June 28, 2019, and signed by S1.
6. On October 22, 2019, Complainant learned that her Common Access Card (CAC) was still active. She asserted that this would allow others to modify her existing work, even though she was no longer with the Agency and had surrendered her CAC when removed from federal service.

## Procedural History

At the close of the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC AJ. Complainant requested a hearing. Over her objections, the assigned AJ granted the Agency's motion for a decision without a hearing and issued summary judgment for the Agency. The Agency then issued a final order fully implementing the AJ's finding that Complainant had not proven discrimination as alleged. This appeal followed.

## The AJ's Decision

Taking the facts as presented in the report of investigation and viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to Complainant, the AJ found that she failed to create a genuine issue of material fact establishing discrimination based on race, sex, color, or age.

The AJ analyzed Claim 6 in the decision. However, Complainant did not list that claim in her appeal brief and did not dispute the AJ's finding of no discrimination on it. It is therefore not addressed further.

For the remaining claims, the AJ found that Complainant could not prove disparate treatment or discriminatory harassment.

### Disparate Treatment (Claims 4 and 5)

The AJ assumed, for the sake of analysis, that Complainant could establish a prima facie case of discrimination as to Claims 4 and 5. The AJ nonetheless found that the Agency had articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions. According to the Notice of Proposed Removal, S1 decided to remove Complainant because of the following:

- "[f]ailure to meet suspenses [i.e., deadlines] in a timely manner";
- "cancelling scheduled events without permission from leadership";
- "not stagger[ing] schedules to meet the needs of [her] events and patrons";
- "not reliev[ing] staff members for lunches or breaks";
- not conducting "staff planning meetings for events";
- staff accusations that she "gossip[s] and release[s] privileged information about employees with other staff members";
- "[s]taff members fear[ing] retaliation, bad feedbacks and appraisals if they speak up"; and
- "[l]ack of attention to detail with funds management."

The AJ also found that Complainant could not identify similarly situated employees who were treated more favorably. She pointed to coworkers who were not disciplined after a fight in front of patrons. The AJ found that "Complainant was terminated for an entirely different set of circumstances and reasons" and that "both women [coworkers] hold a different type of position than Complainant." The coworkers were therefore not adequately similar.

### Hostile Work Environment

The AJ concluded that Complainant had not met her burden on her hostile work environment claim. Her harassment allegations consisted of the following:

- S1's statement in the Notice of Proposed Removal that she had verbally counseled Complainant on three occasions without documenting it in her personnel file;
- S1's issuance of a Letter of Reprimand for "leaving the library unattended to deal with a personal issue, thereby blocking [a summer] intern from the building";
- S1's acceptance, without further investigation, of allegedly inaccurate statements about Complainant's workplace conduct from her White subordinates; and
- Complainant's proposed removal and ultimate termination.

**No link to protected bases.** First, the AJ found that Complainant could not connect any of these allegations to her protected bases. For sex and age, the AJ determined that "Complainant fails to offer any evidence tying her age or sex to any of the listed events other than her own conclusory statements." For race and color, Complainant speculated that, because she is Black and her supervisor and subordinates are not, there was a conspiracy to get her fired. The AJ found that this "does not, by itself, show racial animus."

**The coworker's affidavit.** The AJ also considered an affidavit from a coworker who supported Complainant's contention that the work environment was racially hostile. The coworker averred that unnamed staff members said they disliked being supervised by an African American. He also averred that a subordinate coworker and S1 both complained that Complainant was not experienced enough and that her Caucasian predecessor had done a better job. The AJ found the affidavit "very confusing" and noted that the coworker "appears to have learned of these alleged instances second or third-hand, as opposed to witnessing them himself." Complainant offered no other evidence that the statements were made as alleged. The AJ therefore determined that "the uncorroborated statement of [the coworker], without more, does not establish racial animus on the part of [S1] or Complainant's subordinates."

**Severity and pervasiveness.** Beyond the lack of a nexus to her protected bases, the AJ found that "Complainant has failed to establish that she was subjected to severe and pervasive harassment in the form of unwelcome verbal or physical conduct sufficient to change her employment conditions into an abusive working environment." The Agency's Chief Human Resources Officer, GS-0201-12, stated in her affidavit that verbal counseling need not be added to personnel files. The AJ determined that the events were "no more than ordinary tribulations of the workplace which Complainant interpreted as harassing in nature, including disagreements with supervisors, supervisor attempts to manage the office, communication problems, and a supervisor's implementation of progressive discipline."

## Conclusion

The AJ concluded that Complainant had failed to prove by a preponderance of the evidence that she was subjected to a hostile work environment. Taken together with the findings on disparate treatment, the AJ found that she had not shown discrimination based on race, sex, color, or age.
