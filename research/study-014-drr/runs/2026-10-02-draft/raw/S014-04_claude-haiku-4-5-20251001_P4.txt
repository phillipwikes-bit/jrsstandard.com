# CASE FILE SUMMARY

**Complainant Status:** Level 1 City Carrier at Agency's Meadow Mesa Station facility in North Las Vegas, Nevada

**Complaint Filing Date:** February 25, 2023

**Basis of Complaint:** Discrimination and harassment on the basis of age (born 1973)

---

## ALLEGED INCIDENTS

**Claim 1: Emergency Placement – August 30, 2022**

*Complainant's Account (Investigative Affidavit):*
Complainant asked Supervisor S1 to sign or initial a request for Union time. S1 said she would do so later. Complainant asked why she could not sign it immediately. S1 repeated she would do so later and told him to get back to work. Postmaster (PM) asked what was going on. After Complainant explained, PM asked if S1 gave instructions to get back to work (Complainant said yes) and whether he was refusing (Complainant said no, but wanted to understand why he was being held to policy and not S1). In the locker room, PM asked why Complainant had not left and what he had been doing for the last hour. When Complainant tried to explain, PM interrupted him, yelled at him, and leaned over him to intimidate him. PM returned and told Complainant to deliver to the apartments that day. Complainant asked whether he needed to finish because it would put him over eight hours. PM accused him of failing to follow instructions, refused his request for a union steward, demanded his badge, yelled at him to clean out his locker, and threatened to call authorities to have him removed.

Complainant alleged his age was a factor because he was tasked with completing an overburdened route within eight hours and was compared constantly to younger carriers. Complainant did not provide specific examples of similarly situated employees outside his protected class treated more favorably.

*PM's Account (Affidavit):*
PM stated he observed Complainant engaging in time-wasting practices and failing to follow instructions. PM placed Complainant on Emergency Placement (EP) in off-duty status without pay for failure to follow instructions, in alignment with Agency policy. PM stated the intimidation claim was ridiculous and that since having COVID, he does not get close to any employee. PM stated a union steward was not available and Complainant was told he could speak with his union steward but had to leave the facility immediately.

*S1's Account (Affidavit):*
S1 confirmed she asked Complainant around three times to deliver his route, but he did not listen. S1 stated Complainant became argumentative with PM. S1 stated she had no knowledge of what might have occurred in the men's locker room. S1 stated no employee placed on EP was allowed to have a conversation with the union before leaving the premises.

---

**Claim 2: Suspension – September 14, 2022**

*Complainant's Account (Affidavit):*
On September 14, 2022, after completing vehicle inspection and while loading his mail truck, S1 and S2 approached him and S2 demanded his badge. S2 explained Complainant was being walked out because he did not fasten his seatbelt before starting the vehicle and got out of the driver's seat while the engine was running. Complainant asked for a union steward but S2 denied his request. Complainant understood that Section 812 of Handbook M-41 specified that seatbelts were to be worn at all times the vehicle was in motion. Complainant was placed on EP in off-duty status without pay.

Complainant received a Notice of 14-Day Suspension Letter on September 22, 2022, citing failure to follow instructions on August 30, 2022, as the reason (ROI at 00192-00193).

Complainant stated he did not think his age factored into this incident and did not provide specific examples of similarly situated employees outside his protected class treated more favorably.

*S2's Account (Affidavit):*
S2 stated Complainant was observed exiting the mail truck and walking in front of it while the engine was running. S2 stated this was a safety violation and it was Agency policy for seatbelts to be worn at all times while operating a vehicle. S2 stated that since Complainant was immediately placed on EP, he had to contact the union off postal property.

*S1's Account (Affidavit):*
S1 stated she had no knowledge of this incident since she was not present when it occurred.

---

**Claim 3: Bathroom Incident – September 20, 2022**

*Complainant's Account (Affidavit):*
On September 20, 2022, S2 followed him into the bathroom two separate times and yelled at him. S2 threatened to discipline him.

Complainant believed his age was a factor because now that he is older, he has to use the bathroom more often than when he was younger. Complainant did not provide specific examples of similarly situated employees outside his protected class treated more favorably.

*S2's Account (Affidavit):*
S2 denied raising his voice to Complainant and stated that if he followed him, it was because of Complainant's performance. S2 stated that rather than washing up in the bathroom, Complainant was spending a long time in the locker room and doing something in his locker.

---

**Claim 4: Seatbelt Instruction – September 24, 2022**

*Complainant's Account (Affidavit):*
On September 24, 2022, Complainant started the engine of his mail truck and was fastening his seatbelt when S2 walked up to the passenger-side window and yelled at him for not fastening his seatbelt before he started the engine. Citing Section 812 of Handbook M-41, Complainant stated S2 improperly demanded that seatbelts be fastened before the engine is started as opposed to before the vehicle is shifted into gear and put into motion. S1 walked over and S2 asked her whether she saw anything wrong. S1 said no. While S1 and S2 were talking and making calls, Complainant called the National Association of Letter Carriers (NALC) office. Someone from NALC confirmed his understanding of the Agency's seatbelt policy. When Complainant asked the NALC officer to repeat what he said to S1 and S2, S1 told him it was not necessary and he could go deliver his route. S1 also told him he had to follow all supervisor instructions and starting right then, he needed to fasten his seatbelt before he started the engine.

Complainant stated he did not think his age factored into this incident and did not provide specific examples of similarly situated employees outside his protected class treated more favorably.

*S2's Account (Affidavit):*
S2 stated he thought seatbelts needed to be fastened before starting a vehicle. S2 stated he called safety and was told it was not a written Agency policy. S2 stated he informed Complainant of this conversation and Complainant was then allowed to continue to his route.

*S1's Account (Affidavit):*
S1 confirmed S2 called her over and asked whether she observed Complainant doing anything wrong. S1 stated she did not and ultimately told Complainant to follow the instructions of his supervisor. When asked if the requirement to fasten seatbelts before starting a vehicle applied to all employees going forward, S1 stated she was not sure whether the request was sent to all carriers or if other carriers already fastened their seatbelts before starting their vehicles. S1 stated the relevant conversation was between S2 and Complainant.

**Claim 5: Doctor's Note and Escorted Removal – November 1, 2022**

*Complainant's Account (Affidavit):*
On November 1, 2022, Complainant gave S1 a doctor's note for 30 days of no overtime due to repetitive motion pain in his right shoulder. S1 told him she did not need to honor his doctor's note. Later that evening, Complainant returned with undelivered mail and clocked out. S1 told him he needed to continue delivering the mail until he finished his route. Complainant reminded S1 of his doctor's note and that he was unable to work overtime. S1 asked him whether he was refusing to follow instructions. Complainant told S1 he did not have to follow unsafe supervisor instructions and that ignoring his doctor's note and demanding he work overtime did not seem safe to him. S1 demanded his badge and he was escorted off the premises.

Complainant was placed on EP in off-duty status without pay, effective November 2, 2022.

Complainant believed his age was a factor because he was expected to finish an overburdened route and, now that he is older, he has noticed he does not recover from pain and injuries as quickly as when he was younger. Complainant did not provide specific examples of similarly situated employees outside his protected class treated more favorably.

*S1's Account (Affidavit):*
S1 stated Complainant told her, based on union advice, that he could bring his mail back and did not have to listen to management. S1 stated she asked Complainant three times to finish his route but Complainant kept reiterating what the union told him. S1 stated that while the union could provide guidance, she told Complainant he had to follow supervisor instruction. S1 explained she sent Complainant's medical documentation to Occupational Health Management (OHM) for guidance. OHM advised her that Complainant could finish his route that day. S1 stated OHM advised her they were looking into the matter and to wait for an update as to whether the doctor's note needed to be honored as a limited duty job offer or if it needed to be a light duty request requiring submission to the PM for approval.

## INVESTIGATION CONCLUSION AND HEARING

The Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of his right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ).

Note: Disability was not a basis raised in Complainant's complaint.

Complainant timely requested a hearing. When Complainant did not object, the AJ assigned to the case granted the Agency's December 14, 2023, motion for a decision without a hearing and issued a decision without a hearing on January 4, 2024.

---

## ADMINISTRATIVE JUDGE DECISION

**Decision Date:** January 4, 2024

**Decision-Maker:** Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ)

**Criteria Applied:** 
The AJ evaluated whether the Agency's unrebutted facts established nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions and whether Complainant offered evidence that management made biased comments about his age or treated younger workers more favorably.

**Findings:**
The AJ found that:
1. The Agency's unrebutted facts served to establish nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions.
2. Complainant argued with his supervisors, wasted time on his phone in the locker room, neglected to fasten his seatbelt, and failed to turn his vehicle off when exiting—predicates which Complainant did not dispute.
3. These predicates provided sufficient nondiscriminatory justification for the discipline and other actions management imposed.
4. Complainant did not offer evidence that management made biased comments about his age or that they treated younger workers more favorably.
5. No reasonable factfinder, even viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to Complainant, could conclude the Agency's actions were tainted by considerations of Complainant's age.

**Result:** The Agency failed to prove that the Agency subjected him to discrimination and harassment as alleged.

---

## FINAL ORDER

The Agency subsequently issued a final order fully implementing the AJ's decision.

Complainant appealed.
