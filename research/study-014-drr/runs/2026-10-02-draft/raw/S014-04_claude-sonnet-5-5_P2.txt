# FINDINGS

## I. Summary of Allegations and Scope

Complainant, a Level 1 City Carrier at the Meadow Mesa Station in North Las Vegas, Nevada (born 1973), alleges that the Agency discriminated against him and subjected him to harassment based on age. The five accepted claims are:

1. On August 30, 2022, he was placed on Emergency Placement (EP).
2. On September 14, 2022, he was suspended.
3. On September 20, 2022, a supervisor followed him to the bathroom, yelled at him to return to work, and threatened discipline.
4. On September 24, 2022, a supervisor accused him of not fastening his seatbelt before starting the engine.
5. On November 1, 2022, he was escorted out of the facility.

Disability was not raised as a basis in the complaint. Complainant's November 1, 2022 medical note is therefore treated only as background to Claim 5 and not as a separate claim.

Individuals referenced:
- **PM**: Postmaster
- **S1**: Supervisor
- **S2**: Supervisor

## II. Findings of Fact by Claim

### Claim 1: Emergency Placement, August 30, 2022

**Undisputed:**
- Complainant asked S1 to sign or initial his copy of a Union time request. S1 said she would do so later, and Complainant questioned why she could not do it then.
- S1 told Complainant to return to work, and PM became involved.
- Complainant was placed on EP in an off-duty, no-pay status, and was not permitted to consult a union steward before leaving the premises.

**Disputed:**
- *Reason for EP.* PM stated that Complainant engaged in time-wasting practices and failed to follow instructions. S1 stated that she asked Complainant about three times to deliver his route, that he did not listen, and that he became argumentative with PM. Complainant acknowledged questioning S1 and PM but denied refusing to work. He said he wanted to understand why he was being held to policy when S1 was not.
- *Intimidation in the locker room.* Complainant stated that PM entered the locker room, interrupted him, yelled at him, leaned over him, and later demanded his badge and told him to clean out his locker. PM called the intimidation claim "ridiculous" and said he does not get close to employees since having COVID. S1 had no knowledge of what occurred in the locker room. No witness other than the two principals was identified.
- *Union steward.* PM stated that no steward was available and that Complainant was told he could contact his steward but had to leave immediately. S1 stated that no employee on EP is allowed to speak with the union before leaving the premises. This is consistent with Complainant's account that the request was denied, and it indicates a uniform practice rather than one directed at him.

**Age nexus:**
- Complainant stated that age was a factor because he was assigned an overburdened route to complete in eight hours and was constantly compared to younger carriers.
- He identified no specific comparators outside his protected class who were treated more favorably.
- The record contains no age-related remarks by management.

### Claim 2: Suspension, September 14, 2022

**Record discrepancy requiring attention:**
- Complainant's affidavit describes September 14, 2022 as the date he was walked out of the facility and placed on EP. It does not describe a suspension on that date.
- The Notice of 14-Day Suspension is dated September 22, 2022 (ROI 00192-00193). It cites failure to follow instructions on August 30, 2022 as the basis for the suspension, not the September 14 seatbelt incident.
- The accepted claim's date (September 14) therefore does not match the date of the suspension document. The record supports the following: (a) a September 14 EP, and (b) a September 22 notice of suspension tied to the August 30 incident.

**September 14 incident:**
- Complainant stated that S1 and S2 approached him while he was loading his truck. S2 demanded his badge and told him he was being walked out for not fastening his seatbelt before starting the vehicle and for exiting the driver's seat with the engine running.
- Complainant's request for a union steward was denied. He understood Handbook M-41, Section 812 to require seatbelts at all times the vehicle is in motion.
- S2 stated that he observed Complainant exit the truck and walk in front of it with the engine running. He considered this a safety violation under Agency policy requiring seatbelts while operating a vehicle. S2 stated that because Complainant was placed immediately on EP, he had to contact the union off postal property.
- S1 was not present and had no knowledge of the incident.

**Observation:** The two explanations address different conduct. Complainant's account focuses on seatbelt timing, while S2's focuses on leaving the vehicle with the engine running. Complainant's affidavit does not directly deny that he exited the vehicle with the engine running.

**Age nexus:**
- Complainant stated that he did not think age was a factor in this incident and identified no comparators.

### Claim 3: Bathroom Incident, September 20, 2022

**Disputed:**
- Complainant stated that S2 followed him into the bathroom twice, yelled at him, and threatened discipline.
- S2 denied raising his voice. He stated that if he followed Complainant, it was because of performance concerns. He said Complainant was spending long periods in the locker room, not washing up, and doing something in his locker.
- No witnesses were identified, and no independent evidence resolves the dispute.

**Age nexus:**
- Complainant stated that he believed age was a factor because, being older, he needs to use the bathroom more often.
- He identified no comparators and no age-related remarks.

### Claim 4: Seatbelt Accusation, September 24, 2022

**Largely undisputed:**
- S2 confronted Complainant at the truck about not fastening his seatbelt before starting the engine. Complainant maintained that M-41, Section 812 requires seatbelts only before the vehicle is put into motion, and he contacted the NALC office, which confirmed his understanding.
- S1 was called over, and S2 asked whether she saw anything wrong. S1 said she did not and told Complainant to follow his supervisor's instructions.
- S2 stated that he believed seatbelts had to be fastened before starting the vehicle. He called Agency safety and was told this was not a written Agency policy. He relayed this to Complainant, who was then allowed to proceed with his route.
- Complainant's account differs in that S1 told him that, going forward, he must fasten his seatbelt before starting the engine. S1 did not know whether the instruction was sent to all carriers or whether others already followed that practice.

**Finding:** The evidence indicates that S2's instruction was not supported by a written Agency policy. It also indicates that no discipline resulted from the incident and that Complainant continued on his route. The record does not show whether the instruction was applied to other carriers.

**Age nexus:**
- Complainant stated that he did not think age was a factor and identified no comparators.

### Claim 5: Escort from Facility, November 1, 2022

**Undisputed:**
- Complainant presented S1 with a doctor's note restricting him from overtime for 30 days because of right shoulder pain.
- He returned that evening with undelivered mail, and S1 directed him to finish the route.
- He stated that, on union advice, he did not have to follow what he considered unsafe instructions.
- S1 asked him about three times to finish the route, and he did not. He was escorted off the premises and placed on EP, effective November 2, 2022.

**Additional evidence:**
- S1 stated that she sent the medical documentation to Occupational Health Management (OHM). OHM advised that Complainant could finish his route that day while it determined whether the note should be treated as a limited duty job offer or a light duty request requiring PM approval.

**Age nexus:**
- Complainant stated that age was a factor because he was expected to finish an overburdened route and, being older, he does not recover from pain and injuries as quickly.
- He identified no comparators and no age-related remarks.

## III. Findings on Age Discrimination (Disparate Treatment)

**Standard (to be confirmed by counsel).** Absent direct evidence, the framework is a prima facie case, a legitimate nondiscriminatory reason, and pretext. In the federal sector, age must be shown to have been a but-for cause of, or at least to have tainted, the personnel action.

1. **Legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons.** For each action, management articulated a reason tied to conduct:
   - EP on August 30: failure to follow instructions and time-wasting.
   - Suspension: the August 30 failure to follow instructions.
   - September 14 EP: leaving the vehicle with the engine running and a perceived seatbelt violation.
   - September 20: concerns about performance and time in the locker room.
   - September 24: S2's belief about the seatbelt practice, corrected after consulting safety.
   - November 1: refusal to finish the route after repeated instructions.
2. **Evidence of pretext.** The record contains none of the following:
   - Age-related comments by any manager.
   - Identified younger comparators treated more favorably.
   - Any statement by Complainant tying the actions to age beyond his own belief.
3. **Complainant's own statements.** He said he did not think age was a factor in Claims 2 and 4. For the other claims, his stated connections were general (an overburdened route, comparison with younger carriers, bathroom frequency, slower recovery from injury). These describe workload and physical circumstances rather than any age-based conduct by management.
4. **Points where management's actions are open to question.** These do not by themselves show age bias, but they bear on the quality of management's reasons:
   - The seatbelt-before-engine instruction in Claim 4 was not a written policy.
   - The suspension notice cites August 30 conduct, not the September 14 incident.
   - S1 and PM gave different accounts of the union steward's availability: PM said none was available, while S1 described a rule barring all EP employees from union contact.
   - Directing Complainant to work overtime on November 1 conflicted with a medical note still under OHM review, though OHM's interim advice supported finishing the route that day.

**Finding:** The evidence does not establish that age motivated any of the five actions.

## IV. Findings on Harassment

A harassment claim requires that the conduct was based on a protected class, was unwelcome, and was sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the conditions of employment.

- The record shows tension between Complainant and management over discipline, the seatbelt practice, route length, and union access.
- Nothing links the conduct to age. No age-related comments, no comparator evidence, and no pattern directed at older carriers appear in the record.
- The disputed incidents, namely PM's alleged intimidation in the locker room and S2's alleged yelling in the bathroom, rest solely on Complainant's word against the managers' denials. Even if credited, nothing shows they were motivated by age.
- Because the nexus element is not met, the severity and pervasiveness elements need not be reached.

**Finding:** The evidence does not establish harassment based on age.

## V. Procedural History Relevant to the Findings

- The Agency issued the ROI and gave Complainant notice of hearing rights. He timely requested a hearing.
- Without objection from Complainant, the AJ granted the Agency's December 14, 2023 motion and issued a decision without a hearing on January 4, 2024. The AJ found that the Agency's unrebutted facts established nondiscriminatory reasons and that Complainant offered no evidence of age-based comments or more favorable treatment of younger workers.
- The Agency issued a final order implementing the AJ's decision, and Complainant appealed.

## VI. Record Issues and Items for Follow-Up

1. **Claim 2 date and nature.** Reconcile the September 14 date with the September 22 suspension notice, and clarify whether the claim as accepted should be read as the September 14 EP, the 14-day suspension, or both.
2. **AJ's characterization of the facts.** The AJ's decision refers to Complainant wasting time on his phone in the locker room and describes certain conduct as undisputed. The affidavits summarized here do not mention a phone. S2 said only that Complainant was "doing something in his locker." Confirm the source in the full ROI before relying on that characterization.
3. **Comparator and age data.** The record lacks the ages of PM, S1, and S2, and information on how other carriers were treated for similar conduct, including seatbelt practice, EP, and route completion. Obtain this if the file is to be supplemented.
4. **Workload evidence.** Complainant's claim that his route was overburdened and that he was compared to younger carriers is unsupported by route data or performance comparisons.
5. **Handbook M-41, Section 812.** Obtain the text and any local safety guidance to settle the seatbelt dispute.
6. **Union steward practice.** Resolve the difference between PM's and S1's explanations, including whether any written policy governs union contact for employees on EP.
7. **OHM guidance (Claim 5).** Obtain OHM's communications and the final determination on the doctor's note.
8. **Witnesses.** No witnesses were identified for the locker room and bathroom incidents. Ask whether any other employees were present.

## VII. Conclusion

On the present record, the preponderance of the evidence does not show that the Agency discriminated against Complainant on the basis of age or subjected him to age-based harassment. Management supplied conduct-based reasons for each action, and Complainant offered no evidence of age-related remarks, comparators, or other indicators of age bias. Several record inconsistencies, listed above, should be resolved or noted before this section is finalized.
