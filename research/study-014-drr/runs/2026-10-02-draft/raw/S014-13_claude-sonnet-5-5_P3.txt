**Background and Procedural History**

At the time of the events underlying this complaint, Complainant was employed as an Administrative Technician, GS-0303-05, at the Agency's Chicago Regional Office in Oak Brook, Illinois. On August 24, 2021, he filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against him based on disability (diabetes and liver fibrosis). He also alleged reprisal for prior protected EEO activity (requesting a reasonable accommodation). Both claims concerned his termination, effective July 28, 2021. Complainant does not dispute how the complaint was framed.

After the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the report of investigation and notice of his right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge. Complainant instead requested a final decision, which the Agency issued under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b). The Agency concluded that Complainant had not proven discrimination as alleged. This appeal followed.

**The Agency's Final Decision**

The Agency found that Complainant was an individual with a disability and was qualified within the meaning of the Rehabilitation Act. It assumed that he had established a prima facie case of discrimination and reprisal. It concluded, however, that it had articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for the termination and that Complainant had not shown those reasons to be pretext.

**Employment History and Performance Record**

Complainant began working for the Agency on January 17, 2021, under a Competitive-Career Conditional Appointment that required a one-year probationary period. He had previously worked for the Agency as an Administrative Manager (Excepted Service Appointment) during the decennial census.

A June 15, 2021 progress review by the person identified as Complainant's first-level supervisor stated that he was not performing at the fully successful level 3 or above. It listed areas for improvement, including becoming familiar with the onboarding and payroll processes and completing assignments on time.

In three emails sent in June 2021, S3 (third-level supervisor, Program Coordinator) raised performance concerns with Complainant. These included partial or missing pay for two employees in his service area, payroll processing for pay period 11, and the status of several new hires. On June 17, 2021, S3 also issued him a memorandum titled "Failure to Follow Up on Payroll Issues," which outlined his deficiencies.

S1, an Administrative Officer and another of Complainant's first-level supervisors, became his supervisor in early July 2021. Her affidavit stated that errors and problems in Complainant's work caused several people not to be paid on time, and that some new hires resigned as a result. After learning of the errors, S1 retrained the staff. She stated that even after retraining, Complainant "continued to have unresolved pay issues for the new hires he was responsible for."

On July 28, 2021, S3 issued Complainant a termination letter citing unacceptable performance during his probationary period. The letter stated that he failed to ensure timely onboarding of new hires and to track them through the hiring process. It also stated that he failed to process payroll successfully and on time.

**Statement of the Administrative Specialist**

Another supervisor, an Administrative Specialist (AS), stated that she supervised Complainant until his termination. Her affidavit indicated, however, that she supervised and assigned him tasks on the Census Investigative Service Missing Documents Project, not payroll or timekeeping. AS said she observed no unacceptable performance, and that no such issues were brought to her attention regarding his work on that project. She also said she believed Complainant was terminated because of his disabilities and his request for a reasonable accommodation. She based that belief on her observation of "tension between the Complainant and [S3] regarding his work schedule."

**The Agency's Pretext Analysis**

The Agency found that management's legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for terminating Complainant during his probationary period were his performance problems in payroll processing and new hire onboarding. It found that he often failed to complete these tasks accurately or on time.

In addressing pretext, the Agency considered AS's affidavit stating that she saw no performance problems while supervising Complainant. It determined that AS oversaw only one aspect of his work, the Missing Documents Project, and not timekeeping or new hire processing. Because three of Complainant's supervisors confirmed deficiencies in those areas, the Agency found that AS's statements did not cast doubt on its explanation.

The Agency also reasoned that nothing showed S3 opposed Complainant's reasonable accommodation or telework schedule. It noted that all the Administrative Technicians S3 supervised teleworked two days per week for at least part of the relevant period. The Agency therefore concluded that, beyond Complainant's subjective belief, he could not show that management's reasons were pretext for discrimination or reprisal.
