# FINDINGS

## Background and Jurisdiction

Two Emergency Management Specialists (Regional and National Tribal Government Liaisons) employed by the Agency's First Responder Network Authority filed EEO complaints on June 13, 2019, alleging discrimination and harassment based on race (Native American), national origin (Native American), and retaliation for prior protected EEO activity. The complainants, hired in October 2015 at the Pay Band IV level, worked remotely from locations in California. The complainants initially included sex discrimination allegations but withdrew those claims during the hearing process. Two separate Administrative Judges (AJs) issued summary judgment decisions finding no discrimination or unlawful retaliation, and the Agency adopted those decisions. The complainants appeal.

## Substantive Allegations

### Claim 1: Discriminatory Pay and Hiring Practices

The complainants established a pattern of alleged pay discrimination spanning their tenure with the Agency:

**Initial Hiring Negotiations (Pre-October 2015):** Complainant 1 alleged that during salary negotiations, Agency representatives explicitly stated the Agency would only offer 70% of his previous wages on a "take it or leave it" basis. Complainant 1 further alleged that non-Native candidates were not subjected to equivalent compensation restrictions during the hiring process. Complainant 2 alleged the Agency failed to properly account for her prior salary and teaching experience, offering only compensation equivalent to her current tribal salary.

**Pay Band Disparity:** Both complainants alleged they performed identical work as peers hired at the Pay Band V level while being hired at and maintained at the Pay Band IV level, representing the lowest compensation tier for their positions.

**April 2018 Review and Subsequent Inaction:** The Executive Director directed Complainants' supervisor to conduct a formal review addressing wage and pay band disparities. Following completion of the review, the Executive Director and Senior Director allegedly took no corrective action and failed to acknowledge the review's existence.

**May 31, 2018 Misrepresentation:** The Senior Director claimed unfamiliarity with the April 2018 review during a telephone conversation, but complainants later learned from Supervisor 1 that the Senior Director was "very well aware" of the review.

**June 15, 2018 Administrative Failure:** When complainants inquired about hiring and direct hiring authorities, the Deputy Chief Administrative Officer stated the Agency lacked personnel policies and practices, and failed to inform complainants of their right to pursue the EEO process regarding pay discrimination allegations.

**July 2018 Denial and January 2019 Acknowledgment:** Complainants' new supervisor inquired about the April 2018 review with the Executive Director and Senior Director, who denied knowledge of committing to the review. Subsequently, in January 2019, the Executive Director acknowledged receipt of the review but asserted no obligation to act upon it.

**Instructions Regarding Escalation:** In January 2019, the Executive Director instructed Supervisor 2 to "tamper any escalation of HR issues for the tribal team," though the Executive Director later claimed the supervisor misunderstood these instructions in a subsequent email.

### Claim 2: Exclusion from Senior Leadership Positions

Complainants alleged they were precluded from applying to senior leadership positions because the Agency restricted such positions exclusively to Pay Band V employees, thereby leveraging the discriminatory Pay Band IV designation as a barrier to advancement.

### Claim 3: Discriminatory Reorganization (June 2018)

Complainants alleged that during a June 2018 reorganization, they were deliberately "buried deeper" in the organizational structure for the purpose of forcing them to resign and to "limit any HR escalations from the tribal team."

### Claim 4: Unequal Work Assignments

Complainants alleged that on an ongoing basis since June 2018, they were required to create separate strategic plans while non-Tribal team peers were not subject to this requirement.

### Claim 5: Hostile Treatment by Contractors and Inadequate Agency Response

Between June 2018 and February 2019, complainants and Agency officials repeatedly reported to senior leadership that the Tribal team was subjected to hostile and discriminatory treatment by Agency contractors. Despite these reports, senior leadership allegedly condoned the contractors' conduct and continued placing the team in hostile situations with contracting officials.

### Claim 6: Unequal Access to Resources and Restrictive Procedures

Complainants alleged systematic denial of equal access to resources and non-federal shareholders through multiple mechanisms:
- Requirement to coordinate all activities through a separate, discriminatory process to access resources
- Forced engagement with contracting staff and Tribal shareholders through a designated "gatekeeper"
- Restrictions on meeting participation available to complainants
- Required participation of an Agency Contracting Officer's Representative in daily engagements between contractors and complainants

### Claim 7: Failure to Acknowledge Accomplishments (May 5, 2019)

Complainants alleged the Agency issued a social media post thanking local, state, and federal workers for their dedication and service but failed to reference or acknowledge the Tribal team's contributions.

## Procedural History

Following investigation and provision of investigative files to the complainants, both parties requested hearings before separate AJs in the Commission's Washington Field Office. Complainants' shared counsel filed motions to consolidate the complaints for joint processing. The Agency opposed consolidation. One AJ denied the consolidation motion while the second AJ did not address the motion. The AJs issued separate summary judgment decisions finding no discrimination or unlawful retaliation was established. The Agency adopted the AJs' decisions as final Agency action, prompting the current appeals.
