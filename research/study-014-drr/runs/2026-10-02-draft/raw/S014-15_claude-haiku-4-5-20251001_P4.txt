# CASE FILE SUMMARY

## Complainant Position and Employment
Complainant worked as a City Carrier Technician, A-02/Q, at the Agency's Village Station in New York, New York. (Report of Investigation, Affidavit A at 190, Exhibit 1). The duties of a Carrier Technician included casing and delivering mail for several carrier routes, involving pushing, lifting, and simple grasping daily for eight hours a day. (ROI, Affidavit C2).

## Medical Background
Complainant's medical conditions stemmed from an on-the-job injury first diagnosed on May 10, 2014. (ROI at 64-65). Her diagnosed conditions included back sprain, displacement of lumbar intervertebral disc without myelopathy, and a hernia, which she had repaired after the on-the-job injury. (ROI, Complainant's Affidavit, at 191).

Complainant's medical restrictions included: sitting three hours, walking limited to 30 minutes, standing 10 minutes, avoiding reaching above the shoulder, twisting, bending, stooping, pushing, pulling, lifting, squatting, kneeling, and climbing. She also required 15-minute breaks every hour. (ROI at 191).

## Allegations

The complaint contained three main allegations:

1. **Assignment Outside Medical Restrictions and Payment Issues (Since June 26, 2016, and continuing)**: Management assigned Complainant work outside of her medical restrictions and failed to pay her properly and/or on time.

2. **Deleted Clock Rings (October 4, 2016, and other unspecified dates)**: Complainant's clock rings were deleted.

3. **Denied 204-B Detail (Unspecified dates)**: Complainant's request to be detailed as a 204-B acting supervisor was not granted.

## Modified Job Assignment

On June 22, 2016, an offer was made to Complainant for a limited duty modified assignment to work at the Lenox Hill South Station from 10:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. (ROI at 196, 233). The position was not a vacant, funded position. The assignment involved shuttling carriers by van to and from their routes and delivering express mail under five pounds for up to four hours a day and up to 30 minutes respectively. Complainant signed the modified job offer on July 1, 2016, and began the assignment on or about July 18, 2016. (ROI at 196, 233).

Complainant stated that although she felt the modified job assignment would be too much for her, she told her Manager that she would try it and let him know if she was unable to handle it, and he agreed. (ROI at 196).

The Manager testified that he became aware of Complainant's medical condition through the job offer. He denied receiving or having any medical documentation for Complainant.

## Alternative Accommodation Offered

The record shows that the Agency offered Complainant another light duty job as a lobby attendant. Complainant declined this position, believing it would be beyond what she could do given her restrictions.

On August 29, 2016, Complainant claimed she walked an hour (with breaks), even though she was not supposed to walk more than 30 minutes, and was not permitted the use of the van. (ROI at 196).

## Clock Ring Deletions and Payment Errors

Complainant's clock rings were deleted for dates July 18, 2016, through July 28, 2016, and her time was coded as full workers' compensation. (ROI, Affidavit A). Complainant had just started working at the Lenox Hill Station and the receiving station should have entered her time.

The Manager averred that Complainant had an incorrect leave code in the system. (ROI, Affidavit B).

Complainant was out on leave for significant periods in July and August 2016 and did not return to work until October 4, 2016, to fill out workers' compensation paperwork and again on November 14, 2016, to fill out additional paperwork. Because Complainant switched from Village Station to Lenox Hill, there is insufficient evidence that the supervisor at the receiving facility realized she had returned to work from being out on workers' compensation.

Complainant asserted that her Manager's poor organization was another reason for the errors made. (ROI at 197-198 (209-210)).

On September 14, 2016, Complainant filed a grievance regarding the payment failures and the deleted clock rings. The Agency maintained that the payment failures were due to plain error.

The record shows that manual timecards had been submitted and lost. Complainant believed errors may have been caused by the Agency not having a disk which would have allowed automatic logging of hours rather than manual submission.

## 204-B Detail Request

On or about November 14, 2016, Complainant requested to serve as an acting supervisor (204-B) over the carriers. The Manager did not offer her a 204-B detail as a reasonable accommodation. (ROI, Affidavit B). The Manager averred he did not need any acting supervisors for his operations and believed that supervisors must be punctual and reliable, attributes he did not observe in Complainant. (ROI, Affidavit B).

Complainant claimed the Manager and another manager "brushed her off." Complainant stated that instead of training her for a 204-B position, she was told she could apply for a city-wide position that would take time to obtain.

## Agency Dismissal and Investigation Conclusion

The Agency dismissed an additional claim for failure to state a claim. Complainant raised no challenges regarding this matter.

At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge.

## Administrative Judge Decision

Complainant timely requested a hearing. The assigned Administrative Judge (AJ) issued a summary judgment decision in favor of the Agency, finding that Complainant was not subjected to discrimination or reprisal as alleged.

**Claim (1) - Reasonable Accommodation and Work Outside Restrictions:**

The AJ determined that Complainant was provided a modified limited duty assignment of shuttling carriers in a van and delivering express mail which was within her documented medical restrictions. The AJ noted that Complainant believed the job was too much for her. The Agency offered Complainant another modified assignment of lobby attendant, which she declined. The AJ noted that Complainant claimed another modified assignment that was offered and rescinded would have met her needs, but that position contained similar tasks as the lobby attendant position that she declined.

The AJ determined there was no evidence that Complainant was forced to work outside of her medical restrictions. The AJ found that Complainant appeared to have been unable to perform her job with or without a reasonable accommodation as her medical restrictions were so severe. The AJ concluded that Complainant was not denied reasonable accommodation in violation of the Rehabilitation Act.

**Claims (2) and (3) - Clock Ring Deletions, Payment Issues, and 204-B Detail:**

The AJ determined that the Agency articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions. With respect to claims that Complainant was not paid properly and her clock rings were deleted, Agency officials explained errors occurred for several reasons: Complainant returned to full-time duty from workers' compensation so supervisors at the other facility may not have realized she had returned to work; Complainant switched from one facility to another; manual timecards were submitted and lost instead of digital submission which would have prevented the issue; and Complainant herself noted that the Manager had poor organizational skills which contributed to the errors.

As to the 204-B detail claim, the AJ determined that Complainant wanted a manager to promote her on the spot rather than actually applying for the detail assignment. The AJ found that Complainant failed to show that the denial of the detail was a result of discriminatory or retaliatory animus and failed to show that the Agency's reasons for its actions were pretextual.

The AJ concluded that Complainant was not subjected to discrimination or reprisal as alleged.

## Final Agency Order

The Agency subsequently issued a final order fully adopting the AJ's decision. This appeal followed.
