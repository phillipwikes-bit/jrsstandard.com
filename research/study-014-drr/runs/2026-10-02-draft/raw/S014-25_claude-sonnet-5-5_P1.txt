**Case Summary**

**Parties/Position:** Complainant was a Library Director (NF-1410-IV) at Joint Base Andrews, Maryland. She alleged discrimination based on race (Black), color, sex, and age. A reprisal claim was withdrawn before the EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ).

**Claims:**
1. Undocumented verbal counseling by first-level supervisor (S1) on May 31, July 3, and July 16, 2018.
2. Letter of Reprimand, October 18, 2018.
3. S1's acceptance of allegedly incorrect statements from subordinates (Feb.–Apr. 2019).
4. Notice of Proposed Removal, April 25, 2019.
5. Decision to Remove, June 28, 2019.
6. Complainant's CAC remaining active after removal (learned October 22, 2019).

**Procedural History:** After the investigation, Complainant requested a hearing. Over her objections, the AJ granted the Agency's motion and issued summary judgment for the Agency without a hearing. The Agency issued a final order adopting the AJ's finding of no discrimination. Complainant appealed.

**Findings:**
- **Claim 6:** Not addressed on appeal because Complainant did not list it in her brief or dispute the AJ's finding.
- **Disparate treatment (Claims 4 and 5):** Assuming a prima facie case, the AJ found legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for removal, including missed deadlines, canceling events without permission, scheduling and staffing failures, gossip and disclosure of privileged employee information, staff fear of retaliation, and poor funds management. Complainant identified no similarly situated comparators treated more favorably. The coworkers she cited, who were not disciplined for a fight in front of patrons, held different positions and were involved in different circumstances.
- **Hostile work environment:** The AJ found no link between the alleged incidents and any protected basis. For sex and age, Complainant offered only conclusory statements. For race and color, she offered only speculation based on the racial makeup of her supervisor and subordinates. A coworker's affidavit alleging racially biased remarks was found confusing, based on second- or third-hand information, and uncorroborated. The AJ also found the conduct was not severe or pervasive enough to create an abusive environment. The HR Chief stated that verbal counseling need not be documented in personnel files. The AJ characterized the events as ordinary workplace tribulations, such as supervisory disagreements, management efforts, and progressive discipline.

**Conclusion:** The AJ concluded Complainant failed to prove discrimination or a hostile work environment by a preponderance of the evidence, and the Agency's final order implemented that finding.
