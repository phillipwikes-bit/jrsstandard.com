# FINDINGS

## I. Scope and Basis of Findings

These findings rest only on the documentary record provided. That record is a summary of the complainant's position, the claims accepted for investigation, and the Agency's final decision. It contains no witness statements, affidavits, exhibits, medical or light-duty documentation, schedules, or comparator information. Findings on the merits of individual allegations are therefore marked **[EVIDENCE REQUIRED]** where the record does not support a conclusion.

## II. Undisputed Background

1. At the relevant time, Complainant was employed as a CBP Officer, GS-1895-12, at the El Paso Port of Entry, El Paso Field Office, Office of Field Operations, El Paso, Texas.
2. Complainant identified the following protected bases:
   - Sex (female)
   - Physical disability (cervical myelopathy; cervical fusion with limited neck movement)
   - Age (56)
3. Complainant was on Light Duty during some or all of the period at issue (September 2019 to January 2020). Her return to full duty on her regular shift is referenced in Allegation 10. **[The exact dates, restrictions, and authorizing documentation are not in the record.]**
4. The responsible management officials named are Supervisory CBP Officer 1 (Supervisor 1) and Supervisory CBP Officer 2 (Supervisor 2). A Chief and a Program Manager are also referenced.

## III. Procedural Findings

1. On April 13, 2020, Complainant filed a formal EEO complaint alleging a hostile work environment and discrimination based on sex, disability, and age.
2. The Agency investigated the complaint and gave Complainant a copy of the report of investigation (ROI) and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge.
3. Complainant asked for a final agency decision without a hearing. The Agency issued its decision under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b).
4. The Agency concluded that Complainant did not prove discrimination as alleged and found that she did not show disparate treatment.

## V. Findings on the Merits

### A. Disparate Treatment (Sex, Disability, Age)

**Finding:** The Agency found that Complainant did not show disparate treatment. The record provided does not include the Agency's supporting analysis, so this report cannot verify whether the Agency applied the correct framework or weighed the evidence correctly.

The following elements are not documented in the record and must be completed from the ROI:

- **Prima facie case:** Whether Complainant is a qualified individual with a disability. Whether she was subjected to adverse actions. Whether the circumstances support an inference of discrimination, including comparators outside her protected classes. **[EVIDENCE REQUIRED]**
- **Legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons:** The reasons offered by Supervisors 1 and 2 for each action. These likely concern operational needs, staffing, schedule compliance, and performance or conduct concerns, but the record does not state them. **[EVIDENCE REQUIRED]**
- **Pretext:** Evidence that the stated reasons are unworthy of belief. **[EVIDENCE REQUIRED]**

### B. Hostile Work Environment

**Finding:** The record states that Complainant alleged a hostile work environment but gives no Agency finding on it. The Agency's stated conclusion addresses only disparate treatment. The analysis must still determine whether the conduct was:

1. Unwelcome.
2. Based on a protected class.
3. Sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the conditions of employment.
4. Attributable to the Agency.

Allegations 2, 3, 6, 7, 12, 13, and 14 include alleged remarks that expressly reference Complainant's health, Light Duty status, or work ethic, or that were allegedly made in a demeaning manner. These need close factual review. Whether the remarks occurred, in what context, and whether they were tied to disability or another protected basis is **[EVIDENCE REQUIRED]**.

### C. Disability-Specific Issues

Allegations 1, 5, 7, and 10 concern work assignments and schedules during Light Duty. The record does not say whether Complainant requested a reasonable accommodation or whether the Light Duty assignments were an accommodation. It also does not say whether her assignments conformed to her documented medical restrictions. These questions should be resolved from the ROI and medical documentation, and the report should state whether a failure-to-accommodate theory is properly part of the case. **[EVIDENCE REQUIRED]**

- The credibility assessment of the witnesses, where there are conflicts.
- Whether it was materially adverse or contributed to a hostile environment.

## VI. Observations and Gaps in the Record

1. No evidence on any alleged event is included. The findings are therefore procedural, not factual.
2. The Agency's final decision is summarized only by its conclusion. Its reasoning, including its treatment of the hostile work environment and any accommodation claim, is not provided.
3. Allegations 14 and 15 appear connected, since the administrative leave direction and the Weingarten notice followed the same period of conflict. They should be examined together. The record does not say whether any discipline resulted.
4. Several allegations rest on oral statements, such as 3, 5, 9, 11, 12, and 13. Resolving them will depend on witness testimony, credibility findings, and any corroboration from coworkers or members of the public.
5. The record does not establish the dates Complainant was on Light Duty or her return-to-full-duty date. Those dates affect Allegations 1, 5, 7, 10, and 11.

## VII. Conclusion

The record establishes the complaint's procedural history and the allegations at issue. It also establishes that the Agency's final decision found no discrimination and no disparate treatment. It does not contain enough evidence to substantiate or refute any of the fifteen allegations, or to evaluate the Agency's conclusion. Final merits findings should be entered after review of the full ROI, including affidavits, supervisory explanations, medical and Light Duty documentation, schedules, and comparator data.
