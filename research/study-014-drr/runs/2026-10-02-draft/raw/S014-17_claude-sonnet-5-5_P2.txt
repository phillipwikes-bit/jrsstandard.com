## I. Matters Under Investigation

Complainant was a Customs and Border Protection Officer (CBPO) at the Agency's San Ysidro Port of Entry, San Ysidro, California, and was detailed to the Intelligence Targeting Unit (ITU). He alleges discrimination based on age (YOB 1954) and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity under Title VII and the ADEA. [The record text is truncated before the full list of bases. Confirm whether additional bases, such as race, sex, or national origin, were accepted.]

The accepted claims are:

**Claim 1 – Harassment (beginning May 31, 2018, and continuing)**
- a. On May 31, 2018, the Branch Chief refused to rescind a Memorandum of Counseling concerning the Treasury Enforcement Communication System (TECS).
- b. From February to June 2019, the Branch Chief knew SCBPO-1 bullied and harassed Complainant and took no corrective action.
- c. On April 17, 2019, SCBPO-1 verbally reprimanded Complainant for missing a morning training class.
- d. In June 2019, SCBPO-1 showed a knife to Complainant and CBPO-2 and said he brings his knife collection to work in his backpack.
- e. On June 3, 2019, SCBPO-1 reprimanded Complainant by email about his TECS access expiring.
- f. On June 19, 2019, SCBPO-1 changed Complainant's schedule from 2:00 p.m. to 1:00 p.m.–9:00 p.m. to celebrate the Chief's birthday and solicited $20 from him for the party.
- g. On or around August 16, 2019, SCBPO-1 and SCBPO-2 told Complainant's coworkers he would be reassigned out of the ITU effective September 1, 2019. He was not reassigned at that time.
- h. On or around August 16, 2019, SCBPO-1 and SCBPO-2 told Complainant his September 2019 leave request would not be approved. After he questioned them, the leave was approved.

**Claim 2 – Removal from detail**
Effective March 8, 2020, Complainant was removed from his detail in the ITU.

**Claims dismissed before investigation (not addressed in these findings):**
- Complainant learned on December 23, 2018, that he had not received a year-end monetary award.
- On April 21, 2019, SCBPO-1 charged Complainant Leave Without Pay for untimely counselor contact. [The hours figure is garbled in the source.]
- Three claims that SCBPO-1 spoke ill of three coworkers were dismissed for failure to state a claim, because Complainant did not show he was aggrieved by comments about other employees. (ROI 150-57.)

## II. Procedural Findings

1. **Affidavit.** The investigator gave Complainant two weeks to submit an affidavit. Complainant asked for two weeks of administrative or sick leave to complete it. (ROI 198.)
2. **Extension and leave.** On September 28, 2020, the day before the affidavit was originally due, the investigator granted an extension and advised Complainant he was approved for 16 hours of administrative leave for September 23–24, 2020. (ROI 198.) [The approval is dated after the leave dates. Verify the dates and whether the leave was usable.]
3. **No affidavit submitted.** Complainant never submitted an affidavit. His account of the events is therefore not in the record apart from the claim statements.
4. **Hearing and decision.** Complainant received the Report of Investigation and notice of hearing rights, timely requested a hearing, and later withdrew the request. The Agency issued a final decision under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b).
5. **Appeal.** Complainant contends the investigation was inadequate, asks that the matter be referred to an AJ for further investigation, and contends the Agency's reasons are untrue. The Agency responds that Complainant had adequate time to submit an affidavit and offered no evidence of discriminatory animus.

## IV. Analysis of Discriminatory Motive

1. For the allegations where management gave explanations (1a, 1e, 1f, 2), the stated reasons were non-discriminatory: policy compliance, automated system notices, an optional office event, and job performance.
2. The Agency's final decision found that Complainant did not establish a prima facie case because there was no evidence that employees outside his protected classes were treated differently. It also found that, even assuming a prima facie case, the Agency articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons and Complainant offered no evidence of animus.
3. The record contains no evidence linking any alleged incident to Complainant's age or prior EEO activity. No comparator information, statements of age-related or retaliatory remarks, or temporal evidence connecting the actions to protected activity appears in it. [Verify whether the ROI contains any such evidence not reproduced here.]
4. On the harassment claim as a whole, the record does not show that the incidents were based on a protected basis. Several are explained as ordinary supervisory or administrative actions (1a, 1e, 1f). The remainder (1b, 1c, 1d, 1g, 1h) are unexplained but are not supported by evidence of a protected-basis connection.
