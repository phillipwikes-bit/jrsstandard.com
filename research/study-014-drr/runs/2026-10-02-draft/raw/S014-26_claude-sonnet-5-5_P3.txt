# Investigation Record Summary

## Procedural Background

During the period at issue, Complainant worked as a Contract Specialist, GS-11, at the Agency's facility in Gallup, New Mexico. On August 25, 2015, she filed a formal EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against her based on race (Native American), national origin (Native American), and age, and in reprisal for protected activity (the instant EEO complaint). By letter dated November 23, 2015, the Agency accepted the complaint for investigation. After the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant made a timely hearing request.

On March 22, 2018, the Agency moved for a decision without a hearing. Over Complainant's objections, the AJ granted the motion and issued a summary judgment decision on September 23, 2020. The Agency then issued a final order adopting the AJ's finding that Complainant had not proven that the Agency discriminated against her as alleged. Complainant filed the present appeal.

## The AJ's Findings

**No prima facie case.** The AJ found that Complainant failed to establish a prima facie case of discrimination or reprisal on claims 3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 13, 14, and 16. The reasoning varied by claim:

- Claim 8: Complainant did not apply for the position.
- Claims 3, 4, 7, 11, 13, and 16: Complainant did not meet the minimum qualifications because she lacked the Level II and Level III FAC-C certifications.
- Claim 14: the selectee shared Complainant's national origin and sex and was older than Complainant. The AJ also found no evidence that the selecting official knew of Complainant's EEO activity.

**Legitimate reasons and no pretext.** The AJ found that the Agency articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions and that Complainant failed to show pretext on claims 1, 2, 5, 6, 8, 15, 17, 18, 19, and 20. (The decision lists claim 8 under both findings. As summarized, it does not state the AJ's specific disposition of claims 9 and 10.)

**Hostile work environment.** On claim 12, the AJ noted that Complainant did not claim the hug had any sexual aspect. The AJ found no evidence that Complainant's protected classes motivated the hug and concluded that this single act was not severe or pervasive enough to create a hostile work environment.

## Arguments on Appeal

**Complainant.** Through her attorney, Complainant asserts that the Agency systemically denied promotions to qualified Native Americans. Her brief makes the following specific points:

- C1 decided that her 17-hour class on Analyzing Contract Costs was insufficient for Level II certification, even though another named Agency official had approved it.
- C1 decided, without Office of Personnel Management (OPM) approval, that future contracting jobs would require Level II certification. Complainant asserts these actions were taken to reduce the hiring of Native Americans.
- On claim 10, the Agency failed to articulate a legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason for its action.
- On claim 12, C1's hug could "support an inference that [C1] has at least implicit bias that Native American women are inferior, like children, for whom an embrace from her is a gift. This is quite a different attitude that the equal respect that is normal among professionals." (Complainant's Brief at 25.)

**Agency.** The Agency asks that its final order implementing the AJ's decision without a hearing, which found no discrimination, be affirmed.
