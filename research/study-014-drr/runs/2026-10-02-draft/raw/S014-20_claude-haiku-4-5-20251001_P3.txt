# INVESTIGATION CASE FILE RECORD

## Complainant Background and Claims

The complainant was employed as a Rural Carrier at the Agency's Acton Station in Granbury, Texas. The complainant filed a formal complaint containing three primary allegations against the Agency.

## Statement of Claims

**Claim 1 and 2 – Compensation Disputes:** Beginning January 5, 2018, and continuing to the present, the complainant alleged that she had not received correct payment for work performed on her assigned route following a route count and adjustment. Additionally, from April 25, 2018, forward, the complainant asserted that the Agency failed to compensate her for overtime work performed. The complainant characterized both claims as discriminatory actions in violation of the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA).

**Claim 3 – Denial of Package Assistance:** On December 14, 2018, the complainant requested package assistance from her supervisor, the Customer Service Supervisor, which was subsequently denied. The complainant alleged this denial constituted retaliation.

## Investigation Conclusion

Following completion of the investigation, the Agency provided the complainant with a copy of the investigation report and notified her of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) Administrative Judge (AJ). The complainant exercised this right and requested a hearing.

## Administrative Judgment

The assigned AJ determined sua sponte that the complaint did not warrant a hearing proceeding and issued a Notice of Proposed Summary Judgment. When the complainant failed to submit an objection within the required timeframe, the AJ issued a summary judgment decision in favor of the Agency.

**Analysis of Claim 1 and 2:** The AJ concluded that the complainant failed to establish a prima facie case under GINA. The complainant's affidavit contained no allegations related to genetic information as the basis for alleged discrimination. Additionally, the complainant provided no evidence suggesting that management was motivated by genetic information or utilized genetic information in making employment decisions regarding compensation.

**Analysis of Claim 3:** Regarding the package assistance denial, the AJ found that the Customer Service Supervisor provided legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for the denial. The supervisor documented that the complainant had already received assistance earlier that day and was seeking additional help for only one parcel that had subsequently been removed from her delivery route. The supervisor further noted that remaining packages did not meet established assistance criteria, including considerations regarding vehicle capacity, time constraints, and daily volume requirements. The AJ determined the complainant failed to demonstrate that these stated reasons constituted pretext for retaliation.

## Final Order

The Agency issued a final order fully implementing the AJ's findings. The AJ determined that the complainant failed to prove the Agency engaged in discrimination as alleged.
