# CASE FILE SUMMARY

## Parties and Employment
- **Complainant:** Rural Carrier at the Agency's Acton Station in Granbury, Texas

## Allegations and Timeline

**Claim 1:** Since on or about January 5, 2018, to present, Complainant has not been paid correctly for working her route following route count and adjustment.

**Claim 2:** From April 25, 2018, to present, Complainant has not been paid for overtime work. (Note: Complainant crossed out and initialed the word "holiday" in the definition of this claim in her affidavit in the report of investigation, therefore holiday hours are not included in this claim.)

**Claim 3:** On December 14, 2018, her request for package assistance on her route was denied.

## Legal Basis
Claims 1 and 2 alleged discrimination under GINA (Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act).

## Investigative Procedures
The Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant requested a hearing.

## Administrative Judge Decision

The AJ assigned to the case determined sua sponte that the complaint did not warrant a hearing and issued a Notice of Proposed Summary Judgment. After Complainant failed to object to the Notice within the required timeframe, the AJ issued a decision by summary judgment in favor of the Agency.

**Claim 1 & 2 (GINA Claims):**
The AJ concluded that Complainant failed to establish a prima facie case because she did not allege any facts related to the basis of genetic information. The AJ reasoned that Complainant's responses in her affidavit did not address genetic information, nor did Complainant allege that management was motivated by genetic information or otherwise used genetic information as a basis for its actions.

**Claim 3 (December 14, 2018 Package Assistance Denial):**
The AJ found that Complainant requested package assistance from Supervisor Customer Service, who denied the request. The supervisor explained:
- Complainant had already received help that day but was requesting additional help
- Complainant had only one additional parcel that met the requirements for additional assistance
- That package was taken away from Complainant and therefore did not need to be delivered that day
- The other packages for which Complainant requested assistance did not meet the applicable criteria, which included: Complainant's vehicle could accommodate the volume, she was not at risk of missing the mail truck, and the day's volume did not merit additional assistance

The AJ concluded that the Agency had provided legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for denying Complainant's request for additional package assistance that Complainant had failed to rebut as pretext for retaliation.

## Final Order
The Agency issued a final order fully implementing the AJ's finding that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged.

## Appeal
The instant appeal followed.
