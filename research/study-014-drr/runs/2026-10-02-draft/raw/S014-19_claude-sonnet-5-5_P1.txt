**Case Summary**

**Parties/Posture:** Complainant, a Mail Handler on Modified Assignment (Limited Duty) at the Church Street Station, New York, alleges disability discrimination (lumbosacral sprain) based on the Agency not allowing her to work from July 5 to July 31, 2019. The Agency issued a Final Agency Decision (FAD) finding no discrimination after Complainant withdrew her hearing request. She appealed to the EEOC, and the Agency asks that the FAD be affirmed.

**Key Facts:**
- **Union grievance:** On June 15, 2019, the APWU filed a Step 2 grievance. It alleged that Complainant's performance of Clerk Craft duties in her modified assignment violated the CBA by crossing crafts. Complainant does not dispute that she performed those duties.
- **Medical documentation request:** On June 11 and June 21, 2019, HRM asked Complainant for updated medical restrictions. Her last note on file was dated January 18, 2011. Her supervisors (S1, S2) said the Agency needed the note to draft a new modified assignment within her restrictions that did not cross crafts. Complainant refused, believing the note unnecessary. S2 told her not to return until she submitted one.
- **July 2019 note:** On July 5, 2019, she submitted a July 2, 2019 doctor's letter. According to S2, HRM found it unclear and inadequate, and S2 asked for a revised one. The HRM Manager said S2 decided to keep her out of work, and that he did not know why she was kept out between July 5 and 31.
- **Revised note and return:** Complainant submitted a revised note after July 31 and returned to work on August 1, 2019. She mistakenly alleged the period began July 4, which was a paid holiday.
- **Agency actions:** On July 22, 2019, HRM notified her that her assignment was under review following an arbitration award on non-clerk employees performing clerk duties. On July 25, 2019, it offered her a new modified assignment at the James A. Farley facility, effective August 1. The offer's restrictions are identical to those in her July 2 doctor's letter.

**Agency's FAD Findings:**
- The Agency assumed, without finding, that Complainant is an individual with a disability.
- She was not qualified, because she could not perform the essential functions of an unmodified Mail Handler job with or without accommodation.
- Even if she was otherwise qualified, she failed to provide the medical documentation needed to offer a new modified assignment.
- She did not establish disparate treatment, and she identified no comparators treated more favorably.

**Appeal:** Complainant generally argues she was discriminated against. She also raises allegations beyond her EEO complaint, which will not be considered in this decision.

**Note for file:** The record contains some inconsistencies, including who made the decision to keep her out of work and the HRM Manager's stated lack of knowledge of the reason. The record also contains a garbled passage in the opening paragraph, where the description of the claim is incomplete.
