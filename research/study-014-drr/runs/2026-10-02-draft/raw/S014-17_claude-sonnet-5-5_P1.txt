**Case Summary**

**Parties and Background:** Complainant, born in 1954, was a CBP Officer at the San Ysidro Port of Entry (Intelligence Targeting Unit, ITU). He alleged discrimination based on age and reprisal for prior EEO activity under Title VII and the ADEA. The record's description of the claim is incomplete, and no other protected bases are shown.

**Accepted Claims:**
1. Harassment beginning May 31, 2018, including:
   - The Branch Chief's refusal to rescind a Memorandum of Counseling about TECS entries.
   - The Branch Chief's failure to act on SCBPO-1's alleged bullying (Feb.–June 2019).
   - A verbal reprimand for missing training (Apr. 17, 2019).
   - SCBPO-1 showing a knife and mentioning a knife collection kept in his work backpack (June 2019).
   - An email reprimand over expired TECS access (June 3, 2019).
   - A schedule change and a $20 solicitation for the Chief's birthday party (June 19, 2019).
   - Coworkers being told of a reassignment that did not occur (Aug. 2019).
   - An initial denial of September leave, later approved (Aug. 2019).
2. Removal from his ITU detail, effective March 8, 2020.

**Dismissed Claims:** The Agency dismissed a missed year-end award (Dec. 2018), a Leave Without Pay charge for untimely counselor contact (Apr. 2019), and three claims about SCBPO-1 speaking ill of coworkers (failure to state a claim, since Complainant was not aggrieved by comments about others).

**Agency's Stated Reasons:**
- **Memorandum of Counseling:** Complainant was twice told not to enter "E/O if not in vehicle" on TECS records, and he continued to do so. The CID Chief said he asked CBPOs to "exempt" targeted travelers, which would violate TECS policy.
- **TECS access email:** TECS sends automated recertification reminders. SCBPO-1 received notice that Complainant's access lapsed and sent a reminder email.
- **Birthday event:** The potluck was optional, with a suggested $10 donation. The 2:00 p.m. shift was told it could come in early. CBPO-1 paid $20 for himself and Complainant after Complainant said he wished to participate.
- **Detail removal:** Complainant failed to perform basic ITU duties. He did not act on an expedited Watch Commander request and delegated the task to Tactical Terrorism Response Team members.

**Procedural History:**
- Complainant requested an EEOC hearing, then withdrew the request, so the Agency issued a final decision under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b).
- The Agency found no prima facie case because no similarly situated employees outside his protected classes were treated differently. It also found legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons and no evidence of discriminatory animus or that the incidents were due to protected classes. It concluded discrimination was not proven.
- Complainant never submitted an affidavit during the investigation. He was given two weeks, requested leave to prepare it, and received an extension and 16 hours of administrative leave (Sept. 23–24, 2020).

**Appeal:**
- **Complainant:** The investigation was inadequate, the Agency's reasons are untrue, and the case should go to an AJ for further investigation.
- **Agency:** Complainant had adequate time to submit an affidavit but did not, and he offered no evidence of discriminatory animus.

**Status:** The appeal is pending. The record excerpt includes no appellate disposition.
