# Case File Summary

**Note on the record:** The excerpt has no ROI page numbers or exhibit citations, no stated legal standard or criteria, and no appellate decision or disposition. The summary does not supply any of these.

## Parties and positions
- Complainants 1 and 2 were Emergency Management Specialists (Regional and National Tribal Government Liaisons) at the Agency's First Responder Network Authority. They worked remotely from Pauma Valley and Thousand Palms, California.
- Both were hired in October 2015 at Pay Band IV.

## Complaints
Beginning June 13, 2019, each Complainant filed a formal EEO complaint. They alleged discrimination and harassment based on race (Native American), national origin (Native American), and sex, and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity. Complainants withdrew the sex claims during the hearing process (footnote 3).

**Claim 1: Pay discrimination since hire**
- (a) Complainant 1 said he was told during pre-hire negotiations that the Agency was only obligated to offer 70% of his previous wages and he could "take it or leave it." He alleged non-Natives were not treated this way. Complainant 2 said the Agency did not account for changes in her prior salary or teaching wages and offered only a salary commensurate with her tribal salary.
- (b) Complainants did the same work as peers hired at Pay Band V but were hired at Pay Band IV and paid at the lowest level.
- (c) In or around April 2018, the Executive Director, Public Safety Advocacy Directorate, directed Supervisor 1 to review wage and pay band disparities. Complainants allege he and a Senior Director took no action after the review and did not acknowledge it.
- (d) In a May 31, 2018 call, the Senior Director said he was unaware of the review. Complainants say Supervisor 1 later told them he was "very well aware" of it.
- (e) On June 15, 2018, Complainants asked the Deputy Chief Administrative Officer about hiring and direct hiring authorities. They were told the Agency "does not have personnel policies and practices." They also allege he failed to tell them of their right to use the EEO process.
- (f) In or around July 2018, Supervisor 2 asked the Executive Director and Senior Director about the April 2018 review. They denied knowledge of committing to it.
- (g) In January 2019, the Executive Director acknowledged to Supervisor 2 that he had received the review but felt no obligation to act. Complainants allege he told Supervisor 2 to "tamper any escalation of [human resources (HR)] issues for the tribal team," then backpedaled in a later email, saying Supervisor 2 had misunderstood.

**Claim 2:** Complainants were barred from senior leadership positions because of their Pay Band IV placement, since those positions were open only to Pay Band V employees.

**Claim 3:** In June 2018, a reorganization "buried deeper" Complainants. They allege this was meant to provoke them to quit and to "limit any HR escalations from [the] tribal team."

**Claim 4:** Since at least June 2018, Complainants were tasked with creating separate strategic plans, while non-Tribal peers were not.

**Claim 5:** Between June 2018 and February 2019, Complainants and other Agency officials repeatedly told senior leadership that Tribal team members faced hostile and discriminatory treatment from Agency contractor officials. Senior leadership allegedly condoned it and kept placing the team in hostile situations.

**Claim 6:** Complainants were denied equal access to resources, in that:
- (a) They had to coordinate all activities through a separate, discriminatory process to reach resources and non-federal shareholders.
- (b) They had to work with contracting staff and Tribal shareholders through a "gatekeeper."
- (c) The Agency limited the types of meetings they could attend.
- (d) A Contracting Officer's Representative had to take part in daily engagements between contractors and Complainants.

**Claim 7:** On May 5, 2019, the Agency issued a tweet thanking local, state, and federal workers but did not mention the Tribal team.

## Scope note on Claim 1 (footnote 4)
The deciding authority, which the excerpt does not name, noted that the Agency accepted claim 1 with subparts (a) through (g). One AJ's Case Management Order did not list the subparts. It consolidated them and described claim 1 as including the allegation that management took no remedial action after reviewing wage and pay band disparities. The authority therefore found that subparts 1(c) through 1(g) fall under that allegation.

## Procedural history
- After the investigations, Complainants received the investigative files and requested hearings before an EEOC Administrative Judge.
- The complaints went to different AJs in the Commission's Washington Field Office.
- Complainants' shared attorney moved to consolidate the complaints before each AJ, and the Agency opposed. One AJ denied the motion. The other did not address it.
- The AJs issued separate summary judgment decisions finding no discrimination or unlawful retaliation established.
- The Agency's final actions implemented the AJs' decisions.
- Both Complainants appealed. The excerpt does not give the appellate outcome.
