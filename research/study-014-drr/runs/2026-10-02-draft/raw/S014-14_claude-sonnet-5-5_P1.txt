**Case Summary**

**Parties/Posture:** Complainant, a Supervisory Transportation Security Officer (SV-1802-G) at Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport, filed an EEO complaint on February 2, 2021. He alleged discrimination based on sex (male) and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity, as well as a hostile work environment. The Agency issued a final decision (per Complainant's request under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b)) finding no discrimination or reprisal. Complainant has appealed. The Agency had initially dismissed the complaint for failure to state a claim, but the Commission reversed and remanded it for processing (*Norman S. v. Dep't of Homeland Sec.*, July 22, 2021).

**Claims:**
1. On Nov. 24, 2020, co-worker CW1 claimed to do Voodoo and placed a flower on Complainant's body.
2. (Claim 2 is not stated in the record. Complainant's supporting allegations for Claims 1 and 2 were that CW1 would not step back when asked and backed him into a corner.)
3. After Nov. 24, 2020, management failed to address his concerns and kept him out of management and operational scheduling meetings.
4. On Dec. 30, 2020, a Manager paced, stood over Complainant, and blocked the door when he tried to leave. Complainant said Manager demanded answers about the CW1 allegations and would not show authorization to investigate, clicking off the screen when Complainant tried to view the memo.

**Key Facts:**
- Complainant reported concerns to management on Nov. 24 and 28, 2020, and says no action was taken.
- On Dec. 21, 2020, the Interim Federal Security Director appointed Manager to investigate. Manager denied all allegations against him, including that Complainant was excluded from meetings.
- On Dec. 31, 2020, Complainant objected that Manager was in his management chain. The Interim Director forwarded the objection to the Federal Security Director, who on Jan. 5, 2021, appointed a Transportation Security Inspector to take over.
- On Jan. 22, 2021, the Inspector issued a fact-finding report that did not substantiate the claims. He interviewed Complainant, CW1, and several co-workers and described most of the evidence as "conjecture."
- Everyone agreed that a "contentious conversation" occurred, but its substance was disputed. Complainant said CW1 cornered him. CW1 said Complainant started the conversation by questioning her about her relationship with another employee. Co-workers confirmed that a conversation took place but could not say what was said or done.
- CW1's affidavit denied Complainant's account and was consistent with her statement to the Inspector. Co-worker statements matched their statements during the investigation.
- Complainant believes the events show a pattern in which women, including CW1, avoid discipline while men are treated more harshly. He criticized the fact-finding as biased and artificial, said it did not address his concerns, and suggested security footage would have corroborated his claims.

**Procedural Status:** The Agency gave Complainant the report of investigation and notice of his right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge. Per his request, it issued a final decision finding no discrimination or reprisal. The matter is now on appeal.
