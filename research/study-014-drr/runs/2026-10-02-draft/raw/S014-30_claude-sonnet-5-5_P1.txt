**Case Summary**

**Parties and Position:** Complainant, a Legal Assistant (Court), GS-0986-08, at the Orlando Immigration Court (EOIR), is an African-American male, age 49, with prior EEO activity under Title VII, the ADEA, and the Rehabilitation Act. His first-line supervisor (S1) is a Hispanic female Supervisory Legal Assistant. His second-line supervisor (S2) is a Caucasian male Court Administrator.

**Complaints:** Two EEO complaints were filed. The record's description of Complaint 1 is incomplete, but it alleges discrimination based on race, sex, and reprisal. Complaint 2 (EOI-2016-00061, filed November 5, 2015) alleges discrimination and hostile work environment based on the same bases plus age. Both complaints concern events of October 22, 2015:
- Complaint 1: The mid-year review falsely and negatively characterized his professionalism and customer service, and he was not permitted to submit evidence refuting it.
- Complaint 2: The review was held in a conference room rather than S1's office, and S2 was present.

**Procedural History:** After separate EEO investigations, Complainant requested hearings, and the complaints were joined. The EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ) granted the Agency's motion for summary judgment without a hearing and found no discrimination. The Agency adopted this in its final order. Complainant appealed.

**AJ's Findings:**
- *Review content:* S1 assessed Complainant as performing "at a successful level or higher." No rating of record is given at the mid-year review. The narrative noted needed improvement in communicating with attorneys and returning calls, keeping up with motions and orders, and limiting cell phone and internet use. It also encouraged continued hard work. Complainant contested only the Customer Service assessment.
- *Rebuttal letter:* Complainant cited a September 2015 letter from a defendant praising an unnamed assistant to the judge. He alleged S2 refused it as not "creditable." The AJ found that even if he tried to submit it, it would not have changed anything. No rating is given at mid-year, and the letter concerned only a defendant's courtroom interaction, not the attorney communication and phone-call issues.
- *Conference room:* It was chosen to ensure privacy, which Complainant acknowledged it provided, not to harass or discriminate.
- *S2's presence:* Although reviews typically involve only the evaluator and employee, S2 attended because of Complainant's documented abusive and caustic conduct toward S1 and disciplinary issues. Complainant's rebuttal affidavit, which alleged lies and inconsistencies, did not rebut these explanations.

**Arguments on Appeal:**
- *Complainant:* S2 admitted in his affidavit that reviews are normally in the evaluator's office with only S1 present and that Complainant was treated differently. He argues this creates genuine issues of material fact warranting a hearing. He also "respectfully questions" whether equal justice is possible where Agency counsel and the AJ are white.
- *Agency:* The final order should be affirmed.

**Status:** The appeal is pending, with the question being whether summary judgment was properly granted.
