# FINDINGS

## Overview

This investigation examined eleven allegations of discrimination and harassment filed by the Complainant, a Travel Specialist, GS-6, at the Agency's Office of Infectious Disease (OID), National Center for HIV/AIDS, Viral Hepatitis, STD and TB Prevention (NCHHSTP) in Atlanta, Georgia. The Complainant alleged discrimination and harassment based on race (Caucasian), national origin (German, Persian), sex (female), religion (Jehovah's Witness, Jewish), and reprisal for protected EEO activity occurring between October 2013 and January 2015.

## Findings on Substantive Allegations

### Claims 1, 2, 3, and 5 – Alleged Hostile Work Environment

The investigation examined four separate allegations regarding statements and conduct allegedly directed at the Complainant by her first-level supervisor (S1), a Business Services Support Specialist, and a coworker.

**Claim 1 (October 28, 2013):** The Complainant alleged that S1 told her to leave the Agency, stated that upper management did not like her, claimed the Management Officer was "out to get her," and told her to leave the Union. S1 denied making these comments and denied that the meeting occurred. No corroborating evidence was found to support the Complainant's version of events. The Agency concluded that the Complainant failed to establish this allegation.

**Claim 2 (December 12, 2013):** The Complainant alleged that the Business Services Support Specialist questioned why she did not attend the Christmas party and asked if she celebrated Christmas. When the Complainant indicated she was a Jehovah's Witness and did not celebrate Christmas, the Specialist allegedly looked at her "as if she had a disease" and rolled her eyes. The Support Specialist did not recall this interaction and maintained the alleged incident did not occur. The Agency concluded that insufficient evidence corroborated the Complainant's allegations.

**Claim 3 (January 9, 2014):** The Complainant alleged that she overheard coworkers (C1 and C2) making derogatory comments about her, stating she "thinks she is cute because she is built like a Black girl" and is "bad news and not your friend." The Complainant asserted these comments were made repeatedly in hallways and elevators. C1 denied knowing the Complainant and denied making the alleged comment. C2 did not recall the comment being made. The Agency found insufficient corroboration for the Complainant's version of events.

**Claim 5 (January 13, 2014):** The Complainant alleged that S1 told her to "get the hell out of my office" when she attempted to discuss training concerns. S1 denied this incident occurred, and no corroborating evidence was located. The Agency concluded the Complainant failed to establish this allegation.

**Conclusion on Claims 1-3 and 5:** The Agency determined that the Complainant failed to prove these allegations occurred as alleged. The investigation found no evidence to corroborate the Complainant's version of events regarding S1's alleged hostile conduct. Furthermore, even if the allegations had been substantiated, the Agency determined they were not severe or pervasive enough to constitute a hostile work environment sufficient to support a discrimination claim.

### Claim 4 – Sexual Harassment by Deputy Director

The Complainant alleged that the Deputy Director sexually harassed her from 2006 onward, including unwanted physical contact (touching her leg and rolling his hand down her leg during staff meetings, placing hands on her shoulders as if massaging her), invading her personal space while leaning over her at her computer, and making unwanted social invitations to lunch and/or dinner. The Complainant further alleged that she was punished for rejecting his advances through assignment of extra duties, denial of training requests, duties outside her position description, and verbal abuse including yelling and cursing.

**Supporting Evidence:** Multiple coworkers provided evidence that the Complainant had confided in them about the Deputy Director's conduct:
- The Senior Budget Analyst testified that the Complainant visited nearly weekly to vent about the Deputy Director violating her personal space, making inappropriate comments, and asking her out. The Senior Budget Analyst witnessed the Deputy Director's angry outbursts and observed the Complainant visiting her office crying.
- The Team Lead testified that the Complainant reported inappropriate physical contact during a meeting and discussed her lack of success in addressing the matter with the Management Officer.
- The Resource Management Team Lead described observing the Deputy Director frequently in the Complainant's office and noted unusual encroachment of her personal space.
- The Public Health Advisor Supervisor confirmed that the Complainant reported the Deputy Director had touched her leg and asked her out to dinner. This witness also disclosed that she herself had been harassed by the Deputy Director but had not come forward due to fear and the Deputy Director's high regard within the Agency.
- Additional team leads and employees corroborated that the Complainant reported harassment by the Deputy Director.

**Agency's Conclusion:** Despite the consistent reports from multiple witnesses that the Complainant had disclosed being devastated by and frequently venting about the Deputy Director's harassing conduct, the Agency determined that the Complainant failed to establish that she was subjected to sexual harassment. The Agency noted that while witnesses described the Deputy Director as becoming angry and throwing objects, and while witnesses confirmed the Complainant's distress regarding his conduct, no witnesses directly observed the alleged sexual harassment. The Agency concluded there was insufficient evidence to establish that the Deputy Director sexually harassed the Complainant.

### Claims 6-11 – Performance Management Actions

The Complainant alleged discriminatory motivation behind a series of performance management actions: an unsatisfactory mid-year performance review (Claim 6), placement on a Performance Assistance Plan (Claim 7), placement on a Performance Improvement Plan (Claim 8), issuance of a Counseling Memorandum (Claim 9), a supervisor transfer (Claim 10), and an unsatisfactory year-end performance evaluation (Claim 11).

**S1's Performance Management Documentation:** 
- The PAP, dated May 4, 2014, cited specific performance deficiencies including untimely responses to customers regarding travel tickets and failure to ensure accuracy and completeness of travel reports.
- The PIP, dated September 22, 2014, documented an "Achieved Unsatisfactory Results" rating for Customer Service, citing failure to resolve customer complaints independently, incomplete timely task completion, and inadequate customer communication. The PIP specifically referenced multiple customer complaints and a complaint from the Senior Federal Agency Travel Administrator regarding the Complainant's need for constant assistance in completing tasks.
- The Counseling Memorandum, dated October 16, 2014, documented the Complainant's alleged loud, rude tone, verbal abuse, inability to control her temper during the PIP presentation meeting, and alleged accusations of being retaliated against.

**Noted Context:** The Support Services Specialist observed that the Complainant was under extreme distress during this period, describing an incident in the copy room where the Complainant was shaking and appeared to be in a state requiring possible emergency assistance. The Specialist reported that the Complainant attributed her distress to harassment from S1.

**Supervisor Transfer (Claim 10):** On December 12, 2014, the Complainant was informed of a transfer to another team away from S1's supervision, characterized as an opportunity for "a new start." The Complainant acknowledged that the transfer resulted in a more positive working environment.

**Year-End Performance Evaluation:** Despite the supervisor transfer, the Complainant received an unsatisfactory rating of 2 on her year-end performance evaluation from S1.

**Agency's Conclusion:** The Agency determined that management articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its performance management actions. The Agency found that the Complainant failed to establish that these reasons were pretextual and did not demonstrate that the actions were motivated by discriminatory animus based on her protected classes. Therefore, the Agency concluded the Complainant failed to establish disparate treatment.

## Overall Conclusion

The Agency concluded that the Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. Specifically, the Agency found that:

1. The Complainant did not establish the occurrence of allegations 1-3 and 5 with sufficient corroborating evidence;
2. Even if substantiated, these allegations were insufficiently severe or pervasive to constitute a hostile work environment;
3. The Complainant failed to establish that she was subjected to sexual harassment by the Deputy Director despite credible evidence of her repeated distress regarding his conduct;
4. Management articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for all performance management actions (claims 6-11), which the Complainant did not demonstrate were pretextual.
