# INVESTIGATION CASE FILE RECORD

**CASE SUMMARY AND BACKGROUND**

During the relevant period, the Complainant served as a Senior Special Agent and Polygraph Examiner within the Forensic Services Division located in Washington, D.C. On January 2, 2020, the Complainant filed a formal Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) complaint alleging that the Agency engaged in discriminatory practices and retaliation in response to previously protected EEO activity. The Agency accepted the formal complaint for investigation by letter dated March 11, 2020, and identified three distinct claims for adjudication.

**CLAIMS AND ALLEGATIONS**

The first claim addressed whether the Complainant received a lower Supervisory Evaluation (SE) rating on his 2019 Merit Promotion Plan compared to his previous supervisor's 2018 rating. The second claim alleged a discriminatory hostile work environment based on retaliation, encompassing three subclaims: (a) that on or about September 10, 2019, a supervisory official identified as [S2] obstructed the Complainant's return to Quality Control and mentor duties; (b) that on or about September 13, 2019, [S2] denied the Complainant's request for reconsideration regarding these duties; and (c) that since September 1, 2019, [S2] and another Agency official delegated assignments directly to Washington Field Office examiners while excluding the Complainant from communications, thereby circumventing his role as Senior Polygraph Examiner and Polygraph Backup.

**AGENCY'S INVESTIGATION AND INITIAL DECISION**

Following its investigation, the Agency provided the Complainant with a copy of the investigation report and notice of his right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge. The Agency subsequently issued a final decision pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b), finding no discrimination. Regarding the first claim, the Agency articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for the Supervisory Evaluation rating. For subclaims 2(a) and 2(b), the Agency maintained that the Complainant had been restored to mentoring duties and remained under consideration for Quality Control duties. Concerning subclaim 2(c), the Agency reported that [S2] denied consulting with the Complainant or excluding him from communications.

**COMPLAINANT'S APPEAL ARGUMENTS**

The Complainant appealed the Agency's final decision, raising several contentions. He asserted that the Agency issued an untimely final decision, claiming he submitted his request for a final decision on July 10, 2020, and requested a default judgment sanction. The Complainant further argued that the Report of Investigation was inadequate and contended that his 2019-2020 Supervisory Evaluation represented a continuation of alleged retaliatory conduct. He highlighted that three months prior to receiving his Supervisory Evaluation, he had received an "Outstanding" rating on his annual performance appraisal. Additionally, the Complainant asserted that less qualified employees were selected for mentoring and quality control duties, that one management official failed to respond to testimony requests, and that the Agency had not complied with a previous final decision ordering his reassignment to Quality Control and EEO training for specified management officials.

**AGENCY'S RESPONSE**

The Agency responded that the Merit Promotion Plan Supervisory Evaluation constitutes a separate and distinct process from annual performance evaluations. The Agency noted that [S2] had not completed the Complainant's 2018-2019 Supervisory Evaluation. Regarding the collateral duties, the Agency confirmed the Complainant's approval to resume mentoring duties and stated he remains eligible for Quality Control duties, subject to various selection factors. Concerning the alleged communication exclusion, the Agency asserted that the Complainant received treatment consistent with other examiners. The Agency further argued that any delay in issuing the final decision was minimal and did not warrant sanctions.
