# FINDINGS

## Nature of Allegations

The complainant, an African-American male Legal Assistant employed by the Agency at the Orlando Immigration Court, filed two separate Equal Employment Opportunity complaints alleging discrimination and a hostile work environment based on race, sex, and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity. The complaints were subsequently joined for hearing purposes.

## Factual Findings

**Mid-Year Review Evaluation (October 22, 2015)**

The complainant received a mid-year review from his first-line supervisor (S1), a Hispanic female Supervisory Legal Assistant. The review assessed the complainant's performance as "at a successful level or higher." Notably, EOIR employees do not receive a formal rating of record at their mid-year review. In the narrative portion, S1 identified areas for improvement in three critical performance elements: (1) Customer Service, noting the need to improve communication with attorneys and telephone call responsiveness; (2) Record Management, requiring better timeliness in processing motions and preventing documents from remaining on his desk overnight; and (3) Legal and Technical Support, requesting improved teamwork through reduced cell phone and internet usage. S1 also encouraged the complainant to continue his hard work and strive for excellence.

**Procedural Circumstances of the Review**

The mid-year review was conducted in a lower conference room rather than in S1's office. The complainant's second-line supervisor (S2), a Caucasian male Court Administrator, was present during the review. The complainant was not permitted to provide evidence refuting the review findings. The complainant subsequently attempted to submit a September 2015 letter from a case defendant praising his work as a judge's assistant; S2 rejected this letter as lacking credibility.

**Investigative Findings Regarding Alleged Discrimination**

The Administrative Judge found it uncontroverted that:

1. The choice of the conference room location was made to ensure privacy rather than to harass or discriminate against the complainant.

2. S2's presence at the review was necessitated by the complainant's documented pattern of abusive and caustic misconduct toward S1, coupled with his prior disciplinary issues, requiring close supervisory monitoring.

3. The Customer Service criticism was justified and not refuted by the defendant's letter, which addressed only the complainant's interaction with a defendant in court, not his communication with attorneys as identified in the review.

4. The complainant's assertions regarding allegedly false statements and inconsistencies in management affidavits did not rebut the stated explanations for the review procedures employed.

## Conclusion

The Administrative Judge granted the Agency's motion for summary judgment and found no evidence of discrimination on the bases of race, sex, age, or reprisal. The Agency adopted this determination in its final order. On appeal, the complainant challenges these findings, arguing that genuine issues of material fact exist and that he was treated differently from peers, entitling him to a hearing.
