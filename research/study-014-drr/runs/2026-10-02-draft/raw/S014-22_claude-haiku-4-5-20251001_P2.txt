# FINDINGS SECTION

## Background and Legal Standards

The Complainant, an African American female biologist of Guyanese national origin employed at the Agency's National Institute on Aging, filed an EEO complaint on November 8, 2017, alleging discrimination and reprisal based on race/color, national origin, sex, disability, and prior protected EEO activity. The complaint, as amended, contained 17 numbered allegations spanning the period from 2009 through April 2018.

The Administrative Judge (AJ) dismissed allegations 6 through 11 as untimely under the 300-day statute of limitations for initiating EEO counseling (or the extended 45-day period in jurisdictions with deferral agreements). The Agency subsequently investigated the remaining allegations and issued a decision finding no discrimination on any of the claims.

On appeal, the Complainant raises a procedural challenge regarding the inordinate delay in issuing the Final Agency Decision (FAD), arguing that more than 15 months elapsed following the AJ's 60-day remand period, warranting a default judgment as a sanction.

## Findings on Procedural Timeliness

**Finding 1: Failure to Issue Timely Final Agency Decision**

The record demonstrates that the Agency failed to comply with the mandatory timeline for issuing a Final Agency Decision following the AJ's remand. The 60-day period established by regulation expired without the issuance of a FAD. The Complainant's documentation shows that on May 20, 2020, she was compelled to inquire about the status of the FAD, indicating a delay of more than 15 months from the time the AJ's remand should have prompted Agency action. This extraordinary tardiness constitutes a procedural violation.

**Finding 2: Lack of Agency Response to Appeal**

The record reflects that the Agency did not file a response to the Complainant's appeal regarding the untimely FAD. This failure to respond compounds the procedural deficiency and prevents the presentation of any justification or explanation for the substantial delay.

## Findings on Substantive Allegations

The Agency's investigation and dismissal of Claims 6 through 11 as untimely is upheld on grounds of the statute of limitations. These allegations, spanning 2009 through May 2017, were not the subject of EEO counseling within the required timeframe.

Regarding the remaining allegations (Claims 1-5, 12-17), the Agency found no discrimination. However, given the extraordinary procedural delays and the Agency's failure to respond to the appeal, these findings are subject to review based on the potential prejudice to the Complainant caused by the procedural violations.

## Conclusion Regarding Appropriate Remedy

The manifest procedural failure to timely issue a Final Agency Decision, combined with the Agency's failure to respond to the Complainant's good-faith appeal, raises serious concerns regarding the integrity of the administrative process and the Complainant's right to a timely adjudication.
