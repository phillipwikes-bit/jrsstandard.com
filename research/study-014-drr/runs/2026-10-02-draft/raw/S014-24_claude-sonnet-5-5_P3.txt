# Investigation Record Summary: Complainant v. Agency (EEO Complaint)

## 1. Background and Parties

When the events underlying this complaint occurred, Complainant was a Travel Specialist, GS-6, in the Agency's Office of Infectious Disease (OID), National Center for HIV/AIDS, Viral Hepatitis, STD and TB Prevention (NCHHSTP), in Atlanta, Georgia. (Report of Investigation (ROI) at 49.) Her first-level supervisor (S1) was a Supervisor Program Specialist, GS-12 (female, African American). Complainant was also supervised by the Management Officer (male, Caucasian) and the Deputy Director (male).

*Note on citations:* Page numbers refer to the Bates numbers at the bottom center of each ROI page.

## 2. Allegations Involving S1, the Support Specialist, and Coworkers

### a. The October 28, 2013 meeting with S1

Complainant stated that during a meeting on October 28, 2013, S1 told her to get out of the Agency because upper management did not like her and the Management Officer was out to get her. She also stated that S1 told her to get out of the "god damn" Union because it would never let her advance in her career. (ROI at 127.) S1 denied making these comments. She also denied that the meeting or incident ever took place.

### b. The Christmas party remark

Complainant stated that the Support Specialist asked why she was not attending the Christmas party and whether she celebrated Christmas. Complainant said she replied that she is a Jehovah's Witness and does not celebrate Christmas. She averred that the Support Specialist "looked at her as if she had a disease" and rolled her eyes. (Id. at 129-30.) The Support Specialist did not recall any interaction with Complainant on the day in question. She maintained that the incident did not occur as Complainant described.

### c. The January 9, 2014 comment overheard in the workplace

Complainant averred that on January 9, 2014, she overheard a coworker (C1) tell another coworker (C2) that Complainant thinks "she is cute because she is built like a Black girl" and that Complainant "is bad news and not your friend." (Id. at 131-32.) Complainant said comments of this kind were made about her many times in hallways and in the elevator. C1 said she did not know who Complainant was and denied making any such comment. C2 said she did not recall C1 making the comment to her.

## 3. Allegations of Sexual Harassment by the Deputy Director

### a. Complainant's account

Complainant attested that the Deputy Director sexually harassed her on multiple occasions and that the conduct continued up to the time of her statement. She described the following conduct:

- He stared at her chest.
- During two separate staff meetings, he put his hand on her left leg and "rolled his hand down." (Id. at 133-35.) The first time, she "scooted over." The second time, she left in the middle of the meeting. She also said she told him to stop because the touching made her uncomfortable.
- He often came into her cubicle and put his hands on her shoulders as if massaging her.
- He leaned over her while showing her something on her computer, invading her personal space.
- He asked her to lunch and/or dinner, and she rebuffed him.

Complainant attested that the Deputy Director punished her for rebuffing him in these ways:

- Assigning her extra duties.
- Refusing her requests for training.
- Assigning her duties outside her position description.
- Yelling and cursing at her in his office about the processing of employees' travel preparations. (Id. at 53.)

She also said he often called her into his office and "vent[ed] in a loud, angry voice while throwing things such as pens and pencils across the room." (Id. at 154.)

### b. Complainant's reports to others

Complainant said she discussed the Deputy Director's behavior with several coworkers. She also said she reported it to the Director a few weeks after it began, and that the Director simply shook his head in disbelief. (Id. at 134.) According to Complainant, nothing was done, so the harassment continued. She further stated that she reported the conduct to S1, the Management Officer, and an Agency Consultant, and that these officials ignored her complaints. (Id.)

S1 and the Management Officer both denied that Complainant ever reported the Deputy Director's behavior to them. They said they were therefore unaware of the alleged harassment.

### c. Witness statements

Several coworkers said Complainant had confided in them that the Deputy Director was sexually harassing her.

- **Senior Budget Analyst.** Complainant came to her office almost weekly to vent that the Deputy Director violated her personal space and made her uncomfortable by asking her out and making inappropriate comments. (Id. at 208.) The Analyst said she had witnessed the Deputy Director throw tantrums and yell on a few occasions, as if Complainant were his "soundboard." She also said Complainant visited her office several times crying about his behavior.
- **Team Lead.** Complainant came to him on occasion to discuss the Deputy Director's inappropriate behavior, and said the Deputy Director had touched her inappropriately during a meeting. (Id. at 205.) The Team Lead said Complainant told him she had scheduled a meeting with the Management Officer about the Deputy Director's conduct. She later spoke of the Management Officer's lack of concern about addressing it. The Team Lead added that he told Complainant it seemed nothing would come of that meeting.
- **Resource Management Team Lead.** She was frequently a "comforting ear" for Complainant, who expressed extreme discomfort with the Deputy Director's behavior. (Id. at 223.) She had seen the Deputy Director in Complainant's office many times but had not witnessed anything beyond what she perceived as an encroachment on Complainant's personal space. She thought this unusual, but she had never directly seen him touch or address Complainant inappropriately.
- **Public Health Advisor Supervisor.** Complainant mentioned that the Deputy Director had touched her leg and asked her out to dinner. (Id. at 199.) The record also reports that a Public Health Advisor attested that the Deputy Director had harassed her in the past. She did not complain because she would have been the first woman to come forward, and she feared doing so because he was highly regarded within the Agency. (Id.)
- **Others.** Another Team Lead and two more employees similarly said Complainant had told them the Deputy Director was harassing her.

## 4. Performance-Related Actions

### a. Mid-year review and Performance Assistance Plan (PAP)

Complainant claimed that S1 improperly gave her an unsatisfactory mid-year rating of 2 and placed her on a PAP. (Id. at 157-58.) She believed S1's evaluation was especially improper because S1 reportedly made comments such as "Stop trying to act Black," "Are you sure you're not Black?" and "Don't worry, we all know you're Black." (Id. at 139.)

In the PAP dated May 4, 2014, S1 noted, among other things, that Complainant:

- Failed to respond to customers about their travel tickets in a timely manner.
- Failed to edit documentation to ensure the accuracy and completeness of travel reports. (Id. at 335-40.)

### b. Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)

S1 placed Complainant on a PIP on September 22, 2014. (Id. at 341-44.) The PIP rated Complainant "Achieved Unsatisfactory Results" for Customer Service. S1 specifically noted that Complainant:

- Did not document and resolve complaints without help from peers.
- Did not complete tasks in a timely manner.
- Did not keep customers up to date on their travel.
- Received more than three customer complaints, plus a complaint from the Senior Federal Agency Travel Administrator (FATA).

As an example, S1 wrote that the Senior FATA asked Complainant to correct an employee's travel voucher, and Complainant needed constant help from peers to finish the task.

### c. Counseling Memorandum

On October 16, 2014, S1 issued Complainant a Counseling Memorandum. (Id. at 345-46.) The Memorandum stated that when S1 presented the PIP on September 25, 2014, Complainant spoke in a loud and rude tone throughout the meeting. It also stated that she engaged in verbal abuse, could not control her temper, and said, among other things, that she was being retaliated against. S1 further noted that Complainant complained that S1 allowed her peers to call her a "wigger," which Complainant said meant "white [N-word]."

The Support Services Specialist offered a different view of Complainant's state. She observed that Complainant was under extreme distress because of S1's behavior toward her. (Id. at 202.) She recalled that one day in the copy room Complainant came in shaking, and she thought she might need to call an ambulance. According to the Specialist, Complainant then described the harassment she was experiencing from S1 and how often it occurred.

### d. Reassignment and year-end evaluation

Complainant stated that on December 12, 2014, she was told she would be moved to another team, away from S1's supervision, to get a "new start." She said the move was good for her and she noticed a more positive working environment. (Id. at 146-47.) She nevertheless later received an end-of-year performance evaluation from S1 with an unsatisfactory rating of 2. (Id. at 148-49.)

## 5. The Formal EEO Complaint

On April 8, 2014, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against and harassed her based on:

- Race (Caucasian)
- National origin (German, Persian)
- Sex (female)
- Religion (Jehovah's Witness, Jewish)
- Reprisal for prior protected EEO activity

The complaint identified these incidents:

1. On October 28, 2013, S1 told her to get out of the Agency because management did not like her and the Management Officer was out to get her, and to get out of the "god damn" union or she would never go anywhere in her career.
2. On December 12, 2013, the Business Services Support Specialist approached her and berated her for not attending the office Christmas party.
3. On January 9, 2014, she overheard one coworker tell another that she "thinks she is cute because she is built like a Black girl" and "is bad news and not your friend."
4. Beginning in 2006 and continuing, the Deputy Director sexually harassed her with unwanted advances. He punished her for rebuffing him by assigning extra duties, refusing training requests, assigning duties outside her position description, and yelling and cursing at her in his office about the processing of employees' travel preparations.
5. On January 13, 2014, S1 told her to "get the hell out of my office" when she tried to explain that she had not been trained on proper procedures for processing travel, among other things.
6. On July 28, 2014, she received an unsatisfactory mid-year performance review with a rating of 2.06.
7. On May 5, 2014, she was placed on a PAP.
8. On September 22, 2014, she was placed on a PIP.
9. On October 16, 2014, she was issued a Counseling Memorandum.
10. On December 12, 2014, she was advised that her supervisor would change from S1 to a supervisor on another team.
11. On January 29, 2015, she received a 2 on her end-of-year performance evaluation.

## 6. Procedural History and the Agency's Final Decision

After the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the ROI and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant asked for a final agency decision instead, and the Agency issued one (FAD) under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b). The FAD concluded that Complainant failed to prove the Agency discriminated against her as alleged.

### a. Claims 1-3 and 5

The Agency found that Complainant did not establish that these events occurred as she described. It noted there was no evidence corroborating her version of events on her allegation that S1 subjected her to a hostile work environment. It also determined that her allegations were not severe or pervasive enough to establish a hostile work environment.

### b. Claim 4 (sexual harassment)

The Agency found that Complainant did not establish she was sexually harassed. It acknowledged that several witnesses reported that Complainant was devastated by the Deputy Director's behavior. They said she frequently vented about his conduct and was seen crying and shaking in their offices. But the Agency noted that no witness specifically observed the sexual harassment, even though Complainant asserted that it occurred in the workplace. It further noted that some witnesses described the Deputy Director becoming angry and even throwing things, but found this insufficient to show he sexually harassed Complainant.

### c. Claims 6-11 (performance-related actions)

The Agency found that management articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions. It further found that Complainant did not show those reasons were pretext for discrimination based on her protected classes. It determined that she did not show the actions were motivated by discriminatory animus, and therefore concluded that she did not establish disparate treatment.

## 7. Items for the Investigator's Attention

The following inconsistencies or gaps appear in the source record and may warrant follow-up:

- **Filing date.** The complaint is dated April 8, 2014, yet it lists incidents from May 2014 through January 2015. The date may be incorrect, or the complaint may have been amended.
- **PAP dates.** The PAP is described as dated May 4, 2014, while the claim lists May 5, 2014.
- **Sequence of the PAP and mid-year review.** The PAP (May 2014) appears to precede the mid-year review (July 28, 2014), though Complainant's account links them.
- **Rating.** The mid-year rating appears as "2." in the narrative and "2.06" in the claims list. The year-end rating appears only as "2."
- **Claim 5.** The January 13, 2014 incident appears in the claims list but is not discussed in the narrative testimony summarized above.
- **Claim 2.** The claim places the Christmas party incident on December 12, 2013. The narrative gives no date.
- **Witness titles.** The "Public Health Advisor Supervisor" and "Public Health Advisor" may be the same person. The record does not clarify.
- **Whom Complainant told.** Complainant says she reported the Deputy Director to the "Director," but the record does not identify that person or whether the Director was interviewed.
- **Support Services Specialist.** The Support Services Specialist, who described Complainant's distress over S1's conduct, is not clearly distinguished from the "Support Specialist" in the Christmas party allegation.
