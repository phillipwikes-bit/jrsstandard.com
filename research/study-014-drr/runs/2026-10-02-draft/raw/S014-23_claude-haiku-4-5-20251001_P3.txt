# INVESTIGATION RECORD - CASE FILE DOCUMENTATION

## Case Overview and Background

This investigation record documents an Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) discrimination complaint filed against the Agency by a former employee. At the time of the events in question, the Complainant was employed as a probationary Credentials Assistant at the Agency's Naval Medical Center in Portsmouth, Virginia, at the GS-0303-07 grade level. Her employment was terminated on August 2, 2018. On November 8, 2018, approximately three months following her termination, the Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency had discriminated against her based on her race, specifically identifying herself as African-American. Following the completion of the formal investigation, the Complainant was provided with a copy of the investigation report along with written notification of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge. The Complainant exercised this right and requested a hearing.

## Hearing Proceedings and Direct Evidence Claims

During the hearing conducted by the Administrative Judge (AJ), the Complainant presented testimony along with documentary evidence in support of her discrimination claim. The Complainant's primary allegation centered on the conduct of her second-level supervisor, identified as the Department Head at grade GS-0301-12. The Complainant testified that this supervisor made the decision to terminate her employment upon learning that the Complainant's significant other was White. The Complainant further testified that the supervisor became aware of this interracial relationship in May 2018 and that the supervisor's demeanor and treatment of her changed noticeably following this discovery.

Additionally, the Complainant testified that her first-level supervisor, the Professional Affairs Coordinator serving as Assistant Department Head at grade GS-0301-11, had specifically warned her not to permit the second-level supervisor to learn about her relationship with a White man. This first-level supervisor's alleged warning was presented as evidence that both supervisors were aware of a discriminatory prejudice within the management structure.

## Witness Testimony and Corroboration Attempts

To support her theory that the second-level supervisor discriminated against her based on race, the Complainant presented a key witness—a coworker employed as a Program Analyst at grade GS-0303-07. This coworker testified that the second-level supervisor had made open statements concerning her husband's extreme bias against interracial relationships. The coworker also testified that the second-level supervisor had expressed surprise at the Complainant's dating relationship with a White man and claimed to have heard the supervisor make additional race-related remarks on various occasions. Both the Complainant and the coworker testified that the first-level supervisor had previously warned them about the second-level supervisor's documented prejudicial attitudes.

## Credibility Determinations and Analysis

The Administrative Judge found the testimony regarding the interracial relationship theory to be lacking in credibility. The AJ identified what it characterized as a "glaring omission" of any reference to the interracial relationship theory in the Complainant's original EEO complaint, in the EEO counseling records, or in the Complainant's affidavit submitted during the investigation phase. The AJ reasoned that evidence of this nature, if genuinely credible and material to the Complainant's claim, would have been raised immediately rather than appearing for the first time approximately three years after the termination occurred.

During the EEO counseling process and in her formal affidavit, the Complainant had articulated different theories regarding why her termination occurred. She indicated that she believed the termination resulted from events that took place during a work conference held in Leesburg, Virginia, where the second-level supervisor allegedly engaged in inappropriate conduct. The Complainant had theorized that the supervisor terminated her because the supervisor feared the Complainant would disclose information about these inappropriate incidents. Additionally, the Complainant had suggested that her termination was connected to the second-level supervisor's mistaken belief that the Complainant had filed a formal grievance against her, when in fact the grievance had been filed by a different employee. The AJ noted that these previously articulated theories bore no apparent connection to racial discrimination.

When questioned by Agency counsel during cross-examination about her failure to raise the interracial relationship theory previously, the Complainant provided no explanation for the significant omission. Similarly, the AJ found that any reference to the second-level supervisor's alleged prejudice against interracial couples was "conspicuously missing" from the coworker's affidavit that had been submitted during the investigation. The coworker's affidavit had referenced the Leesburg training events and stated a belief that the Complainant had been discriminated against because she was African-American with greater experience than management. The coworker had also stated that the first-level supervisor had made racially inappropriate remarks but had made no mention whatsoever of the Complainant's partner being White or the second-level supervisor's opposition to such relationships. When the first-level supervisor testified at the hearing, she denied having made any comments characterizing the second-level supervisor as racist.

## Legitimate Nondiscriminatory Reason for Termination

The Agency articulated a legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason for the Complainant's termination as documented in the formal Notice of Termination. The Agency stated that the termination resulted from the Complainant's failure to properly follow the established privileging and credentialing process. Specifically, the Agency alleged that the Complainant had misrepresented to her leadership that a healthcare provider had specifically requested certain privileges when, in fact, the Complainant herself had made the request to the provider. According to the provider's account, the Complainant had attempted to convince him to apply for privileges that he had not previously sought.

The incident in question involved a credentialing and privileges application processed by the Complainant. The provider had selected incorrect privileges based upon the Complainant's mistaken understanding of the proper protocol for credentialing contract providers. The Complainant acknowledged that her guidance to the provider was inaccurate at the time it was provided. This erroneous guidance resulted in a significant delay to the provider's application and prevented him from receiving the proper credentials necessary to practice within the Agency until his existing privileges were scheduled to expire. The provider subsequently contacted the Complainant's supervisors to express frustration regarding the application delay, attributing the problem to the incorrect guidance the Complainant had provided.

## Credibility of the Termination Basis

The Administrative Judge found the provider credible when he testified that he and the Complainant had discussed the privileges application prior to his submission of it and that he had submitted the application based upon the Complainant's instructions. Conversely, the AJ found the Complainant "not credible" in her testimony that she had never communicated with the provider before he submitted the initial application. The AJ concluded that the Complainant had failed to prove pretext because no evidence demonstrated that any similarly situated employee had been involved in a similar episode or had acted dishonestly with managers without being terminated.

The Notice of Termination also cited that the Complainant had failed to initiate the renewal privileging and credentialing process within the required ninety-day timeframe. The AJ found this allegation to be factually inaccurate. The AJ identified a similarly situated employee who had multiple renewals nearing expiration with fewer than ninety days remaining but had not been terminated. However, the AJ found that because this reason alone would not have supported termination, and because the primary reason for termination was the misrepresentation regarding the provider's privileges request, the presence of this secondary reason did not undermine the Agency's stated justification.

## Final Determination

The Administrative Judge issued a decision finding in favor of the Agency. The AJ determined that the Complainant had failed to prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged. When the Agency failed to issue a final order within the required forty-day period following receipt of the AJ's decision, the AJ's decision became the Agency's final action pursuant to federal regulations. The Complainant subsequently appealed this decision.
