# FINDINGS

## Employment Status and Medical Background
The Complainant was employed by the Agency as a Mail Handler (Modified Assignment - Limited Duty), PS 4/P, at the Church Street Station in New York, New York. During the relevant period, Complainant was working under a modified duty assignment due to a medical condition (lumbosacral sprain).

## Modified Assignment and Craft Crossing Issue
On June 15, 2019, the American Postal Workers Union, AFL-CIO, filed a Step 2 grievance alleging that Complainant's performance of Clerk Craft duties violated the collective bargaining agreement because it constituted crossing crafts. Complainant did not contest that she performed Clerk Craft duties during her modified assignment.

## Medical Documentation Request
On June 11, 2019, and again on June 21, 2019, the Agency's Health & Resource Management (HRM) requested that Complainant submit an updated medical note describing her current medical restrictions. The stated purpose, according to both the Manager of Customer Services (S2) and the Supervisor of Customer Services (S1), was to develop a new Modified Assignment that remained within her medical limitations and did not cross crafts. Complainant refused to submit the updated documentation, believing it was unnecessary.

## Work Stoppage Period
On an unspecified date, S2 instructed Complainant not to return to work until she submitted an updated medical note. Consequently, Complainant was not allowed to work from July 5, 2019, through July 31, 2019. The HRM Manager stated that S2 made the unilateral decision to prohibit Complainant from working, and the HRM Manager was not aware of the reason for this action.

## Updated Medical Documentation and Resolution
On July 5, 2019, Complainant submitted a medical letter dated July 2, 2019, specifying restrictions including a 20-pound weight limit and standing/walking for an average of 20 minutes. According to S2, HRM found these limitations unclear and requested another updated letter. On July 25, 2019, HRM offered Complainant a revised Modified Assignment (Modified Handler) effective August 1, 2019, at the James A. Farley Retail Local Operation Center. The new assignment duties were stated to be within the restrictions specified in her July 2, 2019 medical letter. Complainant returned to work on August 1, 2019, after submitting a revised medical note.

## Final Agency Determination
In its Final Agency Decision (FAD), the Agency found no discrimination. The Agency assumed, without making an affirmative finding, that Complainant was an individual with a disability. The Agency concluded that Complainant was not qualified for her unmodified position and that she failed to submit the requested medical documentation necessary for the Agency to craft an appropriate modified assignment. The Agency further found that Complainant did not establish disparate treatment, noting she failed to identify comparative employees treated differently.
