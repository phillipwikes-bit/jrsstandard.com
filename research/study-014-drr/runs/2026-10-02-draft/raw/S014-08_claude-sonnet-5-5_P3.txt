# Case-File Summary of Investigation Record

## 1. Complainant and Position

At the time of the events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Counseling Psychologist/Licensed Marriage and Family Therapist at the Agency's Health Clinic in Baumholder, Germany.

## 2. Claims Raised

Complainant alleged the following:

a) From September 2014 to the present, management denied her training. She was subjected to derogatory comments, unjustified interrogations, nitpicking of her work, and negative facial expressions. She felt her authority was undermined and that coworkers and other employees were encouraged to circumvent the chain of command. Her decisions were overruled, which she said made it impossible to perform the duties of her position. She felt forced to prove negatives in response to false portrayals and claims made by supervisors and other employees. She also said Agency officials used their influence to disparage and isolate her and did not reward her work. Although she reported these experiences up her supervisory chain, she stated that the Agency failed to investigate and address the cause.

b) On unspecified dates, other employees rescheduled her clients with other providers. When clients asked for her by name, they were told they could not meet with her.

c) On October 5, 2016, during a staff meeting, the Captain made decisions about her clients without her input.

d) On October 12, 2016, in a meeting with the Lieutenant Colonel and the Lieutenant to discuss her request to be removed from the Baumholder Team, she felt attacked. She cited their tone of voice, which she considered disrespectful.

e) On or about November 12, 2016, she received a performance evaluation that she said did not accurately and truthfully characterize her work.

f) On returning to work on December 21, 2016, after being out sick December 19-20, 2016, she found that two clients had been removed from her schedule and redirected to other providers.

g) Although the office was short of providers because of the holidays, staff refused to engage her to help cover cases.

h) The Non-Commissioned Officer-In-Charge (NCOIC) brought a new staff member to the office and introduced him to all providers and staff, but not to Complainant, even though they were talking right outside her office.

i) In October 2016, she contacted the Regional Health Command-Europe Inspector General (IG) about discrimination and harassment. On December 6, 2016, a Sergeant First Class contacted her about her complaint of discrimination and reprisal. She told the Sergeant First Class that her legal counsel (Counsel) would be in Germany the week of January 9, 2017, and that she wanted to coordinate a meeting. She forwarded the email to Counsel, who emailed the Sergeant First Class asking about the nature of the meeting. The Sergeant First Class did not respond, and the meeting did not occur.

j) On January 27, 2017, she received a developmental counseling regarding her temporary reassignment.

## 3. Identification of Officials

The Lieutenant Colonel stated in his affidavit that he was the Deputy Chief of Clinical Services and the Chief Nursing Officer. Report of Investigation, Part 1 (ROI1) at 431. The Lieutenant stated in his affidavit that he was the Commander for the Baumholder and Kaiserslautern Clinics. ROI1 at 444. For consistency, each is referred to by the title Complainant used for him: the Lieutenant Colonel and the Lieutenant. Complainant also referred to another official as "the Commander," who is a different individual. ROI2 at 96.

## 4. Prior Administrative Grievance

On November 28, 2014, Complainant filed an Administrative Grievance raising many of the claims included in the present complaint. ROI1 at 203. On January 30, 2015, she received a notice of decision granting her requested remedy, which was formal mediation to work through her concerns and improve the work environment. The grievance was closed. ROI1 at 209.

## 5. Evidence Developed in the Investigation

### Training (Claim a)

The Commander for the Baumholder, Landstuhl and Kaiserslautern Clinics (Commander) stated that Complainant told him she wanted to take part in a non-military professional development session on suicide and military personnel. He told her the Agency could not fund her to do personal research. He offered to give her the time but not the pay to participate. He asserted that he did not deny her training opportunities. ROI, Part 2 (ROI2) at 98.

The Lieutenant addressed a separate request. He stated that he approved Complainant's wish to attend a Suicide Prevention training in Vicenza, Italy, but there were no funds for her to attend. He added that the training was ultimately cancelled. ROI1 at 447.

### Rescheduling of Clients (Claim b)

On September 20, 2016, Complainant emailed the Supervisory Psychologist/Chief of Behavioral Health (Chief). She alleged that the Nurse Practitioner told two of her patients they were being transferred to another provider, and that another patient was told Complainant no longer worked in the clinic and could not see him. ROI1 at 232.

The Social Services Assistant noted that Complainant had previously said she worked only with Marriage and Family counseling patients. This led to some transfers and limited the patients she could see. ROI2 at 113. The Nurse Practitioner said the same. ROI2 at 143.

The Lieutenant Colonel stated that he investigated and resolved the matter. For example, he found that one transfer was unintentional, and the client was switched back. ROI2 at 436.

### Patient Complaints to the IG

On October 5 and 7, 2016, two different patients of Complainant's submitted Interactive Customer Evaluation (ICE) complaints to the IG about her standard of care. ROI1 at 217.

### The October 5, 2016 Meeting (Claim c)

On October 5, 2016, during a standard High-Risk meeting, the Captain raised concerns about two of Complainant's patients. ROI2 at 6. Complainant spoke with the Captain immediately after the meeting. The Captain asserted that Complainant was aggressive, recorded her without her consent, and spoke loudly about the two patients in an open space within earshot of other patients. ROI2 at 6.

The Captain denied making any changes. She stated that the meeting concerned patient care plans, as occurs with every provider. ROI2 at 11. The Social Services Assistant likewise stated that High-Risk meetings let various providers coordinate appropriate care. Everyone offers a perspective on a patient, and no one was telling anyone how to do their job. ROI2 at 110.

Complainant then told the Lieutenant Colonel that she no longer wanted to attend the meetings and wanted a reassignment. ROI1 at 437.

### The October 12, 2016 Meeting (Claim d)

On October 12, 2016, the Lieutenant Colonel and the Lieutenant met with Complainant to discuss her request to be removed from the Baumholder Team. Both said Complainant repeatedly alleged a hostile environment but, when asked for examples, gave no specifics. ROI1 at 437; 448.

During the meeting, Complainant asserted that the Lieutenant Colonel and the Colonel were hostile to her. ROI1 at 447. Both denied being aggressive. They stated that they did not want to continue if she was uncomfortable and offered to have a neutral party present. ROI1 at 455.

### IG Complaint of October 13, 2016

On October 13, 2016, Complainant submitted a complaint to the IG office alleging discrimination, harassment, and abuse of authority. ROI1 at 219.

### Performance Evaluation (Claim e)

On or about November 12, 2016, the Lieutenant Colonel issued Complainant a performance evaluation based on a standard set of 15 metrics. ROI1 at 438. He stated that she received eleven "Exceed" ratings and four "Met" ratings. The "Met" ratings were in areas concerning collaboration with staff. He said he gave Complainant examples to support those ratings. For instance, he noted there was no collaboration with staff for most of the period from May 2016 through October 31, 2016. ROI1 at 439. Her overall rating was "Excellent." The Lieutenant, as second-line rater, agreed with the review. ROI1 at 456.

### IG Report of November 15, 2016

On November 15, 2016, the IG issued its report based on the complaints of October 5, 7, and 13, 2016. ROI1 at 217-26. The report found no evidence to support Complainant's allegations of harassment or discrimination. It also found that Complainant violated German law by voice recording a conversation without the other party's consent. ROI1 at 225. In addition, the IG found that she had created a disruptive environment within the Baumholder Behavioral Health Clinic. The report stated:

> The primary recommendation is to move [Complainant] out of the Baumholder Health Clinic due to the contentious nature of her interactions with the rest of the Behavioral Health Staff. Should she remain assigned to the staff, I believe there will continue to be conflict between her and the rest of the team, which demonstrably impacts patient care and significantly decreases moral throughout the clinic. ROI1 at 217.

### Contact from the IG Sergeant First Class (Claim i)

On December 6, 2016, the Sergeant First Class contacted Complainant about her IG complaint of discrimination and reprisal. She told him that her legal counsel would be in Germany and that she wanted to coordinate a meeting. Complainant asserted that the Sergeant First Class did respond, but by then Counsel was no longer in Germany. ROI1 at 400.

The EEO Investigator noted that she contacted the Sergeant First Class for an interview. The IG Office, including the Sergeant First Class, called her back and stated that internal IG policy is to abstain from involvement when the Agency is taking action. ROI2 at 173. The report generated from the IG investigation is nonetheless part of the record. ROI1 at 217.

### December 21, 2016 Schedule Changes (Claims f and g)

Complainant alleged that after she returned to work on December 21, 2016, following sick leave on December 19-20, two clients were taken from her schedule and redirected to other providers. The Lieutenant Colonel, the Colonel, and the Chief each attested that they were unaware of any issues on that date.

### Introduction of the New Staff Member (Claim h)

The NCOIC brought a new staff member to the office and introduced him to all providers and staff. Complainant alleged he did not introduce the staff member to her, although they were talking outside her office. Complainant stated that she does not know who the new member is. She acknowledged that she did not leave her office to introduce herself and did not tell the NCOIC or anyone else about the matter. ROI1 at 398-99.

The EEO Investigator noted that she contacted the NCOIC, but he had been reassigned at the time and did not respond. ROI2 at 173.

### Temporary Reassignment and Developmental Counseling (Claim j)

On January 4, 2017, Complainant was temporarily assigned to the Landstuhl Regional Medical Center (LRMC). ROI1 at 459. On January 27, 2017, she received a developmental counseling regarding that reassignment. The Chief stated that it was standard counseling and that there was nothing negative about it. ROI1 at 459.

## 6. Procedural History

At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant timely requested a hearing.

On October 18, 2021, the Agency submitted a Motion for a Decision Without a Hearing (Motion). Complainant responded.

On January 31, 2022, the AJ issued his decision. He determined that the investigative record was adequately developed. Making all reasonable inferences in Complainant's favor, he found no material facts in dispute.

On the merits, the AJ determined that the Agency had set forth non-discriminatory reasons for each of the actions detailed above and that Complainant failed to show those reasons were pretext for discrimination. He also determined that Complainant failed to show the Agency's conduct was severe or pervasive enough to sustain a hostile work environment claim. He found that the complaint could essentially be characterized as reflecting Complainant's utter dissatisfaction and personal grievances against her coworkers and management. The AJ granted the Agency's Motion.

The Agency did not issue a final action, so the AJ's decision became the Agency's final action by operation of law. See 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110.

## 7. Notes for the File

The following inconsistencies in the source record may need verification before the summary is relied on:

- **References to a "Colonel."** The record refers to a "Colonel" in the October 12, 2016 meeting and in the December 21, 2016 statements. No affidavit identifying a separate Colonel is cited, so this may be a reference to the Lieutenant Colonel or to another official.
- **Citation format.** The Lieutenant Colonel's statement about resolving the client-transfer matter is cited as ROI2 at 436, though his other statements are cited to ROI1 at 431-39. The cite should be checked.
- **Timing of Complainant's IG contact.** Claim i says she contacted the IG in October 2016, and the record shows submissions on October 13, 2016. The ICE complaints of October 5 and 7 were filed by patients, not by Complainant.
- **Complainant's account of the IG contact.** She asserted that the Sergeant First Class responded after Counsel had left Germany (ROI1 at 400). Claim i states that he did not respond. The two accounts differ.
