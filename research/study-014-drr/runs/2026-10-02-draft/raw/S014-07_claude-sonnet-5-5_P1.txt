**Case Summary**

**Parties and Background:** Complainant, a Hispanic employee of Mexican national origin, worked as a Taxpayer Advocate for the Agency in Laguna Niguel, California. She filed a formal EEO complaint on November 4, 2021, alleging harassment based on race and national origin. The Agency accepted the complaint on November 16, 2021.

**Accepted Claim:** From June 2020 through July 2021, a co-worker who served as On-the-Job Instructor (OJI) and Lead (S1, also Complainant's initial supervisor) allegedly harassed Complainant by:
1. Making fun of her accent and saying she disliked speaking with her.
2. Trying to sabotage her work by creating a list of late inventory actions and expired casework, and giving her misinformation on case processing.
3. Filling her calendar with unrealistic appointments.
4. Advising her manager to have her process a fraudulent tax return.

**Procedural History:** After investigation, the Agency gave Complainant the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing. She did not request one within the 29 C.F.R. § 1614.108(f) time frame, so the Agency issued a final decision under § 1614.110(b) finding no discrimination.

**Agency Decision:**
- For incident 1, the Agency assumed S1 made the comments and that they would constitute unwelcome verbal conduct based on a protected category.
- For incidents 2–4, it found no persuasive evidence that the actions were based on protected classes, characterizing Complainant's claims of discriminatory motive as uncorroborated.
- It noted that an internal anti-harassment inquiry did not substantiate her claims.

**Appeal:**
- *Complainant's position:* The final decision failed to note that S1 was originally her manager before becoming her Lead and OJI. S1 made derogatory comments about her accent from her first day. She notified S2 and asked for a change of supervisor, but S2 ignored the harassment and let it continue.
- *Agency's position:* It asks that the decision be affirmed. Even assuming the allegations are true, S1's comments did not mock her accent or reflect her Mexican descent. At most they showed difficulty communicating, or were insensitive remarks that the Commission has not found discriminatory. The conduct was not severe or pervasive enough for a hostile work environment. Management acted promptly: after Complainant raised the harassment in late July 2021, S4 separated her from S1, and she reported no further contact. Regarding S2, it "appears" she only expressed a "lack of support."

**Status:** The appeal is pending before the Commission. The issues are whether the alleged conduct was based on race or national origin, whether it was sufficiently severe or pervasive, and whether the Agency's response was adequate.
