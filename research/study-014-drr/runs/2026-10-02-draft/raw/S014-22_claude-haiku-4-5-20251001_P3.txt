# CASE FILE MEMORANDUM: INVESTIGATION RECORD

## CASE SUMMARY AND BACKGROUND

This memorandum documents the formal Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) complaint filed by the Complainant, a highly educated research scientist employed by the Agency's National Institute on Aging. During the relevant period, the Complainant worked as a Biologist at the GS-0401-11 level until her promotion to GS-12 in May 2013. She was assigned to the Laboratory of Neurogenetics, specifically within the Neuromuscular Disease Research Section, located in Bethesda, Maryland. On November 8, 2017, the Complainant filed a formal EEO complaint, which was subsequently amended to include additional allegations of discrimination and hostile work environment conditions.

## PROTECTED CLASS CHARACTERISTICS AND BASES FOR COMPLAINT

The Complainant identifies herself as belonging to multiple protected classes under federal employment discrimination law. She alleges discrimination based on the following characteristics: race and color (specifically, African American/Black descent), national origin (Guyanese nationality), and sex (female). Additionally, the Complainant reported several disability conditions that form a basis for her claims, including a diagnosis of bipolar disorder in 2009, which was later re-diagnosed as Post Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) in 2017, generalized anxiety disorder, benign mitral valve prolapse, and fibroid tumors requiring surgical intervention in December 2013. The Complainant also asserts claims of reprisal based on her initiation of EEO counseling proceedings on July 27, 2017, relating to the instant complaint.

## SUPERVISORY RELATIONSHIPS AND KEY PERSONNEL

The Complainant's direct supervisor during the period at issue was the Section Chief, identified as Ireland, a Caucasian male. The Section Chief served as the Complainant's first-line supervisor and maintained responsibility for assigning work, conducting performance-related communications, and overseeing the day-to-day operations of the Section. The Complainant's second-line supervisor was the Lab Chief, identified as British and Caucasian, to whom she was reassigned in February 2018 following the initiation of her EEO complaint.

## SPECIFIC INCIDENTS AND ALLEGATIONS

The Complainant's amended complaint encompasses numerous specific incidents spanning from 2009 through April 2018. In June 2017, the Section Chief failed to conduct a scheduled meeting with the Complainant. Shortly thereafter, on June 15, 2017, the Section Chief notified the Complainant of a reassignment to DNA plate preparation duties, a role typically assigned to summer interns and student employees rather than doctoral-level biologists.

Following the Complainant's initiation of EEO counseling on July 27, 2017, the record reflects several alleged retaliatory actions. Agency management excluded the Complainant from a major project within her laboratory. The Section Chief reportedly ceased communicating with her regarding work assignments, and management failed to assign her additional projects. On February 23, 2018, the Section Chief did not promote the Complainant as allegedly promised.

The allegations of hostile work environment span nearly a decade. According to the Complainant, beginning in 2009, the Section Chief subjected her to inappropriate comments and unfair treatment. In 2009, he directed laboratory employees to engage in a project, made inappropriate comments, then retracted those comments while joking that he should refrain from further remarks lest an EEO complaint be filed against him. In 2010, the Section Chief allegedly stated that the Agency would submit research findings to a top-tier journal without the Complainant's authorship because the work required perfection. During the same year, he purportedly called the Complainant an "idiot" while deliberately distracting her during an experimental procedure and required her to work on Christmas Day. He repeatedly criticized her writing speed and the duration of her doctoral program completion.

In March 2011, after the Complainant was assigned to lead a task force, she experienced difficulties because the Section Chief frequently excluded her from electronic communications concerning task assignments. In April 2011, he made a statement suggesting that no one would tolerate her mental illness outside the current employment relationship. In September 2011, following her meeting with Career Services, the Section Chief questioned her decision to seek career guidance and suggested she remain in her current position. Approximately during the same period, the Complainant alleges the Section Chief placed her name last on a research article. In 2011, he made a comment suggesting she would enjoy the film "The Help." In 2012, he made jokes regarding her African ancestry. By 2013, he had engaged in uncomfortable exchanges concerning her family background and ancestry.

Additional allegations from 2013 include the Section Chief directing post-baccalaureate students to review the Complainant's work, referring to her as a "Diva" in a manner that discouraged colleague interaction, and precluding her from authoring manuscripts for top-tier journals. From 2016 onward, the Section Chief failed to attend their regularly scheduled one-on-one meetings and warned the Complainant not to knock on his closed office door. In May 2017, after initially indicating he would include her, the Section Chief excluded the Complainant from a project. On May 12, 2017, he yelled at her before colleagues, stating "No wonder nothing you do ever works" in response to her answer regarding laboratory protocols.

## MEDICAL PRIVACY VIOLATION AND DISABILITY DISCRIMINATION

In 2013, the Section Chief disclosed to a colleague that the Complainant had a heart condition and expressed dissatisfaction regarding the management of her medical issues. The Complainant denies ever disclosing her heart condition to either the Section Chief or the Lab Chief, asserting the Section Chief made this statement to preemptively protect himself from potential accusations of disclosing confidential medical information. The Complainant underwent surgical removal of fibroid tumors on December 18, 2013, requiring hospitalization and six weeks of recovery. Additionally, in late May or early June 2017, the Section Chief reportedly laughed with a colleague and trainee about an incident requiring the Complainant to work on Christmas Day.

## RESOURCE ALLOCATION AND EQUIPMENT DISCRIMINATION

The Complainant alleges that in June 2015, the Section Chief required her to accept a lower-specification computer compared to other laboratory employees. Additionally, the Section Chief acknowledged the Complainant deserved a promotion in 2015 but deliberately delayed processing the promotion paperwork until 2017, coinciding with an announced hiring freeze. The Complainant was notified of the indefinite hiring freeze in February 2017.

## POST-COMPLAINT RETALIATION AND EXCLUSION

Following the initiation of EEO counseling, the record reflects additional concerning actions. In February 2018, the Agency reassigned the Complainant to report directly to the Lab Chief, ostensibly due to her EEO complaint. In March 2018, the Lab Chief repositioned the Complainant's photograph in the breakroom under a different laboratory group, despite her continued membership in the Neuromuscular Disease Research Section. In March 2018, the Section Chief failed to communicate directly with the Complainant regarding an ongoing collaborative project, preventing her participation. On April 5, 2018, the Section Chief publicly announced to staff that the Complainant no longer reported to him. Subsequently, in April 2018, the Agency removed the Complainant's name from the National Institute on Aging website staff listing—a significant action given that her position required interaction with external agencies and collaborators. Finally, on April 23, 2018, all employees and collaborators began copying the Lab Chief on every email sent to the Complainant, regardless of the message's significance.

## ADMINISTRATIVE PROCESSING AND CURRENT STATUS

The Agency investigated the Complainant's amended EEO complaint. Following an Administrative Judge's remand for final decision, the Agency dismissed claims six through eleven for failure to timely initiate EEO counseling. The Agency found no discrimination on the remaining claims. On appeal, the Complainant documented that more than fifteen months elapsed after the Administrative Judge's prescribed sixty-day period without issuance of a Final Agency Decision. The Complainant submitted evidence that on May 20, 2020, she inquired about the status of the decision. She argues this extraordinary delay warrants a default judgment sanction against the Agency. The Agency did not submit a reply to the appeal.
