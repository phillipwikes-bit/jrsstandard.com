# CASE FILE SUMMARY

**Position and Workplace:**
Complainant worked as a Travel Specialist, GS-6, at the Agency's Office of Infectious Disease (OID), National Center for HIV/AIDS, Viral Hepatitis, STD and TB Prevention (NCHHSTP) in Atlanta, Georgia. (ROI, p. 49)

**Supervisory Structure:**
- First-level supervisor (S1): Supervisor Program Specialist, GS-12 (female, African American)
- Additional supervisors: Management Officer (male, Caucasian) and Deputy Director (male)

---

## ALLEGATIONS AND EVIDENCE

**Claim 1: October 28, 2013 - Alleged Statements by S1**

*Complainant's Account:* S1 told her to get out of the Agency because upper management did not like her, the Management Officer was out to get her, and she needed to get out of the "god damn" Union because it would never allow her to advance her career. (ROI, p. 127)

*S1's Response:* S1 denied making the comments and denied that the meeting or incident ever took place.

---

**Claim 2: December 12, 2013 - Alleged Interaction with Support Specialist**

*Complainant's Account:* The Support Specialist asked why she was not attending the Christmas party and asked if she celebrated Christmas. Complainant replied that she is a Jehovah's Witness and does not celebrate Christmas. The Support Specialist "looked at her as if she had a disease" and rolled her eyes. (ROI, pp. 129-30)

*Support Specialist's Response:* The Support Specialist did not recall having any interaction with Complainant on the day in question and maintained that the incident did not occur as Complainant alleged.

---

**Claim 3: January 9, 2014 - Alleged Comments by Coworkers**

*Complainant's Account:* Complainant overheard coworker C1 make comments to coworker C2 that she (Complainant) thinks "she is cute because she is built like a Black girl" and that she "is bad news and not your friend." Complainant maintained that such comments were made about her multiple times in hallways and in the elevator. (ROI, pp. 131-32)

*C1's Response:* C1 maintained that she had no idea who Complainant was and that she did not make any such comment.

*C2's Response:* C2 averred that she did not recall the comment being made to her by C1.

**Claim 4: Sexual Harassment by Deputy Director (2006 to Present)**

*Complainant's Account:* The Deputy Director sexually harassed her on multiple occasions. Complainant stated that:
- The Deputy Director would stare at her chest (ROI, pp. 133-35)
- He put his hand on her left leg and "rolled his hand down" during two separate staff meetings (ROI, pp. 133-35)
- During the first incident, Complainant "scooted over"
- During the second incident, she left in the middle of the meeting
- Complainant told the Deputy Director to stop because the touching was making her feel uncomfortable (ROI, p. 133-35)
- The Deputy Director would often come into her cubicle and put his hands on her shoulders, as if massaging her (ROI, p. 133-35)
- He would lean over her while showing her something on her computer, invading her personal space (ROI, p. 133-35)
- He asked her to lunch and/or dinner, which she rebuffed (ROI, p. 133-35)

*Alleged Retaliatory Actions:* Complainant attested that the Deputy Director punished her for rebuffing him by:
- Assigning her extra duties
- Refusing her requests for training
- Assigning her duties outside of her position description
- Yelling and cursing at her in his office regarding the processing of employees' travel preparations (ROI, p. 53)
- Calling her into his office and "venting in a loud, angry voice while throwing things such as pens and pencils across the room" (ROI, p. 154)

*Reports Made:* Complainant reported the Deputy Director's behavior:
- To the Director "just a few weeks after it first occurred," who "simply shook his head in disbelief" (ROI, p. 134)
- To S1, the Management Officer, and a Consultant with the Agency
- Complainant maintained that nothing was done and the harassment continued (ROI, p. 134)

*S1 and Management Officer's Response:* Both S1 and the Management Officer denied that Complainant ever reported the Deputy Director's behavior to them, and stated they were unaware of the alleged harassment.

*Corroborating Witness Statements:*

- **Senior Budget Analyst:** Attested that Complainant would come into her office almost on a weekly basis to vent about the Deputy Director violating her personal space and making her feel uncomfortable by asking her out and making inappropriate comments. The Senior Budget Analyst said she had witnessed the Deputy Director throw tantrums and yell as if Complainant was his "soundboard" on a few occasions. The Senior Budget Analyst further averred that Complainant visited her office on several occasions crying over the Deputy Director's behavior towards her. (ROI, p. 208)

- **Team Lead:** Attested that Complainant came to him on occasions to talk about the Deputy Director's inappropriate behavior and that the Deputy Director had touched her inappropriately during a meeting. The Team Lead averred that Complainant explained she had scheduled a meeting with the Management Officer to discuss the Deputy Director's conduct, and that Complainant later discussed the Management Officer's lack of concern for addressing the alleged harassment. The Team Lead added that he told Complainant that it seemed as if nothing would come as a result of her meeting with the Management Officer. (ROI, p. 205)

- **Resource Management Team Lead:** Explained that she was frequently a "comforting ear" for Complainant, and that Complainant expressed her extreme discomfort with the Deputy Director's behavior. The Resource Management Team Lead stated that she had seen the Deputy Director in Complainant's office many times, but she had not witnessed anything more than what she perceived to be an encroachment of Complainant's personal space. She mentioned she felt this to be unusual, but she had never directly witnessed the Deputy Director touch or address Complainant inappropriately. (ROI, p. 223)

- **Public Health Advisor Supervisor:** Maintained that Complainant had mentioned that the Deputy Director had touched her leg and asked her out to dinner. The Public Health Advisor attested that the Deputy Director had harassed her in the past as well, but she did not complain because she would have been the first woman to have come forward. The Public Health Advisor said she was afraid to come forward because the Deputy Director was highly regarded within the Agency. (ROI, p. 199)

- **Additional Witnesses:** Another Team Lead and two more employees similarly stated that Complainant had mentioned that the Deputy Director had been harassing her.

## FINAL AGENCY DECISION (FAD)

**Standard Applied:** Pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b)

**Decision:** The Agency concluded that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged.

**Specific Findings:**

*Claims 1-3 and 5:*
- Complainant did not establish that her allegations occurred as she alleged
- No evidence corroborated Complainant's version of events with regard to her allegations that S1 subjected her to a hostile work environment
- Complainant's allegations were not severe or pervasive enough to establish a hostile work environment

*Claim 4 (Sexual Harassment by Deputy Director):*
- Complainant did not establish that she was subjected to sexual harassment
- The Agency noted that several witnesses reported that Complainant was so devastated by the Deputy Director's harassing behavior that she frequently vented about his conduct and was seen crying and shaking in their offices
- However, no witnesses specifically observed the sexual harassment even though Complainant asserted that the events occurred in the workplace
- While some witnesses described the Deputy Director as becoming angry and even throwing things, there was insufficient evidence to show that the Deputy Director sexually harassed Complainant

*Claims 6-11 (Performance Reviews and Disciplinary Actions):*
- Management articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions
- Complainant did not establish that the reasons were pretextual based on her protected classes
- Complainant did not show that actions were motivated by discriminatory animus based on her protected classes
- Therefore, Complainant did not establish that she was subjected to disparate treatment as alleged
