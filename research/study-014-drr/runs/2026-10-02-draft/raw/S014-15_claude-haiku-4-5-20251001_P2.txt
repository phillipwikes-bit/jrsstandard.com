# FINDINGS SECTION

## Overview of Allegations

The Complainant, employed as a City Carrier Technician at Village Station in New York, raised three primary allegations of discrimination and reprisal: (1) assignment of work outside medical restrictions and improper/untimely compensation since June 26, 2016; (2) deletion of clock rings on October 4, 2016, and other unspecified dates; and (3) denial of a 204-B (acting supervisor) detail assignment on unspecified dates.

## Medical Condition and Restrictions

The Complainant's medical restrictions, stemming from an on-the-job injury first diagnosed May 10, 2014, include significant limitations:
- Sitting: 3 hours maximum
- Walking: Limited to 30 minutes
- Standing: 10 minutes maximum
- Restrictions on reaching above shoulders, twisting, bending, stooping, pushing, pulling, lifting, squatting, kneeling, and climbing
- Required 15-minute breaks every hour

The Complainant's position required pushing, lifting, and simple grasping for eight hours daily, necessitating accommodation upon her return from extended leave.

## Reasonable Accommodation Analysis

**Modified Job Assignment (Lenox Hill South Station):**
The Agency offered a modified duty position beginning July 18, 2016, involving van shuttle services and express mail delivery (under five pounds) for up to four hours daily and 30 minutes of delivery time. The Complainant initially accepted this assignment, though expressing concern about feasibility, and agreed to notify management if unable to continue.

The record documents one instance—August 29, 2016—where the Complainant walked approximately one hour with breaks, exceeding her 30-minute restriction. The Complainant attributed this to lack of van access, noting that vehicle use would require stooping and twisting incompatible with her restrictions.

**Alternative Accommodation—Lobby Attendant Position:**
The Agency offered a lobby attendant position as an alternative accommodation. The Complainant declined this position, believing it exceeded her medical capabilities.

## Payment and Timekeeping Issues

Clock rings were deleted for July 18-28, 2016, with time coded as workers' compensation. The investigation identified multiple contributing factors:
- Insufficient communication regarding the Complainant's transition from Village Station to Lenox Hill Station
- Potential lack of receiving facility awareness of the Complainant's return from workers' compensation status
- Supervisor at receiving facility may not have documented initial hours
- Manager's reported use of incorrect leave codes in the system
- Loss of manually submitted timecards that could have been prevented through digital submission
- Acknowledged poor organizational practices by the Manager

A grievance regarding payment failures and deleted clock rings was filed September 14, 2016. The Agency attributed payment failures to administrative error rather than discriminatory intent.

## 204-B Detail Denial

The Complainant requested assignment as an acting supervisor (204-B) detail. The Manager declined, stating:
- No operational need for additional acting supervisors existed
- The Complainant lacked the punctuality and reliability attributes required for supervisory positions

The Complainant characterized this denial as a failure to accommodate her medical condition, suggesting supervisory work would impose fewer physical demands. The Manager acknowledged offering two alternative accommodations (modified limited duty and lobby attendant positions) as responsive to her medical restrictions.

## Findings

Based on the administrative record and the Administrative Judge's determination:

1. **Reasonable Accommodation**: The Agency provided a modified duty assignment documented as compliant with the Complainant's medical restrictions. No evidence supports that the Complainant was forced to work outside stated restrictions. The severe nature of her medical restrictions limited accommodation options; alternative positions were offered and declined.

2. **Payment and Timekeeping**: Administrative errors resulted from multiple legitimate, nondiscriminatory factors, including facility transition procedures, system coding, loss of manual documentation, and organizational deficiencies unrelated to protected status.

3. **204-B Detail Denial**: The Manager articulated nondiscriminatory reasons (operational needs, concerns regarding punctuality and reliability) for the denial. The Complainant failed to establish that this rationale was pretextual or that the denial constituted retaliation.

The record contains no evidence supporting discrimination or reprisal on a protected basis or in violation of the Rehabilitation Act.
