**Case File Summary**

**Parties and position:** At the time of the events, Complainant was a Carrier Technician at the Agency's Garland-South Station in Garland, Texas.

**Complaint:** On April 17, 2021, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging disability discrimination (physical: neck and shoulder). She raised nine claims:
1. Beginning on or about November 7, 2020, and ongoing, she was continually harassed (questioned) about her work restrictions and not given work assistance.
2. Beginning on or about November 9, 2020, and continuing, she was required to do the walking portion of her route.
3. On December 23, 2020, she was yelled at and embarrassed in front of coworkers.
4. On December 23, 2020, she was required to submit a new CA-17 and was not paid for the time missed to get it completed.
5. On December 23, 2020, she was moved off Route 74 and required to case and carry Route 23.
6. On February 1, 2021, she was called to the supervisor's desk to discuss her medical documents.
7. On March 23, 2021, she was harassed and referred to the District Reasonable Accommodation Committee (DRAC).
8. On April 2, 2021, her leave request was not granted and she was charged Absent Without Leave (AWOL).
9. On or about April 15, 2021, management gave her a modified assignment that changed her start time to 10:00 am and did not provide a mail cart.

**Dismissed claim:** The Agency dismissed a separate claim that Complainant was attacked by a dog on the job and not properly compensated for the time missed. It relied on 29 C.F.R. §1614.107(a)(1), treating the claim as a collateral attack on the Office of Workers' Compensation Programs (OWCP), which has jurisdiction over compensation for work-related injuries. (ROI at 71-75.) The Commission affirmed the dismissal. It stated that a complainant cannot use the EEO process to challenge actions that occurred during the OWCP adjudicatory process, citing *Janiece H. v. U.S. Postal Serv.*, EEOC [number removed] (Jan. 8, 2016).

**Characterization of the claims:** The Commission described the gravamen of the claims as an alleged Rehabilitation Act violation. Complainant alleged that the Manager of Customer Service (Manager) harassed her because of her disability and forced her to work outside her medical restrictions. Complainant specifically argued that:
- the Manager marked her AWOL even though the Supervisor of Customer Service (Supervisor) had previously approved her leave request, and
- the Agency denied her a mail cart.

**Procedural history:**
- After the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ).
- Complainant requested a hearing. The AJ denied it as a sanction because Complainant failed to submit the required Preliminary Case Information and failed to respond to the AJ's Order to Show Cause.
- The AJ remanded the complaint to the Agency. The Agency then issued a final decision under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b), concluding that Complainant failed to prove the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged.
