**Case Summary**

**Parties and Background:** Complainant, born 1973, was a Level 1 City Carrier at the Agency's Meadow Mesa Station in North Las Vegas, Nevada. On February 25, 2023, he filed an EEO complaint alleging age discrimination and harassment.

**Claims Accepted for Investigation:**
1. On August 30, 2022, he was placed on Emergency Placement (EP).
2. On September 14, 2022, he was suspended.
3. On September 20, 2022, a supervisor (S2) followed him to the bathroom, yelled at him, and threatened discipline.
4. On September 24, 2022, S2 accused him of not fastening his seatbelt before starting the engine.
5. On November 1, 2022, he was escorted out of the facility.

**Evidence Gathered:**
- **Claim 1:** The dispute began when Complainant asked S1 to initial a Union time request. Complainant said the Postmaster (PM) then yelled at and tried to intimidate him in the locker room, refused a union steward, and ordered him out. PM and S1 said Complainant was wasting time, did not follow repeated instructions to deliver his route, and was argumentative. They also said no employee on EP could speak with the union before leaving. PM denied intimidating him.
- **Claim 2:** Complainant was walked out on September 14 and placed on EP for allegedly not fastening his seatbelt before starting the engine and leaving the vehicle running. S2 described this as a safety violation. The 14-day suspension letter (September 22) cited only the August 30 failure to follow instructions.
- **Claim 3:** S2 denied yelling. He said he followed Complainant because of performance concerns, as Complainant spent long periods at his locker.
- **Claim 4:** S2 insisted on seatbelts being fastened before the engine started. Safety later told him this was not written policy, and Complainant was allowed to proceed. S1 told Complainant to follow supervisors' instructions.
- **Claim 5:** Complainant gave S1 a doctor's note restricting overtime. He returned with undelivered mail and said he did not have to follow what he considered unsafe instructions. S1 said she asked him three times to finish his route and that Occupational Health Management advised he could finish it that day. He was placed on EP effective November 2, 2022.
- **Age link:** Complainant tied age to overburdened routes, comparisons with younger carriers, and his need for more bathroom breaks and slower recovery from pain. On Claims 1–5 he identified no similarly situated younger employees treated more favorably. For Claims 2 and 4 he said he did not think age was a factor.

**Procedural History:** Complainant received the ROI and requested an EEOC hearing. The Agency moved for a decision without a hearing on December 14, 2023, and Complainant did not object. The AJ granted the motion and issued a decision on January 4, 2024, finding no discrimination or harassment.

**AJ Findings:** The Agency's unrebutted facts established nondiscriminatory reasons. Complainant argued with supervisors, wasted time on his phone in the locker room, failed to fasten his seatbelt, and did not turn off his vehicle when exiting. These facts were undisputed and justified the discipline and other actions. There was no evidence of age-based comments or more favorable treatment of younger workers, so no reasonable factfinder could find age bias. Disability was not raised as a basis.

**Current Status:** The Agency issued a final order fully implementing the AJ's decision. Complainant has appealed.
