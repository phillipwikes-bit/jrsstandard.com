# INVESTIGATION RECORD AND CASE FILE DOCUMENTATION

## COMPLAINANT POSITION AND BACKGROUND

At the time of the events giving rise to this Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) complaint, the Complainant was employed as a GS-0343-13 Management Analyst within the Acquisitions Policy Oversight and Business Strategies Division. This division operates under the Office of the Senior Procurement Executive (OSPE), which is located within the Office of the Secretary (OST) in Washington, D.C. The Complainant's employment record indicates that she had previously filed an EEO complaint in 2016 and subsequently alleged that she had been subjected to ongoing retaliation in connection with that prior protected activity.

## POSITION CLASSIFICATION DISPUTE

A desk audit of the Complainant's position was completed by the Agency in 2016, and the findings determined that her position was properly classified and graded at the GS-13 level. However, despite this official determination, the Complainant identified what she believed to be significant inaccuracies in her position description. Specifically, she contended that errors existed in the "Scope and Effect" and "Supervisory Controls" sections of the official documentation. The Complainant indicated that she repeatedly attempted to address these inaccuracies with management beginning in February 2017, and she asserted that her efforts to correct these errors were unsuccessful.

A meeting between the Complainant and the OSPE Deputy Director (DD-1) was conducted on February 6, 2017. According to DD-1's account, this meeting occurred as the Complainant was preparing to return to OSPE following a detail assignment. During this meeting, the Complainant requested that DD-1 review the alleged inaccuracies in her position description. Following this request, DD-1 stated that she reviewed the position description in consultation with Human Resources and subsequently determined that the position description accurately and appropriately reflected the actual responsibilities and duties associated with the Complainant's position.

The Complainant disputed DD-1's assessment, arguing that neither DD-1 nor the newly assigned Associate Director (AD) of the Acquisitions Policy Oversight and Business Strategies Division possessed sufficient knowledge of her actual job duties to make an informed evaluation. The Complainant asserted that both supervisors were relatively new to OSPE at the time they reviewed the documentation and therefore lacked the institutional knowledge and professional background necessary to assess whether the position description was accurate.

## SHARED DRIVE INCIDENT

According to the Complainant's account, the M-60 shared drive functions as a repository for employees and contractors to archive non-personnel-related work documentation. In June 2017, the Complainant noticed that a coworker had saved her investigative affidavit, which had been submitted in connection with her prior EEO complaint, to the M-60 shared drive. The Complainant alleged that the posting of this sensitive document on a shared drive that was accessible to all office personnel constituted a deliberate action undertaken to damage her professional reputation.

In response to this discovery, the Complainant took a screenshot of the affidavit before removing it from the shared drive and subsequently reported the incident to DD-1. DD-1 provided an account stating that the coworker had inadvertently saved a copy of the affidavit to the shared drive and that she immediately directed the coworker to delete the document once the matter was brought to her attention.

The coworker offered a different explanation for the incident. According to her statement, she had accidentally saved a copy of her own affidavit (which related to the Complainant's EEO complaint) to the M-61 shared drive rather than to her personal "H" drive. The coworker stated that she did not realize the error had occurred until DD-1 brought the matter to her attention. However, the Complainant provided documentary evidence in the form of a screenshot that appears to show the affidavit on the M-60 shared drive, creating a factual discrepancy regarding which shared drive contained the document. The Complainant contended that DD-1 should have imposed disciplinary measures against the coworker for this incident.

## DIGITAL IT ACQUISITION PROGRAM DISPUTE

The Complainant indicated that she had communicated to the Associate Director on multiple occasions that she would need to consult with her attorney before providing information or completing work assignments related to the Digital IT Acquisition Program. The Complainant explained that she held this position because the Digital IT Acquisition Program was directly related to the subject matter of her prior EEO complaint.

On June 29, 2018, the Associate Director met with the Complainant to discuss this matter. The Complainant characterized the meeting as an interrogation, stating that the tone and manner of questioning were aggressive and inappropriate. She further stated that after leaving the Associate Director's office and returning to her cubicle, she received another email from the Associate Director within minutes. When the Complainant approached the Associate Director's doorway to inquire about the timing and purpose of the follow-up email, she alleges that the Associate Director responded by stating, "You think everyone is out to get you."

The Associate Director's version of these events differs significantly from the Complainant's account. According to the Associate Director, the Complainant had indicated that she was unable to complete a work assignment because her attorney had advised her not to respond. The Associate Director stated that during the June 29, 2018 meeting, she asked the Complainant why she could not complete the assignment, at which point the Complainant disclosed that she had a pending EEO complaint related to the topic of the assignment. The Associate Director further stated that the Complainant mentioned during this conversation that she had not enjoyed working for her previous supervisors because she believed they had treated her unfairly. The Associate Director asserted that she responded to this comment by stating that she had no intention of treating the Complainant unfairly, which was intended as a reference to address the Complainant's expressed concerns about past unfair treatment. The Associate Director explicitly denied making the statement attributed to her by the Complainant regarding everyone being out to get her.

## ALLEGATIONS OF MICROMANAGEMENT AND TELEWORK RESTRICTIONS

The Complainant alleged that she was subjected to excessive micromanagement and overmanagement by her supervisory chain, and that she also faced unjustified restrictions on her telework arrangements. To illustrate these allegations, the Complainant cited specific incidents occurring in September 2018. During this period, DD-1 and the Associate Director requested additional documentation to support managerial approvals for the Warrior to DOT Contracting Intern Program (W2DOT Program). The Complainant alleged that after providing the requested information, neither supervisor responded to her subsequent emails seeking approval for the W2DOT Program initiatives.

Regarding the telework allegations, the Complainant indicated that a new telework policy implemented by OSPE was significantly more restrictive for employees who had been operating under "ad hoc" telework arrangements. Under this new policy, such employees were permitted to telework only one day per week, and this arrangement required supervisory notification and consideration of extenuating circumstances.

DD-1 provided an alternative perspective on these matters. She stated that she had requested multiple times that the Complainant produce a consolidated program and project schedule for the W2DOT Program and that the Complainant had responded to these requests by complaining that she was being micromanaged rather than providing the requested documentation. DD-1 further stated that she never actually produced an adequate program and project schedule.

Concerning telework, DD-1 asserted that the Complainant had been operating under a "very liberal ad hoc telework schedule" before any policy changes were implemented. According to DD-1, the Complainant generally arrived at the office around 8:30 or 9:00 in the morning and departed at 3:00 in the afternoon, after which she would telework for the remainder of the day. DD-1 stated that she did not impose any restrictions on the Complainant's telework arrangement even after implementing the OSPE Timekeeping and Telework Standard Operating Procedure on July 1, 2018. The Associate Director corroborated this statement, noting that the Complainant's telework schedule had been "grandfathered" in because it predated the implementation of the new procedure.

## FAC-P/PM-IT CERTIFICATION DISPUTE

The Complainant described herself as the Agency's acquisition workforce program manager and stated that she is responsible for various functions including Agency system administrative support, system configurations, controls, workflows, and all aspects of Agency user interface issues and technical support. The Complainant further asserted that despite completing all required training for the Federal Acquisition Certification for Program and Project Managers Information Technology Core-Plus Specialization (FAC-P/PM-IT), the Associate Director denied her application for certification without providing an explanation of the deficiencies in her application.

The Complainant claimed that her role includes responsibility for approving bureau-level FAC-P/PM-IT certification applications before their submission to the IT Office for final approval. However, both the Associate Director and DD-1 contradicted this characterization of her duties. They stated that the Complainant is neither required nor expected to hold FAC-P/PM-IT certification for her current position. According to their statements, Agency policy establishes that this specialized certification is intended primarily for individuals who are principally responsible for the acquisition of IT investments. They further noted that the Complainant does not hold such responsibility in her position and that her application failed to document the minimum two years of required experience mandated by Agency policy.

The Complainant responded to these denials by asserting that the Associate Director was incorrect in her assessment and that the Complainant's position is inherently an IT position. She also contended that the Associate Director was attempting to marginalize her work and professional contributions.

## SUPERVISORY REASSIGNMENT REQUEST

The Complainant submitted a request to be reassigned to a supervisor outside of the direct chain of command encompassing both DD-1 and the Associate Director. This request was denied. Additionally, the Complainant requested a detail assignment to the Assistant Secretary for Administration (M) during the summer of 2018. DD-1 denied this request, citing inadequate resources to fulfill the Complainant's existing duties during her potential absence.

According to DD-1's account, the Complainant had specifically asked to directly report to the Senior Procurement Executive (SPE), who was DD-1's supervisor. However, the SPE denied this request. The Complainant subsequently proposed two alternative supervisors as potential solutions. On August 7, 2018, DD-1 informed the Complainant that neither proposed supervisor was a logical option given the nature of her duties. DD-1 urged the Complainant to improve her working relationship with the Associate Director, noting that the Associate Director had only been serving as her supervisor for approximately one month at that time.

The Complainant argued that it was inappropriate for DD-1 to be involved in reviewing her request for reassignment away from DD-1's chain of command, as this created an inherent conflict of interest.

## AGILE INITIATIVES PANEL REQUEST

The Complainant stated that she had been invited by a coach to participate as a speaker on a panel discussing Agile initiatives. However, she alleged that the Associate Director marginalized this professional opportunity and refused to permit her to speak on the panel. The Complainant was offered the alternative of participating as an audience member rather than as a panelist.

The Associate Director's explanation differed from the Complainant's characterization. According to the Associate Director, she denied the Complainant's request to speak because OSPE had not implemented substantial Agile practices beyond hosting a few limited-scope events. The Associate Director emphasized that she had extended the offer for the Complainant to participate in the panel as an attendee.

DD-1 provided additional context regarding the panel, noting that it had been organized by a contractor to discuss changes that federal employees would experience as various agencies adopted Agile Unleashed methodologies. DD-1 asserted that because OSPE had not implemented any Agile initiatives outside of the technology sector, the Complainant did not possess Agency-specific knowledge that would have been valuable to contribute to the panel discussion.

## COMPLAINT FILING AND INVESTIGATION PROCESS

The Complainant initiated contact with an EEO counselor on July 19, 2018. Following the conclusion of the counseling process, she filed a formal EEO complaint on October 2, 2018, alleging that the Agency had discriminated against her on the basis of reprisal for prior protected EEO activity in violation of Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. The complaint enumerated seven distinct incidents that the Complainant characterized as retaliatory actions. These incidents spanned from February 2017 through September 2018 and encompassed the various matters detailed above.

Upon completion of the investigation, the Agency provided the Complainant with a copy of the Report of Investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge. The Complainant timely requested such a hearing but subsequently withdrew her request. Consequently, the Agency issued a final decision pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b), concluding that the Complainant had failed to substantiate the discrimination allegations as presented.

## APPEAL ARGUMENTS

On appeal, the Complainant contends that the investigative record is incomplete and requests that the Agency be sanctioned for such inadequacy. She also presents new evidence in support of her appeal and argues that she has established both a prima facie case of reprisal and that the Agency's stated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons are pretextual. The Complainant requests a favorable decision from the Commission and the opportunity to submit evidence regarding her damages.

In response, the Agency contends that the investigative record is complete and adequate. The Agency argues that it would be inappropriate for the Complainant to raise arguments about record incompleteness on appeal, particularly given that she failed to submit documents requested by the EEO investigator and failed to notify the Agency of any perceived deficiencies after reviewing the investigative record. The Agency further notes that the Complainant withdrew her hearing request the day before scheduled discovery was set to commence. The Agency suggests that evidence submitted on appeal was reasonably available during the investigation and should not be considered at this stage. The Agency requests that the Commission affirm the final decision in favor of the Agency.
