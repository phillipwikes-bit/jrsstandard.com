# INVESTIGATION RECORD - CASE FILE DOCUMENT

## CASE BACKGROUND AND COMPLAINANT'S EMPLOYMENT STATUS

At the time of the events forming the basis of this complaint, the Complainant was employed as a Mail Processing Clerk at the Agency's Atlanta Processing and Distribution Center located in Atlanta, Georgia. Prior to filing the current complaint, the Complainant had submitted an Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) complaint in September 2018, which remained pending before an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ) at the time the Complainant provided her affidavit for the instant complaint. The Complainant's prior complaint involved allegations of sexual harassment by a co-worker, a matter that would subsequently prove relevant to her current claims.

## COMPLAINANT'S MEDICAL CONDITION AND DOCUMENTED RESTRICTIONS

The Complainant reported suffering from post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and depression resulting from an incident of sexual harassment and assault by a co-worker. Beyond her mental health conditions, the Complainant also documented physical limitations affecting her ability to perform standard work duties. Specifically, she suffers from back, foot, and knee issues that prevent her from standing or walking for extended periods. Additionally, the Complainant has documented restrictions on climbing and descending stairs, as well as limitations on bending, stooping, and lifting activities.

Most significantly, the Complainant's physician provided written documentation stating that due to her PTSD resulting from the co-worker's sexual assault, she could no longer work in the same facility as the individual who assaulted her. This medical restriction established a clear occupational need for relocation to accommodate her documented disability.

## TIMELINE OF EVENTS AND ALLEGED FAILURES TO ACCOMMODATE

The sexual harassment and assault incident occurred in September 2019. Following this incident, the Complainant took Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) leave. In April 2020, after her medical leave concluded, the Complainant was cleared to return to work at a facility different from her harasser. However, upon her return, the Complainant discovered that her harasser remained at the original facility, apparently without any transfer or reassignment action having been taken.

In June 2020, the Complainant sustained a workplace knee injury. She submitted documentation regarding this injury to the Agency in July 2020 but received no response. When the Agency's Reasonable Accommodation Committee eventually requested additional documentation, the Committee ignored the Complainant's PTSD-related limitation requiring separation from her harasser and instead focused exclusively on her physical restrictions stemming from the knee injury.

The record reflects significant delays in addressing the Complainant's accommodation needs. Not until July 2021—nearly two years after the initial sexual assault and approximately one year after her return-to-work clearance—was the Complainant finally provided a modified assignment at a different location that accommodated her physical restrictions. Notably, the record contains no explanation or documentation concerning the nature of this new position or why the Agency required a full year to relocate the Complainant away from her harasser.

The Complainant went on COVID-related leave on August 16, 2021, and was subsequently hospitalized for sepsis. She was not cleared to return to work until September 21, 2021. However, from that date forward, the Complainant was not returned to work. According to her account, management informed her only that no work was available based on her documented restrictions. Critically, the record contains no statement from any Agency official documenting that any effort was undertaken to accommodate the Complainant's restrictions from September 2021 onward, nor any evidence that such accommodation efforts were attempted and failed.

## LEAVE AND BID POSITION DENIAL

The Complainant alleged that she should not have been placed on leave without pay (LWOP) beginning November 21, 2021, given that her inability to work resulted from the Agency's failure to accommodate her documented medical restrictions. In January 2022, the Complainant submitted a bid for a position. However, her bid was denied, and the position was awarded to another employee with less seniority. The Complainant was marked "ineligible" with no explanation provided at the time of the denial.

When questioned about the bid denial, the Supervisor of Distribution Operations stated in her affidavit that the Complainant's bid was rejected because the Complainant had not provided updated medical documentation demonstrating that she could fully perform her duties within six months. However, the record contains no evidence that the Complainant was ever formally requested to provide such documentation or informed that such documentation was required. The Complainant consistently stated she received no explanation for the denial.

The Collective Bargaining Agreement governing this matter specifically provides that management may request medical certification at the time of bid submission or thereafter, but no evidence exists that the Complainant was informed of this procedural requirement.

## THE FORMAL EEO COMPLAINT

On May 13, 2022, the Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging discrimination by the Agency on the bases of race (African-American, Native American, and Cuban), sex (female), color (Brown), disability (both physical and mental), and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity. These allegations were made under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and Section 501 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973. The complaint specifically addressed three primary claims: failure to accommodate her medical restrictions since November 10, 2021; charging her LWOP since November 10, 2021; and denying her a bid position on January 24, 2022.

The Complainant also asserted three additional claims: denial of accommodation between April 2020 and November 9, 2021; improper coding of leave as workers' compensation (OWCP) leave on unspecified dates; and denial of insurance benefits on unspecified dates.

## AGENCY DISMISSALS AND PROCEDURAL DETERMINATIONS

The Agency issued a final decision on December 6, 2022, pursuant to applicable federal regulations. The Agency found that the Complainant had not submitted required medical documentation to support reasonable accommodation requests or to demonstrate her ability to perform the bid position within six months. The Agency also determined that the Complainant had not established pretext for discrimination.

Regarding the additional claims, the Agency dismissed the denial of reasonable accommodation claim for dates prior to November 10, 2021, citing untimely EEO counselor contact. The Agency further dismissed the leave coding and insurance benefits claims as collateral attacks on the workers' compensation adjudicatory process. The Complainant subsequently appealed this decision.
