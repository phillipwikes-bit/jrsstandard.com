# INVESTIGATION SUMMARY FOR CASE FILE

## COMPLAINANT POSITION AND TIMEFRAME
Complainant worked as a Counseling Psychologist/Licensed Marriage and Family Therapist at the Agency's Health Clinic in Baumholder, Germany, from September 2014 onward.

## ALLEGATIONS AND RELEVANT DATES

### a) General Workplace Conduct (September 2014 to present)
Complainant alleged that from September 2014 onward, management failed to provide training, subjected her to derogatory comments, unjustified interrogations, nitpicking of work, and negative facial expressions. She alleged her authority was undermined, coworkers were encouraged to circumvent the chain-of-command, her decisions were overruled, and she was forced to prove negatives regarding false portrayals. She further alleged Agency officials used their influence to disparage and isolate her, did not reward her work, and failed to investigate despite her reports to her supervisory chain.

### b) Client Rescheduling (Unspecified dates)
Complainant alleged other employees rescheduled her clients with other providers, resulting in clients being told they could not meet with her when they requested her by name.

### c) October 5, 2016 Staff Meeting
Complainant alleged the Captain made decisions about her clients without her input at a staff meeting.

### d) October 12, 2016 Meeting
Complainant alleged that during a meeting with the Lieutenant Colonel and Lieutenant to discuss her request to be removed from the Baumholder Team, she felt attacked based on their tone of voice, which she considered disrespectful.

### e) November 12, 2016 Performance Evaluation
Complainant alleged she was issued a performance evaluation that failed to accurately and truthfully characterize her work performance.

### f) December 21, 2016 Client Removal
Upon returning to work on December 21, 2016, after being out sick on December 19-20, 2016, Complainant alleged two clients were taken from her schedule and redirected to other providers.

### g) Staff Cooperation During Shortage
Complainant alleged that despite a shortage of providers due to the holidays, staff refused to engage her to help cover cases.

### h) December 2016 Introduction of New Staff Member
The Non-Commissioned Officer-In-Charge (NCOIC) brought a new staff member down and introduced him to all providers and staff in the office, but allegedly did not introduce him to Complainant even though they were talking right outside her office.

### i) Inspector General Contact and Legal Counsel Coordination (October 2016 – January 2017)
On October 5, 7, and 13, 2016, Complainant submitted complaints to the Regional Health Command-Europe Inspector General (IG) regarding discrimination, harassment, and abuse of authority. On December 6, 2016, Complainant was contacted by Sergeant First Class regarding her IG complaint. Complainant informed the Sergeant First Class that her legal counsel (Counsel) would be in Germany the week of January 9, 2017, and she wished to coordinate a meeting. Counsel emailed the Sergeant First Class asking about the nature of the meeting, but the Sergeant First Class did not respond and the meeting did not occur. Complainant later asserted that the Sergeant First Class did respond, but by then her Counsel was no longer in Germany.

### j) January 27, 2017 Developmental Counseling
Complainant was given developmental counseling regarding her temporary reassignment.

## PRIOR ADMINISTRATIVE GRIEVANCE
On November 28, 2014, Complainant filed an Administrative Grievance bringing up many claims included in the present complaint (ROI1 at 203). On January 30, 2015, Complainant received a notice of decision granting her requested remedy of formal mediation to work through her concerns to improve the work environment, and the grievance was closed (ROI1 at 209).

## RESPONSES TO ALLEGATIONS

### Training
The Commander for Baumholder, Landstuhl and Kaiserslautern Clinics (Commander) stated that Complainant informed him of her desire to participate in a non-military professional development session regarding suicide and military personnel (ROI2 at 98). The Commander informed her that the Agency could not fund her to do personal research but could provide her the time without pay. The Commander asserted he did not deny her training opportunities (ROI2 at 98). In another request, the Lieutenant stated that he approved Complainant's wishes to attend Suicide Prevention training in Vicenza, Italy, but there were no funds to attend, noting the training was ultimately cancelled (ROI1 at 447).

### Client Rescheduling
On September 20, 2016, Complainant sent the Supervisory Psychologist/Chief of Behavioral Health (Chief) an email alleging the Nurse Practitioner told two of her patients they were transferring to another provider and told another patient Complainant no longer worked in the clinic and could not see him (ROI1 at 232). The Social Services Assistant noted that Complainant had previously stated she only worked with Marriage and Family counseling patients, which led to some transfers and limited the patients she could see (ROI2 at 113). The Nurse Practitioner reiterated this (ROI2 at 143). The Lieutenant Colonel stated that he investigated the situation and resolved the matter, finding that in one situation the transfer was unintentional and the client was switched back (ROI2 at 436).

### October 5, 2016 Meeting
On October 5, 2016, two Interactive Customer Evaluation (ICE) complaints were submitted by two different patients regarding Complainant's standard of care to the Inspector General (IG) (ROI1 at 217). On October 5, 2016, during a standard High-Risk meeting, the Captain discussed concerns regarding two of Complainant's patients (ROI2 at 6). Immediately following the meeting, Complainant spoke with the Captain. The Captain asserted that Complainant was aggressive, recorded her without consent, spoke loudly regarding the two patients in an open space and within earshot of other patients (ROI2 at 6). The Captain denied making any changes (ROI2 at 11) and stated the meeting was discussing care plans for patients, which occurs with every provider (ROI2 at 11). The Social Services Assistant stated High-Risk meetings were so various providers could coordinate appropriate care (ROI2 at 110) and that everyone provides their perspective on a patient, but no one was telling anyone how to do their jobs (ROI2 at 110). Complainant spoke to the Lieutenant Colonel informing him she no longer wanted to be part of the meetings and wanted a reassignment (ROI1 at 437).

### October 12, 2016 Meeting
On October 12, 2016, the Lieutenant Colonel and Lieutenant met with Complainant to discuss her request to be removed from the Baumholder Team. The Lieutenant Colonel and Lieutenant noted that Complainant repeatedly alleged the environment was hostile but when asked for examples, failed to provide any specifics (ROI1 at 437; 448). Complainant asserted that the Lieutenant Colonel and Colonel were hostile to her (ROI1 at 447). Both denied being aggressive, stated they did not want to continue if she was uncomfortable, and offered to have another neutral party present (ROI1 at 455).

### Performance Evaluation
On or about November 12, 2016, the Lieutenant Colonel issued Complainant a performance evaluation based on a standard of 15 metrics (ROI1 at 438). The Lieutenant Colonel stated that Complainant received eleven "Exceed" ratings and four "Met" ratings (ROI1 at 438). The areas marked as "Met" were areas regarding collaboration with staff. The Lieutenant Colonel noted he provided Complainant with examples to support his rating, noting that for most of May 2016 through October 31, 2016, there was no collaboration with staff (ROI1 at 439). Complainant was provided with an overall Excellent rating. The Lieutenant, as Complainant's second-line rater, agreed with the review (ROI1 at 456).

### December 21, 2016 Client Removal
After returning to work on December 21, 2016, after being out sick on December 19-20, 2016, Complainant alleged two clients were taken from her schedule and redirected to other providers. The Lieutenant Colonel, the Colonel, and the Chief all attested that they were unaware of any issues on that particular date.

### Introduction of New Staff Member
Complainant stated she does not know who the new staff member was, acknowledged she did not leave her office to introduce herself, and did not inform the NCOIC or anyone else about the matter (ROI1 at 398-99).

### IG Communication Issue
The EEO Investigator noted she contacted the Sergeant First Class for an interview (ROI2 at 173). The IG Office, including the Sergeant First Class, called the EEO Investigator and stated that internal IG policy is that if the Agency is taking action, then the IG Office abstains from involvement (ROI2 at 173).

## INSPECTOR GENERAL INVESTIGATION

On November 15, 2016, the IG issued a report based on the October 5, 7, and 13, 2016 complaints (ROI1 at 217-26). The IG report found that Complainant violated German law by unlawfully voice recording the conversation without the other party's consent (ROI1 at 225). The report concluded there was no evidence to support Complainant's allegations of harassment or discrimination. However, Complainant was found to have created a disruptive environment within the Baumholder Behavioral Health Clinic. The primary recommendation stated: "to move [Complainant] out of the Baumholder Health Clinic due to the contentious nature of her interactions with the rest of the Behavioral Health Staff. Should she remain assigned to the staff, I believe there will continue to be conflict between her and the rest of the team, which demonstrably impacts patient care and significantly decreases moral throughout the clinic" (ROI1 at 217). The IG report is part of the record (ROI1 at 217).

## TEMPORARY REASSIGNMENT AND FINAL COUNSELING

On January 4, 2017, Complainant was temporarily assigned to the Landstuhl Regional Medical Center (LRMC) (ROI1 at 459). On January 27, 2017, she was given developmental counseling regarding her temporary reassignment. The Chief stated this was standard counseling and that there was nothing negative about it (ROI1 at 459).

## ADMINISTRATIVE JUDGE DECISION

The Agency submitted a Motion for a Decision Without a Hearing (Motion) on October 18, 2021. Complainant responded to the Motion. On January 31, 2022, the Administrative Judge (AJ) issued his decision. The AJ determined that the investigative record was adequately developed and that after making all reasonable inferences in favor of Complainant, there were no material facts in dispute. Regarding the merits of Complainant's claim, the AJ determined that the Agency had set forth non-discriminatory reasons for the actions detailed and that Complainant failed to demonstrate such reasons were pretext for discrimination. The AJ determined that Complainant failed to demonstrate the Agency's conduct met the severe and pervasive elements needed to sustain a proper unlawful hostile work environment claim. The AJ found Complainant's complaint could be characterized by her utter dissatisfaction and personal grievances against her coworkers and management. The AJ granted the Agency's Motion. When the Agency failed to issue a final action, the AJ's decision became the Agency's final action by operation of law.
