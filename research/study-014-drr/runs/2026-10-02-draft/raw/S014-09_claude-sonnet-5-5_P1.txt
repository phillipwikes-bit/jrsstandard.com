**Summary of Investigation Record**

**Parties and background:** Two Complainants worked remotely from California (Pauma Valley and Thousand Palms) as Emergency Management Specialists and Tribal Government Liaisons at the Agency's First Responder Network Authority. Both were hired in October 2015 at Pay Band IV.

**Complaints:** Beginning June 13, 2019, each Complainant filed a formal EEO complaint alleging discrimination and harassment based on race (Native American), national origin (Native American), and sex, as well as reprisal for prior protected EEO activity. They withdrew the sex claims during the hearing process. The claims were:

1. **Pay discrimination since hire.**
   - Complainant 1 was told during salary negotiations that the Agency had to offer only 70% of his prior wages ("take it or leave it"), and he alleged non-Natives were not treated this way. Complainant 2 alleged her prior salary and teaching wages were not accounted for.
   - Complainants did the same work as Pay Band V peers but were hired at Band IV and paid at the lowest level.
   - Management took no action on an April 2018 review of wage and pay band disparities, and the Executive Director and Senior Director denied or downplayed knowledge of it (subparts 1(c)–1(g)). Complainants allege the Deputy Chief Administrative Officer told them in June 2018 the Agency had no personnel policies and practices and failed to inform them of their EEO rights. In January 2019 the Executive Director told Supervisor 2 to "tamper any escalation" of HR issues for the tribal team, then retracted the instruction. One AJ consolidated these subparts into a single allegation of no remedial action after the review, and the decision treats 1(c)–1(g) as falling under it.
2. **Barred from senior leadership positions** because those roles were open only to Pay Band V employees.
3. **June 2018 reorganization** allegedly "buried" Complainants deeper in the organization to push them to quit and limit HR escalations.
4. **Separate strategic plans** required of Complainants but not of non-Tribal peers, ongoing since at least June 2018.
5. **Contractor hostility** toward the Tribal team, reported to senior leadership between June 2018 and February 2019 but allegedly condoned.
6. **Unequal access to resources**, including a separate coordination process, a "gatekeeper" for contractor and Tribal stakeholder engagement, limits on meeting participation, and a required Contracting Officer's Representative in daily contractor engagements.
7. **May 5, 2019 tweet** that thanked local, state, and federal workers but did not mention the Tribal team.

**Procedural history:** After investigation, Complainants received the investigative files and requested EEOC hearings. The complaints went to two different AJs in the Washington Field Office. Complainants' shared attorney moved to consolidate, and the Agency opposed. One AJ denied the motion and the other did not address it. Each AJ separately issued summary judgment decisions finding no discrimination or retaliation. The Agency's final actions implemented those decisions, and both Complainants have appealed.
