# INVESTIGATION RECORD

**Case File Summary: Employment Discrimination Investigation**

---

## Background and Employment Status

During the period under investigation, the Complainant was employed by the Agency as a Mail Handler in a Modified Assignment with Limited Duty status, classified as PS 4/P, at the Church Street Station location in New York, New York. The Complainant was on an approved modified assignment due to a documented lumbosacral sprain. During the specified period from July 5, 2019, through July 31, 2019, the Complainant was not permitted to work. The Agency accepted the complaint for formal investigation, and the following pertinent facts were established through the investigative process.

## Union Grievance and Craft Assignment Issues

On June 15, 2019, the American Postal Workers Union, AFL-CIO, initiated a Step 2 grievance against the Agency. The grievance alleged that the Complainant's assignment to perform Clerk Craft duties while maintaining her Modified Assignment status violated the collective bargaining agreement between the union and the Agency. Specifically, the union contended that the assignment crossed craft lines, as it involved someone outside the Clerk Craft performing duties designated for Clerk Craft employees. Documentation supporting this grievance, including the formal grievance filing, a union request for a detailed list of the Complainant's Modified Assignment duties, and affidavits from the Manager of Customer Services (identified as S2), who served as the Complainant's second-line supervisor, were included in the investigation record.

The Complainant did not dispute that she had performed Clerk Craft duties during her modified assignment.

## Medical Documentation Requests

On June 11, 2019, the Agency's Health & Resource Management (HRM) department contacted the Complainant. The HRM office noted that her most recent medical documentation on file was dated January 18, 2011—more than eight years prior—and requested that she submit an updated medical note from her treating physician that included her current medical restrictions. This request was reiterated on June 21, 2019.

According to statements from both S2 and the Complainant's first-line supervisor (identified as S1), the purpose of requesting updated medical documentation was to enable the Agency to draft a revised Modified Assignment that would align with her current medical restrictions while eliminating the craft-crossing issue identified in the union grievance. Management sought to develop an assignment within appropriate craft lines and within the scope of her medical limitations.

Despite these requests, the Complainant refused to submit an updated doctor's note. She believed the submission was unnecessary and declined to provide the documentation. Subsequently, S2 directed the Complainant not to return to work until she provided an updated doctor's note describing her current medical restrictions. The Complainant anticipated that once she submitted the updated medical documentation, she would be permitted to resume work.

## Submission of Medical Documentation

On July 5, 2019, the Complainant reported to work and submitted a medical letter dated July 2, 2019. The doctor's letter contained the following restrictions: lifting, pushing and pulling not to exceed twenty pounds; reaching above shoulder height not to exceed twenty pounds; standing and walking for an average duration of twenty minutes.

According to S2's account, HRM determined that the submitted medical letter was insufficient because the limitations were not stated with adequate clarity. S2 reported that he subsequently requested that the Complainant submit another updated doctor's letter providing more precise restrictions. S1 corroborated that S2 had relayed this information to him, though S1 did not reference direct communication with HRM. The Complainant ultimately submitted a revised doctor's note after July 31, 2019, and returned to work on August 1, 2019.

The HRM Manager stated that S2 had made the independent decision to prohibit the Complainant from working during this period. The HRM Manager indicated that he became aware of this decision only after S2 implemented it and acknowledged that he was not initially informed of the rationale for Complainant's removal from the limited duty assignment.

## Management's Resolution and New Modified Assignment

On July 22, 2019, the HRM Manager sent correspondence to the Complainant indicating that the Agency was reviewing her Modified Assignment (Limited Duty) status. This review was prompted by an arbitration award addressing the assignment of non-Clerk Craft employees to Clerk Craft duties. The HRM Manager advised that management would conduct a search for appropriate and available work within her medical restrictions and would offer a revised Modified Assignment if suitable positions were identified.

On July 25, 2019, the HRM Manager presented the Complainant with a formal Offer of Modified Assignment (Limited Duty) to begin August 1, 2019. The new position was Modified Handler (Modified Assignment – indefinite duration) at the James A. Farley Retail Local Operation Center. The job duties differed from her previous assignment. Importantly, the Offer letter explicitly stated that the proposed duties fell within the restrictions outlined in her July 2, 2019 treating physician's letter. The restrictions listed in the formal Offer were identical to those documented in her physician's letter.

## Administrative and Legal Proceedings

Following completion of the investigation, the Complainant requested a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge. When the Complainant subsequently withdrew this request, the Administrative Judge directed the Agency to issue a Final Agency Decision (FAD).

In its FAD, the Agency concluded that no discrimination had occurred. The Agency assumed, without making an explicit finding, that the Complainant constituted an individual with a disability. However, the Agency determined that the Complainant was not qualified to perform the essential functions of the unmodified Mail Handler position, either with or without reasonable accommodation. The Agency further reasoned that even if the Complainant were otherwise qualified, she failed to submit the requested medical documentation necessary for management to develop an appropriate modified assignment within her medical limitations. Additionally, the Agency found that the Complainant had not established discrimination under a disparate treatment theory, noting specifically that she had not identified any comparable employees who received differential treatment.

The Complainant subsequently appealed the Agency's decision.
