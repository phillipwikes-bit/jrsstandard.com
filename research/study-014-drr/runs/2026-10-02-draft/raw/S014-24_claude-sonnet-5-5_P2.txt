## 1. Scope and Standard

These findings address the eleven allegations accepted in the EEO complaint. Complainant alleges discrimination and harassment based on race (Caucasian), national origin (German, Persian), sex (female), religion (Jehovah's Witness, Jewish), and reprisal. Each allegation is assessed on a preponderance-of-the-evidence standard: whether the event is more likely than not to have occurred as alleged, and, if so, whether the evidence shows it was motivated by a protected basis or was sufficiently severe or pervasive to create a hostile work environment.

"Not substantiated" means the evidence does not establish the allegation by a preponderance. It does not mean the event is found not to have occurred.

### 3.2 Allegation 2: Christmas Party Exchange

Complainant states that the Support Specialist asked why she was not attending the party and whether she celebrated Christmas. Complainant replied that she is a Jehovah's Witness. She says the Support Specialist "looked at her as if she had a disease" and rolled her eyes. ### 3.4 Allegation 4: Sexual Harassment and Retaliatory Treatment by the Deputy Director

**Alleged conduct.** Complainant alleges that the Deputy Director:
- stared at her chest;
- twice placed his hand on her left leg and "rolled" it down during staff meetings (she "scooted over" the first time and left the second meeting midway);
- put his hands on her shoulders in a massaging manner in her cubicle;
- leaned over her at her computer, invading her space; and
- repeatedly asked her to lunch or dinner.

She states that she told him to stop. She alleges that after she rebuffed him, he assigned extra duties, refused training, assigned work outside her position description, and yelled and cursed at her about travel processing. She also alleges that he called her into his office to vent loudly and throw pens and pencils.

**Evidence bearing on the allegation.**
- *No eyewitness to physical contact.* No witness directly observed the alleged touching, staring, or invitations.
- *Consistent contemporaneous disclosures.* At least seven coworkers state that Complainant told them of the conduct. The accounts are specific and come from different people:
  - The Senior Budget Analyst states that Complainant came to her almost weekly to vent about the Deputy Director violating her personal space, asking her out, and making inappropriate comments, and that she sometimes came in crying.
  - The Team Lead states that Complainant told him the Deputy Director touched her inappropriately in a meeting and that she had scheduled a meeting with the Management Officer about it.
  - The Public Health Advisor Supervisor states that Complainant said the Deputy Director touched her leg and asked her to dinner.
  - The Resource Management Team Lead states that Complainant described extreme discomfort.
  - Another Team Lead and two other employees report similar disclosures.
- *Partial direct observation.*
  - The Senior Budget Analyst saw the Deputy Director throw tantrums and yell at Complainant as if she were his "soundboard."
  - The Resource Management Team Lead saw him in Complainant's work area many times and perceived an unusual encroachment on her personal space, though not touching or inappropriate remarks.
- *Pattern evidence.* The Public Health Advisor states that the Deputy Director harassed her in the past and that she did not report it because she would have been the first to come forward and he was highly regarded within the Agency. This is uncorroborated, and its weight should be assessed in light of the Deputy Director's response.
- *Deputy Director's response.* [His statement is not included in the record provided. It must be reviewed and summarized here before any finding is finalized.]

**Analysis.** Disclosures to coworkers do not independently prove the underlying events, but they are relevant to credibility. They are numerous and consistent, were made over time, and came with observed emotional distress. The absence of an eyewitness is not dispositive, because the conduct alleged (touching under a table, one-on-one remarks, private invitations) is of a kind that often has no third-party witness. The Agency's final decision treated the lack of eyewitnesses as a reason to find insufficient evidence. That reasoning gives little weight to the corroborative evidence described above.

**Finding (investigator to select):**
- *Option A:* The preponderance of the evidence supports that the Deputy Director engaged in unwelcome conduct of a sexual nature toward Complainant, and that he engaged in angry, volatile conduct toward her, including yelling and throwing objects. The latter is directly witnessed.
- *Option B:* The record is insufficient to resolve the allegation until the Deputy Director and the Director are interviewed or their statements are located. The yelling and throwing of objects is substantiated by a witness. Whether it was linked to Complainant's rebuffing of him is unresolved.

**Retaliatory-treatment component.** The record contains no documents, such as training requests and denials, assignment records, or the position description, showing extra duties, denied training, or out-of-scope assignments. [These should be obtained.] A causal link between any such actions and the alleged rebuffing is not yet established.

**Notice to management.** Complainant states she reported the conduct to the Director, S1, the Management Officer, and a Consultant, and that nothing was done. S1 and the Management Officer deny that she reported it. The Team Lead's statement that Complainant told him of a meeting with the Management Officer, and later of the Management Officer's lack of concern, is consistent with Complainant's account but is secondhand. The statements of the Director and the Consultant are [not in the record provided]. Whether management knew, or should have known, and whether it took prompt corrective action remains **unresolved** pending those statements.

### 3.6 Allegations 6–9 and 11: Performance Evaluations, PAP, PIP, and Counseling Memorandum

**Undisputed facts.** S1 issued the following:
- a mid-year review with an unsatisfactory rating (2.06);
- a PAP dated May 4, 2014, citing untimely responses to customers about travel tickets and failure to edit travel reports for accuracy and completeness;
- a PIP dated September 22, 2014, rating Complainant "Achieved Unsatisfactory Results" in Customer Service. It cites failure to document and resolve complaints without peer assistance, untimely task completion, failure to keep customers updated, more than three customer complaints, and a complaint from the Senior FATA. In one example, Complainant needed constant peer assistance to correct a travel voucher the Senior FATA requested; and
- a Counseling Memorandum dated October 16, 2014, describing Complainant's conduct at the PIP meeting as loud, rude, verbally abusive, and uncontrolled. It also records her statement that she was being retaliated against and her complaint that S1 allowed peers to call her a "wigger."

Complainant received a 2 on her end-of-year evaluation from S1 on January 29, 2015, after her transfer.

**Legitimate reasons.** S1 articulated documented, performance-based reasons for each action. The specific deficiencies are recorded in the PAP and PIP.

**Evidence bearing on motive.**
- *Race-based remarks.* Complainant states that S1 made comments such as "Stop trying to act Black," "Are you sure you're not Black?" and "Don't worry, we all know you're Black." [S1's response to these remarks is not in the record provided and must be confirmed. They are not among the enumerated allegations.] If made, they would be evidence of race-related bias by the same official who issued the ratings and plans.
- *Distress attributed to S1.* The Support Services Specialist observed Complainant shaking and in extreme distress, said she thought an ambulance might be needed, and said Complainant attributed the distress to S1's treatment. This is relevant to the working environment but does not address the accuracy of the evaluations.

[The "prior protected EEO activity" cited in the complaint is not identified in the record, and S1's awareness of any protected activity and its date is not shown.]

**Finding:** The record does not establish that the actions were motivated by Complainant's race, national origin, sex, religion, or protected activity. The stated performance reasons are documented. This finding is conditional on:
- S1's response to the alleged race-based remarks;
- verification of the underlying performance data (customer complaints, the Senior FATA complaint, comparative treatment of peers); and
- confirmation of S1's knowledge of protected activity.

### 3.7 Allegation 10: Reassignment (December 12, 2014)

Complainant was told she would move to another team under a different supervisor "so she could get a new start." She stated the move was good for her and that she noticed a more positive working environment.

**Finding:** Not substantiated as an adverse action. The record indicates a change Complainant considered beneficial. The transfer is relevant as evidence that management recognized a problem in the S1 working relationship.

## 4. Hostile Work Environment

Allegations 1–3 and 5 are not established as alleged. Considered together, the remaining established events (the documented performance actions) are supported by stated legitimate reasons and are not linked to a protected basis on the present record. On that basis, the non-sexual-harassment claims do not meet the severe-or-pervasive threshold.

The sexual-harassment claim (Allegation 4), if substantiated under Option A, involves repeated physical contact, repeated invitations after rebuffing, and a continuing pattern. Those facts, if proven, would meet the severe-or-pervasive threshold. Employer liability would then turn on the unresolved notice and corrective-action questions in § 3.4.

## 5. Credibility Observations

- On Allegations 1–3 and 5, the conflicting accounts are individually uncorroborated, so credibility could not be resolved in Complainant's favor.
- On Allegation 4, Complainant's account is supported by the consistent, independent statements of multiple coworkers and by observed distress. These weigh in favor of her credibility.
- S1 and the Management Officer both deny being told of the harassment. Their denials conflict with Complainant's account and with the Team Lead's secondhand account.
