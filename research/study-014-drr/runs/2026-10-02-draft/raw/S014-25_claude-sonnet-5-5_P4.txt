# Case File Summary

## Parties and Background
- At the time of the events, Complainant was a Library Director, NF-1410-IV, at the Agency's Joint Base Andrews in Prince George's County, Maryland.
- Complainant originally included a reprisal claim for prior EEO activity. She withdrew it during a conference before the EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ).
- The remaining bases are race, sex, color, and age. The record also identifies Complainant as Black.

## Claims
1. On May 31, 2018, July 3, 2018, and July 16, 2018, Complainant's first-level supervisor (S1), Force Development Flight Chief, GS-0301-13, claimed to have given her verbal counseling. No documentation was generated in her personnel file.
2. On October 18, 2018, S1 gave Complainant a Letter of Reprimand.
3. On February 12, 2019, March 14, 2019, March 21, 2019, April 2, 2019, and April 3, 2019, S1 accepted incorrect statements from Complainant's subordinates.
4. On April 25, 2019, Complainant received a Notice of Proposed Removal signed by S1.
5. On or about June 28, 2019, Complainant was mailed a Decision to Remove letter dated June 28, 2019, signed by S1.
6. On October 22, 2019, Complainant learned her Common Access Card (CAC) was still active. She alleged this would allow others to modify her existing work although she was no longer with the Agency.

## Procedural History
- After the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing, and she requested one.
- Over Complainant's objections, the AJ granted the Agency's motion for a decision without a hearing and issued summary judgment for the Agency.
- The Agency then issued a final order fully implementing the AJ's finding that Complainant did not prove discrimination.
- Complainant appealed.

## Decision-Maker and Standard Applied
- **Decision-maker:** The AJ made the decision on summary judgment. The Agency's final order implemented it. S1 decided to remove Complainant, according to the AJ.
- **Standard:** The AJ took the facts as presented in the report of investigation and viewed the evidence in the light most favorable to Complainant. The AJ found she failed to create a genuine issue of material fact establishing discrimination based on race, sex, color, or age.
- **Claim 6:** The AJ analyzed it, but Complainant did not list it in her appeal brief or dispute the AJ's no-discrimination finding. The appellate decision therefore does not address it further.

## Disparate Treatment (Claims 4 and 5)
- **Prima facie case:** The AJ assumed Complainant could establish one.
- **Agency's articulated reasons:** The AJ found legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons. S1 decided to remove Complainant for the reasons stated in the Notice of Proposed Removal:
  - "[f]ailure to meet suspenses [i.e., deadlines] in a timely manner"
  - "cancelling scheduled events without permission from leadership"
  - "not stagger[ing] schedules to meet the needs of [her] events and patrons"
  - "not reliev[ing] staff members for lunches or breaks"
  - not conducting "staff planning meetings for events"
  - staff accusations that Complainant "gossip[s] and release[s] privileged information about employees with other staff members"
  - "[s]taff members fear[ing] retaliation, bad feedbacks and appraisals if they speak up"
  - "[l]ack of attention to detail with funds management"
- **Comparators:** The AJ found Complainant could not identify similarly situated employees treated more favorably. She pointed to coworkers who were not disciplined for a fight in front of patrons. The AJ found "Complainant was terminated for an entirely different set of circumstances and reasons" and "both women [coworkers] hold a different type of position than Complainant," so they were not adequately similar.

## Hostile Work Environment / Harassment
- **Incidents alleged as harassment:**
  - S1's statement in the Notice of Proposed Removal that she gave verbal counseling on three occasions that was not documented in the personnel file.
  - The Letter of Reprimand for "leaving the library unattended to deal with a personal issue, thereby blocking [a summer] intern from the building."
  - S1's acceptance, without further investigation, of allegedly inaccurate statements from Complainant's White subordinates about her conduct.
  - The proposed removal and ultimate termination.
- **Nexus to protected bases:** The AJ found Complainant could not link any allegation to a protected basis.
  - **Sex and age:** The AJ found "Complainant fails to offer any evidence tying her age or sex to any of the listed events other than her own conclusory statements."
  - **Race and color:** Complainant speculated there was a conspiracy to get her fired because she is Black and her supervisor and subordinates are not. The AJ found this "does not, by itself, show racial animus."
- **Coworker affidavit:** A coworker supported Complainant's contention that the environment was racially hostile.
  - He averred "that unnamed staff members stated they disliked being supervised by an African American." He also averred that a subordinate coworker and S1 both "complained that Complainant was not experienced enough and that Complainant's predecessor, who is Caucasian, did a better job."
  - The AJ found the affidavit "very confusing" and noted "[h]e appears to have learned of these alleged instances second or third-hand, as opposed to witnessing them himself."
  - Because Complainant offered no other evidence that the statements occurred, the AJ determined "the uncorroborated statement of [the coworker], without more, does not establish racial animus on the part of [S1] or Complainant's subordinates."
- **Severity and pervasiveness:** The AJ found "Complainant has failed to establish that she was subjected to severe and pervasive harassment in the form of unwelcome verbal or physical conduct sufficient to change her employment conditions into an abusive working environment."
  - The Agency's Chief Human Resources Officer, GS-0201-12, stated in her affidavit that verbal counseling is not required to be added to personnel files.
  - The AJ characterized the events as "no more than ordinary tribulations of the workplace which Complainant interpreted as harassing in nature, including disagreements with supervisors, supervisor attempts to manage the office, communication problems, and a supervisor's implementation of progressive discipline."
- **Conclusion:** The AJ concluded Complainant failed to prove, "beyond a preponderance of the evidence," that she was subjected to a hostile work environment.
