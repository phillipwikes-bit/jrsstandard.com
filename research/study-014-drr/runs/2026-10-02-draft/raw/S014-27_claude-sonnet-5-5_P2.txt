## II. Findings of Fact

**A. Employment and complaint history**

1. During the relevant period, Complainant was a Senior Special Agent, Polygraph Examiner in the Forensic Services Division, Washington, D.C.
2. On January 2, 2020, Complainant filed a formal EEO complaint alleging reprisal for prior protected EEO activity.
3. By letter dated March 11, 2020, the Agency accepted the complaint for investigation.
4. After the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant the ROI and notice of his right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge.
5. Complainant asked for a final decision instead. The Agency issued a final decision under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b) finding no discrimination, and Complainant appealed.

**B. Accepted claims**

- **Claim 1:** Complainant alleges his 2019 MPP Supervisory Evaluation was discriminatory in that he was not rated as well as his prior supervisor had rated him in 2018. *[The claim text in the record is truncated. Confirm the full wording from the March 11, 2020 acceptance letter.]*
- **Claim 2:** Complainant alleges a retaliatory hostile work environment, based on three incidents:
  - **2(a):** On or about September 10, 2019, S2 obstructed Complainant's return to Quality Control (QC) and mentor duties.
  - **2(b):** On or about September 13, 2019, S2 denied Complainant's request for reconsideration of the denial of QC and mentor duties.
  - **2(c):** From September 1, 2019 to the present, S2 and another Agency official delegated assignments directly to the Washington Field Office (WFO) examiner and excluded Complainant from those communications. Complainant alleges this circumvented him as the designated Senior Polygraph Examiner/Polygraph Backup.

## III. Findings by Claim

**Claim 1: 2019 MPP Supervisory Evaluation**

- *Agency position (final decision):* The Agency articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for the rating. *[The record does not state those reasons. Insert from the final decision or ROI.]*
- *Agency position (appeal response):* The MPP SE is a process separate and distinct from the annual performance evaluation. S2 did not complete Complainant's 2018-2019 MPP SE.
- *Complainant's position:* The 2019-2020 MPP SE continues the reprisal. Three months before the MPP SE, he was rated "Outstanding" on his 2019-2020 annual appraisal. *[Verify the dates and appraisal cycle. The sequence as stated is unclear.]*
- *Finding:* The existence of the "Outstanding" appraisal and the identity of the MPP SE rater are undisputed or uncontradicted in the record. Whether the two evaluations are comparable, and whether the Agency's stated reasons are credible or pretextual, cannot be resolved without the evaluation documents and rating-official testimony.

**Claims 2(a) and 2(b): Mentor and QC duties**

- *Agency position (final decision):* Complainant was restored to mentoring duties and was under consideration for QC duties.
- *Agency position (appeal response):* Complainant was approved to resume mentoring duties. He remains eligible for QC duties, but S2 states that various factors are considered in selecting QC personnel.
- *Complainant's position:* Less qualified employees are being selected for mentoring and QC collateral duties.
- *Finding:* The Agency's account is that mentoring was restored. The record does not give the date of restoration, so it does not show whether Complainant was denied mentoring duties between September 2019 and restoration. The record does not identify the "various factors" for QC selection, who was selected, or their qualifications. Complainant's comparative-qualification claim is unsupported by evidence in the record as provided.

**Claim 2(c): Exclusion from assignment communications**

- *Agency position (final decision):* S2 asserted that he never consulted with Complainant or any other WFO examiner regarding assignments and did not exclude Complainant from communications.
- *Agency position (appeal response):* Complainant has not been treated differently from other examiners.
- *Complainant's position:* Assignments were delegated directly to the WFO examiner, bypassing him as the designated Senior Polygraph Examiner/Polygraph Backup.
- *Finding:* The Agency's position rests on S2's own assertion. The record does not show whether Complainant's designated role entitled him to be included in assignment communications, or whether any documentary evidence (emails, assignment logs) supports or refutes either account. There is also an apparent tension: S2 states he never consulted WFO examiners, while the claim concerns direct delegation to a WFO examiner. *[Review the ROI affidavit to see whether this is an inconsistency or a drafting issue in the summary.]*
