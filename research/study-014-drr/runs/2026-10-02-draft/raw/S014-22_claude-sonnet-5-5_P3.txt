**Investigation Record Summary**

**Background**

During the period at issue, Complainant worked as a Biologist, GS-0401-11, in the Agency's National Institute on Aging, Laboratory of Neurogenetics ("Lab"), Neuromuscular Disease Research Section ("Section"), in Bethesda, Maryland. She was promoted to GS-12 in May 2013.

On November 8, 2017, Complainant filed an EEO complaint, later amended. She alleged discrimination on the following bases:

- Race/color (African American/Black)
- National origin (Guyanese)
- Sex (female)
- Disability: bipolar disorder (diagnosed in 2009, re-diagnosed in 2017 as Post Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)), generalized anxiety disorder, benign mitral valve prolapse, and fibroid tumors requiring surgical removal in December 2013
- Reprisal for prior protected EEO activity (initiating EEO counseling on July 27, 2017, on the instant complaint)

Her first-line supervisor was the Section Chief ("Section Chief"), who is Caucasian and male.

**Claims**

1. On or about June 13, 2017, Section Chief did not hold a scheduled meeting with her.
2. On or about June 15, 2017, Section Chief told her he had changed her work assignment to making DNA plates. She states this task usually suits summer students, not a biologist with a Ph.D.
3. After she initiated EEO counseling on July 27, 2017, management excluded her from a major project in her office/lab.
4. After that date, Section Chief did not communicate with her about work assignments.
5. Management assigned her no projects, in retaliation for her informal complaint.
6. Section Chief subjected her to a hostile work environment through the following acts:
   a. Beginning in 2009, inappropriate comments and unfair treatment.
   b. In 2009, directing lab employees to work on a project, making inappropriate comments and retracting them, and joking that he should stop before an EEO complaint was filed against him.
   c. In 2010, telling her the Agency would submit findings to a top-tier journal without her writing because it had to be perfect.
   d. In 2010, purposely distracting her during an experiment and calling her an "idiot."
   e. In 2010, requiring her to work on Christmas Day.
   f. In 2010, repeatedly telling her she wrote slowly and took forever to finish her Ph.D.
   g. After assigning her to lead a task force in March 2011, often excluding her from emails assigning tasks to the group, which created difficulties for her.
   h. In April 2011, telling her no one would put up with her mental illness if she left.
   i. Around September 2011, after she said she had met with Career Services, questioning why she would discuss her career with anyone and saying she should stay and be comfortable where she was.
   j. Around September 2011, listing her name last on a research article.
   k. In 2011, telling her she would like the movie "The Help."
   l. In 2012, joking with her about being from Africa.
   m. In 2013, having an uncomfortable exchange with her about her ancestry.
   n. In 2013, advising post-baccalaureate students to check her work.
   o. In 2013, calling her a "Diva," causing colleagues to avoid approaching her.
   p. In 2013 and ongoing, precluding her from writing top-tier papers.
   q. In 2016 and ongoing, not attending her standing one-on-one meetings.
   r. In 2016 and ongoing, warning her not to knock on his office door when it was closed.
   s. On May 5, 2017, excluding her from a project after saying he would include her.
   t. Around May 12, 2017, yelling at her in front of colleagues, "No wonder nothing you do ever works," when she answered his questions about protocols.
7. In 2010, Section Chief told her the Agency would submit findings to a top-tier journal without her writing because it had to be perfect.
8. In 2013, Section Chief told a colleague that Complainant had a heart problem and expressed dissatisfaction with managing her medical issues.
9. In 2013 and 2017, Section Chief repeatedly told her to avoid working with a colleague because she had filed EEO complaints.
10. Management subjected her to a hostile work environment when:
    a. In June 2015, Section Chief told her to accept a lower-model computer than other laboratory employees received.
    b. In 2015, Section Chief acknowledged she deserved a promotion but delayed the paperwork until 2017, during a hiring freeze.
    c. On May 12, 2017, Section Chief loudly yelled and laughed at her, saying "no wonder nothing you ever do works," within earshot of colleagues, after she answered his question about a protocol.
    d. In late May or June 2017, Section Chief laughed with a colleague and a new trainee about the time he required her to work on Christmas Day.
    e. Section Chief approached her and pressured her to disclose her political affiliation.
    f. In late December 2013 or 2014, Section Chief expressed surprise that she wrote well.
11. On February 23, 2018, Section Chief failed to promote her.
12. In February 2018, the Agency assigned her to report to the Lab Chief ("Lab Chief"), who is British, Caucasian, and male and was her second-line supervisor, because of her EEO complaint.
13. In March 2018, Lab Chief moved her photograph in the breakroom to a different lab group, although she remained a Section member.
14. On March 14, 2018, Section Chief did not communicate with her about an ongoing collaborative project. Because he also did not speak directly to RMO-2 about it, she could not work on the project.
15. On April 5, 2018, Section Chief told staff she no longer reported to him.
16. In April 2018, the Agency removed her name from the Lab staff listing on the National Institute on Aging website. She says this harmed her because her job involved working with outside agencies, individuals, and groups.
17. On April 23, 2018, all employees and collaborators began copying Lab Chief on every email sent to her, including on minor matters.

**Clarifications Drawn from the Record**

Several claims were stated by the Agency without full detail. The record supplies the following:

- *Claim 8:* Complainant had surgery to remove her fibroid tumors on December 18, 2013. This required a five-day hospital stay and six weeks of recovery at home. A colleague said he had been concerned about her well-being and told Section Chief, who responded that she had a heart condition. Complainant believes she never disclosed a heart condition to Section Chief or Lab Chief. She stated that Section Chief made the remark "in an effort to protect himself of [sic.] disclosing my medical information."
- *Claim 9:* The Agency's definition of this claim omitted the month in 2017. Complainant recalled two incidents and said the second occurred in April 2017. (Report of Investigation (ROI), Ex. F-1, Complainant's affidavit, Bates No. 123.)
- *Claim 10b:* The Agency's definition omitted the month in 2017. Complainant stated she was notified of the hiring freeze in February 2017 and told it was indefinite. (ROI, Ex. F-1, Bates No. 127.)
- *Claim 10f:* The Agency's definition gave no dates. The record shows the incident occurred in December 2013 or early 2014. (ROI, Ex. F-1, Bates Nos. 120–121, 129.)

**Procedural History**

The Agency investigated the complaint, as amended. After the Administrative Judge (AJ) remanded the complaint to the Agency for a final decision, the Agency dismissed claims 6 through 11 for failure to timely initiate EEO counseling. It found no discrimination on the remaining claims.

**Complainant's Appeal**

Complainant filed her appeal before the Final Agency Decision (FAD) was issued. She argues that more than 15 months had passed since the AJ's 60-day time limit expired and that no FAD had yet issued. She submitted documentation showing that on May 20, 2020, she asked the EEO office about the FAD's status. Citing caselaw, she contends this extraordinary delay warrants a default judgment against the Agency as a sanction.

The Agency did not respond to Complainant's appeal.
