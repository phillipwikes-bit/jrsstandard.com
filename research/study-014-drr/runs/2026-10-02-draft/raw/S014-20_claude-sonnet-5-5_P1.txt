**Case Summary**

**Parties/Background:** Complainant was a Rural Carrier at the Agency's Acton Station in Granbury, Texas. She filed an EEO complaint alleging discrimination under GINA (genetic information) and retaliation.

**Claims:**
1. From about January 5, 2018, to present, and after the route count and adjustment, she was not paid correctly for working her route.
2. From April 25, 2018, to present, she was not paid for overtime. (She crossed out and initialed "holiday" in her affidavit, so the claim does not include holiday hours.)
3. On December 14, 2018, her request for package assistance was denied.

**Procedural History:** After the investigation, the Agency issued the report of investigation and notice of hearing rights. Complainant requested an EEOC hearing. The Administrative Judge (AJ) determined sua sponte that no hearing was warranted and issued a Notice of Proposed Summary Judgment. Complainant did not object within the deadline, and the AJ issued summary judgment for the Agency. The Agency's final order fully implemented that decision. Complainant appealed.

**AJ Findings:**
- **Claims 1 and 2:** Complainant did not establish a prima facie case of GINA discrimination. Her affidavit did not address genetic information, and she did not allege that management was motivated by, or used, genetic information.
- **Claim 3:** The Supervisor Customer Service gave legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for denying the request. Complainant had already received help that day. Only one additional parcel met the criteria, and it had been taken away and did not need delivery that day. The other packages did not meet the criteria: her vehicle could hold the volume, she was not at risk of missing the mail truck, and the day's volume did not merit more assistance. Complainant did not show these reasons were a pretext for retaliation.

**Outcome:** The AJ found Complainant failed to prove discrimination as alleged. The Agency's final order adopted that finding, and the matter is before the Commission on appeal.
