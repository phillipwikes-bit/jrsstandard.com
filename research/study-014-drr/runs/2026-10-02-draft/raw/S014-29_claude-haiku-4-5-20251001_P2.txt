# FINDINGS

## Incident Details

On August 19, 2015, Complainant, employed as a Mail Handler at the Agency's Incoming Mail Processing & Distribution Center in Linthicum, Maryland, engaged in a series of confrontational and disruptive behaviors following a meeting regarding mislabeled containers.

During the August 19, 2015 incident, Complainant:

- Called the Manager "sorry" and/or "a sorry ass" after the supervisory meeting;
- Refused to immediately comply with the Manager's instruction to leave the work floor;
- Stated "He's a Bitch! He's a Bitch!" in reference to the Manager while in the conference room;
- Yelled "Manager is a Bitch—Bitch Ass!" while retrieving his personal items from the work floor;
- Punched a hole in the wall and exclaimed "[Manager] is a Bitch—Fuck Him!" while being escorted from the building.

Complainant disputed some details of his conduct, claiming he said "This is bullshit" on the way to the conference room and did not precisely recall his statements while being escorted out, though he acknowledged taking "a swipe" at a wall.

## Administrative Actions

Following the August 19, 2015 incident, the Agency placed Complainant on Emergency Placement status. On August 24, 2015, Complainant attended a Pre-Disciplinary Interview with union representation, during which management indicated its intent to recommend removal. On August 31, 2015, Complainant's first-line supervisor recommended termination based on profanity use, failure to follow instructions, and damage to Agency property. The Agency cited prior disciplinary history, including a 10-day suspension for Failure of Obedience to Orders and a 14-day suspension for violation of the Workplace Violence Zero Tolerance Policy. On September 11, 2015, the Agency issued a Notice of Removal.

## Administrative Hearing Determination

An Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge found that:

- The Agency had legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for the termination based on Complainant's confrontational, disrespectful, and violent conduct violating the Agency's Zero Tolerance Policy;
- Complainant failed to present evidence demonstrating that race and/or gender played a role in the termination decision;
- Complainant failed to establish a prima facie case of genetic information discrimination under the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act, lacking evidence of protected status, adverse employment action causation, and discriminatory inference;
- By a preponderance of the evidence, Complainant did not prove discrimination based on race, gender, or genetic information.

## Conclusion

The Agency's final order adopted the Administrative Judge's findings that Complainant failed to prove the Agency subjected him to discrimination as alleged.
