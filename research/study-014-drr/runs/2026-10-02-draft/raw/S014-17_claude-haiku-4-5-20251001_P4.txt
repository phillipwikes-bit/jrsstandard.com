# CASE FILE SUMMARY

## COMPLAINANT AND POSITION
Complainant was employed as a Customs and Border Protection Officer (CBPO) at the San Ysidro Port of Entry in San Ysidro, California, with Year of Birth 1954.

## ALLEGATIONS
Complainant alleged discrimination based on age (under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967) and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity (under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964).

## HARASSMENT ALLEGATIONS (Beginning May 31, 2018 and Continuing)

**May 31, 2018:** The Branch Chief refused to rescind a Memorandum of Counseling regarding Treasury Enforcement Communication System (TECS) that Complainant received. (ROI at 205)

**February 2019 to June 2019:** The Branch Chief was aware that the Supervisory CBP Officer (SCBPO-1) bullied and harassed Complainant yet took no corrective actions. (ROI at 205)

**April 17, 2019:** SCBPO-1 verbally reprimanded Complainant for missing a morning training class. (ROI at 205)

**June 2019:** SCBPO-1 showed a knife to Complainant and another CBPO (CBPO-2) and informed them that he brings his knife collection to work in his backpack. (ROI at 205)

**June 3, 2019:** SCBPO-1 reprimanded Complainant by email regarding his TECS access expiring. (ROI at 205)

**June 19, 2019:** SCBPO-1 changed Complainant's schedule from 2:00 p.m. to 1:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m. in order to celebrate the Chief's birthday, and solicited $20 from him for the birthday party. (ROI at 205)

**On or around August 16, 2019:** SCBPO-1 and another SCBPO (SCBPO-2) informed Complainant's coworkers that he would be reassigned and leaving the Intelligence Targeting Unit effective September 1, 2019, although Complainant was not reassigned at that time. (ROI at 205)

**On or around August 16, 2019:** SCBPO-1 and SCBPO-2 informed Complainant that his request for leave during the month of September 2019 would not be approved; however, after Complainant questioned them about the matter, his leave was ultimately approved. (ROI at 205)

## REMOVAL FROM DETAIL
**Effective March 8, 2020:** Complainant was removed from his detail in the Intelligence Targeting Unit (ITU). (ROI at 205)

## DISMISSED CLAIMS
The Agency dismissed two claims: (1) that on December 23, 2018, Complainant learned he had not received a year-end monetary award; and (2) that on April 21, 2019, SCBPO-1 charged Complainant with 1 hour of Leave Without Pay for untimely counselor contact. (ROI at 150-57)

The Agency dismissed three claims alleging that SCBPO-1 was unprofessional in speaking ill about three coworkers for failure to state a claim, as Complainant did not show that he was aggrieved by comments made about other employees. (ROI at 150-57)

## AGENCY'S STATED JUSTIFICATIONS

**Memorandum of Counseling (May 31, 2018):**
The Branch Chief stated that she requested that Complainant be issued the Memorandum of Counseling after he was twice instructed not to put the terms "E/O if not in vehicle" on a TECS record as it creates a violation and Complainant continued not to comply. (ROI at 205)

The Intelligence Division Chief (CID Chief) explained that Complainant requested CBPOs to "exempt" travelers on primary which had illegal activity links/ties or which he targeted for illegal activity, which was essentially asking CBPOs to violate TECS policy. The CID Chief approved the issuance of the Memorandum of Counseling due to Complainant's failure to follow instructions even after being asked twice to do so. (ROI at 259)

**June 3, 2019 TECS Access Email:**
SCBPO-1 explained that the TECS system sends automated periodic reminders when a user is required to recertify in order to maintain their access from 30 days prior to the due date of recertification. (ROI at 223-24)

SCBPO-1 stated that he received an automated email from the TECS system informing him that Complainant had lost his access and he sent Complainant an email counseling him on the loss of access and reminding Complainant that he was required to maintain TECS access. (ROI at 223-24)

**June 19, 2019 Schedule Change and Birthday Solicitation:**
SCBPO-1 explained that the ITU had a tradition of organizing "potlucks" or obtaining catering for the group for certain situations and that such events are announced as optional participation. (ROI at 224)

SCBPO-1 stated that he told the ITU employees that the potluck would be held at 1:00 p.m. and that the 2:00 p.m. shift was told they could come in an hour early in order to participate. (ROI at 224)

SCBPO-1 stated that each employee was asked to donate $10 if they wished to and that CBPO-1 gave him $20 to pay for both himself and Complainant after Complainant verbally told him that he wished to participate. (ROI at 224)

**Removal from Detail (March 8, 2020):**
The CID Chief stated that he removed Complainant from his detail due to Complainant's failure to perform his basic duties as an ITU officer. The CID Chief explained that the Watch Commander specifically contacted Complainant to request assistance on a particular case and requested an expeditious response but Complainant failed to perform the task and also delegated the task to other members of the Tactical Terrorism Response Team. (ROI at 260)

## INVESTIGATION PROCESS

The investigator gave Complainant two weeks to submit his affidavit. (ROI at 198)

Complainant responded by requesting two weeks of administrative or sick leave to complete his affidavit. (ROI at 198)

On September 28, 2020, the day before Complainant's affidavit was originally due, the investigator provided Complainant an extension to complete his affidavit and Complainant was advised that he was approved for 16 hours of administrative leave for September 23-24, 2020. (ROI at 198)

Complainant did not submit an affidavit.

## AGENCY'S FINAL DECISION

At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the Report of Investigation and notice of his right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ).

Complainant timely requested a hearing but subsequently withdrew his request.

The Agency issued a final decision pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b).

The decision found that Complainant did not establish a prima facie case because there was no evidence that other employees who were not members of his protected classes were treated differently.

The decision further found that even so, the Agency articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions and Complainant did not submit any evidence of discriminatory animus.

The decision further found that Complainant did not establish that any of the alleged incidents of harassment were due to his protected classes.

The decision therefore concluded that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected him to discrimination as alleged.

## APPEAL

Complainant appealed, contending that the investigation was inadequate and requesting that his case be referred to an AJ for additional investigation, as well as contending that the Agency's reasons are untrue.

In response, the Agency contends that Complainant was given adequate time to submit his affidavit but failed to do so and also failed to submit any evidence supporting his assertions of discriminatory animus.
