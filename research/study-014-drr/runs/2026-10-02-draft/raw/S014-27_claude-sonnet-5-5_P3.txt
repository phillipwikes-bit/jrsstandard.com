**INVESTIGATION RECORD SUMMARY**

**Background**

During the period at issue, Complainant worked for the Agency as a Senior Special Agent, Polygraph Examiner, in the Forensic Services Division in Washington, D.C. On January 2, 2020, he filed a formal EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against him in reprisal for prior protected EEO activity. By letter dated March 11, 2020, the Agency accepted the complaint for investigation and defined the claims as follows:

1. Complainant was not rated as well on his 2019 Merit Promotion Plan (MPP) Supervisory Evaluation (SE) as his prior supervisor had rated him in 2018. (The source text of this claim is incomplete, and the wording here reflects only the legible portion.)

2. Complainant was subjected to a hostile work environment based on reprisal for prior EEO activity when:
   a. On or about September 10, 2019, S2 obstructed his return to Quality Control (QC) and mentor duties.
   b. On or about September 13, 2019, S2 denied his request for reconsideration of the denial of QC and mentor duties.
   c. From September 1, 2019, to the present, S2 and another Agency official delegated assignments directly to the Washington Field Office (WFO) examiner and excluded Complainant from those communications, circumventing him as the designated Senior Polygraph Examiner/Polygraph Backup.

**Procedural History**

After the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the report of investigation (ROI) and notice of his right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge. At Complainant's request, the Agency instead issued a final decision under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b), finding no discrimination. Complainant then filed the present appeal.

**The Agency's Final Decision**

- **Claim 1:** The Agency found that it had articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for Complainant's MPP SE.
- **Claims 2(a)–2(b):** The Agency stated that Complainant had been restored to mentoring duties and was under consideration for QC duties.
- **Claim 2(c):** The Agency relied on S2's assertions that he never consulted Complainant or any other WFO examiner about assignments and that he had not excluded Complainant from communications.

**Complainant's Contentions on Appeal**

Complainant asks that the Agency's final decision be reversed. He raises the following points:

- He submitted his request for a final decision on July 10, 2020, and the Agency's decision was untimely. He asks for default judgment against the Agency as a sanction.
- The ROI was inadequate, and one management official did not respond to a request to provide testimony.
- His 2019–2020 MPP SE continues the reprisal he has experienced. Three months before that rating, he had been rated "Outstanding" on his 2019–2020 annual performance appraisal.
- Less qualified employees are being selected for the collateral duties of mentoring and quality control.
- In a prior EEO case, the Agency issued a final decision ordering it to reassign him to Quality Control and to provide EEO training to specified management officials. He asserts that the Agency has done neither.

**The Agency's Response**

- **Claim 1:** The MPP SE is separate and distinct from the annual performance evaluation process. S2 did not complete Complainant's 2018–2019 MPP SE.
- **Claims 2(a)–2(b):** Complainant was approved to resume mentoring duties. He remains eligible for QC duties, though S2 noted that various factors are weighed when selecting an employee for them.
- **Claim 2(c):** Complainant has not been treated differently from other examiners in Agency communications.
- **Timeliness:** The delay in issuing the final decision was minimal, and sanctions are not warranted.
