# CASE FILE RECORD

## SUBJECT AND NATURE OF COMPLAINT

The complainant was employed as a Mail Handler at the Agency's Incoming Mail Processing & Distribution Center in Linthicum, Maryland. On September 4, 2015, the Agency accepted a complaint for investigation alleging improper conduct, violation of the zero-tolerance policy, and violation of the joint statement on violence and behavior in the workplace.

## FACTUAL CIRCUMSTANCES

On August 19, 2015, following a meeting regarding mistaken labels on containers, a confrontation occurred between the complainant and management personnel. After the meeting concluded, the complainant referred to his second-line supervisor (Manager) as "sorry" and/or "a sorry ass." When the Manager instructed the complainant to leave the work floor, the complainant did not immediately comply. Only after the Manager threatened to contact police did the complainant proceed with his first-line supervisor (Supervisor) to the conference room.

While in the conference room, the complainant made derogatory statements about the Manager, stating "He's a Bitch! He's a Bitch!" Subsequently, as the complainant was being escorted to retrieve his belongings, he yelled additional profanities including "Manager is a Bitch—Bitch Ass!" During this same incident, the complainant punched a hole in the wall while making further inflammatory remarks directed at the Manager. Following these events, management placed the complainant on Emergency Placement status.

## COMPLAINANT'S ACCOUNT

The complainant provided a different account of the events. He stated that he said, "This is bullshit," while walking to the conference room with his supervisor. Regarding the incident occurring as he was being escorted from the building, the complainant indicated he could not remember the exact words he had used. However, he acknowledged striking the wall with what he characterized as "a swipe."

## ADMINISTRATIVE PROCEEDINGS

On August 24, 2015, the complainant attended a Pre-Disciplinary Interview accompanied by his union representative, his first-line supervisor, and a second supervisor. At this meeting, the complainant was informed that the Agency would recommend his removal. Subsequently, on August 31, 2015, the first-line supervisor formally recommended removal to the Labor Manager.

The removal recommendation cited three primary reasons: the complainant's use of profanity, his failure to follow instructions, and the damage to Agency property caused by punching a hole in the wall. The recommendation also noted the complainant's prior disciplinary history, which included a 10-day suspension for Failure of Obedience to Orders and a 14-day suspension for violation of the Workplace Violence Zero Tolerance Policy.

## EQUAL EMPLOYMENT OPPORTUNITY COMMISSION PROCEEDINGS

Following the Agency's investigation, the complainant received a copy of the investigative report and written notice of his right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge. The complainant timely submitted a hearing request.

The complainant made allegations that he had been subjected to discrimination based on race, gender, and genetic information under the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA). On April 24, 2017, the Agency filed a motion for decision without hearing. The complainant did not file an objection, and the Administrative Judge granted the motion.

## ADMINISTRATIVE JUDGE DECISION

On August 21, 2020, the Administrative Judge issued a decision without hearing. The Judge found that the Agency possessed legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for the complainant's termination, specifically citing his confrontational, disrespectful, and violent conduct in the workplace as violating the Agency's Zero Tolerance Policy. The Judge concluded that the complainant failed to present evidence suggesting that his race, gender, or genetic information played any role in the termination decision.

Regarding the complainant's GINA claim, the Judge determined he could not establish a prima facie case of genetic information discrimination, as he failed to demonstrate statutory protection eligibility, an adverse employment action, or discriminatory intent based on genetic information. Applying the appropriate evidentiary standard, the Judge held that the complainant did not prove by a preponderance of the evidence that discrimination occurred when the Notice of Removal was issued on September 11, 2015.

## FINAL AGENCY ACTION

The Agency subsequently issued a final order adopting the Administrative Judge's findings and affirming that the complainant failed to prove the alleged discrimination claims.
