# CASE FILE SUMMARY

## PARTIES AND POSITION
Complainant worked as a GS-15 Primary Care Physician at the Agency's East Orange Veterans Affairs Medical Center in East Orange, New Jersey. Complainant was appointed to this position on March 3, 2019, subject to a two-year probationary period.

## ESSENTIAL JOB DUTIES
As a Primary Care Physician, Complainant's essential duties included: interviewing and examining patients daily (typically 12) via in-person and virtual; examining patients who were present for face-to-face visits; documenting patient visits in the Agency's electronic medical record; and ordering consults, labs, and medications using the electronic medical record.

## CLAIMS
1. Denied repeated request for reasonable accommodation from April 21, 2020, to termination on January 22, 2021
2. Charged as Absent Without Leave (AWOL) from August 10, 2020, to termination on January 22, 2021

## MEDICAL BACKGROUND

**Complainant's Statements:**
- Sustained shoulder injury on July 12, 2019 (no claim of notification to supervisors or accommodation request at that time)
- Aggravated prior injury to right shoulder in December 2019 and notified supervisors shortly thereafter
- Claimed inability to lift right arm, use mouse or keyboard, or write; unable to perform physical examinations, document notes, or use computer for more than a few minutes at a time

**Medical Documentation Provided:**

## REASONABLE ACCOMMODATION PROCESS

**Supervisor (S1) Statement:** Became aware of Complainant's right shoulder condition in December 2019. On February 25, 2020, Complainant made a verbal reasonable accommodation request and submitted FMLA requesting no lifting, no prolonged upper extremity fine manipulation with right arm/hand, part-time, light duties to include 20 hours work week, no grabbing, and assistance with computer tasks as needed.

**March 5, 2020 Meeting:** S1, Complainant's second level supervisor (S2), and the Reasonable Accommodation Coordinator initiated interactive process. During the meeting, Complainant stated she could not perform patient examinations, was unable to complete patient paperwork, and was unable to use a computer for more than a few minutes at a time. The Agency decided to grant Complainant's alternative accommodation of continuing LWOP to rehabilitate her injury for a six-week period.

**Complainant's Return to Work:** After being on leave since December 2019, Complainant returned to work on March 2, 2020, with work shift restrictions (four hours per day and four hours Leave Without Pay, five days per week). She was required to complete the Agency's Talent Management System training modules online and see two or three patients by end of week if needed. Complainant failed to see a patient or complete required training.

**March 4, 2020:** Medical Assistant reported that Complainant declined to see a patient in waiting room who had already waited several months. Complainant stated she could not see the patient because her shoulder was bothering her and she could barely hold a pen to write notes.

**March 25, 2020:** S1 requested Complainant provide physician documentation regarding her ability to perform essential functions of her position, including limitations with typing notes, placing orders, sending lab letters, and filling out paperwork.

**Accommodations Prepared by Agency:**
- Dictation software prepared for installation
- Two ergonomic chairs prepared for delivery
- Ergonomic mouse and keyboard shipped to home and office
- Laptop approved on March 3, 2020 (delivery delayed due to equipment shortage)
- Second laptop request sent through Computer/Electronic Accommodation program (CAP); CAP denied request because Complainant failed to submit sufficient medical documentation and telework agreement

**Telework Request:** Complainant requested 100% telework two weeks after the March 5, 2020 meeting. On June 2, 2020, Agency secured a laptop for Complainant, but she never signed a telework agreement addressing teleworking for one to three months due to temporary disability. S1 indicated Agency could not offer unconditional 100% telework for at least a year because Complainant failed to provide medical documentation. Agency decided to offer telework agreement for one to three months, dependent upon updates to medical documentation. Complainant did not accept the Agency's telework agreement.

**April 21, 2020 Accommodation Request:** Reasonable Accommodation Coordinator indicated Complainant made several accommodation requests, including one on April 21, 2020, requesting 100% telework, 20 hours per work week, and modified work assignments for one year to permanent basis.

**Requests for Medical Documentation:** Coordinator made numerous requests, including several emails in June 2020, for medical documentation of disability. Coordinator specifically requested medical documentations relating to: effectiveness of telework/telemedicine accommodation with medical condition; information relating to duration of medical condition; and consent to contact physician to proceed with accommodation request. Complainant failed to provide such information.

**Closure of Accommodation Request:** On August 7, 2020, Coordinator closed Complainant's request for reasonable accommodation and informed her that the case would be reopened if she provided the requested documentation.

## LEAVE AND AWOL STATUS

**Leave Usage:** Complainant used all annual and sick leave from December 20, 2019 through February 6, 2020; subsequently granted LWOP on February 7–29, 2020 (80 hours), March 2–26, 2020 (480 hours), and March 26–August 7, 2020 (421 hours).

**Complainant's Statement:** Remained in approved LWOP status from March 4, 2020, through August 10, 2020.

**AWOL Charging:** S1 and S2 indicated that after Complainant's reasonable accommodation case was closed, the Coordinator suggested it was reasonable to charge Complainant AWOL for her absence. Considering the Coordinator's suggestion and the fact that Complainant had not worked for nearly eight months without providing medical documentation as requested, management decided to place her in AWOL starting August 10, 2020. Complainant remained in AWOL since August 10, 2020.

## TERMINATION

On January 22, 2021, Complainant's employment was terminated, recommended by the Professional Standards Board.

## EEO COMPLAINT AND INVESTIGATION

On August 2, 2021, Complainant filed an EEO complaint, [number removed], regarding her January 22, 2021 termination. The Agency issued its final Agency decision finding no discrimination.

**Affidavit Issue:** An investigator noted in the report of investigation that Complainant did not provide an affidavit requested during the investigation of the complaint. The investigator noted that when asked on several occasions, Complainant stated she sent her affidavit to a "higher authority." Complainant's affidavit was not included in the Report of Investigation. Complainant indicates she sent her affidavit to the then Secretary of the Agency. The record indicates that Complainant provided the affidavit to the Administrative Judge (AJ).

## ADMINISTRATIVE JUDGE DECISION

At the conclusion of the investigation, Complainant requested a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge. The AJ issued a decision without holding a hearing, finding no discrimination.

**AJ's Findings on Claim 1 (Denied Reasonable Accommodation):**

The AJ stated that Complainant did not establish that the Agency failed to provide her with a reasonable accommodation in that she failed to provide medical documentation the Agency requested showing that she was able to perform her position duty—examining patients physically.

The AJ found:

> Complainant alleges only that she suffered from her inability to use her right arm. She has provided limited documentation to support having any disability, and all the documentation provided stated the treating physician would reassess in weeks to come. Despite repeated requests, Complainant failed to offer any medical documentation of a diagnosis of her condition, or a duration; i.e. whether it was temporary or permanent. She failed to sign a release authorizing the Agency to obtain medical information; and she failed to offer a release authorizing medical information to be disclosed even to the investigator in this case.

The AJ further found:

> Complainant has not shown that she would be able to work from home as, by her own admission, she was unable to use a pen or computer on her own. Additionally, the permanent accommodation requested, telework, is one that Complainant is not qualified for as telework would require an increase in use of the computer. Further, as, part of her responsibilities as a [Primary Care Physician], Complainant is required to examine patients physically even as a telework clinician. Complainant stated she was unable to do so on March 4, 2020[,] and after that never provided any documentation that she was able to examine patients. Complainant refused to engage with the Agency on discussions regarding this issue by failing to provide medical documentation, therefore causing the Agency to close her case without a denial or approval.

The Agency did not issue a final order and the AJ's decision effectively became its final action pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.109(i).

## APPEAL

Complainant appeals from the Agency's final order, contesting its finding of no discrimination. On appeal, Complainant submits numerous documents, including subsequent doctors' statements.
