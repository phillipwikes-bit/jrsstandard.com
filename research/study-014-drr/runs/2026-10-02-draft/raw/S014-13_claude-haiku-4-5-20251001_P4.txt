- Agency issued final decision pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b), in accordance with Complainant's request

## FINAL DECISION FINDINGS

**Decision-Maker:** The Agency

**Agency's Conclusions:**
1. Complainant was an individual with a disability and qualified within the meaning of the Rehabilitation Act
2. Assuming Complainant established a prima facie case of discrimination and retaliation, the Agency articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons
3. Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected him to discrimination as alleged
4. Complainant failed to rebut the Agency's reasons as pretextual

## DECISION RATIONALE & CRITERIA APPLIED

**Legitimate, Nondiscriminatory Reasons Identified by Agency:**
Performance issues in processing payroll and new hire onboarding, which Complainant often failed to complete without errors or on time

**Pretext Analysis:**
The Agency addressed whether Complainant established pretext by considering AS's affidavit, where she stated she did not observe any performance problems while Complainant was under her supervision. The Agency determined that AS only supervised one aspect of Complainant's work (the Missing Documents Project), not timekeeping and processing of new hires. Because three of Complainant's supervisors (S3, S1, and an unnamed first-level supervisor) confirmed his deficiencies in the payroll and new hire areas, the Agency found that AS's statements did not cast doubt on the Agency's explanation.

**Regarding Retaliation Claim:**
The Agency reasoned that there was no evidence that S3 voiced opposition to Complainant's reasonable accommodation or his telework schedule, especially given that all of the Administrative Technicians S3 supervised teleworked two days per week for at least a portion of the period at issue.

**Conclusion on Pretext:**
The Agency concluded Complainant could not show management's reasons were pretext for discrimination or reprisal, beyond Complainant's subjective belief.
