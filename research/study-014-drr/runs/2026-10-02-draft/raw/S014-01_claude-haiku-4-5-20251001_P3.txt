# INVESTIGATION RECORD AND ADMINISTRATIVE DECISION

## CASE BACKGROUND AND COMPLAINANT STATUS

At the time of the events giving rise to this complaint, the Complainant was employed as a Grade 12 (GS-12) Senior Bankruptcy Specialist with the Agency's Small Business/Self-Employed Division, Specialty Collection–Insolvency Office, located in Houston, Texas. On September 17, 2021, the Complainant filed a formal complaint with the Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) Commission, alleging that the Agency had discriminated against her and subjected her to a hostile work environment. The Complainant based her allegations on two protected grounds: disability and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity.

The Complainant identified multiple physical and mental health conditions as the basis for her disability claim, specifically: Major Depression with Recurrence, Somatic Symptoms Disorder, Generalized Anxiety Disorder, Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA), Myofascial Pain Syndrome, and Acute Stress Disorder.

## SUMMARY OF ALLEGED DISCRIMINATORY ACTS

The Complainant's complaint enumerated five separate incidents that she contended constituted discrimination and/or retaliation. These allegations span a period from February 2021 through October 2021 and include the following: (1) denial of paid leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) from February 2021 onward; (2) reassignment of complex cases from a retired co-worker on June 19, 2021; (3) denial of eight hours of paid FMLA sick leave on August 3, 2021; (4) denial of her final appeal regarding a requested reasonable accommodation on August 24, 2021; and (5) denial of an official time request to work on her EEO complaint on October 26, 2021.

## ANALYSIS OF LEAVE ISSUES (CLAIMS 1 AND 3)

The Complainant raised leave-related grievances that had been previously addressed in prior EEO proceedings before an EEOC Administrative Judge. In that prior proceeding, dated May 25, 2022, the Administrative Judge found that the Agency had properly established its case regarding the Complainant's Absent Without Leave (AWOL) charges and Leave Without Pay (LWOP) status during February and March 2021. The Administrative Judge determined that these placements were justified because the Complainant had exhausted her available paid leave balances and failed to report to work on the relevant dates.

In the present complaint, the Complainant again raised substantially similar leave issues, including the February and March 2021 AWOL charges and LWOP status. Additionally, the Complainant alleged that on August 3, 2021, her manager denied her request for eight hours of paid FMLA sick leave.

The Complainant's first-line supervisor (referred to herein as Supervisor1) provided documentation showing that management's calculations had determined that the Complainant had exhausted her available paid sick leave by March 2021. Specifically, a letter dated February 26, 2021, addressed to the Complainant regarding her FMLA request indicated that she had only 32 hours of unpaid FMLA available for use between March 1, 2021, and December 3, 2021. Accordingly, beginning March 1, 2021, and on subsequent dates, Supervisor1 notified the Complainant that no paid leave remained available for use under FMLA provisions. On the various dates in question, including August 3, 2021, Supervisor1 granted the Complainant FMLA Leave Without Pay status rather than paid leave, as no paid leave was available according to the Agency's calculations.

However, the Complainant did not request a formal audit of her leave balance until August 5, 2021. Upon conducting this audit, a payroll representative discovered that the Complainant actually had some paid leave available for use under FMLA. Following this discovery, the Agency retroactively granted the Complainant paid leave status for the disputed dates, including August 3, 2021. Significantly, the Complainant acknowledged and conceded that she ultimately received all paid leave to which she was entitled.

In evaluating this claim, the record was examined for evidence of similarly situated employees who had been treated more favorably with respect to leave entitlements and FMLA administration. No such evidence was identified.

## ANALYSIS OF CASE REASSIGNMENT (CLAIM 2)

In June 2021, one of the Complainant's GS-12 Bankruptcy Specialist co-workers retired from the Agency. Following this retirement, the Complainant's supervisor emailed the Complainant and two other GS-12 employees in their work group to inform them that the retiring employee's cases would be reassigned among the three remaining GS-12 Bankruptcy Specialists.

The Complainant objected to receiving any of the reassigned cases, asserting that she should not have been included in the distribution because she believed she was entitled to indefinite weather and safety leave status and that a prior reasonable accommodation request should have been honored. An Administrative Judge in a separate proceeding found that the Complainant was not entitled to indefinite weather and safety leave and that the Agency had satisfied all its obligations under the Rehabilitation Act. The appeal of this determination remains pending.

Supervisor1 explained that the reassignment of cases was performed as part of her regular managerial duties to distribute work inventory among employees in her group. Supervisor1 further explained that all reassigned work was appropriate and suitable for the Complainant's grade level. The supervisor noted that work was distributed among the Complainant and the two other GS-12 Bankruptcy Specialists based upon an objective, neutral method: the alphabetical order of case names. Supervisor1 provided a spreadsheet documenting the case assignments to the Complainant and her two co-workers, which supported her testimony regarding the allocation method.

The investigative record contained no evidence of similarly situated employees who were treated more favorably regarding case assignments and workload distribution.

## ANALYSIS OF REASONABLE ACCOMMODATIONS (CLAIM 4)

On May 6, 2021, the Complainant submitted a formal request for reasonable accommodations under the Rehabilitation Act. This initial request sought two specific accommodations: (1) that her manager or secretary assistant print and mail her letters to taxpayers, and (2) a reduction in her workload. On May 18, 2021, the Complainant amended her request to include job reassignment as an additional accommodation.

Notably, the Complainant conceded during the investigative process that her medical impairments did not limit her ability to perform any essential functions of her position as a Bankruptcy Specialist.

The Agency's Reasonable Accommodations Coordinator conducted an interactive meeting with the Complainant on May 21, 2021, as required by law. Following this meeting, the Agency issued a denial of the requested reduced workload and reassignment accommodations. The denial letter explained that no medical documentation had been submitted that supported the necessity for such accommodations. The Agency further noted that reducing the Complainant's caseload would lower performance and production standards, thereby compromising the Agency's ability to achieve its mission and serve the public effectively.

The record demonstrated, without dispute, that the Complainant maintained a caseload of only 25 cases at the time of her request. This caseload was substantially lower than those carried by other Bankruptcy Specialists in the office and below the typical number of cases the Complainant herself had historically managed.

With respect to the reassignment request, the Agency denied this accommodation, explaining that reassignment is considered the accommodation of last resort and is granted only when an employee is unable to perform the essential functions of their position. The Agency cited evidence from a 2018 request in which the Complainant's psychologist had noted that the Complainant desired a reduced inventory of approximately 38 to 40 cases. When the Complainant had previously requested reassignment as a reasonable accommodation in March 2020, the Agency noted that she had failed to submit medical documentation establishing that she was unable to meet the essential functions of her position. Rather, the evidence suggested that the Complainant sought reassignment due to interpersonal difficulties with her supervisors, not because of any limitation imposed by her medical conditions on job performance.

With respect to the letter printing and mailing request, the Agency approved the Complainant's request and offered to provide her with a small, lightweight, portable printer that she could operate independently. While awaiting delivery of this printer, the Agency agreed to temporarily have the Complainant's correspondence printed and mailed by her manager. However, before the printer was delivered, the Complainant submitted a cancellation ticket, stating that she did not wish to be held accountable for the printer and its materials. The Complainant subsequently asserted that she would be unable to lift either the printer or its paper supplies. Despite this change in position, the Agency's management continued to print and mail the Complainant's correspondence for her.

On July 12, 2021, the Complainant appealed the Agency's denial of reconsideration regarding her reasonable accommodation requests. On August 24, 2021, the Director of Collection Operations issued a decision denying the Complainant's appeal. The Director's decision noted that the Complainant had summarily rejected the Agency's offer to provide an accommodating device that was specifically designed to address her stated needs. The decision further emphasized that the Complainant maintained an already-reduced workload relative to peers and was not eligible for reassignment, as the record demonstrated she was capable of performing all essential functions of her position.

## ANALYSIS OF OFFICIAL TIME REQUEST (CLAIM 5)

On October 20, 2021, the Complainant requested official time to respond to an EEO Investigator's request that she complete and submit an affidavit in connection with her discrimination complaint. The Complainant's request specified a time period extending from approximately 10:30 a.m. on October 20, 2021, through 11:30 a.m. on October 21, 2021.

Supervisor1 approved the Complainant's request. The Complainant utilized six hours of official time on October 20, 2021, and four additional hours on October 21, 2021, for a total of ten hours. Despite the provision of these hours, the Complainant did not complete her affidavit and requested additional official time on October 25, 2021. Supervisor1 approved this subsequent request as well.

On October 26, 2021, the Complainant emailed the Agency's Department of Treasury, Office of Civil Rights and Diversity (OCRD) to request an extension of her deadline for completing the affidavit. OCRD responded by informing the Complainant that she could directly request an extension from the EEO Investigator if she required additional time.

Following receipt of OCRD's response, the Complainant requested additional official time from Supervisor1 on October 26, 2021. When Supervisor1 inquired regarding the Complainant's progress on the affidavit, the Complainant indicated that she had only two remaining questions. Given that the Complainant had already been granted approximately eighteen hours of official time for this task and given that OCRD had explicitly directed the Complainant to contact the investigator directly for extension requests, Supervisor1 denied the Complainant's request for continued official time on October 26, 2021.

## ADMINISTRATIVE JUDGE DECISION

Upon conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided the Complainant with a copy of the investigative report and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge. The Complainant timely requested such a hearing.

The Administrative Judge issued a summary judgment decision in favor of the Agency on all claims. The Administrative Judge determined that the alleged incidents were neither sufficiently severe nor sufficiently pervasive to constitute a hostile work environment under applicable law. Furthermore, the Administrative Judge found that no evidence in the record demonstrated that the conduct at issue was motivated by discriminatory or retaliatory animus.

Specifically with respect to the leave claims, the Administrative Judge noted that the Complainant had failed to demonstrate that similarly situated employees outside of her protected classes had been treated more favorably regarding FMLA and other leave entitlements.

Regarding the case reassignment, the Administrative Judge found that the record evidence clearly established that cases from the retired co-worker were split equally among the Complainant and two other Bankruptcy Specialist co-workers based on unrebutted, legitimate reasons related to grade level, skill level, and appropriateness of assignment given the Complainant's comparatively low existing workload.

With respect to the reasonable accommodations claim, the Administrative Judge noted that the Complainant's May 6, 2021 request specifically sought assistance with printing and mailing letters and requested a reduced workload. The amended request on May 18, 2021 included a request for reassignment. The Administrative Judge emphasized that the Complainant had admitted during investigation that her impairments did not limit her ability to perform essential job duties. Furthermore, the Complainant had produced no probative evidence that the accommodations previously offered by the Agency—including a post of duty with telework options away from her manager and communication primarily by email—did not enable her to perform her essential functions.

The Administrative Judge found that the Agency properly denied the request for reduced inventory, noting that agencies are not required to lower production standards or employee quotas. The Complainant provided no evidence establishing that she could not adequately perform her assigned cases given her unusually low inventory of 25 cases.

Regarding the printer accommodation, the Administrative Judge found that the Agency had, following the interactive meeting, approved the Complainant for a lightweight, portable printer. While awaiting delivery, the Agency had agreed to print and mail the Complainant's correspondence. However, before delivery, the Complainant cancelled the order, stating she did not wish accountability for the equipment and supplies. The Agency continued to provide printing and mailing services. Accordingly, the Administrative Judge concluded that the Agency did not deny reasonable accommodation in violation of the Rehabilitation Act.

Finally, the Administrative Judge found that eighteen hours of official time was objectively reasonable for completing an affidavit, particularly given that the Complainant was largely restating information and documentation from previous complaints.

## FINAL AGENCY ACTION

The Agency subsequently issued a final order adopting the Administrative Judge's findings and conclusions. The Agency determined that the Complainant failed to establish by preponderant evidence that the Agency had subjected her to discrimination, reprisal, or hostile work environment as alleged in her complaint.
