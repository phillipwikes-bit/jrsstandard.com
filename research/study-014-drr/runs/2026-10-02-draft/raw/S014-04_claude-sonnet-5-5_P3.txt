# Summary of Investigation Record

## Background

At the time of the events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant worked as a Level 1 City Carrier at the Agency's Meadow Mesa Station facility in North Las Vegas, Nevada. On February 25, 2023, he filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against him and subjected him to harassment on the basis of age (born 1973). He identified the following five incidents:

1. On August 30, 2022, Complainant was put on Emergency Placement.
2. On September 14, 2022, Complainant was suspended.
3. On September 20, 2022, one of Complainant's supervisors followed him to the bathroom, yelled at him to get back to work, and threatened him with discipline.
4. On September 24, 2022, one of Complainant's supervisors accused him of not fastening his seatbelt before starting the engine.
5. On November 1, 2022, Complainant was escorted out of the facility.

The Agency accepted these claims and conducted an investigation. The evidence it produced is summarized below, claim by claim. The individuals involved are the Postmaster (PM) and two supervisors, identified as S1 and S2.

## Claim 1: Emergency Placement on August 30, 2022

### Complainant's account

Complainant stated in his investigative affidavit that on August 30, 2022, he asked S1 to sign or initial his copy of a request for Union time. S1 said she would do so later. Complainant asked why she could not do it at that moment. S1 repeated that she would do it later and told him he needed to get back to work.

Complainant stated that PM then asked what was going on. After Complainant explained, PM asked whether S1 had instructed him to get back to work. Complainant said yes, and PM asked whether he was refusing. Complainant said he was not refusing to return to work but wanted to understand why he was being held to policy and S1 was not.

Complainant stated that after he left the office area, PM came into the locker room and asked why he had not yet left. PM questioned what Complainant had been doing for the last hour. When Complainant tried to explain, he said PM interrupted him, yelled at him, and leaned over him in an attempt to intimidate him.

According to Complainant, PM returned to the locker room and told him he needed to deliver to the apartments that day. Complainant asked whether he had to finish the apartments that day, since doing so would put him over eight hours. Complainant stated that PM then accused him of failing to follow instructions, refused his request for a union steward, demanded his badge, and yelled at him to clean out his locker. He also stated that PM threatened to call the authorities to have him removed from the premises.

### Management's account

PM stated in his affidavit that he observed Complainant engaging in several time-wasting practices and gave him instructions that Complainant failed to follow. In keeping with Agency policy, Complainant was placed on Emergency Placement (EP) in an off-duty status without pay for failure to follow instructions. PM called Complainant's intimidation claim ridiculous and said that since having COVID he does not get close to any employee. He also stated that no union steward was available. He said Complainant was told he could speak with his steward but had to leave the facility immediately.

S1 confirmed in her affidavit that she asked Complainant about three times to deliver his route and that he did not listen to her. She stated that Complainant then became argumentative with PM. She said she had no knowledge of what may have occurred in the men's locker room. She also stated that no employee placed on EP was allowed to speak with the union before leaving the premises.

### Complainant's age allegation

Complainant stated that his age was a factor in his placement on EP. He said he was tasked with completing an overburdened route within eight hours and was constantly compared to younger carriers. He did not identify any specific similarly situated employees outside his protected class who were treated more favorably under similar circumstances.

## Claim 2: Events of September 14, 2022

### Complainant's account

Complainant stated in his affidavit that on September 14, 2022, he had completed his vehicle inspection and was loading his mail truck when S1 and S2 approached him. S2 demanded his badge and explained that he was being walked out because he had not fastened his seatbelt before starting the vehicle and had gotten out of the driver's seat while the engine was running. Complainant stated that he asked for a union steward and S2 denied the request. He also stated that, as he understood it, Section 812 of Handbook M-41 required seatbelts to be worn at all times the vehicle was in motion.

### The record

The record reflects that Complainant was again placed on EP in an off-duty status without pay. Complainant received a Notice of 14-Day Suspension on September 22, 2022. That letter cites the August 30, 2022, incident of failing to follow instructions as the reason for the suspension. Report of Investigation (ROI) at 00192-00193.

### Management's account

S2 stated in his affidavit that he observed Complainant exiting the mail truck and walking in front of it while the engine was running. He considered this a safety violation and stated that Agency policy required seatbelts to be worn at all times while operating a vehicle. S2 added that because Complainant was immediately placed on EP, he had to contact the union off postal property.

S1 stated that she had no knowledge of the incident because she was not present when it occurred.

### Complainant's age allegation

Complainant stated that he did not think his age factored into this incident. He did not identify any specific similarly situated employees outside his protected class who were treated more favorably under similar circumstances.

## Claim 3: Bathroom Incident on September 20, 2022

### Complainant's account

Complainant stated in his affidavit that on September 20, 2022, S2 followed him into the bathroom on two separate occasions and yelled at him. He also stated that S2 threatened to discipline him.

### Management's account

S2 denied raising his voice to Complainant. He stated that if he followed Complainant, it was because of Complainant's performance. S2 stated that rather than washing up in the bathroom, Complainant was spending a long time in the locker room doing something in his locker.

### Complainant's age allegation

Complainant said he believed his age was a factor in being yelled at and threatened with discipline. He explained that, being older, he has to use the bathroom more often than he did when he was younger. He did not identify any specific similarly situated employees outside his protected class who were treated more favorably under similar circumstances.

## Claim 4: Seatbelt Accusation on September 24, 2022

### Complainant's account

Complainant stated in his affidavit that on September 24, 2022, he started the engine of his mail truck and was fastening his seatbelt when S2 walked up to the passenger-side window. S2 yelled at him for not fastening his seatbelt before starting the engine. Citing Section 812 of Handbook M-41, Complainant stated that S2 was improperly demanding that seatbelts be fastened before the engine is started rather than before the vehicle is shifted into gear and put in motion.

Complainant stated that S1 walked over and S2 asked her whether she saw anything wrong. S1 said no. While S1 and S2 talked to each other and made calls, Complainant called the National Association of Letter Carriers (NALC) office. Someone there confirmed his understanding of the Agency's seatbelt policy. When Complainant asked the NALC officer to repeat that statement to S1 and S2, S1 told him it was not necessary and he could go deliver his route. S1 also told him he had to follow all supervisor instructions and, starting right then, needed to fasten his seatbelt before starting the engine.

### Management's account

S2 stated that he thought seatbelts needed to be fastened before starting a vehicle. He called safety and was told this was not a written Agency policy. He informed Complainant of that conversation, and Complainant was then allowed to continue to his route.

S1 confirmed that S2 called her over and asked whether she had observed Complainant doing anything wrong. She stated that she had not and ultimately told Complainant to follow his supervisor's instructions. Asked whether the requirement to fasten seatbelts before starting a vehicle applied to all employees going forward, S1 said she was not sure whether the request had been sent to all carriers or whether other carriers already fastened their seatbelts before starting their vehicles. She added that the relevant conversation was between S2 and Complainant.

### Complainant's age allegation

Complainant stated that he did not think his age factored into this incident. He did not identify any specific similarly situated employees outside his protected class who were treated more favorably under similar circumstances.

## Claim 5: Escort from the Facility on November 1, 2022

### Complainant's account

Complainant stated in his affidavit that on November 1, 2022, he gave S1 a doctor's note calling for 30 days of no overtime because of repetitive motion pain in his right shoulder. S1 told him she did not need to honor the note. Later that evening, Complainant returned with undelivered mail and clocked out. S1 told him to continue delivering until he finished his route. Complainant reminded her of the doctor's note and said he could not work overtime. S1 asked whether he was refusing to follow instructions. Complainant stated that he told her he did not have to follow unsafe supervisor instructions, and that ignoring his doctor's note and demanding overtime did not seem safe to him. S1 then demanded his badge, and he was escorted off the premises.

### The record

The record reflects that Complainant was again placed on EP in an off-duty status without pay, effective November 2, 2022.

### Management's account

S1 stated that Complainant told her, based on union advice, that he could bring his mail back and did not have to listen to management. She asked him three times to finish his route, but he kept repeating what the union had told him. She told him that while the union could provide guidance, he had to follow supervisor instruction.

S1 explained that she sent Complainant's medical documentation to Occupational Health Management (OHM) for guidance. OHM advised that Complainant could finish his route that day. OHM also said it was looking into the matter and told her to wait for an update. The open question was whether the doctor's note needed to be honored as a limited duty job offer or treated as a light duty request requiring submission to the PM for approval.

### Complainant's age allegation

Complainant said he believed his age was a factor in his being escorted off the premises. He explained that he was expected to finish an overburdened route and that, being older, he has noticed he does not recover from pain and injuries as quickly as he did when he was younger. He did not identify any specific similarly situated employees outside his protected class who were treated more favorably under similar circumstances.

## Procedural History and Decision

At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the report of investigation and notice of his right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Disability was not a basis raised in Complainant's complaint. Complainant timely requested a hearing.

On December 14, 2023, the Agency moved for a decision without a hearing. Complainant did not object, and the AJ granted the motion and issued a decision without a hearing on January 4, 2024.

The AJ found that the Agency's unrebutted facts established nondiscriminatory reasons for its actions. The AJ noted, for example, that Complainant argued with his supervisors, wasted time on his phone in the locker room, neglected to fasten his seatbelt, and failed to turn his vehicle off when exiting. Complainant did not dispute these predicates. The AJ found that they provided sufficient nondiscriminatory justification for the discipline and other actions management took.

The AJ also found that Complainant offered no evidence that management made biased comments about his age or treated younger workers more favorably. The AJ therefore concluded that, even viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to Complainant, no reasonable factfinder could conclude that the Agency's actions were tainted by considerations of his age.

The Agency then issued a final order adopting the AJ's finding that Complainant failed to prove he was subjected to discrimination and harassment as alleged. The final order fully implemented the AJ's decision. Complainant then filed the instant appeal.
