# INVESTIGATION CASE FILE RECORD

## COMPLAINANT AND EMPLOYMENT BACKGROUND

At the time of events giving rise to this complaint, the Complainant was employed as a Customs and Border Protection Officer (CBPO) at the U.S. Customs and Border Protection Agency's San Ysidro Port of Entry located in San Ysidro, California. The Complainant, born in 1954, filed this complaint alleging discrimination based on age under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967 (ADEA) and retaliation for prior protected Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) activity under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964.

## ALLEGATIONS OF HARASSMENT AND ADVERSE ACTIONS

### Initial Counseling and Management Actions

Beginning May 31, 2018, the Complainant alleged he was subjected to ongoing harassment. On that date, the Branch Chief refused to rescind a Memorandum of Counseling the Complainant had received regarding his use of the Treasury Enforcement Communication System (TECS). According to Agency documentation, the Branch Chief had requested the issuance of this memorandum after the Complainant was instructed twice not to place the terms "E/O if not in vehicle" on TECS records, as such notations create system violations. Despite these explicit instructions, the Complainant continued this practice, prompting the corrective action.

### Allegations of Bullying and Supervisory Harassment

From February 2019 through June 2019, the Complainant alleged that his Supervisory CBP Officer (identified as SCBPO-1) engaged in bullying and harassment, with the Branch Chief remaining aware of this conduct yet taking no corrective measures. Specific incidents cited include:

On April 17, 2019, SCBPO-1 verbally reprimanded the Complainant for missing a morning training class. In June 2019, SCBPO-1 displayed a knife to the Complainant and another CBPO (CBPO-2), indicating he regularly brings his knife collection to work in his backpack. On June 3, 2019, SCBPO-1 sent the Complainant an email reprimand concerning his TECS access expiration.

### Schedule Change and Birthday Celebration Incident

On June 19, 2019, SCBPO-1 altered the Complainant's work schedule from 2:00 p.m. to 1:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m. to accommodate a celebration for the Branch Chief's birthday. During this incident, SCBPO-1 solicited twenty dollars from the Complainant for the birthday party.

### Reassignment and Leave Approval Issues

On or around August 16, 2019, SCBPO-1 and another Supervisory CBP Officer (SCBPO-2) informed the Complainant's coworkers that he would be reassigned and leaving the Intelligence Targeting Unit effective September 1, 2019, although no actual reassignment occurred at that time. On the same date, these supervisors informed the Complainant that his requested leave for September 2019 would not be approved. However, following the Complainant's inquiry regarding this decision, his leave request was ultimately approved.

### Removal from Detail

Effective March 8, 2020, the Complainant was formally removed from his detail in the Intelligence Targeting Unit (ITU).

## INVESTIGATIVE FINDINGS AND AGENCY RESPONSES

### Dismissed Claims

The Agency dismissed two separate claims. First, a claim regarding the Complainant's non-receipt of a year-end monetary award, which he learned about on December 23, 2018. Second, a claim involving SCBPO-1 charging the Complainant with one hour of Leave Without Pay on April 21, 2019, for untimely counselor contact. Additionally, the Agency dismissed three claims alleging that SCBPO-1 spoke unprofessionally about coworkers, finding these claims failed to establish that the Complainant was aggrieved by comments made about other employees.

### Agency Explanations for Challenged Actions

The Intelligence Division Chief provided explanations for the TECS-related counseling, stating that the Complainant had requested that CBPOs "exempt" travelers on primary who had illegal activity links or ties, or whom he had targeted for illegal activity. This action constituted a request to violate TECS policy. The Chief approved the Memorandum of Counseling due to the Complainant's failure to follow explicit instructions despite being asked twice to comply.

Regarding the June 3, 2019 email regarding TECS access, SCBPO-1 explained that the TECS system sends automated periodic reminders when recertification is required, commencing thirty days before the certification due date. SCBPO-1 stated he received an automated system notification that the Complainant had lost access and subsequently sent a counseling email reminding the Complainant of his obligation to maintain active TECS access.

### Birthday Celebration Explanation

SCBPO-1 explained that the Intelligence Targeting Unit maintained a tradition of organizing potlucks or obtaining catering for group events, with participation announced as optional. He stated that employees were asked to donate ten dollars if they wished to participate. According to his account, CBPO-2 provided twenty dollars to cover both his own participation and the Complainant's after the Complainant verbally indicated his desire to participate.

### Removal from Intelligence Targeting Unit

The Intelligence Division Chief explained that the Complainant was removed from his ITU detail due to his failure to perform basic duties as an ITU officer. Specifically, a Watch Commander contacted the Complainant requesting assistance on a particular case and sought an expeditious response. The Complainant failed to perform the requested task and instead delegated the assignment to other members of the Tactical Terrorism Response Team.

## PROCEDURAL HISTORY AND AFFIDAVIT SUBMISSION

During the investigation process, the Complainant was provided two weeks to submit an affidavit but requested two weeks of administrative or sick leave to complete this document. On September 28, 2020, one day before the original deadline, the investigator granted an extension and approved sixteen hours of administrative leave for September 23-24, 2020. Despite these accommodations, the Complainant failed to submit an affidavit.

## AGENCY FINAL DECISION

The Agency's final decision found that the Complainant failed to establish a prima facie case of discrimination, noting the absence of evidence that employees outside his protected classes received differential treatment. The Agency further articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for all challenged actions and found no evidence of discriminatory animus. The decision concluded that the Complainant did not establish that alleged harassment incidents were motivated by his protected class status and therefore failed to prove discrimination as alleged.

## APPEAL AND CURRENT STATUS

The Complainant appealed the Agency's decision, contending that the investigation was inadequate and requesting referral to an Administrative Judge for additional investigation. He also asserted that the Agency's stated reasons were untrue. The Agency responded that the Complainant received adequate time to submit his affidavit but failed to do so and submitted no evidence supporting allegations of discriminatory animus.
