# FINDINGS

## I. Scope and Limitations of the Record

These findings rest solely on the procedural and background summary in the record provided. The record does not include witness affidavits, the Report of Investigation (ROI) exhibits, the Agency's stated reasons for its actions, comparator information, or the text of the Agency's final decision. Findings are therefore limited to what the record establishes. Where the record is silent, this is noted rather than inferred. No finding is made on whether discrimination occurred.

## II. Findings of Fact

**A. Parties and Employment**
1. At the time of the events, Complainant was a Carrier Technician at the Agency's Garland-South Station in Garland, Texas.
2. Complainant alleges a physical disability affecting her neck and shoulder. The record does not state whether the Agency conceded that she is an individual with a disability, and the record does not describe her specific work restrictions.
3. Two management officials are identified by title only: the Manager of Customer Service (Manager) and the Supervisor of Customer Service (Supervisor).

**B. The Formal Complaint**
4. On April 17, 2021, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging discrimination on the basis of disability (physical: neck and shoulder).
5. The complaint consists of the following nine claims:
   1. Beginning on or about November 7, 2020, and ongoing, she was continually harassed (questioned) about her work restrictions and not provided work assistance.
   2. Beginning on or about November 9, 2020, and continuing, she was required to do the walking portion of her route.
   3. On December 23, 2020, she was yelled at and embarrassed in front of coworkers.
   4. On December 23, 2020, she was required to submit a new CA-17 and was not paid for time missed to have it completed.
   5. On December 23, 2020, she was moved off Route 74 and required to case and carry Route 23.
   6. On February 1, 2021, she was called to the supervisor's desk to discuss her medical documents.
   7. On March 23, 2021, she was harassed and referred to the District Reasonable Accommodation Committee (DRAC).
   8. On April 2, 2021, her leave request was not granted and she was charged Absent Without Leave (AWOL).
   9. On or about April 15, 2021, management gave her a modified assignment that changed her start time to 10:00 am and did not provide a mail cart.

**C. Dismissed Claim**
6. The Agency dismissed a separate claim alleging that Complainant was attacked by a dog while on duty and not properly compensated for time missed.
7. The Agency dismissed it under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.107(a)(1) as a collateral attack on the Office of Workers' Compensation Programs (OWCP), which has jurisdiction over compensation for work-related injuries (ROI at 71-75).
8. The dismissal is consistent with Commission precedent that the EEO process may not be used to challenge actions occurring during the OWCP adjudicatory process. See *Janiece H. v. U.S. Postal Serv.*, EEOC [number removed] (Jan. 8, 2016). The dismissal was affirmed.

**D. Substance of Complainant's Allegations**
9. The core of the complaint is an alleged Rehabilitation Act violation: that the Manager harassed Complainant because of her disability and forced her to work outside her medical restrictions.
10. Complainant specifically contends that:
    - the Manager marked her AWOL even though the Supervisor had previously approved her leave request; and
    - the Agency denied her a mail cart to assist with her duties.
11. These are Complainant's contentions. The record contains no corroborating or contradicting evidence, such as the leave approval, the AWOL record, or the restriction documentation.

**E. Procedural History**
12. At the close of the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the ROI and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ).
13. Complainant requested a hearing.
14. The AJ denied the hearing request as a sanction. Complainant had not submitted the required Preliminary Case Information and had not responded to the AJ's Order to Show Cause.
15. The AJ remanded the complaint to the Agency.
16. The Agency issued a final decision under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b), concluding that Complainant had not proven that the Agency discriminated against her as alleged.

## III. Conclusions Supported by the Record

1. The complaint was timely processed through the investigation, hearing-request, remand, and final-decision stages, as far as the record shows.
2. The dog-attack compensation claim was properly excluded from the EEO complaint as a collateral attack on the OWCP process.
3. The hearing was denied because of Complainant's noncompliance with the AJ's orders, not on the merits. The AJ made no merits findings.
4. The Agency's final decision found no discrimination. The record does not show the basis for that conclusion, so the reasoning cannot be assessed here.

## IV. Matters Not Resolved by the Record

The following would be needed to make merits findings:

- Whether Complainant is a qualified individual with a disability, and the content and dates of her medical restrictions.
- The Agency's explanations for each of the nine claims, especially the AWOL charge, the DRAC referral, the route reassignment, the 10:00 am start time, and the denial of a mail cart.
- Whether the Supervisor approved the April 2, 2021 leave request, and on what authority the Manager changed that outcome.
- Whether the Agency's actions complied with Complainant's restrictions, and whether any accommodation request was made, granted, or denied.
- Testimony from the Manager, the Supervisor, and any coworkers present on December 23, 2020.
- Evidence on whether the alleged conduct was sufficiently severe or pervasive to constitute harassment, and whether any similarly situated employees were treated differently.
- Any explanation for Complainant's failure to file the Preliminary Case Information or respond to the Order to Show Cause.
