# FINDINGS SECTION

## Background and Jurisdiction

The complainants, both Emergency Management Specialists (Regional and National Tribal Government Liaisons) at the Agency's First Responder Network Authority, filed formal Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) complaints on June 13, 2019. At the time of the events at issue, the complainants were employed remotely from locations in California and had been with the Agency since October 2015, initially hired at Pay Band IV level. The complaints alleged discrimination and harassment based on race (Native American), national origin (Native American), and retaliation for prior protected EEO activity. The complainants subsequently withdrew their claims of sex discrimination during the hearing process.

## Alleged Discriminatory Actions

### 1. Discriminatory Pay and Compensation Practices

The complainants alleged systematic discrimination in pay beginning at the point of hire, asserting that:

- During initial salary negotiations, Complainant 1 was explicitly told the Agency would only offer 70% of his previous wages on a "take it or leave it" basis, and that non-Native employees did not receive comparable treatment during hiring.
- Complainant 2 was not credited for prior salary history or teaching wages and received only compensation matching her tribal salary level.
- Both complainants performed identical work to peers hired at Pay Band V but were hired and maintained at the lower Pay Band IV level.

### 2. Failure to Remediate Known Disparities

The investigation revealed that:

- In April 2018, the Executive Director directed Complainants' supervisor to conduct a review of wage and pay band disparities.
- Despite completion of this review, the Executive Director and Senior Director took no remedial action and subsequently denied awareness of or commitment to the review.
- In January 2019, the Executive Director acknowledged receiving the review but stated he felt no obligation to act on its findings.
- The Executive Director instructed supervisory staff to "tamper any escalation of HR issues for the tribal team"—an instruction he later claimed was misunderstood.
- The Deputy Chief Administrative Officer failed to inform the complainants of their rights to pursue EEO processes to address discriminatory pay.

### 3. Exclusion from Career Advancement Opportunities

The complainants alleged that the discriminatory Pay Band IV classification precluded them from applying to senior leadership positions, as the Agency made such positions available exclusively to Pay Band V employees.

### 4. Reorganizational Retaliation

In June 2018, the complainants were "buried deeper" in the organizational structure through a reorganization process, which they alleged was undertaken to provoke resignation and limit HR escalations from the tribal team.

### 5. Unequal Task Assignments

Beginning in June 2018, the complainants were tasked with creating separate strategic plans on an ongoing basis, a requirement not imposed on their non-tribal peers.

### 6. Hostile Work Environment and Contractor Conduct

Between June 2018 and February 2019, the complainants repeatedly notified senior leadership of hostile and discriminatory treatment by contractor officials. Senior leadership condoned this treatment and continued placing the tribal team members in hostile situations with contracting personnel.

### 7. Denial of Equal Resources and Access

The complainants alleged systematic denial of equal access to resources and stakeholders, including:

- Forced coordination of activities through a separate, discriminatory resource access process
- Required use of a "gatekeeper" to engage with contractor staff and tribal shareholders
- Restrictions on the types of meetings complainants could attend
- Mandatory participation of a Contracting Officer's Representative in daily engagements between contractors and complainants

### 8. Failure to Acknowledge Contributions

On May 5, 2019, the Agency issued a public communication thanking local, state, and federal workers for their dedication but failed to acknowledge the tribal team members' contributions and accomplishments.

## Procedural Matters

The two complaints were assigned to different Administrative Judges (AJs) in the Commission's Washington Field Office. Complainants' counsel filed motions to consolidate the complaints for joint processing; the Agency opposed consolidation. One AJ denied the consolidation motion while the other AJ did not address it. The AJs issued separate summary judgment decisions finding no discrimination or unlawful retaliation was established. The Agency implemented the AJs' decisions as its final actions. These findings are subject to the instant appeals.
