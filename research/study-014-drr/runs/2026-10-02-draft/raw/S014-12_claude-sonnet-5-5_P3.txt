**Background**

During the relevant period, Complainant was employed as an Aircraft Mechanic at the Agency's Aircraft Maintenance Squadron facility at Robins Air Force Base, Georgia. At the time of the events at issue, his first-level supervisor was the Aircraft Overhaul Supervisor (Supervisor), and his second-level supervisor was the Production Team Lead (Team Lead). Report of Investigation (ROI) at 161-62, 169.

On May 25, 2021, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency subjected him to a hostile work environment based on race (African-American), color (Black), and age (52). He alleged that on December 11, 2020, Supervisor refused to give him first aid for a bleeding head wound and verbally assaulted him by screaming, "Get your ass back on the plane, you do not do shit every day, I want you off my fucking team immediately, and I am tired of your shit."

**Procedural History**

After the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the ROI and notice of his right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant timely requested a hearing. On January 21, 2022, the AJ issued a Notice of Proposed Summary Judgment (Notice) and gave both parties an opportunity to respond.

**The AJ's Notice**

The Notice set out the relevant facts. Complainant had an accident that caused his head to bleed. While he was looking for a band-aid, Supervisor saw him away from his work area and began yelling at him, including the statement quoted above. The AJ noted that Complainant conceded he did not believe the incident was based on age or race. Complainant added, however, that Supervisor had treated him differently because of his protected classes in past, unspecified incidents, though he never approached Supervisor about them. Supervisor did not deny yelling at Complainant but stated that he had numerous performance concerns about him.

After the incident, Complainant contacted Team Lead and another Agency official. Supervisor was suspended without pay for 10 days, reassigned to the day shift, and barred from supervising anyone for 90 days. His role as Complainant's supervisor ended immediately after the incident. ROI at 162.

The AJ found Supervisor's behavior unprofessional and unacceptable but not harassment. The AJ characterized it as a single incident in which Supervisor lost his temper and used facially neutral language, an isolated outburst rather than conduct tied to a protected basis. Even considering the background allegations, the AJ found that the totality of the circumstances did not show a work environment permeated with hostility, and that the cursing was not sufficiently severe. The AJ therefore indicated an intent to issue a decision without a hearing in favor of the Agency.

**Responses to the Notice**

Complainant submitted stipulations of fact that, for the first time, included additional allegations. Without supporting evidence, he stated that Supervisor had a history of hatred toward him and of mistreating other African-Americans at work. He also asserted for the first time that Supervisor made comments about President Obama and displayed images of the Confederate flag in the hangar. The Agency did not respond by the deadline but did give the AJ Supervisor's suspension paperwork. Its submission was thus limited to documenting the discipline imposed.

**The AJ's Decision and the Agency's Final Order**

Over Complainant's objections, the AJ issued a decision without a hearing on February 25, 2022, incorporating the Notice. The AJ again noted that Complainant initially reported he did not believe the incident was due to his race or age. The AJ further observed that the background allegations never appeared in the EEO Counselor Report, the formal complaint, or Complainant's affidavit. Considering the totality of the circumstances, the AJ concluded that Complainant had not proven he was discriminated against as alleged. The Agency then issued a final order adopting that finding. This appeal followed.

**Contentions on Appeal**

Complainant contends he was "humiliated, intimidated, bullied and exposed to racist comments and treatment by [Supervisor]." He alleges he gave investigators a list of racially motivated incidents and a list of witnesses to those events. He says allegations of race, age, or color discrimination were made several times before summary judgment. Finally, he claims the Agency withheld "vital information" about Supervisor and that summary judgment denied him the chance to obtain it. He asserts this information "will prove a history of racial discrimination [of Supervisor] towards African Americans." The Agency responds that the AJ's summary judgment was proper and should stand.

**Issue Presented**

The question on appeal is whether the AJ properly issued a decision without a hearing and correctly found that Complainant failed to establish a hostile work environment based on race, color, or age.
