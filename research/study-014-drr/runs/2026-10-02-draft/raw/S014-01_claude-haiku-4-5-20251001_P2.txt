# FINDINGS

## Overview
This investigation examined five distinct claims of discrimination and reprisal filed by Complainant, a GS-12 Senior Bankruptcy Specialist, based on disability and prior protected EEO activity. The investigation included a detailed review of documentary evidence, witness statements, and prior administrative determinations. An EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ) issued a summary judgment decision in favor of the Agency, which was subsequently adopted by final order.

## Claims 1 & 3 – Denial of FMLA Leave

**Leave Depletion and AWOL/LWOP Status (February-March 2021)**

Complainant's claims regarding FMLA leave denials in February and March 2021 were previously adjudicated by an EEOC Administrative Judge on May 25, 2022. That Judge determined that the Agency properly placed Complainant in Absent Without Leave (AWOL) and Leave Without Pay (LWOP) status because Complainant had depleted her available paid leave balances and failed to report to work. Complainant's re-litigation of these same issues in the instant complaint does not present new evidence or circumstances warranting reconsideration.

**August 3, 2021 Denial of Eight Hours of Paid FMLA Sick Leave**

Supervisor1 maintained records indicating that Complainant had exhausted her paid, available sick leave by March 2021. A February 26, 2021 Agency letter informed Complainant that she possessed only 32 hours of unpaid FMLA leave available between March 1, 2021, and December 3, 2021. Consequently, on March 1, 2021, and subsequent dates, Supervisor1 notified Complainant that no paid leave remained available.

However, when Complainant requested an audit of her leave on August 5, 2021, a payroll representative's audit revealed that Complainant retained some paid leave available under FMLA. The Agency retroactively granted Complainant paid leave for August 3, 2021, and other dates as warranted. Complainant conceded that she ultimately received all paid leave to which she was entitled.

Complainant did not identify any similarly situated employees who received more favorable treatment regarding leave entitlements.

## Claim 2 – Reassignment of Casework (June 2021)

When a GS-12 Bankruptcy Specialist retired in June 2021, Supervisor1 reassigned the retired employee's cases among three GS-12 level employees, including Complainant. Complainant objected to the reassignment, asserting she should have been on indefinite weather and safety leave and therefore should not have received additional work.

The record establishes that:

- Supervisor1's reassignment decision fell within her authority to assign inventory to employees in her group
- All reassigned work was appropriate for Complainant's grade level (GS-12)
- Cases were distributed among the three GS-12 Bankruptcy Specialists based on alphabetical order of case names
- Documentary evidence, including a spreadsheet provided by Supervisor1, corroborates the equitable distribution of cases

An Administrative Judge in a separate proceeding determined that Complainant was not entitled to indefinite weather and safety leave and that the Agency met all its obligations under the Rehabilitation Act. That appeal remains pending.

No evidence in the record demonstrates that similarly situated employees received more favorable treatment.

## Claim 4 – Reasonable Accommodations

**Initial Request and Interactive Meeting**

On May 6, 2021, Complainant requested: (1) assistance from her manager and/or secretary to print and mail letters; and (2) a reduced workload. On May 18, 2021, Complainant amended her request to include reassignment. The Agency's Reasonable Accommodations Coordinator conducted an interactive meeting on May 21, 2021.

**Complainant's Medical Limitations**

Critically, Complainant conceded that her medical impairment does not limit her ability to perform any essential job duties. This admission substantially undermines the factual foundation for requesting reduced workload or reassignment accommodations.

**Agency Denial of Reduced Workload and Reassignment**

The Agency denied both requests based on the following:

- No medical documentation supported the need for reduced workload
- Lowering Complainant's caseload would compromise performance standards and Agency mission accomplishment
- At the time of the request, Complainant maintained only 25 cases—substantially below other Bankruptcy Specialists and below her typical historical caseload
- Reassignment constitutes an accommodation of last resort, available only when an employee cannot meet essential job functions
- Complainant failed to demonstrate inability to perform essential functions

The record reflected that Complainant's 2018 request for reduced inventory stated she desired approximately 38-40 cases, yet her contemporaneous caseload of 25 cases already fell below this threshold.

**Approval of Printer Accommodation**

The Agency approved Complainant's request for assistance printing and mailing correspondence. To address her physical limitations, management approved a small, portable printer that Complainant could operate. While awaiting delivery, management agreed to print and mail Complainant's correspondence.

However, before the printer's delivery, Complainant cancelled the order, stating she did not wish to be held accountable for the printer and its paper. Complainant later asserted she would be unable to lift either the portable printer or its paper—a position inconsistent with her earlier request for the device. Management continued to print and mail Complainant's letters notwithstanding the cancellation.

**Reconsideration and Appeal**

On July 12, 2021, Complainant appealed the denial of her reconsideration request. The Director of Collection Operations denied the appeal on August 24, 2021, reiterating that Complainant had rejected the Agency's offer of a lightweight portable printer and noting that she already maintained a reduced workload and was capable of performing essential job functions.

## Claim 5 – Denial of Official Time (October 26, 2021)

On October 20, 2021, Complainant requested official time to complete an affidavit in response to an EEO Investigator's request. The Agency granted Complainant:

- Six hours of official time on October 20, 2021
- Four hours of official time on October 21, 2021
- Additional official time approved on October 25, 2021

Despite receiving approximately 18 hours of official time, Complainant did not complete her affidavit. On October 26, 2021, Complainant requested additional official time, stating she had two questions remaining on the affidavit.

Supervisor1 denied this request based on: (1) the substantial official time already provided (approximately 18 hours); and (2) OCRD's instruction that Complainant contact the investigator directly to request an extension of her deadline if additional time was needed. The record indicates that Complainant's previous official time was partially devoted to information and documentation she had previously submitted in earlier complaints.

## Administrative Judge Determination

The EEOC Administrative Judge issued a summary judgment decision finding:

1. **No Hostile Work Environment**: The alleged incidents were insufficiently severe or pervasive to constitute a hostile work environment. Complainant failed to establish that conduct was based on discriminatory or retaliatory animus.

2. **No Comparative Evidence of Discrimination**: Complainant did not identify similarly situated employees outside her protected classes who received more favorable treatment regarding FMLA leave, case reassignments, or other employment decisions.

3. **Leave Claims**: The Agency properly maintained records of Complainant's leave balances. Upon Complainant's belated request for an audit, any discrepancies were corrected and Complainant received all paid leave to which she was entitled.

4. **Case Reassignment**: The reassignment of cases from the retired employee was appropriate, based on grade level, skill level, and Complainant's unusually low existing workload. Cases were distributed equitably among three similarly situated GS-12 employees.

5. **Reasonable Accommodations**: The Agency met its obligations under the Rehabilitation Act by:
   - Conducting an interactive meeting
   - Offering a lightweight portable printer accommodation
   - Arranging for management to print and mail correspondence during the interim period
   - Maintaining a caseload already below typical levels for Complainant's position

   Complainant's admission that her impairment did not limit essential job functions, combined with her rejection of offered accommodations, negated any violation.

6. **Official Time**: Eighteen hours of official time was objectively reasonable for completing an affidavit. The Agency's direction that Complainant request additional time directly from the investigator, consistent with OCRD guidance, was appropriate.

## Conclusion

The record supports the Administrative Judge's determination that Complainant failed to establish discrimination, reprisal, or a hostile work environment based on disability or prior protected EEO activity. The Agency's personnel decisions were supported by legitimate, non-discriminatory reasons documented in the record.
