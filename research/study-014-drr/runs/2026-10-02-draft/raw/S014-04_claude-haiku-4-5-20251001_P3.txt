# INVESTIGATIVE CASE FILE RECORD

## COMPLAINANT EMPLOYMENT STATUS AND INCIDENT TIMELINE

At the time of the events that precipitated this employment discrimination complaint, the Complainant was employed by the United States Postal Service (Agency) as a Level 1 City Carrier assigned to the Meadow Mesa Station facility located in North Las Vegas, Nevada. On February 25, 2023, the Complainant filed a formal Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) complaint with the Agency, alleging unlawful discrimination and harassment based on his age. According to records, the Complainant was born in 1973, which would have made him approximately 49-50 years old at the time the alleged discriminatory incidents occurred. The complaint encompasses five distinct incidents occurring between August 30, 2022, and November 2, 2022, each of which the Complainant contends was motivated by discriminatory considerations related to his age.

## CLAIM 1: EMERGENCY PLACEMENT ON AUGUST 30, 2022

### Complainant's Account

In his investigative affidavit, the Complainant provided a detailed narrative of the events leading to his initial Emergency Placement (EP) on August 30, 2022. According to his account, he approached Supervisor S1 and requested that she sign or initial his request form for Union time. The Complainant stated that when S1 declined to execute the request immediately, informing him that she would attend to the matter later, he inquired why the signature could not be provided at that moment. S1 allegedly reiterated that the task would be completed at a later time and instructed him to return to work activities.

The Complainant further stated that Postmaster (PM) then intervened in the conversation, asking what situation was developing. After the Complainant explained the circumstances, PM inquired whether S1 had issued instructions to return to work. The Complainant confirmed that she had, and PM then questioned whether the Complainant was refusing to comply with those instructions. The Complainant denied refusing to work but expressed his desire to understand why he was being held to established policy while S1 apparently was not.

Subsequently, while the Complainant was in the employee locker room, PM allegedly entered and asked why he had not yet left the facility. PM further questioned what the Complainant had been doing for the preceding hour. When the Complainant attempted to provide an explanation, he stated that PM interrupted him, raised his voice, and leaned toward him in what the Complainant characterized as an intimidation tactic. PM then re-entered the locker room and directed the Complainant to deliver mail to apartments that day. When the Complainant asked whether he needed to complete the apartment delivery, noting that doing so would exceed his eight-hour work limit, PM allegedly accused him of failing to follow instructions, refused his request for union representation, demanded his work badge, yelled at him to remove his personal items from his locker, and threatened to contact law enforcement to have him forcibly removed from the facility.

### Agency Respondents' Accounts

In his affidavit, the Postmaster acknowledged observing the Complainant engaging in what he characterized as time-wasting activities and stated that he issued instructions that the Complainant failed to follow. The PM asserted that, in accordance with established Agency policy, he placed the Complainant on Emergency Placement in off-duty, non-compensated status due to the failure to follow management instructions. Regarding the Complainant's allegation of intimidation, the PM characterized the claim as without merit. He further explained that owing to health considerations following his recovery from COVID-19, he maintains distance from all employees and therefore would not have leaned toward or attempted to intimidate the Complainant.

The PM also addressed the union representation issue, indicating that a union steward was unavailable at the time but that the Complainant was informed he could subsequently speak with his union representative after departing the facility immediately.

Supervisor S1 corroborated elements of the PM's account. She stated in her affidavit that she had instructed the Complainant approximately three times to proceed with mail delivery, but he did not comply with those directives. S1 indicated that the Complainant subsequently became argumentative with the Postmaster. Regarding the intimidation allegation, S1 stated she possessed no knowledge of events that may have transpired in the men's locker room, as she was not present in that location. She further clarified that Agency policy prohibited employees placed on Emergency Placement from engaging in conversations with union representatives before departing the premises.

### Complainant's Age-Based Theory

The Complainant advanced the theory that his age was a contributing factor in his placement on EP, arguing that he was assigned an overburdened mail route that management expected him to complete within an eight-hour timeframe. He further alleged that he was subjected to constant unfavorable comparisons with younger carriers. However, the Complainant could not identify specific examples of younger employees who were similarly situated in all material respects yet received more favorable treatment.

## CLAIM 2: SUSPENSION AND SEATBELT VIOLATION ON SEPTEMBER 14, 2022

### Complainant's Allegations

According to his affidavit, on September 14, 2022, following completion of his vehicle inspection and while loading mail into his assigned truck, Supervisor S2 approached the Complainant and demanded his work badge. S2 allegedly explained that the Complainant was being removed from the premises because he had not fastened his seatbelt before starting the vehicle and had exited the driver's seat while the engine remained running. The Complainant stated that he requested union representation, but S2 denied this request.

The Complainant further noted that, according to his understanding, Section 812 of Postal Service Handbook M-41 specified that seatbelts must be worn at all times when a vehicle is in motion, not before the engine is started. The Complainant was placed on EP in off-duty, unpaid status. On September 22, 2022, he received a formal Notice of 14-Day Suspension Letter; however, this letter cited the August 30, 2022 failure-to-follow-instructions incident as the rationale for the suspension.

### Supervisor S2's Account

In his affidavit, S2 stated that he observed the Complainant exiting the mail truck and walking in front of the vehicle while its engine was still running. S2 characterized this conduct as a safety violation and asserted that Agency policy required seatbelts to be worn at all times while operating a vehicle. S2 explained that because the Complainant was immediately placed on EP, union contact was required to occur off postal property.

### Supervisor S1's Statement

S1 stated she possessed no knowledge of the September 14 incident because she was not present when it occurred.

### Complainant's Age-Based Theory

The Complainant stated that he did not believe his age was a factor in this particular incident. He also could not provide specific examples of younger employees similarly situated who received more favorable treatment.

## CLAIM 3: BATHROOM INCIDENT AND ALLEGED YELLING ON SEPTEMBER 20, 2022

### Complainant's Allegations

The Complainant alleged that on September 20, 2022, Supervisor S2 followed him into the facility bathroom on two separate occasions and yelled at him. The Complainant further asserted that S2 threatened him with disciplinary action.

### Supervisor S2's Response

S2 denied raising his voice toward the Complainant. He stated that if he did follow the Complainant to the bathroom area, such action was warranted based on concerns regarding the Complainant's job performance. S2 further explained that rather than performing standard hygiene activities, the Complainant appeared to be spending an extended amount of time in the locker room and engaging in unspecified activities at his locker.

### Complainant's Age-Based Theory

The Complainant advanced the theory that his age was a factor in this incident, reasoning that as he has aged, he requires more frequent bathroom breaks than he did during his younger years. He could not, however, provide specific examples of younger employees treated more favorably under similar circumstances.

## CLAIM 4: SEATBELT POLICY DISPUTE ON SEPTEMBER 24, 2022

### Complainant's Account

On September 24, 2022, the Complainant started his vehicle's engine while fastening his seatbelt when S2 approached the passenger-side window and yelled at him for not having the seatbelt fastened before starting the engine. The Complainant cited Section 812 of Handbook M-41, arguing that the correct policy required seatbelts to be fastened before the vehicle was shifted into gear and put into motion, not before the engine is merely started.

The Complainant stated that S1 then approached, and S2 asked her whether she observed anything improper. S1 replied in the negative. While S1 and S2 engaged in conversation and made telephone calls, the Complainant called the National Association of Letter Carriers (NALC) office. According to the Complainant, an NALC representative confirmed his interpretation of the Agency's seatbelt policy. When the Complainant asked to have the NALC officer repeat this confirmation to S1 and S2, S1 stated this was unnecessary. S1 told the Complainant he could proceed with his mail delivery route. S1 also instructed him that he must comply with all supervisor instructions and, going forward, must fasten his seatbelt before starting the engine.

### Supervisor S2's Account

S2 stated he believed that seatbelts needed to be fastened before a vehicle is started. He indicated that he subsequently called the Agency's safety office and was informed that no written Agency policy exists establishing when seatbelts must be fastened. S2 stated he informed the Complainant of this conversation, after which the Complainant was permitted to proceed to his delivery route.

### Supervisor S1's Account

S1 confirmed that S2 called her over and asked whether she had observed any improper conduct by the Complainant. S1 stated she did not observe anything wrong. S1 stated she ultimately directed the Complainant to comply with supervisor instructions. When asked whether the requirement to fasten seatbelts before starting an engine was applied to all employees going forward, S1 expressed uncertainty, stating she was not sure whether this directive was communicated to all carriers or whether other carriers were already fastening seatbelts before starting their vehicles. S1 further stated the relevant conversation was between S2 and the Complainant.

### Complainant's Age-Based Theory

The Complainant stated he did not believe his age factored into this incident and could not provide examples of younger employees treated more favorably in similar circumstances.

## CLAIM 5: MEDICAL ACCOMMODATION DENIAL AND REMOVAL ON NOVEMBER 1-2, 2022

### Complainant's Account

On November 1, 2022, the Complainant provided S1 with a physician's note recommending thirty days of no overtime work due to repetitive motion pain affecting his right shoulder. The Complainant stated that S1 told him she was not obligated to honor the medical documentation. Later that evening, when the Complainant returned to the facility with undelivered mail and clocked out, S1 allegedly told him he needed to continue delivering mail until completing his assigned route. When the Complainant reminded S1 of his medical restriction and explained his inability to work overtime, S1 allegedly asked whether he was refusing to follow instructions.

The Complainant stated he responded that he was not obligated to follow instructions he considered unsafe, and that ignoring physician guidance while demanding overtime work did not constitute a safe work practice. S1 allegedly then demanded his work badge, and he was escorted from the facility. The Complainant was placed on EP in off-duty, unpaid status, effective November 2, 2022.

### Supervisor S1's Account

S1 provided a different characterization of events. She stated that the Complainant told her, based on union advice, that he could return his undelivered mail and was not required to listen to management directives. S1 stated she requested that the Complainant complete his route three times, but he repeatedly reiterated what union counsel had allegedly told him. S1 explained that while the union could provide guidance, the Complainant remained obligated to follow supervisor instructions.

S1 further explained that she forwarded the Complainant's medical documentation to the Agency's Occupational Health Management (OHM) department for guidance. According to S1, OHM advised her that the Complainant could complete his delivery route that day. S1 stated that OHM indicated they were investigating the matter and would provide updated guidance regarding whether the physician's note constituted a limited duty job offer or required submission to the Postmaster as a formal light duty request requiring PM approval.

### Complainant's Age-Based Theory

The Complainant argued that age was a factor in his removal from the facility, asserting that he was expected to complete an overburdened mail route and that, having grown older, he has noticed that his body does not recover from pain and injuries as quickly as it did during his youth. He could not, however, provide specific examples of younger employees treated more favorably in similar circumstances.

## ADMINISTRATIVE ADJUDICATION

At the conclusion of the Agency's investigation, the Complainant received a copy of the Report of Investigation and written notice of his right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge. The Complainant timely requested a hearing. However, when he failed to lodge objections to the Agency's motion, the assigned Administrative Judge granted the Agency's December 14, 2023 motion for a decision rendered without an evidentiary hearing and issued a written decision on January 4, 2024.

The Administrative Judge found that the Complainant failed to establish that the Agency subjected him to discrimination and harassment as alleged. The judge determined that the Agency's unrebutted factual findings established legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for its personnel actions. The Administrative Judge specifically noted that the Complainant had argued with supervisors, engaged in time-wasting activities while using his cellular telephone in the locker room, neglected to properly fasten his seatbelt, and failed to turn off his vehicle when exiting it. The judge found that these established facts, which the Complainant did not dispute, provided substantial nondiscriminatory justification for the disciplinary measures and other management actions imposed.

The Administrative Judge concluded that the Complainant failed to present evidence demonstrating that management made age-based biased comments or treated younger workers more favorably. Based on this analysis, the judge determined that no reasonable factfinder, even when viewing evidence in the light most favorable to the Complainant, could rationally conclude that the Agency's actions were motivated by age-related considerations.

The Agency subsequently issued a final order fully adopting and implementing the Administrative Judge's decision. This action prompted the Complainant to file the instant appeal.
