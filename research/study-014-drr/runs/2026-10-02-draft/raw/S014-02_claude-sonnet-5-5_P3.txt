**Investigation Record Summary**

**Background and Claims**

At the time of the events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant was employed as a Carrier Technician at the Agency's Garland-South Station in Garland, Texas. On April 17, 2021, she filed a formal EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against her on the basis of disability (physical – neck and shoulder) in the following instances:

1. Beginning on or about November 7, 2020, and ongoing, she was continually harassed (questioned) about her work restrictions and was not provided work assistance;
2. Beginning on or about November 9, 2020, and continuing, she was required to perform the walking portion of her route;
3. On December 23, 2020, she was yelled at and embarrassed in front of coworkers;
4. On December 23, 2020, she was required to submit a new CA-17 and was not paid for the time she missed from work to have it completed;
5. On December 23, 2020, she was moved off Route 74 and required to case and carry Route 23;
6. On February 1, 2021, she was called to the supervisor's desk to discuss her medical documents;
7. On March 23, 2021, she was harassed and referred to the District Reasonable Accommodation Committee (DRAC);
8. On April 2, 2021, her leave request was not granted, and she was charged Absent Without Leave (AWOL); and
9. On or about April 15, 2021, management gave her a modified assignment that changed her start time to 10:00 a.m. and did not provide her with a mail cart.

**Dismissed Claim**

The Agency dismissed a separate claim alleging that Complainant was attacked by a dog while on the job and was not properly compensated for the time she missed. It dismissed the claim under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.107(a)(1) as a collateral attack on the Office of Workers' Compensation Programs (OWCP), which has jurisdiction over compensation for work-related injuries. See Report of Investigation (ROI) at 71-75. The dismissal is affirmed. The Commission has repeatedly held that a complainant cannot use the EEO process to challenge actions that occurred during the OWCP adjudicatory process. See Janiece H. v. U.S. Postal Serv., EEOC [number removed] (Jan. 8, 2016).

**Nature of the Remaining Claims**

The gravamen of Complainant's claims is an alleged violation of the Rehabilitation Act. She asserts that the Manager of Customer Service (Manager) harassed her because of her disability and forced her to work outside her medical restrictions. Specifically, Complainant argues that the Manager marked her AWOL even though she had submitted a leave request that the Supervisor of Customer Service (Supervisor) had previously approved. She also argues that the Agency denied her a mail cart to assist her with her duties.

**Procedural History**

At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the ROI and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant requested a hearing. The AJ denied the request as a sanction because Complainant failed to submit the required Preliminary Case Information and failed to respond to the AJ's Order to Show Cause. The AJ remanded the complaint to the Agency, which issued a final decision under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b).

**Disposition**

The matter proceeded on the nine claims listed above. The Agency's final decision concluded that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged.
