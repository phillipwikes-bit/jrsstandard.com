# Case File Summary

## Parties and Background
- Complainant was a Senior Special Agent, Polygraph Examiner in the Agency's Forensic Services Division in Washington, D.C., during the period at issue.
- On January 2, 2020, Complainant filed a formal EEO complaint alleging discrimination in reprisal for prior protected EEO activity.
- By letter dated March 11, 2020, the Agency accepted the complaint for investigation.

## Accepted Claims (as stated in the record)
1. Claim 1 concerns Complainant's 2019 Merit Promotion Plan (MPP) Supervisory Evaluation (SE). The record text of this claim is incomplete. It refers only to Complainant not being rated as well as his prior supervisor had rated him in 2018.
2. Hostile work environment based on retaliation (prior EEO activity):
   - **2(a):** On or about September 10, 2019, S2 obstructed Complainant's return to Quality Control (QC) and mentor duties.
   - **2(b):** On or about September 13, 2019, S2 denied his request for reconsideration of the denial of QC and mentor duties.
   - **2(c):** Since September 1, 2019, to the present, S2 and another Agency official delegated assignments directly to the Washington Field Office (WFO) examiner and excluded Complainant. This circumvented him as the designated Senior Polygraph Examiner/Polygraph Backup.

## Procedural History
- After the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant the report of investigation (ROI) and notice of his right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge.
- At Complainant's request, the Agency issued a final decision under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b) finding no discrimination. The record does not name an individual decision-maker. The decision was issued by the Agency.
- Complainant then filed this appeal.

## Agency's Final Decision
- **Claim 1:** The Agency found it had articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for the MPP SE.
- **Claims 2(a)–2(b):** The Agency asserted that Complainant was restored to mentoring duties and was under consideration for QC duties.
- **Claim 2(c):** The Agency stated that S2 asserted he never consulted Complainant or any other WFO examiner regarding assignments and had not excluded Complainant from communications.

## Complainant's Arguments on Appeal
Complainant asks that the final decision be reversed. He states or asserts that:
- He requested a final decision on July 10, 2020, and the Agency's decision was untimely. He requests default judgment against the Agency as a sanction.
- The Agency's ROI was inadequate.
- His 2019-2020 MPP SE continues the reprisal against him.
- About three months before the MPP SE rating, he was rated "Outstanding" on his 2019-2020 annual performance appraisal.
- Less qualified employees are being selected for the collateral duties of mentoring and QC.
- One management official did not respond to a request to provide testimony.
- In a prior EEO case, the Agency issued a final decision ordering it to reassign him to QC and to provide EEO training to specified management officials, and it has not done so.

## Agency's Response on Appeal
- **Claim 1:** The MPP SE is a separate and distinct process from the annual performance evaluation process. S2 did not complete Complainant's 2018-2019 MPP SE.
- **Claims 2(a)–2(b):** Complainant was approved to resume mentoring duties. He remains eligible for QC duties, but S2 noted that various factors are considered when selecting an employee for QC.
- **Claim 2(c):** Complainant has not been treated differently from other examiners in Agency communications.
- **Timeliness:** The delay in issuing the final decision was minimal, so sanctions are not warranted.
