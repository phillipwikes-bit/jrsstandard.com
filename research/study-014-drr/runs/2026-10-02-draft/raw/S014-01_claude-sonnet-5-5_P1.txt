**Case Summary**

**Complainant:** GS-12 Senior Bankruptcy Specialist, Small Business/Self-Employed Division, Specialty Collection–Insolvency Office, Houston, TX.

**Complaint:** On September 17, 2021, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging disability discrimination (multiple mental and physical conditions), reprisal for prior EEO activity, and a hostile work environment. She raised five claims:
1. Denial of paid FMLA leave (Feb. 2021 to present).
2. Reassignment of a retired co-worker's complex cases to her (June 19, 2021).
3. Denial of eight hours of paid FMLA sick leave (Aug. 3, 2021).
4. Denial of her final appeal of a reasonable accommodation request (Aug. 24, 2021).
5. Denial of an official time request (Oct. 26, 2021).

**Findings by claim:**
- **Claims 1 & 3 (FMLA leave):** An EEOC AJ in a prior case (May 25, 2022) found that the AWOL and LWOP placements in Feb.–Mar. 2021 resulted from Complainant exhausting her paid leave and failing to report. Management initially treated her leave as unpaid FMLA based on its calculations. After Complainant requested an audit on Aug. 5, 2021, it showed she had some paid leave available, and management retroactively granted it, including for Aug. 3. Complainant conceded she ultimately received all leave she was entitled to. She identified no more favorably treated comparators.
- **Claim 2 (case reassignment):** The retired employee's cases were divided among three GS-12 specialists, including Complainant, by alphabetical order of case names. The work was appropriate for her grade, and a spreadsheet documented the assignments. No comparators were identified. The AJ in the prior case had found that she was not entitled to indefinite weather and safety leave and that the Agency met its Rehabilitation Act obligations (appeal pending).
- **Claim 4 (reasonable accommodation):** Complainant requested printing and mailing assistance and a reduced workload (May 6, 2021), and later reassignment (May 18, 2021). She conceded her impairment does not limit her ability to perform essential job duties.
  - The Agency denied the reduced workload and reassignment. No medical support was provided, lowering production standards is not required, her inventory of 25 cases was already unusually low, and reassignment is a last-resort accommodation.
  - The Agency approved a lightweight portable printer, and management mailed her correspondence in the meantime. Complainant cancelled the printer, and management continued to print and mail her letters.
  - The Director denied her appeal on Aug. 24, 2021.
- **Claim 5 (official time):** Complainant received about 18 hours of official time (Oct. 20–21 and Oct. 25) to complete her EEO affidavit. On Oct. 26, her supervisor denied additional time because she had two questions remaining and the Treasury Office of Civil Rights and Diversity had directed her to ask the investigator for an extension.

**Procedural history and outcome:** The Agency issued a report of investigation, and Complainant timely requested a hearing. The AJ issued summary judgment for the Agency. The AJ found the incidents were not severe or pervasive enough for a hostile work environment, and no evidence of discriminatory or retaliatory animus. The AJ also found no denial of reasonable accommodation under the Rehabilitation Act, and that the 18 hours of official time was objectively reasonable. The Agency issued a final order adopting the AJ's finding that Complainant failed to prove discrimination, reprisal, or a hostile work environment.
