# Case File Summary: Investigation Record and Procedural History

## Background

At the time of the events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant was a Mail Handler at the Agency's Incoming Mail Processing & Distribution Center in Linthicum, Maryland. The record indicates that action was taken against him effective September 4, 2015, for improper conduct, violation of the zero-tolerance policy, and violation of the joint statement on violence and behavior in the workplace. ## Facts Established in the Investigative Record

**The August 19, 2015 incident.** After a meeting with mail handlers about mistaken labels on containers, led by Complainant's first-line supervisor (Supervisor) and second-line supervisor (Manager), Complainant called Manager "sorry" and/or "a sorry ass." (Report of Investigation (ROI) at 19-24, 78, 85, 89-90.) When Manager instructed him to leave the work floor, Complainant did not do so immediately. (ROI at 21, 78.) He walked with Supervisor to the conference room only after Manager threatened to call the police. (ROI at 78.)

In the conference room, Complainant said of Manager, "He's a Bitch! He's a Bitch!" (ROI at 78-79, 84, 90.) On the way back to the work floor to retrieve his belongings, he yelled, "Manager is a Bitch—Bitch Ass!" (ROI at 79, 84, 90.) While being escorted out of the building, he punched a hole in a wall and said, "[Manager] is a Bitch—Fuck Him!" (ROI at 80, 90.)

**Complainant's account.** Complainant stated that on the way to the conference room he said only, "This is bullshit." (ROI at 23.) He did not remember exactly what he said to himself while being escorted out, but he acknowledged taking "a swipe" at a wall. (ROI at 23-24.)

**Agency response.** Management placed Complainant on Emergency Placement status after the confrontation. (ROI at 23-24.) On August 24, 2015, he attended a Pre-Disciplinary Interview about the incident with his union representative, Supervisor, and another supervisor (Supervisor 2). (ROI at 25, 79.) At that meeting, Supervisor told him the Agency would recommend his removal. (ROI at 79.) On August 31, 2015, Supervisor recommended removal to the Labor Manager. (ROI at 83-86.)

Supervisor issued the removal notice because Complainant had used profanity, failed to follow instructions, and punched a hole in a wall. She also noted his prior discipline, which included a 10-day suspension for Failure of Obedience to Orders and a 14-day suspension for violating the Workplace Violence Zero Tolerance Policy.

## Procedural History

When the investigation ended, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the report of investigation and notice of his right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant timely requested a hearing.

The Agency moved for a decision without a hearing on April 24, 2017. Complainant did not object, and the AJ granted the motion and issued a decision without a hearing on August 21, 2020.

## The AJ's Decision

The AJ found that the Agency had legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for the termination. Its actions rested on Complainant's confrontational, disrespectful, and violent workplace conduct, which violated the Agency's Zero Tolerance Policy.

The AJ also concluded that Complainant offered no evidence to rebut those reasons or to suggest that his race or gender played any role in the decision.

Complainant also asserted discrimination based on his size, in violation of the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA). The AJ found that he could not establish a prima facie case of genetic information discrimination because he failed to show:

1. that he is entitled to protection under the statute;
2. that he suffered an adverse employment action; and
3. that any adverse action supports an inference of discrimination based on his genetic information.

Viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to Complainant, the AJ held that he did not prove by a preponderance of the evidence that he was discriminated against on the basis of race, gender, or genetic information when he was issued a Notice of Removal.

## Final Agency Action

The Agency subsequently issued a final order adopting the AJ's finding that Complainant had not proven that the Agency subjected him to the alleged discrimination.
