# INVESTIGATION RECORD - CASE FILE DOCUMENTATION

## BACKGROUND AND COMPLAINANTS' EMPLOYMENT STATUS

The complainants in this matter were employed by the Agency as Emergency Management Specialists with responsibilities as Regional and National Tribal Government Liaisons. At the time the events giving rise to these complaints occurred, both complainants were performing their duties remotely—one from Pauma Valley, California, and the other from Thousand Palms, California. The complainants initiated their employment relationship with the Agency in October 2015, beginning at the Pay Band IV compensation level. On June 13, 2019, both complainants filed formal Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) complaints with the Agency, alleging discrimination and harassment based on their race (Native American), national origin (Native American), and in retaliation for prior protected EEO activity. Additionally, complainants initially alleged discrimination based on sex; however, this claim was subsequently withdrawn during the hearing process.

## ALLEGED DISCRIMINATORY COMPENSATION PRACTICES

### Initial Hiring and Salary Negotiations

The complainants alleged systematic discrimination in compensation dating from their initial hire. Specifically, Complainant 1 reported that during pre-employment salary negotiations, he was informed by the Agency that it was obligated only to offer him seventy percent of his previous wage, with the explicit statement that he could "take it or leave it." Complainant 1 further contended that non-Native employees were not subjected to comparable treatment during their respective hiring processes. Complainant 2 alleged that the Agency failed to properly account for changes in her prior salary history or her teaching background, instead offering only compensation commensurate with her then-current tribal salary.

### Disparate Pay Bands and Job Classification

Both complainants asserted that they performed substantially identical work as peer employees who were hired at the Pay Band V level, yet they themselves were hired at Pay Band IV and maintained at the lowest compensation tier. This disparity in classification, the complainants argued, directly resulted from discriminatory intent based on their Native American status.

### Failure to Address Documented Pay Disparities

The complainants documented what they characterized as management's deliberate inaction regarding known compensation inequities. In approximately April 2018, the Executive Director of the Public Safety Advocacy Directorate directed Complainants' supervisor to conduct a comprehensive review addressing wage and pay band disparities within the department. However, subsequent to the completion of this review, the Executive Director and a Senior Director allegedly took no remedial action and failed to acknowledge that such a review had even been commissioned.

This pattern of non-responsiveness continued when, during a May 31, 2018 telephone conversation, the Senior Director claimed to have no knowledge of the April 2018 review. The complainants later learned from their supervisor that the Senior Director was, in fact, "very well aware" of the review's existence and findings.

When complainants inquired on June 15, 2018, with the Deputy Chief Administrative Officer regarding hiring authorities and direct hiring procedures, they were informed that the Agency "does not have personnel policies and practices." This same official also failed to advise the complainants of their rights to pursue the EEO process to address their allegations of discriminatory compensation.

By July 2018, when Complainants' new supervisor inquired about the April 2018 review, the Executive Director and Senior Director denied any knowledge of having committed to such an investigation. Most significantly, in January 2019, the Executive Director acknowledged to Supervisor 2 that he had indeed received the review but asserted that he felt no obligation to act upon its findings. The Executive Director then instructed the supervisor to "tamper any escalation of HR issues for the tribal team." In a subsequent email, the Executive Director attempted to retreat from these instructions, claiming that the supervisor had misunderstood his directive.

## CAREER ADVANCEMENT RESTRICTIONS AND ORGANIZATIONAL CHANGES

The complainants alleged that the discriminatory establishment of their compensation at the Pay Band IV level effectively precluded them from applying to senior leadership positions, as the Agency made such positions available exclusively to Pay Band V employees. This created a structural barrier to advancement based on the initial discriminatory classification.

Additionally, complainants contended that in June 2018, they were "buried deeper" within the organizational structure through a reorganization process undertaken with the express intent of provoking them to resign and as a mechanism to "limit any HR escalations from the tribal team."

## DIFFERENTIAL TREATMENT IN WORK ASSIGNMENTS AND RESOURCES

From June 2018 onward, complainants were required to create separate strategic plans on an ongoing basis, while their non-Tribal team peers were not subjected to this requirement. Furthermore, complainants alleged that they were systematically denied equal access to resources necessary to perform their duties in the same manner as their non-Tribal counterparts.

Access to resources and non-federal shareholders was forced through a separate, discriminatory process. Complainants were required to engage with contracting staff and Tribal shareholders through an intermediary "gatekeeper." The Agency further restricted the categories of meetings in which complainants were permitted to participate and required that an Agency Contracting Officer's Representative be present during all daily engagements between contractors and complainants.

## HOSTILE WORK ENVIRONMENT AND CONTRACTOR CONDUCT

Between June 2018 and February 2019, complainants and other Agency officials repeatedly informed senior leadership that Tribal team members were subjected to hostile and discriminatory treatment by representatives of an Agency contractor. Despite these repeated notifications, senior leadership condoned the contractors' conduct and continued to place the Tribal team in situations involving hostile interactions with contracting officials.

## PUBLIC RECOGNITION OMISSION

On May 5, 2019, the Agency issued a public statement via social media thanking local, state, and federal workers for their dedication and service in connection with their work. However, the Agency failed to acknowledge or reference the Tribal team members, despite their substantial contributions to the same initiatives.

## ADMINISTRATIVE PROCEEDINGS

Following the completion of investigations, the complainants received copies of their investigative files and requested hearings before EEOC Administrative Judges. The complaints were assigned to different Administrative Judges within the Commission's Washington Field Office. Complainants' legal representative filed motions to consolidate the complaints for joint processing before each judge. The Agency opposed these consolidation motions. One Administrative Judge denied the consolidation motion while the other judge did not issue a ruling on the motion. Both judges subsequently issued separate summary judgment decisions concluding that no discrimination or unlawful retaliation had been established. The Agency implemented the Administrative Judges' decisions as its final action. Both complainants subsequently filed appeals challenging these determinations.
