**Case File Summary: EEO Complaint, Contract Specialist GS-11, Gallup, New Mexico**

**Procedural history**
- Complainant, a Contract Specialist GS-11 at the Agency's Gallup, New Mexico facility, filed a formal EEO complaint on August 25, 2015.
- She alleged discrimination based on race (Native American), national origin (Native American), and age, and reprisal for protected activity (the instant EEO complaint).
- By letter dated November 23, 2015, the Agency accepted the complaint for investigation.
- After the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ). She timely requested a hearing.
- Over her objections, the AJ granted the Agency's March 22, 2018 motion for a decision without a hearing. The AJ issued a summary judgment decision on September 23, 2020.
- The Agency then issued a final order adopting the AJ's finding that Complainant did not prove discrimination as alleged. Complainant appealed.

**Decision-maker and criteria applied**
The AJ decided the case on summary judgment, and the Agency adopted that decision in its final order. The AJ applied three standards.

*Prima facie case.* The AJ found Complainant failed to establish a prima facie case of discrimination and/or reprisal on claims 3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 13, 14, and 16.
- **Claim 8:** She did not apply for the position.
- **Claims 3, 4, 7, 11, 13, and 16:** She did not meet the minimum qualifications because she lacked the Level II and Level III FAC-C certifications.
- **Claim 14:** The selectee was of the same national origin and sex as Complainant and was older than she was. There was also no evidence that the selecting official knew of her EEO activity.

*Legitimate reasons and pretext.* The AJ found the Agency articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons and Complainant failed to establish pretext on claims 1, 2, 5, 6, 8, 19, 15, and 17-20.

*Harassment (claim 12).* The AJ noted Complainant did not claim any sexual aspect to the hug and found no evidence that her protected classes motivated it. The AJ also found that this single act was not severe or pervasive enough to create a hostile work environment.

**Responses considered on appeal**
- **Complainant (through her attorney):**
  - The Agency systemically denied promotions to qualified Native Americans.
  - **In her brief:** C1 decided her 17-hour class on Analyzing Contract Costs was insufficient for Level II certification, though another named Agency official approved it.
  - **In her brief:** C1 decided, without OPM approval, that future contracting jobs would require Level II certification.
  - She asserts these actions were taken to reduce hiring of Native Americans.
  - **Claim 10:** the Agency failed to articulate a legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason.
  - **Claim 12:** C1's hug could "support an inference that [C1] has at least implicit bias that Native American women are inferior, like children, for whom an embrace from her is a gift. This is quite a different attitude that the equal respect that is normal among professionals." (Complainant's Brief at 25.)
- **Agency:** It asks that its final order implementing the AJ's decision without a hearing, finding no discrimination, be affirmed.
