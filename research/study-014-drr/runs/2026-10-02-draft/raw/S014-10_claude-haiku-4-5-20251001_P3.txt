# INVESTIGATION CASE FILE RECORD

## COMPLAINANT EMPLOYMENT AND SUPERVISORY RELATIONSHIP

At the time of the events giving rise to this formal complaint, the Complainant was employed as a Registered Nurse assigned to the 4 East Medical-Surgical Unit at the Veterans Affairs Medical Center (VAMC) located in West Los Angeles, California. During the relevant period covered by this investigation, the Complainant's immediate supervisor and first-line manager was the Nurse Manager for the 4 East Medical-Surgical Unit, hereinafter referred to as "S1" in this investigative record.

## SUMMARY OF ALLEGATIONS

The Complainant raised fourteen distinct allegations against her employer and supervisor, which are detailed below. The Agency's Office of Resolution Management (ORM) initially issued a Notice of Partial Acceptance, which dismissed a portion of Claim 7 for failure to state a claim, while accepting the remaining allegations for investigation. The Agency ultimately accepted thirteen claims for formal investigation and analysis.

### Claim 1: Scheduling Dispute and Shift Modification Demand

The Complainant alleged that S1 demanded that she work twelve-hour shifts, which the Complainant refused to do. During the investigation, it was discovered that the original claim description was inaccurate. The Complainant later clarified in a supplemental affidavit that the event did not occur in May 2018 as initially stated, but rather took place in November or December 2017. The Complainant further clarified that she had not requested to work twelve-hour shifts; rather, S1 had allegedly demanded that the Complainant change her work schedule from eight-hour shifts to twelve-hour shifts. The ORM noted that Claim 1 constituted a timely raised independently actionable claim and was also incorporated into the broader harassment allegations.

### Claim 2: Unauthorized Disclosure of Confidential Medical Information

In May 2018, the Complainant alleged that S1 disclosed the Complainant's confidential medical information in violation of the Rehabilitation Act. Specifically, the Complainant claimed that S1, speaking loudly within earshot of coworkers, stated: "And you, you need to go somewhere else to have your surgery. If the VA is not taking care of you, you need to go to UCLA." This statement allegedly occurred as S1 was walking down the hallway between the reception station and the nurses' station, following the Complainant's presentation of leave documentation in S1's private office.

The Complainant identified two witnesses to this incident. Witness 1 allegedly heard S1 discussing the Complainant's surgery and asked if the Complainant was okay. Witness 13 reportedly observed S1 speaking loudly from the reception station and questioned why S1 was telling everyone about the Complainant's surgery. The Complainant contended that this public disclosure violated her rights under the Rehabilitation Act.

### Claim 3: Improper Access to Confidential Medical Records

During January 2019, the Complainant alleged that she was required to work with coworkers who had improperly and without authorization accessed her confidential medical records. The Complainant suspected that multiple employees had violated her privacy by reviewing her Veterans Affairs medical records without legitimate clinical justification.

In April 2019, the Complainant formally contacted the VAMC Privacy Office requesting a comprehensive printout of all individuals who had accessed her medical records over the preceding three years. Upon receiving the report, the Complainant reviewed the documentation and identified six coworkers whom she believed had accessed her medical records improperly. The report provided included four columns: employee names, dates and times of access, specific notes regarding the type of access (such as "CPRS Chart version 1, Appointment Management, Case No. Exam Edit, Register Patient, Clinical Scheduling"), and designations indicating "Yes" or "No." The Complainant marked six names with arrows and added handwritten notes indicating where each employee worked.

Subsequently, on June 18, 2019, the Complainant submitted an email to the Privacy Office identifying three specific nurses—Coworker 2, Coworker 3, and Coworker 4—as having inappropriately accessed her medical record. The Privacy Office investigation determined that Coworker 4 accessed the Complainant's medical records on June 15, 2018, and had no valid clinical reason for doing so. Coworker 4 subsequently stated that "everyone was talking about [Complainant's] surgery" and that she had witnessed another employee accessing the Complainant's medical record. The investigation further found that Coworker 3 had accessed the Complainant's medical records six times throughout 2018 and 2019. Coworker 3 stated she accessed the records because the Complainant had written her up on several occasions and she did not want to work on the same shift as the Complainant. The Privacy Investigation Report concluded that Coworker 3 accessed this information intentionally, despite it having no relevance to scheduling purposes or job-related duties.

On July 1, 2019, the Privacy Officer issued a letter to the Complainant confirming that two coworkers—identified only as Coworker 4 and one additional employee—had inappropriately accessed her medical record outside the scope of their duties and responsibilities. The letter noted that the other employees identified by the Complainant had accessed her records within their job responsibilities. Supervisory staff of the identified employees were notified, and both employees were recommended to immediately retake mandatory privacy and security training.

S1 submitted documentation confirming that, in response to the Complainant's April 9, 2019 email alleging unauthorized medical record access, S1 immediately initiated an inquiry with the Privacy Officer. The investigation confirmed that coworkers had indeed violated the Complainant's privacy rights. Documentation in the case file includes two disciplinary letters from the Acting Director: one dated September 4, 2019 (designated as "Employee 1"), upholding a proposed seven-calendar-day suspension for a Privacy Act Violation; and another dated September 5, 2019 (designated as "Employee 2"), mitigating a proposed seven-day suspension to a one-day suspension for a Privacy Act Violation. S1 provided evidence that two coworkers working with the Complainant received disciplinary action as a result of the privacy violations.

### Claim 4: Failure to Timely Resolve Sick Leave Issues

During January 2019, the Complainant alleged that management failed to resolve her sick leave issues in a timely manner. The Complainant noted that she had been scheduled to undergo surgery at the VAMC, and this surgery had been canceled on multiple occasions. Following each cancellation, the Complainant was required to redo her request for leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) and obtain S1's signature on the new form. After the fourth cancellation, occurring in November or December 2017, the Complainant had to present an additional leave form to S1 for signature.

### Claim 5: Derogatory Reference to Mental Health Status

On March 5, 2019, the Complainant alleged that a coworker referred to her as "a 50-year-old mental patient." This remark constituted alleged harassment based on the Complainant's disability status.

### Claim 6: Supervisory Support for Alleged Harasser

On May 1, 2019, the Complainant alleged that S1 told a coworker who was allegedly bullying and harassing the Complainant not to worry and that she "had her back." The Complainant contended that this statement constituted supervisory endorsement and encouragement of harassment directed at the Complainant.

### Claim 7: Accusations of Intimidating, Aggressive, and Harassing Conduct

On May 1, 2019, the Complainant was accused by a coworker—who the Complainant contended was actually bullying and harassing her—of being intimidating and aggressive and of engaging in harassing behavior. The Agency's ORM partially dismissed this claim for failure to state a claim but accepted the remaining portion as part of the overall harassment allegation.

### Claim 8: Accusation of Unauthorized Email Access

On May 3, 2019, the Complainant was accused of sending an email from another employee's email account. This allegation was investigated as part of the broader disciplinary actions taken against the Complainant.

### Claim 9: Demand to Sign Misconduct Form

On May 3, 2019, S1 requested that the Complainant sign a misconduct form in connection with the allegations raised in Claim 8.

### Claim 10: Intimidating Supervisory Conduct

On May 28, 2019, the Complainant alleged that S1 paced in front of the Complainant's work area, which the Complainant characterized as intimidating conduct.

### Claim 11: Accusation of Misconduct

Also on May 28, 2019, S1 accused the Complainant of engaging in misconduct. This accusation was made contemporaneously with S1's alleged pacing behavior described in Claim 10.

### Claim 12: Interference with Privacy Violation Investigation

On June 6, 2019, the Complainant alleged that an unnamed employee interfered with the Complainant's privacy violation investigation. The Complainant did not provide specific details regarding the nature of this alleged interference.

### Claim 13: Posted Article Regarding Mental Disability

On August 20, 2019, the Complainant discovered an article posted on the report table regarding nurses with mental disabilities. The Complainant believed this article had been directed at her personally, thereby constituting harassment based on her disability status.

### Claim 14: Pattern of Supervisory Mismanagement

On unspecified dates, the Complainant alleged that S1 "split the staff against each other" and failed to properly resolve issues that occurred on the unit floor. This allegation was characterized as part of a broader pattern of hostile work environment conduct.

## INVESTIGATIVE FINDINGS

### Analysis of Claim 1: Scheduling Dispute

The Agency's investigation established through undisputed evidence that the Complainant remained on her eight-hour tour of duty at all times. The Agency therefore found that Claim 1 was not supported by the record. Furthermore, even accepting the Complainant's allegation as true, the Agency found no indication of discriminatory motive behind any alleged demand to change her work schedule.

### Analysis of Claim 2: Unauthorized Medical Information Disclosure

Regarding the alleged public disclosure of the Complainant's surgical needs, S1 asserted that she had discussed the Complainant's surgery only in private to offer emotional support. S1 further argued that there was no evidence beyond the Complainant's subjective testimony that anyone actually overheard the statement made in the hallway.

The investigative record indicates that six coworkers were interviewed as part of the privacy investigation process. All six coworkers confirmed that S1 had not disclosed any information about the Complainant's surgery or any other confidential medical information to them. Instead, these coworkers stated they learned of the Complainant's surgery because the Complainant herself shared this information directly with them. The investigative record further documents that the Complainant acknowledged telling at least one coworker that she was going to have surgery, though it had been canceled.

The Agency concluded that the Complainant failed to demonstrate that S1 improperly disclosed her confidential medical information. The Agency further noted that even if it were to credit the Complainant's version of the incident and assume that others overheard the statement—which the Agency found no evidence to support—the vague reference allegedly made by S1 would not constitute a disclosure of confidential medical information.

### Analysis of Claim 3: Improper Medical Record Access

The Agency noted that, pursuant to established Equal Employment Opportunity Commission precedent, allegations that the Privacy Act has been violated are not within the purview of EEOC regulations absent a further allegation that the Privacy Act violation resulted from prohibited discrimination. The Agency determined that the Complainant failed to provide evidence of discriminatory animus based on her disability.

The privacy investigation established that the two coworkers who accessed the Complainant's medical records did so to determine when the Complainant would be out for surgery and, in one instance, because the coworker did not want to work during the same shifts as the Complainant. The Agency found no indication and no evidence of discriminatory motivation or wrongdoing beyond the Privacy Act violation itself. Although the investigation confirmed that privacy violations had occurred, the Agency concluded that these violations were not motivated by prohibited discrimination related to disability status.

### Analysis of Claim 12: Alleged Interference with Investigation

Regarding the alleged interference with the privacy violation investigation, the Agency stated there was no indication of any alleged "resistance" from the Complainant's coworkers during the Privacy Investigation process. The Agency noted that even if such resistance were accepted as true, there was no evidence that any such resistance was based on discriminatory animus.

The Agency emphasized that S1 had promptly reported the Complainant's concern regarding privacy violations to the Privacy Office. A comprehensive privacy investigation was subsequently conducted, and all employees identified by the Complainant as potentially accessing her medical records were interviewed. Two employees were identified as having improperly accessed the Complainant's records, and both were subsequently disciplined and ordered to retake privacy training. The Agency found no evidence that any failure to take additional or more severe disciplinary measures was motivated by discriminatory animus.

### Analysis of Hostile Work Environment Claim

The Agency conducted a comprehensive analysis of all harassment allegations raised by the Complainant. Even accepting all of the Complainant's allegations as true, the Agency concluded that she had presented no evidence that she was singled out for treatment based on her disability status.

The Agency noted that Witness 1, who had not been employed by the VAMC since 2018, provided only a vague statement alleging that S1 had told him the Complainant was having "mental issues." This statement was directly contradicted by S1's testimony. S1 indicated that she was unaware the Complainant had a disability. S1 noted that although she was aware the Complainant had taken bereavement leave following the death of a parent, to S1's knowledge, this leave was not related to any mental or other disability.

Witness 2's statement gave no indication that S1 harbored discriminatory animus against the Complainant based on disability status. The Agency noted that both witnesses expressed personal complaints about S1, rendering their statements less credible in the investigative context.

The Agency concluded that the Complainant failed to establish that she had been subjected to unlawful harassment based on her disability or other protected status.

## ADMINISTRATIVE PROCEDURES

Following the completion of the investigation, the Agency provided the Complainant with a copy of the full Report of Investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). The Complainant initially requested a hearing but subsequently withdrew that request. Consequently, the Agency issued a Final Decision pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b), adopting the investigative findings and providing the Complainant with the agency's formal determination on each claim.
