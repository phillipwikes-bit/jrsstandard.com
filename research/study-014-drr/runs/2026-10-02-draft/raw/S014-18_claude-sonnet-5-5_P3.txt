**Summary of Investigation Record**

**Background**

At the time of the events underlying this complaint, Complainant was employed as a Customs and Border Protection (CBP) Officer, GS-1895-12, at the Agency's El Paso Port of Entry (POE), El Paso Field Office, Office of Field Operations, in El Paso, Texas. On April 13, 2020, she filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency subjected her to a hostile work environment and discriminated against her based on sex (female), physical disability (cervical myelopathy and cervical fusion with limited neck movement), and age (56).

**Alleged Incidents**

Complainant identified the following fifteen incidents:

1. From September 8, 2019, to January 2020, the Supervisory CBP Officer (Supervisor 1) assigned Complainant regular cashier duties in addition to issuing I-94s, even though Complainant was on Light Duty.

2. In September 2019, Supervisor 1 told the Program Manager that she did not want to work with Complainant in Passport Control Secondary because Complainant was on Light Duty and had no weapon.

3. On September 13, 2019, Supervisor 1 told Complainant that she did not want to work with her and that Complainant was a liability because of her health issues.

4. From October 2019 to February 2020, on roughly 10 to 15 occasions, the Supervisor interrupted Complainant's conversations with other employees and told them to stop talking.

5. On December 24, 2019, Supervisor 1 assigned Complainant passport control duties despite her Light Duty status. When Complainant explained that she could help but could not perform regular duties for a full eight hours, the Supervisor replied, "it is what it is."

6. On December 24, 2019, in front of the public, Supervisor 1 yelled at Complainant to start issuing permits and said Complainant did not want to do her job. She then told members of the public they could file a complaint against Complainant for not attending to them.

7. On January 1 and 3, 2020, Supervisor 1 yelled at Complainant, accused her of conducting a pat down and processing a case without a weapon while on Light Duty, and accused her of abusing her light duty schedule.

8. On January 2, 2020, Supervisor 1 accused Complainant of making false statements in a memorandum.

9. On January 3, 2020, Supervisor 1 commented that Complainant did not want to work.

10. On January 3, 2020, after Supervisor 1 called the Chief about Complainant's schedule, Complainant's Light Duty shift was changed from 10:00 p.m.–6:00 a.m. to midnight–8:00 a.m. for two days, before she returned to full duty on her regular shift.

11. On January 6, 2020, Supervisor 1 yelled at Complainant over the phone, accusing her of working the wrong schedule and of not wanting to perform her duties.

12. On January 15 and 16, 2020, Supervisor 1 raised her tone and claimed Complainant did not want to do her job. She threatened Complainant with a verbal warning and reprimand for not taking over another officer's duties, and with a write-up for insubordination and disobeying a direct order.

13. On January 16, 2020, Complainant asked another Supervisory CBP Officer (Supervisor 2) to initial an assignment, and he said, "I'm not signing shit for you." Later, after Complainant refused to perform cashier duties, Supervisor 2 said he did not need a nonproductive, worthless person there and told her to go to the back of the office.

14. On January 31, 2020, Supervisor 1 approached Complainant in a threatening voice and demanded that she perform cashier duties even though three other officers were available. After Supervisor 1 called Supervisor 2, he reassigned Complainant to detention log officer duties, raised his voice, and demanded that she leave the work area and work from a different location. When she did not, he told her to go home on administrative leave. Complainant did not go home.

15. On February 6, 2020, Complainant received a Weingarten notice informing her that Supervisor 1 and Supervisor 2 had accused her of not wanting to work, disobedience, neglecting duties, and disobeying an order. She was asked to provide a written statement to the Chief.

**Procedural History and Agency Decision**

At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). At Complainant's request, the Agency instead issued a final decision pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b).

The Agency concluded that Complainant had not proven that it subjected her to discrimination as alleged. In particular, it found that she failed to show she was subjected to disparate treatment.
