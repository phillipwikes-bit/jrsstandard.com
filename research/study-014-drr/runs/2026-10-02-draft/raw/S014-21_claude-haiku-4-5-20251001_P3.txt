# INVESTIGATION CASE FILE REPORT

## CASE SUMMARY AND BACKGROUND

This case involves an Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) complaint filed by a former employee of the Department of Veterans Affairs, East Orange Veterans Affairs Medical Center, located in East Orange, New Jersey. At the time of the events that gave rise to this complaint, the Complainant held the position of Primary Care Physician at the Grade Level GS-15. The Complainant's employment relationship with the Agency culminated in her termination on January 22, 2021, following a recommendation from the Agency's Professional Standards Board. The Complainant filed her EEO complaint on August 2, 2021, challenging her termination and seeking relief based on allegations of employment discrimination. Subsequently, the Agency issued its final decision finding no discrimination, which the Complainant then appealed to the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission. This matter remains pending before the Commission.

## EMPLOYMENT POSITION AND ESSENTIAL DUTIES

The Complainant was appointed to her position as a Primary Care Physician at the East Orange Veterans Affairs Medical Center on March 3, 2019, subject to completion of a two-year probationary period. In her capacity as a Primary Care Physician, the Complainant's position entailed several essential job functions and responsibilities. These duties required her to conduct daily patient interviews and examinations, typically involving approximately twelve patients per day. Her clinical responsibilities encompassed both in-person, face-to-face patient visits and virtual patient consultations. Additionally, the Complainant was responsible for documenting all patient visits within the Agency's electronic medical record system, which required proficiency in computer usage and data entry. As part of her comprehensive patient care responsibilities, the Complainant was also required to order consultations, laboratory tests, and medications using the Agency's electronic medical record system. The performance of these duties was essential to the position and constituted the core responsibilities of a Primary Care Physician at the facility.

## MEDICAL CONDITIONS AND ACCOMMODATION REQUESTS

The Complainant's medical history relevant to this case began on July 12, 2019, when she sustained an initial injury to her shoulder. However, the record indicates that the Complainant did not immediately notify her supervisors of this injury or request reasonable accommodation at that time. Subsequently, in December 2019, the Complainant aggravated a prior injury to her right shoulder and notified her supervisors of this additional injury shortly after its occurrence. The Complainant attributed multiple functional limitations to her physical condition, including an inability to lift her right arm, inability to operate a computer mouse or keyboard, inability to write, and general limitations in fine motor manipulation of her right hand. As a result of these claimed limitations, the Complainant asserted that she was unable to perform necessary physical examinations on patients, unable to document clinical notes in the electronic medical record system, and unable to use a computer for extended periods.

The Complainant's immediate supervisor, hereinafter referred to as S1, became aware of the Complainant's right shoulder condition in December 2019. On February 25, 2020, the Complainant made an initial verbal request for reasonable accommodation and subsequently submitted a formal request under the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA). This formal accommodation request specified several modifications to her work duties, including no lifting restrictions, prohibition on prolonged upper extremity fine manipulation with the right arm and hand, reduction to part-time status with a maximum of twenty hours per week, restrictions on grabbing or grasping activities, and provision of assistance with computer-related tasks as needed.

## INTERACTIVE PROCESS AND ACCOMMODATION ATTEMPTS

Following her return to work on March 2, 2020, after a period of leave beginning in December 2019, the Complainant was granted work shift restrictions as she had requested. These restrictions permitted her to work four hours per day while taking four hours of Leave Without Pay (LWOP), continuing this schedule for five days per week. Upon her return to work, the Complainant was required to complete mandatory online training modules through the Agency's Talent Management System and to see between two and three patients by the end of that first week if operationally necessary. The Complainant, however, failed to see any patients during this initial return period and did not complete the required training modules.

A Medical Assistant employed at the facility documented that on March 4, 2020, the Complainant declined to see a patient who was already waiting in the clinic and who had been on the scheduling list for several months. When questioned about her refusal, the Complainant indicated that she could not see the patient due to her shoulder condition and stated that her shoulder was bothering her considerably, to the extent that she could barely hold a pen to write clinical notes.

On March 5, 2020, the Complainant's immediate supervisor, her second-level supervisor (hereinafter referred to as S2), and the Agency's Reasonable Accommodation Coordinator held a formal meeting to initiate the interactive process regarding the Complainant's accommodation request. During this meeting, the Complainant explicitly stated that she could not perform physical patient examinations, was unable to complete necessary patient paperwork, and was unable to utilize a computer for more than a few minutes at any given time. Given the Agency's concern that even part-time in-person work might exacerbate her medical condition, the Agency decided to grant the Complainant an alternative accommodation consisting of continued LWOP status to allow for rehabilitation of her injury over a specified six-week period.

The Complainant remained in an approved LWOP status from March 4, 2020, through August 10, 2020. Approximately two weeks following the March 5 meeting, the Complainant submitted a request for one hundred percent telework accommodations. In response, on March 25, 2020, S1 requested that the Complainant provide physician documentation addressing her ability to perform the essential functions of her position, specifically including documentation of her limitations with typing notes, placing orders and consults, sending laboratory letters, and completing paperwork and forms required for patient care.

During this period, the Agency was simultaneously responding to the COVID-19 pandemic, during which non-urgent medical appointments were cancelled, and telework arrangements for hospital physicians were implemented on an ad hoc basis, typically involving fifty percent telework with alternating weeks of in-person hospital work and remote teleworking from home. Concurrently, the Agency made substantial efforts to accommodate the Complainant's needs through various technological and ergonomic modifications. These accommodations included preparation and installation of dictation software on the Complainant's computer to minimize the necessity for typing; procurement and delivery of two ergonomic chairs, one for each of her examination rooms; shipment of ergonomic mice and keyboards to both her home and office locations; and approval for issuance of a laptop computer on March 3, 2020, though delivery was delayed due to equipment shortages related to the pandemic.

S1 subsequently submitted a second laptop request through the Computer and Electronic Accommodation (CAP) program, which operates within the Department of Defense, in an effort to expedite equipment delivery. However, the CAP program denied this request, citing insufficient medical documentation submitted during the Needs Assessment to support the necessity for a second laptop, as well as the absence of a signed telework agreement. On June 2, 2020, the Agency eventually secured a laptop for the Complainant; however, the Complainant never executed the required telework agreement, which would have addressed her teleworking arrangement for a temporary period of one to three months in light of her temporary disability status.

S1 indicated that the Agency could not accommodate the Complainant's request for unconditional one hundred percent telework for an extended period of at least one year because the Complainant had failed to provide adequate medical documentation to justify such an extended accommodation. Furthermore, S1 noted that the hospital was experiencing a significant shortage of physicians, that certain providers were absent due to illness from COVID-19, and that the Complainant's patient caseload was being managed by other physicians during her absence. Based on these considerations, the Agency offered the Complainant a telework agreement for a temporary period of one to three months, with the duration to be determined based on updates to her medical documentation and reassessment by her treating physician. The Complainant declined to accept the Agency's offered telework agreement.

## MEDICAL DOCUMENTATION AND ACCOMMODATION COORDINATOR ACTIONS

The Reasonable Accommodation Coordinator documented that the Complainant made several separate accommodation requests, including a formal request on April 21, 2020. In that request, the Complainant sought one hundred percent telework, a reduction to twenty hours per work week, and modified work assignments to be implemented either for a one-year period or on a permanent basis. The Reasonable Accommodation Coordinator stated, and the record confirms, that she made numerous requests of the Complainant, including several written emails in June 2020, requesting provision of medical documentation to support the Complainant's reasonable accommodation requests and to establish the existence and nature of her claimed disability.

The Coordinator specifically requested that the Complainant provide medical documentation demonstrating the effectiveness of telework and telemedicine accommodations in relation to her medical condition, as well as information establishing the expected duration of her medical condition, including whether her condition was temporary or permanent in nature. The Coordinator also requested that the Complainant provide authorization and consent for the Agency to contact her treating physician in order to gather necessary information and proceed with processing her accommodation request. Despite these repeated and documented requests, the Complainant failed to provide the requested medical documentation or authorization.

Consequently, on August 7, 2020, the Reasonable Accommodation Coordinator closed the Complainant's reasonable accommodation request file and formally notified the Complainant that the case would be reopened if she subsequently provided the requested documentation.

## MEDICAL RECORDS ANALYSIS

The record reflects that the Complainant provided the Agency with copies of Return to Work statements from her treating physicians. In a Return to Work statement dated January 13, 2020, the Complainant's first treating physician, hereinafter referred to as DR1, documented that the Complainant had been seen in his office on that date and could return to work after a follow-up examination scheduled for February 10, 2020. In a subsequent Return to Work statement dated February 10, 2020, DR1 documented that the Complainant had been examined on that date and was medically cleared to return to work on March 2, 2020.

Additionally, the record contains a medical statement from a second treating physician, hereinafter referred to as DR2, dated February 25, 2020. In that statement, DR2 documented that the Complainant was under his care for her right shoulder condition and recommended that she be assigned light duty and part-time work with restrictions on lifting. DR2 further recommended that the Complainant be provided assistance with computer-related tasks and stated that her status and readiness to return to full duty would be reevaluated in six weeks.

In a subsequent statement dated April 21, 2020, DR2 recommended that the Complainant be allowed to continue on light duty status, work from home utilizing a laptop computer, and provide telemedicine services to patients. DR2 indicated that the Complainant's status for return to full duty would be reevaluated on May 19, 2020.

In two separate medical statements, both dated May 19, 2020, DR2 documented that the Complainant had been evaluated on that date for her right shoulder condition. The physician noted that the Complainant continued to experience dexterity issues and recommended that she continue physical therapy to address shoulder exercises and functional limitations. DR2 stated that the Complainant was able to use a computer and mouse with appropriate ergonomic support and indicated that her status for return to normal work activities would be reevaluated in six weeks.

On July 15, 2020, the Complainant submitted a statement from DR2 dated June 22, 2020. In this statement, DR2 recommended that the Complainant be allowed to work from home via telemedicine services, with a maximum of twenty hours per week. The physician documented that the Complainant continued to experience pain with associated dexterity issues, including difficulties with gripping and right arm and hand manipulation, and reported pain and functional difficulties when performing activities such as gripping the steering wheel while driving. DR2 recommended continuation of physical therapy and noted that the Complainant was able to use a computer and mouse with ergonomic support. The physician indicated that the Complainant's status for return to normal work activities would be reevaluated in six weeks.

In a final statement dated July 30, 2020, DR2 reiterated the foregoing clinical findings and recommendations. Additionally, DR2 noted that he was recommending evaluation by a spine specialist for the Complainant's condition, stated that her return to work status would be reevaluated, and documented that the Complainant's "shoulder symptoms are well controlled and is discharged at this time" from his direct care.

## AWOL DETERMINATION AND TERMINATION

Following the closure of the Complainant's reasonable accommodation case by the Coordinator, S1 and S2 indicated that the Coordinator suggested it would be appropriate and reasonable to place the Complainant in Absent Without Leave (AWOL) status for her continued absence from work. Management considered the Coordinator's recommendation in conjunction with the fact that the Complainant had not reported for work for nearly eight months and had not provided the medical documentation that the Agency had repeatedly requested. Based on these factors, management determined that it was appropriate to place the Complainant in AWOL status beginning August 10, 2020.

The Agency documented that the Complainant had utilized all available annual leave and sick leave accruals during the period from December 20, 2019, through February 6, 2020. Subsequently, the Complainant was granted LWOP in increments as follows: eighty hours for February 7 through February 29, 2020; four hundred eighty hours for March 2 through March 26, 2020; and four hundred twenty-one hours for March 26 through August 7, 2020. The Complainant remained in AWOL status from August 10, 2020, until her subsequent termination on January 22, 2021.

## ADMINISTRATIVE JUDGE DECISION

The Administrative Judge (AJ) issued a decision finding no discrimination, concluding that the Complainant failed to establish that the Agency had wrongfully denied her request for reasonable accommodation. The AJ determined that the Complainant had failed to provide adequate medical documentation demonstrating her ability to perform the essential functions of her position, specifically her ability to perform physical examinations of patients.

The AJ found that although the Complainant alleged she suffered from limitations in using her right arm, she had provided limited documentation to support the existence of a disability as defined under applicable law. The AJ noted that all medical documentation provided indicated that her treating physician would conduct reassessments at future dates. Despite the Agency's repeated requests, the Complainant failed to provide any medical documentation establishing a formal diagnosis of her condition or its duration—specifically, whether her condition was temporary or permanent in nature. The AJ further found that the Complainant had failed to execute a release authorization permitting the Agency to obtain medical information from her treating physicians and had declined to provide even the investigator with a release authorizing disclosure of her medical information.

The AJ additionally found that the Complainant had not demonstrated that she would be able to work from home through telework, as the Complainant herself had admitted that she was unable to use a pen or computer without assistance. The AJ noted that the permanent accommodation the Complainant was requesting—telework—was one for which she was not qualified, as telework would require an increase in computer usage, and as a Primary Care Physician, the Complainant was required to perform physical examinations of patients even in a telework arrangement. The AJ found that the Complainant had stated she was unable to perform such physical examinations on March 4, 2020, and had never subsequently provided documentation demonstrating that she had regained the ability to perform this essential function. The AJ concluded that the Complainant had refused to engage with the Agency regarding these critical issues by failing to provide the requested medical documentation, thereby causing the Agency to close her accommodation case without issuing either a formal denial or approval of her request.

## APPEAL AND PENDING MATTER

The Agency did not issue a separate final order, and the Administrative Judge's decision effectively became the final action of the Agency. The Complainant has appealed from this final action, contesting the Agency's finding of no discrimination.
