**Background**

At the time of the events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant was employed as a Rural Carrier at the Agency's Acton Station in Granbury, Texas. Her complaint raised three claims:

1. From on or about January 5, 2018, to the present, and following the route count and adjustment, she has not been paid correctly for working her route.
2. From April 25, 2018, to the present, she has not been paid for overtime and/or holiday work.
3. On December 14, 2018, her request for package assistance on her route was denied.

**Procedural History**

At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant requested a hearing. The assigned AJ determined sua sponte that a hearing was not warranted and issued a Notice of Proposed Summary Judgment. Complainant did not object within the required timeframe, and the AJ issued a decision by summary judgment in the Agency's favor. The Agency then issued a final order fully implementing the AJ's finding that Complainant had not proven that the Agency discriminated against her as alleged. This appeal followed.

**The AJ's Findings**

*Claims 1 and 2.* Complainant alleged discrimination under GINA on these claims. The AJ concluded that she failed to establish a prima facie case because she alleged no facts relating to the basis of genetic information. The AJ reasoned that Complainant's affidavit responses did not address genetic information. She also did not allege that management was motivated by genetic information or otherwise used it as a basis for its actions.

*Claim 3.* The AJ found that on December 14, 2018, Complainant asked one of her supervisors, a Supervisor Customer Service, for package assistance, and the supervisor denied the request. The supervisor explained that Complainant had already received some help that day but was asking for more, even though only one additional parcel met the requirements for further assistance. According to the supervisor, that package was taken away from Complainant and so did not need to be delivered that day.

The supervisor also stated that the other packages for which Complainant sought help did not meet the applicable criteria. Her vehicle could accommodate the volume, she was not at risk of missing the mail truck, and the day's volume did not merit additional assistance. On this basis, the AJ concluded that the Agency had articulated legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons for denying the request on that day. The AJ also concluded that Complainant had not shown those reasons to be pretext for retaliation.

**Scope of the Overtime/Holiday Claim**

In her affidavit in the report of investigation, Complainant crossed out and initialed the word "holiday" in the definition of this claim. We therefore find that the claim does not include allegations regarding holiday hours.
