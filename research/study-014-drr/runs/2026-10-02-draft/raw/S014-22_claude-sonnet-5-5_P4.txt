# Case-File Summary

## Parties and setting
- **Complainant** was a Biologist, GS-0401-11, until promoted to GS-12 in May 2013. She worked at the Agency's National Institute on Aging, Laboratory of Neurogenetics ("Lab"), Neuromuscular Disease Research Section ("Section"), in Bethesda, Maryland.
- **Section Chief** (Ireland, Caucasian, male) was her first-line supervisor.
- **Lab Chief** (British, Caucasian, male) was her second-line supervisor.

## Complaint
On November 8, 2017, Complainant filed an EEO complaint, as amended. She alleged discrimination based on:
- race/color (African American/Black)
- national origin (Guyanese)
- sex (female)
- disability: diagnosed with bipolar disorder in 2009 but re-diagnosed with PTSD in 2017; generalized anxiety disorder; benign mitral valve prolapse; and fibroid tumors requiring surgical removal in December 2013
- reprisal for prior protected EEO activity (initiating EEO counseling on July 27, 2017)

## Claims as framed in the record
1. On or about June 13, 2017, Section Chief did not conduct a scheduled meeting with her.
2. On or about June 15, 2017, Section Chief told her he changed her assignment to making DNA plates, which she says is usually a role for summer students, not a biologist with a Ph.D.
3. After July 27, 2017, Agency management excluded her from a major project in her office/lab.
4. After July 27, 2017, Section Chief did not communicate with her on work assignments.
5. Agency management has not assigned her any projects, in retaliation for the informal complaint.
6. Section Chief subjected her to a hostile work environment through the following:
   - a. In 2009, he began subjecting her to inappropriate comments and unfair treatment.
   - b. In 2009, he directed lab employees to work on a project, made inappropriate comments, retracted them, and joked he should stop before an EEO complaint was filed against him.
   - c. In 2010, he told her findings would go to a top-tier journal without her writing because it had to be perfect.
   - d. In 2010, he purposely distracted her during an experiment and called her an "idiot."
   - e. In 2010, he required her to work on Christmas Day.
   - f. In 2010, he repeatedly said she wrote slowly and took forever to finish her Ph.D.
   - g. After assigning her to lead a task force in March 2011, he often excluded her from emails communicating assigned tasks to the group.
   - h. In April 2011, he told her no one would put up with her mental illness if she left.
   - i. On or about September 2011, after she said she met with Career Services, he questioned why she would talk to anyone about her career and said she should stay and be comfortable where she was.
   - j. On or about September 2011, he put her name last on a research article.
   - k. In 2011, he told her she would like the movie "The Help."
   - l. In 2012, he joked to her about being from Africa.
   - m. In 2013, he had an uncomfortable exchange with her about her ancestry.
   - n. In 2013, he advised post-baccalaureate students to check her work.
   - o. In 2013, he called her a "Diva," causing colleagues not to approach her.
   - p. In 2013 and ongoing, he precluded her from writing top-tier papers.
   - q. In 2016 and ongoing, he was not present for her 1:1 standing meetings.
   - r. In 2016 and ongoing, he warned her not to knock on his office door when closed.
   - s. On May 5, 2017, he excluded her from a project after telling her he would include her.
   - t. On or about May 12, 2017, he yelled "No wonder nothing you do ever works" in front of colleagues when she answered his questions on protocols.
7. In 2010, he told her findings would go to a top-tier journal without her writing because it had to be perfect (repeats 6c).
8. In 2013, Section Chief told a colleague she had a heart problem and disclosed his dissatisfaction with managing her medical issues.
9. In 2013 and 2017, Section Chief notified her multiple times to avoid working with a colleague because she had filed EEO complaints. This concerns two incidents, the second in April 2017.
10. Agency management subjected her to a hostile work environment when:
    - a. In June 2015, Section Chief told her to accept a lower-model computer than other lab employees.
    - b. In 2015, Section Chief acknowledged she deserved a promotion but delayed the paperwork until 2017, during a hiring freeze. She was notified of the freeze in February 2017 and told it was indefinite.
    - c. On May 12, 2017, he yelled loudly and laughed at her, saying "no wonder nothing you ever do works," within earshot of colleagues, when she answered his question about a protocol.
    - d. In late May or June 2017, he laughed with another colleague and a new trainee about the time he required her to work on Christmas Day.
    - e. He approached her and pressured her to disclose her political affiliation.
    - f. In late December 2013 or 2014, he expressed surprise that she wrote well.
11. On February 23, 2018, Section Chief failed to promote her.
12. In February 2018, the Agency assigned her to report to Lab Chief due to her EEO complaint.
13. In March 2018, Lab Chief re-positioned her photograph under a different lab group in the breakroom, although she was still a member of the Section.
14. On March 14, 2018, Section Chief did not communicate with her about an ongoing collaborative project. Because he did not speak directly to RMO-2 about it, she was unable to work on it.
15. On April 5, 2018, Section Chief told staff she no longer reported to him.
16. In April 2018, the Agency removed her name from the Lab staff listing on the National Institute on Aging website, which she says caused harm because her job involved working with outside agencies, individuals, and groups.
17. On April 23, 2018, all employees and collaborators copied Lab Chief on every email sent to her, including on minor issues.

## Statements and citations to the record
- **Claim 8 (heart condition):**
  - Complainant stated she had surgery to remove her fibroid tumors on December 18, 2013, which required a five-day hospital stay and six weeks of home recovery.
  - Her colleague stated that he had earlier been concerned about her well-being and told Section Chief, who advised that she had a heart condition.
  - Complainant believed she never disclosed a heart condition to Section Chief or Lab Chief. She stated Section Chief said this in "an effort to protect himself of [sic.] disclosing my medical information."
- **Claim 9 (date clarification):** The Agency's definition of the incident did not include the month in 2017. Complainant stated she recalled two incidents, the second in April 2017. *Report of Investigation (ROI), Ex. F-1 (Complainant's affidavit), Bates No. 123.*
- **Claim 10b (date clarification):** The Agency's definition did not include the month in 2017. Complainant stated she was notified of the hiring freeze in February 2017 and that it was indefinite. *ROI, Ex. F-1 (Complainant's affidavit), Bates No. 127.*
- **Claim 10f (date clarification):** The Agency's definition did not include dates, but the record reflects the incident occurred in December 2013 or early 2014. *ROI, Ex. F-1 (Complainant affidavit), Bates Nos. 120–121, 129.*

## Procedural history and decision
- The Agency investigated the complaint, as amended.
- After the AJ remanded the complaint to the Agency for a final decision, the Agency (the decision-maker) dismissed claims 6–11 for failure to timely initiate EEO counseling. That was the criterion applied to those claims.
- The Agency found no discrimination on the remaining claims.

## Appeal and responses considered
- Complainant filed her appeal before the Final Agency Decision (FAD) issued.
- She argues that more than 15 months had passed since the AJ's 60-day time limit with still no FAD.
- She submitted documentation showing that on May 20, 2020, she asked the EEO office about the status of the FAD.
- Citing caselaw, she argues this extraordinary tardiness warrants a default judgment against the Agency as a sanction.
- The Agency did not reply to the appeal.
