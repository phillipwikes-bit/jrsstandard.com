**Case Summary**

**Parties/Background:** Complainant, a Mail Processing Clerk at the Agency's Atlanta Processing and Distribution Center, had a prior EEO complaint (filed September 2018) pending before an EEOC AJ. She reports PTSD and depression stemming from a September 2019 sexual harassment/assault by a co-worker (the subject of the prior complaint), along with back, foot, and knee conditions limiting standing, walking, stairs, bending, stooping, and lifting. Her doctor stated she cannot work in the same facility as the co-worker.

**Key Facts (per Complainant):**
- After the September 2019 incident, she took FMLA leave. She was cleared in April 2020 to return at a different facility, but the harasser was still there.
- She injured her knee at work in June 2020 and submitted documentation in July 2020, with no response. The Reasonable Accommodation Committee requested documents she says she had already provided, and it addressed only her physical restrictions, not the PTSD-related need for relocation.
- In July 2021 she received a modified assignment at a different location. She went on COVID leave on August 16, 2021, was hospitalized for sepsis, and was cleared to return September 21, 2021. Management has since said no work is available within her restrictions.
- She has been on LWOP since November 21, 2021.
- In January 2022 her bid was denied and the position went to a less senior employee, and she was marked "ineligible" without explanation.

**EEO Complaint (filed May 13, 2022):** Alleged discrimination based on race, sex, color, disability, and reprisal under Title VII and Section 501 of the Rehabilitation Act. The accepted claims are:
1. Failure to accommodate since November 10, 2021.
2. Being charged LWOP since November 10, 2021.
3. Denial of a bid position on January 24, 2022.

**Dismissed Claims:** The Agency dismissed the following:
- Failure to accommodate from April 2020 to November 9, 2021, as untimely under 29 C.F.R. §1614.107(a)(1). The AJ in her prior case had denied her motion to amend and tolled the counseling time limits from November 11, 2021.
- Improper OWCP leave coding and denial of insurance benefits, as collateral attacks on the workers' compensation process.

**Agency Position:** The Supervisor of Distribution Operations stated the bid was denied because Complainant provided no updated medical documentation showing she could fully perform the duties within 6 months. The Agency's December 6, 2022 final decision (29 C.F.R. §1614.110(b)) found that she did not submit the required medical documentation and did not show pretext, and concluded she was not subjected to discrimination. She appealed.

**Record Gaps Noted:**
- No explanation or documentation of her new position or why relocation away from the harasser took about a year.
- No statement from any Agency official that any effort was made to accommodate her restrictions from September 2021 onward, or that such an effort failed.
- No evidence that she was asked for the 6-month medical documentation or told it was needed. The Collective Bargaining Agreement allows management to request such certification.
- The record text is incomplete on the dismissed claims. The sentence about the Agency's treatment of the OWCP-coding and insurance claims is cut off.
