# CASE FILE SUMMARY

## COMPLAINANT AND POSITION
Complainant worked as a Supervisory Transportation Security Officer, SV-1802-G Band, at Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport in Atlanta, Georgia.

## COMPLAINT FILING DATE
February 2, 2021

## ALLEGATIONS
Complainant alleged discrimination and hostile work environment on the bases of sex (male) and in reprisal for prior protected EEO activity based on four claims:

**Claim 1:** On November 24, 2020, a co-worker (CW1) claimed to do Voodoo and placed a flower on Complainant's body.

**Claim 2:** Sometime after November 24, 2020, management failed to address Complainant's concerns regarding the November 24, 2020 incident with CW1 and prevented Complainant from attending management meetings and scheduling operational meetings.

**Claim 3:** On December 30, 2020, a manager (Manager) paced back and forth, stood over Complainant, and blocked the door when Complainant tried to leave.

**Claim 4:** Manager demanded Complainant answer questions about his allegations against CW1 but refused to show Complainant his authorization to investigate, offered to show the memorandum on his computer but "would click off the screen to prevent [Complainant's] view of it," and would not allow Complainant to leave without answering questions.

## COMPLAINANT'S SUPPORTING ALLEGATIONS
Regarding Claims 1 and 2, Complainant alleged that he asked CW1 to step back and stay away from him, but CW1 would not, and that CW1 backed him into a corner. Complainant complained to Agency management on November 24 and 28, 2020, with no action taken.

Complainant testified that he believed the entire sequence of events revealed a discriminatory pattern because CW1 and other women are not subjected to discipline whereas men are treated more harshly. Complainant stated that "[a] fact finding inquiry was conducted, but this did not address [his] concerns, and did not prevent or lessen the acts of discrimination and retaliation, including subjecting [him] to a hostile work environment." Complainant criticized the investigation as biased and artificial and suggested that security footage would have corroborated his claims.

## INVESTIGATION APPOINTMENT AND PROCEDURE

**December 21, 2020:** The Interim Federal Security Director (Interim Director) appointed Manager to investigate Complainant's claims by memorandum.

**Manager's Response:** Manager denied each of Complainant's allegations against him, including Complainant's claim that he was excluded from meetings.

**December 31, 2020:** Complainant emailed Manager and objected to Manager's role in investigating Complainant's claims because Manager was part of Complainant's management chain.

**Subsequent Action:** Complainant emailed the Interim Director to similar effect. The Interim Director forwarded Complainant's concerns to the Federal Security Director (Director).

**January 5, 2021:** Director appointed a Transportation Security Inspector (Inspector) to continue the investigation into Complainant's allegations.

**Complainant's Agreement:** Complainant agreed to be interviewed by the Inspector.

## FACT-FINDING INVESTIGATION

**January 22, 2021:** Inspector issued a fact-finding report in which he could not substantiate Complainant's claims.

**Investigative Process:** Inspector interviewed Complainant, CW1, and several other employees who worked in the same area with both Complainant and CW1.

**Findings Regarding the Contentious Conversation:** The Inspector acknowledged that Complainant and CW1 had a "contentious conversation" but that the details were in dispute.

- **Complainant's Version:** Alleged that CW1 asked to speak with him privately and backed her into a corner.
- **CW1's Version:** Responded that Complainant initiated the conversation and questioned CW1 about her relationship with another employee, which upset her.
- **Coworkers' Statements:** Confirmed that a conversation took place but could not confirm the substance of the conversation.

**Inspector's Reasoning:** In failing to substantiate Complainant's allegations, the Inspector noted that most of the evidence he discovered was "conjecture."

**CW1's Affidavit:** CW1 provided an affidavit in which she denied Complainant's version of the events and provided testimony consistent with the statement she provided to the Inspector.

**Coworkers' Statements at Conclusion:** The record contains statements from Complainant's coworkers who stated as they did in the investigation. Specifically, they claimed that Complainant and CW1 had a contentious conversation but they could not hear what was said or see what was done.

## EEOC REMAND

The Agency initially dismissed the complaint for failure to state a claim, but in **Norman S. v. Dep't of Homeland Sec., EEOC [number removed] (July 22, 2021)**, the Commission reversed the Agency's decision and remanded the matter for further processing.

## AGENCY FINAL DECISION

At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of his right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). In accordance with Complainant's request, the Agency issued a final decision pursuant to **29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b)**.

**Decision-Maker:** The Agency

**Criteria Applied:** 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b)

**Decision:** The Agency found that Complainant was not subjected to discrimination or reprisal as alleged.
