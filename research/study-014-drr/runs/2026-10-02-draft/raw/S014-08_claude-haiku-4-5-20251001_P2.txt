# FINDINGS

## Overview of Allegations

Complainant, a Counseling Psychologist/Licensed Marriage and Family Therapist employed at the Agency's Health Clinic in Baumholder, Germany, filed a discrimination and harassment complaint alleging a pattern of mistreatment from September 2014 through January 2017. The complainant alleged that management subjected her to derogatory comments, undermined her authority, failed to provide training, deliberately rescheduled her clients, excluded her from professional activities, and issued an inaccurate performance evaluation.

## Factual Findings

### Training Denial Claims

Complainant alleged she was denied professional development opportunities. The record shows that:

- Complainant requested funding for a non-military professional development session on suicide and military personnel. The Commander stated he offered Complainant time to participate but could not provide funding, asserting he did not deny training opportunities.
- Complainant also requested attendance at Suicide Prevention training in Vicenza, Italy. The Lieutenant approved her request but noted insufficient funds and that the training was ultimately cancelled.

**Finding:** The evidence does not support a denial of training opportunities. Funding constraints and program cancellations, rather than discriminatory intent, explain the limitations on professional development.

### Client Reassignment Claims

Complainant alleged that employees rescheduled her clients without authorization and that clients requesting her by name were told they could not see her. Additionally, she alleged that upon returning to work on December 21, 2016, two clients were taken from her schedule.

The record indicates:

- The Social Services Assistant noted that Complainant had previously stated she only worked with Marriage and Family counseling patients, which led to some transfers and limited her patient pool.
- The Nurse Practitioner confirmed this limitation in her specialty.
- The Lieutenant Colonel investigated situations involving client transfers, finding that at least one transfer was unintentional, and the client was switched back.
- Regarding the December 21, 2016 reassignments, the Lieutenant Colonel, Colonel, and Chief all attested they were unaware of any issues on that date.

**Finding:** While some client reassignments occurred, the evidence indicates they resulted from Complainant's stated specialty limitations rather than discriminatory intent.

### October 5, 2016 Staff Meeting

Complainant alleged that during a staff meeting, the Captain made decisions about her clients without her input. The investigation revealed:

- The meeting was a standard High-Risk meeting where various providers coordinated appropriate patient care.
- The Captain discussed care plan concerns regarding two of Complainant's patients, a practice conducted with every provider.
- The Social Services Assistant confirmed that High-Risk meetings allowed all providers to offer perspectives, though no one was directing how others should perform their jobs.
- The Captain denied making unilateral changes to Complainant's cases.

**Finding:** The October 5 meeting was a standard clinical coordination meeting conducted with all providers, not an action targeted at Complainant.

### October 12, 2016 Reassignment Meeting

Complainant alleged that during a meeting with the Lieutenant Colonel and Lieutenant to discuss her reassignment request, she felt attacked based on their tone of voice, which she considered disrespectful.

The record shows:

- Complainant repeatedly alleged the environment was hostile but failed to provide specific examples when asked.
- Both the Lieutenant Colonel and Lieutenant denied being aggressive and offered to have a neutral third party present.
- Complainant characterized their demeanor as disrespectful based primarily on tone of voice.

**Finding:** The record does not support a finding of hostile or aggressive conduct by management during this meeting. The officers' offer of a neutral party demonstrates responsiveness to Complainant's concerns.

### November 12, 2016 Performance Evaluation

Complainant alleged that her performance evaluation inaccurately and untruthfully characterized her work performance. Investigation findings indicate:

- The evaluation was based on 15 standard metrics.
- Complainant received eleven "Exceed" ratings and four "Met" ratings.
- The four "Met" ratings pertained to collaboration with staff, specifically noting lack of collaboration from May through October 2016.
- The Lieutenant Colonel provided specific examples to support his ratings.
- Complainant ultimately received an overall "Excellent" rating.
- The Lieutenant, as second-line rater, agreed with the evaluation.

**Finding:** The evaluation was based on documented performance metrics and specific examples. The overall "Excellent" rating contradicts any claim that the evaluation was inaccurate or punitive.

### December 21, 2016 Exclusion from Coverage

Complainant alleged that despite a shortage of providers due to holidays, staff refused to engage her to help cover cases after she returned from sick leave on December 21, 2016.

**Finding:** The record provides no evidence that Complainant was specifically excluded from coverage opportunities. No investigation findings directly address this allegation.

### NCOIC Introduction of New Staff Member

Complainant alleged that the NCOIC introduced a new staff member to all providers and staff but deliberately omitted introducing him to Complainant despite their proximity outside her office.

The investigation revealed:

- Complainant acknowledged she did not leave her office to introduce herself.
- Complainant did not inform the NCOIC or anyone else about the matter.
- The NCOIC was reassigned at the time of contact and did not respond to investigative inquiries.

**Finding:** This allegation lacks factual support. Complainant's failure to take independent action to meet the new staff member, combined with her lack of communication to management, does not establish discriminatory conduct.

### IG Complaint and Legal Counsel Coordination

Complainant alleged that after contacting the IG office and arranging for her legal counsel to visit in January 2017, the Sergeant First Class failed to respond to facilitate a meeting.

The record indicates:

- Complainant was contacted on December 6, 2016, regarding her IG complaint.
- She indicated her counsel would be in Germany the week of January 9, 2017.
- The IG Office policy is to abstain from involvement when the Agency is taking action.
- The IG report dated November 15, 2016, found no evidence to support allegations of harassment or discrimination.

**Finding:** The IG Office's policy to refrain from involvement when the Agency is investigating explains the lack of coordination, not discriminatory intent.

### IG Report Findings

The November 15, 2016 IG report, based on Complainant's October 5, 7, and 13, 2016 complaints, concluded:

- There was no evidence to support Complainant's allegations of harassment or discrimination.
- Complainant created a disruptive environment within the Baumholder Behavioral Health Clinic.
- The IG recommended removing Complainant from the clinic due to contentious interactions with staff that impacted patient care and clinic morale.
- Complainant violated German law by unlawfully recording a conversation without the other party's consent.

**Finding:** An independent investigative body found no substantiation for discrimination or harassment claims.

### January 27, 2017 Developmental Counseling

Complainant alleged she received developmental counseling regarding her temporary reassignment in a manner that suggested negative intent.

The Chief attested that this was standard counseling with no negative connotation and was consistent with protocol for temporary reassignments.

**Finding:** The counseling was procedurally standard and not indicative of discriminatory intent.

## Cumulative Conduct Analysis

Complainant characterized her experience as part of a pattern of mistreatment intended to undermine her authority and isolate her professionally. However, when examined individually and collectively, the alleged incidents either:

1. Reflect standard clinical or management practices applied to all providers;
2. Result from documented performance or conduct issues;
3. Lack factual support in the investigative record;
4. Are contradicted by management affidavits or documented evidence; or
5. Result from Complainant's own choices or omissions.

## Administrative Grievance History

Complainant filed an Administrative Grievance on November 28, 2014, raising many claims included in the present complaint. On January 30, 2015, she was granted her requested remedy of formal mediation to improve the work environment, and the grievance was closed. The present complaint, filed approximately two years later, revisits substantially similar allegations.

## EEO Investigative Conclusion

The Administrative Judge determined that:

- The investigative record was adequately developed.
- After making all reasonable inferences in favor of Complainant, there were no material facts in dispute.
- The Agency provided non-discriminatory reasons for all alleged adverse actions.
- Complainant failed to demonstrate pretext for discrimination.
- Complainant failed to meet the severe and pervasive standard required for a hostile work environment claim.
- Complainant's complaint reflected personal grievances and dissatisfaction with coworkers and management rather than unlawful discrimination or harassment.

The Administrative Judge granted the Agency's Motion for Decision Without a Hearing on January 31, 2022.
