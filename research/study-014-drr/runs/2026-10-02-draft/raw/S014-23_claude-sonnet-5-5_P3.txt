# Investigation Record Summary

## Background and Procedural History

At the time of the events giving rise to this complaint, Complainant was a probationary Credentials Assistant, GS-0303-07, at the Agency's Naval Medical Center in Portsmouth, Virginia. On November 8, 2018, she filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against her based on race (African-American) when it terminated her from her probationary position on August 2, 2018. At the close of the investigation, the Agency gave Complainant a copy of the report of investigation and notice of her right to request a hearing before an EEOC Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant requested a hearing. The AJ held the hearing and then issued a decision in favor of the Agency.

## The AJ's Treatment of Complainant's Direct-Evidence Theory

The AJ first determined that Complainant's theory of discrimination based on direct evidence was unreliable, and found that neither Complainant nor her supporting witness was credible. At the hearing, Complainant testified that her second-level supervisor (S2), a Department Head, GS-0301-12, "made the decision to terminate her after learning that her 'significant other' was [W]hite." She stated that S2 became aware in May 2018 of Complainant's relationship with a White man, and that Complainant's relationship with S2 changed afterward. She also testified that her first-level supervisor (S1), a Professional Affairs Coordinator (Assistant Department Head), GS-0301-11, "had warned [Complainant] not to let [S2] know she was dating a [W]hite man."

To corroborate her theory, Complainant presented a key witness, a coworker (CW), Program Analyst, GS-0303-07, who "testified that [S2] openly stated that [S2's] husband was extremely biased against interracial relationships, and that she was surprised Complainant was dating a [W]hite man. [CW] claims she heard [S2] make other race-related remarks." Both Complainant and CW testified that S1 had warned them about S2's prejudice.

The AJ found this testimony lacked credibility "due to the glaring omission of any theory about Complainant's interracial relationship in her complaint, EEO counseling records, or her affidavit [during the investigation]." The AJ reasoned that "[t]his type of evidence, if credible, would not have come up for the first time some three years after her termination." During EEO counseling and in her affidavit, Complainant had instead explained that she believed her termination stemmed from events at a work conference in Leesburg, Virginia, where S2 allegedly engaged in inappropriate behavior, and that S2 "did not trust [her] to not share what happened." Complainant also indicated that her termination was related to S2's belief that Complainant had filed a grievance against her, although another employee had filed it. The AJ noted that these earlier theories "have no apparent connection to her race." On cross-examination by Agency counsel about her failure to raise the theory of S2's prejudice against interracial couples earlier, Complainant offered no explanation.

The AJ similarly found that any reference to S2's prejudice against interracial relationships was "conspicuously missing from [CW's] affidavit" submitted during the investigation. That affidavit referred to the events at the Leesburg training and stated CW's belief that "Complainant was discriminated against because she was an African-American with more experience than management." CW also stated that S1 had made racially inappropriate remarks, but the affidavit said nothing about Complainant's partner being White or S2's opposition to the relationship. At the hearing, S1 testified and denied making any comments about S2 being racist.

## The Agency's Articulated Reason for Termination

The AJ found that the Agency articulated a legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason for the termination. According to the Notice of Termination, Complainant failed to "follow the privileging/credentialing process when [she] misrepresented to [her] leadership that a provider had specifically requested privileges when in fact [Complainant] made the request. According to the provider, [Complainant was] the one who tried to convince him to apply for privileges he did not previously hold."

The incident involved a credentialing/privileges application that Complainant helped process. The provider selected incorrect privileges on the application, based on Complainant's mistaken belief about the proper protocol for credentialing contract providers. Complainant's guidance, which she acknowledged at the time was inaccurate, delayed the application and prevented the provider from receiving the credentials he needed to practice within the Agency until the day his then-current privileges were set to expire. The provider then contacted Complainant's supervisors "to express frustration with the delay in his application . . . [because Complainant] had provided him with incorrect guidance."

When her supervisors asked about the incident, Complainant told S1 that, before her discussion with the provider, she had received an application from him in which he had improperly requested privileges he did not previously hold, and that her mistaken guidance was based on that earlier application. After S2 obtained a written statement from the provider, S2 concluded that Complainant had misrepresented what happened, which formed part of the basis for the termination.

## Credibility Findings and Pretext

The provider testified at the hearing, and the AJ found him credible when he stated that "he and Complainant discussed the [privileges] application before he submitted it" and that he submitted it based on her instructions. The AJ found Complainant "not credible in her testimony that she never spoke to [the provider] prior to him submitting the [first] application." The AJ therefore concluded that "Complainant has failed to prove pretext because there is no evidence that" the similarly situated employee Complainant identified "was involved in a similar episode, or that she was dishonest with her managers." The AJ found Complainant's testimony "simply unreliable" and observed that her "credibility is doubly important in this case because her lack of candor was part of why she was terminated." The AJ accordingly found that the Agency had not discriminated against Complainant as alleged.

The Notice of Termination also stated that Complainant "failed to initiate the renewal privileging/credentialing process within 90 days as required." The AJ found that this was untrue, and that a similarly situated employee had several renewals nearing expiration with fewer than 90 days remaining but was not terminated. The AJ stated that "[i]f this were the only reason for Complainant's termination, she would be able to demonstrate pretext. But it was not." The unsupported renewal rationale therefore did not establish pretext by itself, because the misrepresentation ground independently supported the decision.

## Final Agency Action and Appeal

The Agency did not issue a final order within forty days of receiving the AJ's decision. Accordingly, the AJ's decision finding that Complainant failed to prove the Agency subjected her to discrimination as alleged became the Agency's final action under 29 C.F.R. § 1614.109(i). The instant appeal followed.
