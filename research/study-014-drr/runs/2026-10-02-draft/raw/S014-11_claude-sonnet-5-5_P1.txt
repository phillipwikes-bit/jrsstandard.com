**Case Summary**

**Parties and Background:** Two Complainants worked remotely from California (Pauma Valley and Thousand Palms) as Emergency Management Specialists (Regional and National Tribal Government Liaisons) at the Agency's First Responder Network Authority. Both were hired in October 2015 at Pay Band IV.

**Complaints:** Beginning June 13, 2019, each Complainant filed a formal EEO complaint alleging discrimination and harassment based on race (Native American), national origin (Native American), and sex, as well as reprisal for prior protected EEO activity. The sex-discrimination claims were later withdrawn during the hearing process.

**Claims:**
1. **Discriminatory pay (since hire):**
   - Complainant 1 was told during salary negotiations that the Agency need only offer 70% of his prior wages ("take it or leave it"), and he alleges non-Natives were not treated this way. Complainant 2 alleges the Agency offered a salary based only on her current tribal salary and ignored her prior salary and teaching wages.
   - Complainants did the same work as Pay Band V peers but were hired at Band IV and paid at the lowest level.
   - Management took no remedial action after an April 2018 review of wage and pay band disparities. Specifically, the Executive Director and Senior Director allegedly denied or ignored the review, and the Deputy Chief Administrative Officer allegedly gave no guidance on hiring authorities or EEO rights. In January 2019, the Executive Director allegedly told Supervisor 2 to "tamper any escalation" of HR issues for the tribal team, then retracted the instruction. (Subparts 1(c)–(g) were treated as falling under the "no remedial action" allegation in the AJ's Case Management Order.)
2. **Barred from senior leadership positions:** Those positions were open only to Pay Band V employees.
3. **June 2018 reorganization:** Complainants were allegedly "buried deeper" in the organization to provoke them to quit and limit HR escalations.
4. **Strategic plans:** Since at least June 2018, only the Tribal team was required to create separate strategic plans.
5. **Hostile contractor treatment:** From June 2018 to February 2019, senior leadership allegedly condoned hostile and discriminatory treatment of the Tribal team by contractor officials.
6. **Unequal access to resources:** Complainants allegedly had to use a separate process, work through a "gatekeeper," face limits on the meetings they could attend, and have a Contracting Officer's Representative present at daily contractor engagements.
7. **May 5, 2019 tweet:** The Agency thanked local, state, and federal workers but did not mention the Tribal team.

**Procedural History:** After the investigations, Complainants received the investigative files and requested EEOC hearings. The complaints were assigned to different AJs in the Washington Field Office. Their shared attorney moved to consolidate the complaints, and the Agency opposed. One AJ denied the motion, and the other did not address it. The AJs separately issued summary judgment decisions finding no discrimination or unlawful retaliation. The Agency's final actions implemented those decisions, and both Complainants have appealed.
