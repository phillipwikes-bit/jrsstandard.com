**Case Summary: EEO Complaint, City Carrier Technician, Village Station, New York, NY**

**Claims:**
1. Since June 26, 2016, management assigned Complainant work outside her medical restrictions and failed to pay her properly or on time.
2. Her clock rings were deleted on October 4, 2016, and other dates.
3. Her requests to be detailed as a 204-B acting supervisor were not granted.

(The Agency dismissed an additional claim for failure to state a claim. Complainant did not challenge the dismissal, and the Commission found no basis to disturb it.)

**Background:**
- Complainant has back and lumbar disc conditions and a repaired hernia, which she attributes to a May 2014 on-the-job injury. Her restrictions are extensive, including limits on sitting, walking, and standing, and no bending, stooping, twisting, pushing, pulling, or lifting. She also needs 15-minute breaks every hour.
- After an extended leave, she was offered a limited-duty assignment at Lenox Hill South Station. She would shuttle carriers by van and deliver express mail under five pounds. She signed the offer on July 1, 2016, and began about July 18, 2016. On paper it met her restrictions. She said she would try it, but she later claimed it exceeded them. She said she walked an hour on August 29, 2016, and was not permitted to use the van, which would have required stooping and twisting.
- She declined a lobby attendant position because she believed it exceeded her restrictions.
- The Manager said he knew of her condition only through the job offer and had no medical documentation.

**Pay and clock-ring issues:**
- Her clock rings for July 18–28, 2016, were deleted, and her time was coded as full workers' compensation.
- Agency explanations:
  - She had just transferred facilities and returned from full-time workers' compensation, so the receiving supervisor may not have known she was back at work.
  - Her leave code was incorrect.
  - Manual timecards were lost.
  - The Agency called the payment failures plain error.
- Complainant cited the Manager's poor organization and the lack of a disk for automatic time logging.
- She filed a grievance on September 14, 2016.

**204-B detail:**
- Around November 14, 2016, the Manager did not offer her a 204-B detail. He said he needed no acting supervisors and did not see the punctuality and reliability the role requires. Complainant said she was told to apply for a city-wide position, which would take time.

**Procedural history and AJ decision:**
- Complainant requested a hearing. The AJ granted summary judgment for the Agency, finding no discrimination or reprisal.
- **Reasonable accommodation:** The modified assignment was within her documented restrictions, she declined the lobby attendant offer, and there was no evidence she was forced to work outside her restrictions. Her restrictions appeared so severe that she could not perform her job with or without accommodation.
- **Pay and clock rings:** The Agency gave legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons (the facility transfer, the return from workers' compensation, lost manual timecards, and the Manager's poor organization).
- **204-B detail:** The AJ found Complainant wanted to be promoted on the spot rather than apply, and she did not show discriminatory or retaliatory animus.
- **Pretext:** Complainant did not show that the Agency's reasons were pretextual.
- The Agency issued a final order fully adopting the AJ's decision, and Complainant appealed.
