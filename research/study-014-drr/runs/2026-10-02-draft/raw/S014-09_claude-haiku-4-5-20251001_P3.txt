# INVESTIGATION RECORD - CASE FILE DOCUMENTATION

## CASE BACKGROUND AND PARTIES

This investigation record documents formal Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) complaints filed by two individuals (hereinafter "Complainants") against a federal agency (hereinafter "Agency"). At the time of the events giving rise to the complaints, both Complainants were employed as Emergency Management Specialists in the positions of Regional and National Tribal Government Liaisons at the Agency's First Responder Network Authority. Complainants performed their duties remotely from locations in California, with one working from Pauma Valley and the other from Thousand Palms. Both individuals commenced employment with the Agency in October 2015, initially hired at the Pay Band IV level.

## FORMAL COMPLAINTS AND PROTECTED GROUNDS

On June 13, 2019, each Complainant independently filed comprehensive formal EEO complaints alleging unlawful discrimination, harassment, and retaliatory actions by the Agency. The complaints identified three protected bases for discrimination: race (specifically, Native American status), national origin (Native American ancestry), and sex. Additionally, Complainants alleged that they experienced reprisal for engaging in prior protected EEO activity. It should be noted that Complainants subsequently withdrew their claims of sex discrimination during the formal hearing process, narrowing the focus of their allegations to the remaining protected bases.

## SUBSTANTIVE ALLEGATIONS - DISCRIMINATORY PAY AND COMPENSATION

The primary allegation centers on systematic discrimination in pay and compensation. Complainants assert that since their initial hiring, they have been subjected to disparate treatment regarding salary negotiations and pay band assignments. Complainant 1 specifically alleges that during pre-employment negotiations, Agency representatives informed him that the Agency would only offer him 70 percent of his previous wages and stated that he could "accept this offer or decline it entirely." Complainant 1 further contends that non-Native employees did not encounter similar constraints during their hiring negotiations. Complainant 2 claims that the Agency failed to properly account for her prior salary history and teaching wages in calculating her initial offer, instead providing compensation based solely on her current tribal salary.

Both Complainants assert that they performed substantially identical work to peers who were hired at the Pay Band V level, yet they themselves were hired at the lower Pay Band IV level and received compensation at the lowest tier available. This disparity in pay classification created a cascading barrier to career advancement, as Complainants later discovered that senior leadership positions were available exclusively to employees at the Pay Band V level.

## MANAGEMENT RESPONSE TO PAY DISPARITIES

In April 2018, the Executive Director of the Public Safety Advocacy Directorate directed Complainants' supervisor ("Supervisor 1") to conduct a comprehensive review addressing wage and pay band disparities within the organization. However, after the review was completed, both the Executive Director and a Senior Director took no corrective action and failed to acknowledge that such a review had ever been commissioned or completed.

During a telephone conversation on May 31, 2018, the Senior Director claimed to have no knowledge of the April 2018 review. Subsequently, Complainants learned from Supervisor 1 that the Senior Director was, in fact, fully aware of the review and its contents. In January 2019, the Executive Director acknowledged to Supervisor 2 (Complainants' subsequent supervisor) that he had received the completed review but stated that he felt no institutional obligation to act upon its findings or recommendations.

## PERSONNEL POLICIES AND PROCEDURAL FAILURES

On June 15, 2018, Complainants inquired with the Deputy Chief Administrative Officer regarding hiring authorities and direct hiring procedures available within the Agency. They were informed that the Agency "does not have established personnel policies and practices." The Deputy Chief Administrative Officer and Chief Administrative Officer further failed to advise Complainants of their rights to pursue formal EEO processes to address and remediate their allegations of discriminatory compensation.

## ORGANIZATIONAL RESTRUCTURING AND HARASSMENT ALLEGATIONS

In June 2018, Complainants underwent a reorganization process that they characterize as deliberately designed to "bury" them more deeply within the organizational hierarchy. Complainants allege that this restructuring was undertaken in an effort to provoke them to voluntarily resign from their positions and, additionally, served as a mechanism to "limit any escalation of human resources issues originating from the tribal team."

## DISCRIMINATORY WORKING CONDITIONS AND RESOURCE DENIAL

Since June 2018 on an ongoing basis, Complainants have been required to develop and maintain separate strategic plans, while none of their non-Tribal team peers have been subjected to similar requirements. Additionally, Complainants were precluded from accessing resources on an equal basis with their counterparts. They were required to coordinate all activities through a separate, discriminatory process to access resources and tribal shareholders. This included being compelled to engage with contracting staff and tribal shareholders exclusively through a designated "gatekeeper" and being prohibited from participating in certain meetings. An Agency Contracting Officer's Representative was mandated to attend all daily engagements between contractors and Complainants.

## HOSTILE CONTRACTOR TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT INACTION

Between June 2018 and February 2019, Complainants and other Agency officials repeatedly notified senior leadership that tribal team members were experiencing hostile and discriminatory treatment from officials employed by an Agency contractor. Despite these persistent complaints, senior leadership neither remedied the situation nor took disciplinary action against the contractor. Instead, senior leadership allegedly condoned the contractor's conduct and continued to place Complainants in hostile working environments with these contractor officials.

## PUBLIC RECOGNITION FAILURE

On May 5, 2019, the Agency issued a public social media communication thanking local, state, and federal workers for their service and dedication. Notably, this communication failed to acknowledge or reference Complainants and other tribal team members, despite their contributions to the Agency's mission.

## ADMINISTRATIVE PROCEEDING AND APPEALS

Following investigation, Complainants received copies of investigative files and requested hearings before EEOC Administrative Judges. The complaints were assigned to different Administrative Judges in the Commission's Washington Field Office. Both Complainants' shared legal counsel filed consolidation motions, which the Agency opposed. One Administrative Judge denied consolidation while the other did not address the motion. Both Administrative Judges issued separate summary judgment decisions concluding that no discrimination or unlawful retaliation had been established. The Agency subsequently implemented these decisions as final actions. Both Complainants have filed appeals from these determinations.
