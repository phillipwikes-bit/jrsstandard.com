Complainant initiated EEO contact on October 12, 2018. On June 6, 2019, Complainant filed an EEO complaint alleging that the Agency discriminated against him and subjected him to a hostile work environment on the bases of national origin (Romanian), age (48 [YOB: 1971]), and reprisal for prior protected EEO activity (HS-ICE- 21417-2012 and HS-ICE-22663-2012) under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967 when: 1. On July 26, 2018, Supervisor denied Complainant’s request to attend a concert with Complainant’s daughter; 2. On July 27, 2018, Supervisor asked Complainant to return to work after Complainant had already left for the day; 3. On October 1, 2018, Complainant became aware of pay discrepancies made by his supervisor; and 4. On October 12, 2018, Supervisor admonished Complainant several times in his Performance Work Plan (PWP) and rated Complainant in an area which was not in his official PWP. The Agency conducted an investigation into the complaint. At the conclusion of the investigation, the Agency provided Complainant with a copy of the report of investigation and notice of his right to request a hearing before an Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Administrative Judge (AJ). Complainant timely requested a hearing. On June 7, 2021, the AJ assigned to the case issued an Order to Show Good Cause as Complainant failed to provide a completed investigative affidavit in response to the EEO investigator's multiple requests. According to the AJ, Complainant also failed to file the affidavit pursuant to an order the AJ issued on May 11, 2021. When no affidavit was filed, the AJ ordered Complainant to provide a statement by June 17, 2021 stating why there was good cause for failing to submit the completed affidavit. On June 21, 2021, Complainant emailed the AJ stating his apologies but still provided no affidavit. On June 22, the AJ dismissed the complaint from the EEOC hearing process and remanded it to the Agency to issue a Final Agency Decision (FAD) as a sanction for Complainant’s failure to comply with the AJ’s orders. Consequently, on August 10, 2021, the Agency issued a FAD pursuant to 29 C.F.R. § 1614.110(b). The Agency concluded that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected him to discrimination as alleged. The Agency analyzed Complainant’s disparate treatment allegations raised in claims 1, 3, and 4 under the three-part evidentiary scheme fashioned by the Supreme Court in McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green, 411 U.S. 792 (1973) and determined that management articulated legitimate nondiscriminatory reasons for the challenged actions. Regarding claim 1, the Agency noted Supervisor’s statement that Complainant did not request time off but decided to report to work; and that Complainant was not treated differently than DO 1. ROI at 77-80. Regarding claim 3, the Agency noted Supervisor’s assertion that DO 2 had been available in the office when Supervisor had discovered the AUO discrepancy, and therefore, Supervisor was able to discuss the change with DO 2. Supervisor denied that he treated Complainant differently based on Complainant’s protected classes. ROI at 84-5. Regarding claim 4, the Agency noted Supervisor’s assertion that he did not admonish Complainant in his PWP or rate him in areas which were not included on Complainant PWP. The Agency found that Complainant failed to prove by a preponderance of the evidence that management’s legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons were pretexts for discrimination. Nor did Complainant provide evidence that the Agency’s actions were based on his protected classes. The Agency analyzed Complainant’s remaining claims, including the non-discrete allegation raised in claim 2, along with the discrete acts raised in each of the remaining claims, under the legal standard for harassment. The Agency ultimately found that Complainant failed to prove that the Agency subjected him to an unlawful, discriminatory, hostile work environment. According to the Agency, the record did not contain evidence demonstrating that management’s actions were motivated by a discriminatory intent, or that Complainant was subjected to unwelcome conduct based on his membership in a protected class. Accordingly, asserted the Agency, Complainant’s allegations did not constitute the kind of severe or pervasive harassment that Title VII was designed to prevent. The Agency concluded that Complainant failed to prove that management’s actions bore a relationship to his claimed protected bases. The Agency also observed that the record did not support Complainant’s claim that he was subjected to a hostile work environment since there was no evidence demonstrating that the alleged harassing conduct was based on those protected bases. Nor did the Agency subject Complainant to a hostile work environment based on a discriminatory animus. This appeal followed. However, neither Complainant nor the Agency submitted an appeal statement.
