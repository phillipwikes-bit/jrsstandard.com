# What this demonstration does and does not show

**DRAFT 2026-10-01. Shown on screen during the demonstration itself, not only in an appendix.**

1. **Records.** Five fictional supplier-access exception records. No real record from any organization has been evaluated.
2. **Who built the records.** An AI model from the same family as the Engine wrote them to a designed list of properties. They may share the Engine's blind spots, and that risk has not been reduced.
3. **Reference.** The expected outcomes are the properties the records were built to have. No person has labelled these records. When Phillip Wikes, the creator of JRS, labels records, agreement with him will show conformity to his interpretation, not independent correctness.
4. **Mode.** Everything shown is a recorded replay of one live test run on 2026-10-02 (10 calls, USD 0.035). It is not a new run.
5. **Scale.** Ten calls on five records. This shows the Engine connects and returns the documented format. It does not establish accuracy, stability across repeated runs, or performance on any real workflow.
6. **Injection.** Two records contain hostile instructions. The Engine ignored both, but it did not flag them. Two cases cannot establish general resistance to prompt injection.
7. **What the Engine judges.** Whether the record documents its basis, reasoning, reconstructability, evidence and chronology. It does not decide whether the approval was right, lawful or compliant.
8. **Known limits of this version.** It returns written notes, not quoted evidence spans. It is limited to 8,000 characters, and the evaluation package rejects longer records. It uses one model (`claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`), and changing the model changes the system under test.
9. **Data.** In the evaluation package, nothing is written to JRS systems. Record text is sent to the model provider under the buyer's own account and terms.
10. **Not for.** Employment, housing, lending, insurance, medical or legal decisions, or any live consequential decision.
