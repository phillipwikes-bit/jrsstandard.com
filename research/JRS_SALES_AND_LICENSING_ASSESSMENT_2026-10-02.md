## 3. What does a buyer get that the public cannot download?

Your checklist asks for one honest paragraph. Here it is.

**Already readable by anyone:** the standard, codebook, training and guides; the Engine source and its system prompt; the research manuscripts; the detection answer key; and the sale strategy.

**Not obtainable by download:**
1. **Legal permission.** Being able to read code is not permission to use it. With no LICENSE file, default copyright applies to whatever is protectable (*Inference*; a counsel question). Protection is weakest exactly where a buyer might look first: methods and ideas are not protected by copyright, and copyright in AI-generated code is uncertain (F-11).
2. **The name.** The JRS and DRR names and their goodwill, once the marks are filed. They are unfiled today.
3. **The research base as a whole:** the panel, the consents, the study records and the credibility of an external evidence base. A competitor cannot quickly re-create 36 full-set reviewers across 16 countries.
4. **You:** your know-how, your time during a transition, and the trade-press authorship.
5. **Speed:** months saved compared with building an evidence-backed review standard internally.

**What follows (*Inference*).** The sellable asset is mainly the **named, research-backed standard plus its creator**, not secret software. The buyer with the clearest reason to pay is therefore a vendor that already sells "defensible" documentation and wants an external, research-based measure to stand behind that claim. That makes the research's credibility (R2) worth more than any Engine feature. It also means engineering work such as 0.1.1, Manifest polish or new gates adds little sale value until a buyer asks for it.
