In July, you suggested we reconnect following publication of my article in Compliance & Ethics Professional. With "When the Record Cannot Speak for Itself" appearing in the November issue, I wanted to follow up and propose a separate article for Compliance Today.

The proposed article, "Testing Employment Files Before They Become Evidence," is coauthored with Tanvi Pokhriyal, an organizational psychologist with more than 15 years of experience in human resources and organizational development.

It draws on an original study of 20 adjudicated employment matters examining whether the documentation supporting consequential workplace decisions could withstand independent scrutiny. Records classified as having material documentation deficiencies were associated with adverse findings in 75% of cases, compared with 16.7% of records classified as complete. The findings are exploratory, but they raise important questions about how organizations review and preserve employment-decision evidence.

The article translates those findings into practical guidance for compliance professionals, including a structured documentation review, evidence-verification checkpoints, independent sampling, and remediation procedures that can be incorporated into existing compliance programs. For healthcare organizations, the review applies directly to workforce actions such as HIPAA sanctions and discipline that follows a compliance hotline report.

Unlike my CEP article, which examines the broader risks of AI-assisted documentation, this piece focuses on original employment research and a specific, actionable review process. It is unpublished and is no longer under consideration by ISACA.

I would welcome your thoughts on whether this would be a suitable contribution to Compliance Today. I'd be happy to send the manuscript for your consideration.
