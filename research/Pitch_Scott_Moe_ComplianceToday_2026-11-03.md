In July, you suggested we reconnect following publication of my article in Compliance & Ethics Professional. With "When the Record Cannot Speak for Itself" appearing in the November issue, I wanted to follow up and propose a separate article for Compliance Today.

The proposed article, "Testing employment files before they become evidence: Lessons for healthcare," is coauthored with Tanvi Pokhriyal, an organizational psychologist with more than 15 years of experience in human resources and organizational development.

It draws on an original study of 20 adjudicated employment matters from federal agencies, unemployment boards, courts, and tribunals, examining whether the documentation supporting consequential workplace decisions could withstand independent scrutiny. None of the matters comes from healthcare, which I think makes the findings more useful to your readers rather than less. Hospitals face the same forums, including EEOC charges, unemployment appeals, arbitration, and the courts, and those forums ask the same questions of a nurse's file as of anyone else's. In the pilot, six of eight records with material documentation gaps were associated with adverse findings, compared with two of twelve complete records. The findings are exploratory, but they raise important questions about how organizations review and preserve employment-decision evidence.

The article translates those findings into practical guidance for compliance professionals, including six lessons from the cases, a structured documentation review, evidence-verification checkpoints, independent sampling, and remediation procedures. It closes with a worked example of a HIPAA privacy sanction file.

Unlike my CEP article, which examines the broader risks of AI-assisted documentation, this piece focuses on original employment research and a specific, actionable review process. It is unpublished and is no longer under consideration by ISACA.

I would welcome your thoughts on whether this would be a suitable contribution to Compliance Today. I'd be happy to send the manuscript for your consideration.
