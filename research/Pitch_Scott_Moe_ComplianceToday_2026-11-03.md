In July, you suggested we reconnect following publication of my article in Compliance & Ethics Professional. With "When the Record Cannot Speak for Itself" appearing in the November issue, I wanted to follow up and propose a separate article for Compliance Today.

The proposed article, "Testing Employment Files Before They Become Evidence," is coauthored with Tanvi Pokhriyal, an organizational psychologist with more than 15 years of experience in human resources and organizational development.

The article starts from a simple observation: the forums that test healthcare employment files, including EEOC charges, unemployment appeals, arbitration and the courts, ask the same questions of every sector. Who saw it, when, under which policy, and after what warning. It draws on an original study of 20 adjudicated employment matters from federal agencies, unemployment boards, courts and tribunals, which examined whether the documentation behind consequential workplace decisions could withstand independent scrutiny. In that exploratory pilot, six of eight records with material documentation gaps were associated with adverse findings, compared with two of twelve complete records.

The article draws six lessons from those cases that carry over directly to healthcare, among them that a summary cannot replace a firsthand witness, that a prior warning must match the conduct, and that an organization must retain the evaluator's records. It then turns them into practical guidance for compliance professionals: a structured documentation review, evidence-verification checkpoints, independent sampling, and remediation procedures. The review applies to HIPAA sanctions, discipline that follows a hotline report, and other workforce actions that may later be examined.

Unlike my CEP article, which examines the broader risks of AI-assisted documentation, this piece focuses on original employment research and a specific, actionable review process. It is unpublished and is no longer under consideration by ISACA.

I would welcome your thoughts on whether this would be a suitable contribution to Compliance Today. I'd be happy to send the manuscript for your consideration.
