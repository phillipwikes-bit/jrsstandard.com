You kindly asked about my other current manuscripts, and I'd like to submit a second article to SocioEconomic Challenges:

"Socioeconomic Challenges of AI-Drafted Decision Records: Can Professional Reviewers Detect Unsupported Conclusions?"
Authors: Phillip Wikes and Ubayet Hossain, FRM

The paper reports a pre-registered study in which sixteen credentialed experts in eleven countries assessed whether conclusions in AI-drafted decision records were supported by the evidence in the record. It examines what reviewer performance means for how institutions design and resource human review of AI-drafted decisions, and who bears the cost when review fails. It has been prepared in the journal's format.

A few points for transparency:

1. The manuscript has not been posted as a preprint and is not under consideration elsewhere. An earlier version was declined by another journal on scope, without peer review.
2. Every participant was a credentialed expert in compliance, audit, human resources, investigations, privacy, records management or law, who volunteered in a professional capacity and gave informed consent. The documents they reviewed were constructed for the study, and the manuscript's Informed Consent Statement describes the arrangements in full.
3. The study materials, aggregate data and analysis scripts are available from the authors on reasonable request. Individual participant responses are not shared publicly, because participants consented to aggregate use only.
