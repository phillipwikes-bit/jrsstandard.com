I hope you're well. Thank you again for inviting my work. I've just emailed you a second manuscript, co-authored with Ubayet Hossain, FRM: "Socioeconomic Challenges of AI-Drafted Decision Records: Can Expert Reviewers Detect Unsupported Conclusions?"

I think it's a strong fit for SocioEconomic Challenges. Employers and public bodies are now using AI to draft the records behind decisions on jobs, benefits and investigations, and they rely on a human reviewer to catch weak ones. Our study tests whether that safeguard actually works.

In a pre-registered study, sixteen credentialed experts in eleven countries made 384 independent judgments. As a panel they caught unsupported records 83.9 percent of the time, clearing our target even at the lower end of the confidence interval. Individually, though, they ranged from 37.5 to 100 percent. In practice that means whether a flawed decision gets caught can depend on who happens to read the file, a cost that falls hardest on the people affected and on smaller institutions with fewer resources.

The paper builds on recent work in your journal, including van der Nest and Sheik, Oluka, and Mushkudiani et al., and it complements Stacyann Young's public-records paper, which is already with you. Together the two studies look at the same problem from both sides: real documents, and an independent expert panel.
