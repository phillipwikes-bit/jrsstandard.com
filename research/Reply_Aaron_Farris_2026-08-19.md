## 1. He is not "nothing to offer," and the distinction matters

The dismissal is close but not exact, and the inexact part is where the risk sits.

**What he actually has that is adjacent to your work.** Fieldborne Control Plane is described
as separating evidence from interpretation, preserving source links and limitation language,
documenting authorization decisions, and producing reconstructable review records. Controlled
claim states, provenance records, human authorization, rejection logic, audit trails. That is
not a paraphrase of DRR. It is the same failure being attacked one layer down: **you built a
review standard, he is building a capture-and-provenance system.** Those are complements, not
competitors, and the relationship between them is the one every standard eventually has with
its implementers.

**Where the asymmetry is.** You have 61 participations across 58 people, blinded panels,
pre-registered criteria, an answer key, chance-corrected reliability, and a co-authored
article at final editorial stage with a publisher. He has a research prototype under
"external software-engineering evaluation," a pilot in preparation, and his own disclaimer
that the software is "not production ready, regulator approved, clinically validated." A
methodology comparison between those two positions transfers information in one direction.

**So the accurate version of your read is not "nothing to offer." It is "nothing to offer
*yet*, and a specific reason to keep distance now."**

## 2. Three risks, in order of how much they can cost you

### Risk 1: QCE. This is the one that matters.

From his About section, verbatim: he maintains "QCE, a coherence-based research framework in
Quantum Measurement, Biological Coherence, and Collapse Probability," whose objective is "to
test whether structured Biological Coherence Signals correlate with measurable statistical
deviations in quantum measurement distributions under blinded, auditable, pre-registered
conditions."

**Be precise about what is wrong here, because the sloppy version of the objection is wrong.**
His stated method is not sloppy: null model comparison, empirical p-values, threshold
comparison, pre-registration, explicit rejection criteria, independent replication. On form,
that is more rigour than most people bring. He also discloses plainly that QCE is not
empirically validated.

The problem is not rigour. **It is that consciousness-affects-quantum-measurement research
sits in a field with a long and contested history, and reception risk attaches to anyone
standing next to it.** Your entire asset class is evidentiary credibility: a standard about
whether records can be trusted, heading into a compliance publication and, separately, a
possible sale. A reviewer, an editor, or an acquirer who searches your co-signatories and
finds a methodology comparison with a quantum-biological-coherence project does not evaluate
that on the merits. They downgrade you and move on.

**The cost is asymmetric.** Associating with you raises his credibility. Associating with him
lowers yours. That asymmetry is the whole analysis, and it holds even if QCE turns out to be
entirely sound.

### Risk 2: the blind

`research/IP_SALE_TRACKER.md:155` and `research/Path_to_Sale_Action_Plan.md:77`, standing:
**"Protect the blind. Nothing shared reveals the Arm B method or the arm split."**

"Comparing methodologies" is exactly the conversation in which the arm split leaks. Not
through carelessness, through ordinary technical candour: one sentence about what the
comparison condition was and it is gone. Until Arm B closes, your methodology is not
available for comparison with anyone outside the study.

### Risk 3: reviewer capture

He wrote: "preparing a bounded Pilot 001 with a **separate independent methodology-review
track**," and his About section says he is preparing "a bounded **paid** pilot."

Read those two together. The plausible ask underneath the friendly framing is that you
populate or lend standing to the independent review track of a pilot he is commercialising.
That is a real request wearing the clothes of a peer exchange. It costs you your independence
as a reviewer and hands him third-party validation, and he has not asked for it in terms, so
you would be agreeing to something never actually put to you.

**None of this requires him to be acting in bad faith.** He is a founder with a small network
looking for credible adjacency. That is a normal thing to want. It is also a thing you should
decline politely and without explanation.

## 3. What he could legitimately be worth later

Do not burn it. There are two futures where he is useful:

1. **After Arm B closes and the standard is published**, a tooling vendor implementing JRS
   checks in a control plane is a distribution channel. Standards spread through
   implementers.
2. **He is genuinely inside the problem.** People who can talk about rejection logic and
   authorization records without needing it explained are not common, and there are few of
   them in your network.

The correct posture is therefore **warm, unhurried, closed on specifics, and dated**, not
cold.
